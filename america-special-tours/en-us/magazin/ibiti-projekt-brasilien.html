The Ibiti Project: Regeneration in the Heart of Brazil

A model for the future: The Ibiti project shows how nature conservation, tourism and social responsibility go hand in hand in Brazil. Learn more now!

07.04.2026

In the south-east of Brazil, in the green hills of Minas Gerais, there is a place where nature and man enter into a new relationship. The Ibiti project is not a classic nature reserve, but a vision that goes far beyond environmental protection – an interplay of regeneration, community and quiet, sustainable hospitality. What began as a private initiative is now one of the most interesting rewilding projects in South America and a model for how nature conservation and social commitment can go hand in hand.

The idea of a new balance

When Renato Machado saw the deserted areas around the old Fazenda do Engenho in the early 1980s, he asked himself an unusual question: What if you gave the country the time to become wild again? What began as a personal heart project is now a landscape in transition. For decades, Barreto gradually bought plots of former cattle pastures and coffee plantations to return them to nature. A contiguous reserve of more than 6,000 hectares emerged from this piece by piece, which today is in the focus of regeneration. The vision: restoring nature, strengthening community – and showing that sustainable luxury is not at odds with it.

Where nature returns

Whoever travels through Ibiti sees traces of a quiet but visible return. Forests that were once deforested close again. Rivers that seemed dried up flow again. And animals that were thought to be missing there are returning. Above all, the Northern Muriqui, the largest monkey in South America and symbol of the Brazilian Atlantic Forest. Together with biologists and environmental institutes, its population is specifically supported here – as well as the return of endangered bird species, belt animals, tapires and even pumas, which have now been sighted again. Around 96 percent of the site is under protection or are in rewilding processes – a measure that is extraordinary even in Brazil.

A social experiment with roots

But Ibiti is not just a natural project. It is also a social experiment that shows that sustainable development is possible in rural regions. More than 300 people from the surrounding communities are working in the project today – with fair wages, social benefits and training opportunities. A visible core of this work is the revitalized village of Vila Mogol. Once almost abandoned, it was revived as part of the project – houses restored, new workshops opened, communal spaces created. Today families and artisans live and work here. Vila Mogol is now regarded as a model for sustainable village development and was included in the international "Green List" for regenerative communities in 2025.

Luxury as an attitude

In small boutique lodges such as Engenho Lodge, Ibiti Village or Muriqui House, guests can participate in this change without disturbing it. The houses were built from local materials, blend into the landscape and offer the comfort that discerning travelers appreciate – but without abundance. Guests enjoy regional cuisine, pool and wide views of the mountains of Minas Gerais. The income from tourism flows back partly into nature conservation, education and social programmes. So each stay contributes something. We asked our local partner if there are similar projects in South America. The answer: to this extent there is nothing comparable. One more reason to take a closer look.
