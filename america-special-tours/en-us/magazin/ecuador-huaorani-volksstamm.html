Huaorani - once the most dangerous Indians at the upper reaches of the Amazon

In 1958, the Huaorani Indians living in Ecuador had their first contact with the outside world – with devastating consequences for the invaders. Today you are a peaceful people and look forward to visitors.

The Huaorani were once the most dangerous Indians on the upper reaches of the Amazon. They had one of the highest internal killing rates ever observed in a human society. They were called "Auca" by their enemies, which means in Quechua language as much as "wild" or "uncultivated". Ethnologists also first adopted this term, which is now long since frowned upon.
In 1958, five missionaries wanted to make contact with the Huaorani – and were cruelly killed by them. The world press rushed to the scene at that time, so that the Huaorani gained sad fame.
However, two women of the killed missionaries were brave enough to approach the Huaorani again and actually managed to build a friendly relationship with them. They lived with the Indians for some time and taught their children, for example.
Years later, when oil production continued to advance and approached the Huaorani area, they saw no other way at first than to attack the invaders. Soon, however, they realized how futile this struggle was. Some gave up, the others disappeared into the jungle.

Malnutrition due to Sessibility

The Huaorani still live in piled buildings covered with palm fronds or tin roofs. Or they live in three by six or four by eight meters of buildings, which have two entrances opposite and are windowless. Chonta tribes bound together with lianas form the construction of the house. The roof of these huts reaches to the ground and is covered with palm fronds.

So today they are sedentary and therefore often suffer from malnutrition, as there is little game left in their areas. Sometimes they can compensate for the lack of iron by eating large ants and other insects. Otherwise, there are cookbananas, sweet potatoes, non-toxic manioc (root nodules) and fish on the menu.

They don't know how to store food, so they are busy daily in finding food. However, due to the lack of vitamins, they often suffer from paradontosis and caries. Positive: Since the Huaorani have been in contact with civilization, infant mortality has declined significantly. The Huaorani are armed with blow pipes and spears up to two and a half meters long, which are decorated with colors or feathers and can therefore be distinguished from the Huaorani.

Today the Huaorani are peaceful and they enjoy visitors. They live in an area of more than 10,000 square kilometers. The number of tribesmen is about 600.
