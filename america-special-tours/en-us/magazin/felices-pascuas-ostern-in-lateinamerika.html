Felices Pascuas - Easter in Latin America

The "Semana Santa" in Latin America offers an atmospheric celebration every year around the Easter week. Literally translated, it means "Holy Week" and usually lasts from Palm to Easter Sunday. With Christmas, Easter is probably one of the most important celebrations in predominantly Catholic South America. There is a distinction between the festivities in the different countries.

Easter in Peru

All Easter processions take place in Peru from Palm Sunday. During this time, many areas are in a state of emergency, as numerous cities turn into a sea of flowers. A famous example of this is the city of Tarma, which translates as "pearl of the Andes." Here, petals are scattered on the streets to form images representing motifs from the Andes. In Huaraz, the holidays are celebrated with elaborate parades. For this, an oversized statue of Christ is carried through the city. After that, hundreds of birds are released into freedom and the festival is rounded off with fireworks.

In the former center of Cusco, the "Señor de los Temblores" is thanked at this special time. This is the so - called "Lord of the Earthquakes." A statue was erected in his honor many centuries ago. A legend says that after an earthquake in 1650, the statue took on a dark color and therefore became the image of the inhabitants. Since then she has been revered as "Christ of the Earthquake".

Easter in Mexico

Similar to Peru, the traditional festival is celebrated extensively in Mexico and begins with the annual Palm Sunday procession. The solemnity lasts almost two weeks and includes numerous colorful Masses to symbolize the path of Jesus Christ's suffering. The Mexican Easter is a combination of "Semana Santa" from Palm Sunday to Easter Sunday and "Pascua", the week between Easter and the following "White Sunday". From Holy Thursday on, no bells will be ringed throughout the country and all holy images will be covered.

In addition, it is a custom for everyone to visit seven churches that day. On Good Friday, so-called passion games are performed in many places, which are usually so elaborate that they last several days. These performances include Christ's path of suffering, his capture, condemnation, the Passion Way, death on the cross and finally the resurrection. The largest Easter procession in Mexico is celebrated in the district of Iztapalapa on Good Friday. As the culmination of the ceremony, the crucifixion of Jesus Christ is reproduced at the so-called Star Hill "Cerro de la Estrella". Every year, up to two million visitors flock to the mountain to participate in the traditional event. It was first performed in 1843.

Easter is one of the most important celebrations in Mexico, and ancient Indian rituals are fused with Catholic traditions. The streets are solemnly decorated and people wear colorful hats. Instead of colorful Easter eggs, children usually receive a piñata, a colorful figure made of cardboard mesh filled with sweets. She is hung up on a tree and her eyes are attached to the children. You get a stick in your hand to hit the piñata and thus get to the sweets. The Easter holidays are also used for family visits.

Easter in Ecuador

In Quito, the capital of Ecuador, many traditions and celebrations are held in the "Semana Santa". At this time you can get to know Ecuador from its spiritual side. Already on Palm Sunday, Quito's churches are ceremonially decorated and the crosses decorated with floral garlands. The main spectacle takes place in the middle of Holy Week, a famous custom called "Arrastre de Caudas". The clergy process through the city in heavy, meter - long "penitents. " These heavy garments represent the remission of sins. On this day a highlight hunts the next, because in the afternoon also the procession of light takes place. For this purpose, a two-hour parade through Quito's center is held. Figures carry traditional lanterns and altar candles. They are accompanied by the penitents, the veiled "Cucuruchos" with the pointed huts and the "Almas Santas", the so-called "holy souls". At Easter, Quito is a real feast.
