Discover Chilean wine on a round trip

In Chile, the Andes are only 400 kilometers away from the Pacific – ideal climatic conditions for excellent wines. Chile is already regarded as the Bordeaux of South America. Take América Special Tours on a trip to Chile's finest wines.

Few countries offer such good conditions for wine growing as Chile. Evenly dry, warm climate and rare vine diseases make the best grapes grow in Chile. The Chilean soil is a mineral-rich, dry base due to the Andean rubble that was swept from the rivers into the valley. If it rains, usually in the winter months. 90% of the vineyards are located in Valle Central, which is divided into four subregions: Maipo, Rapel, Curicó and Maule, and lies between the Andes and the coastal cordilleras. While the temperatures are evenly warm during the day, the values fall in part at night. This cooling keeps the grapes fresh.

Wine from the Maipo Valley

The cradle of Chilean wines is located about 40 kilometers from the capital Santiago de Chile: the Maipo Valley, where Spanish conquistadors once planted the first wine plantations during the colonial period. In the Maipo Valley, wine has been cultivated since the 16th century – which makes the valley the oldest and most important wine-growing area in the country. The valley of the Maipo is considered to be the wine valley par excellence.

Climate and topography make it possible to grow different grape varieties at different heights. Especially the grapes for the Cabernet Sauvignon, which enjoys worldwide popularity, thrive in the Maipo Valley. In addition to the elegant red wine, mainly dark grape varieties such as Merlot and Syrah grow in the valley of Maipo. But the white Chardonnay grape also thrives here. Due to the Mediterranean climate with mild winters and warm, dry summers, smooth wine matures with a lot of fruit aroma that can be drunk young.

Chile's large, traditional wineries such as Concha y Toro (the largest winery in Chile), Baron Philippe de Rothschild, Santa Rita and Cousiño Macul are located in the Maipo Valley. On a visit to the Maipo ValleysOf course, the heart of Chilean wine-growing must not miss a detour into one of the wineries. Let yourself be guided through the lushly covered vines, learn exciting things about the wine growing of Chile, smell the scent of the ripening grapes. Coronation of a trip to the world of Chilean wine is a tasting of the same.

Wine paradise Cachapoal Valley

Crossed by the river of the same name, one hundred kilometers south of Santiago de Chile stretches the most prestigious and internationally famous wine-growing region in Chile: Valle Cachapoal. Also known as the valley of Rancagua, Cachapoal is home to unique wines thanks to its low altitudes in some areas of the coastal cordillera. Carménère and Cabernet Sauvignon are mainly cultivated, but Merlot is also occasionally found. Cachapoal Valle red wine stands for a flowery to spicy, elegantly sweet bouquet and a full-bodied taste.

The temperate climate of the region contributes its part to the exquisite quality of the noble drops. In the direction of the coastal mountains, the near sea promises gentle, cooling winds that allow a mild warm climate. Temperatures – made for the typical fruity Carménère. A special wine experience awaits you on a visit to the Cachapoal Valley. Enjoy a taste experience that is unparalleled.

The Colchagua Valley: Home of the best red wines

Colchagua, "place of the small lagoons", is known for its wealth of traditions and fine red wines. A visit to the famous wine route of Colchagua, which combines prized wineries, is a must for every wine fan during a trip to Chile. A ride with the Tren del Vino, the wine train, in a distinguished atmosphere is a unique attraction of this wine region. The old salon train slowly creeps through miles of vines. Here the grapes for Pinot Noir, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot thrive. In addition to great red grape varieties, there are also fine-bodied rosé wines in the Colchagua Valley. Although only about 20 percent of the total area is cultivated, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc matured there regularly achieve top qualities, they are even popular worldwide. White, red, rosé – the Colchagua Valley is the best place for wine lovers.

Cool white wines in the Casablanca Valley

The Casablanca Valley extends between the Andes and the Coastal Cordillera. Thanks to the proximity to the sea, there is a cooler climate, the strong morning mist requires constant humidity. Ideal conditions for slow ripening white grapes. Thus, almost three quarters of the cultivation is determined by the popular Chardonnay. Even the Riesling grape, which is actually rare in Chile, enjoys excellent climatic conditions in the Casablanca valley. Today it is even considered one of the most important valleys for the production of white wine. Characteristic for the wines are a bright, pale yellow color and delicate fruity aromas reminiscent of lemons and pineapples.
