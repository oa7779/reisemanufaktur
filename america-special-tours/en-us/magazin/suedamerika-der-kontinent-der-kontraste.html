South America - the continent of contrasts and contrasts

Latin America is a political-cultural term that distinguishes the Spanish and Portuguese-speaking countries of America from the English-speaking countries. The 20 countries of Latin America have an area of about 20 million square kilometers and are home to around 500 million people. The term South America, on the other hand, describes the southern part of the Double Continent America and ends geographically in the north at the Panama Strait. From a cultural and historical point of view, Central America belongs to South America.

Christopher Columbus discovered the "New World" in 1492, laying the foundation stone for the colonization of South America. However, this was done at the expense of the local population. The three high cultures of the Inca, Maya and Aztecs were partly forced by the Spaniards and Portuguese. The most important concern of the colonial lords was to export valuable raw materials to Europe.

Numerous testimonies of these long since disappeared cultures are today UNESCO World Heritage sites and attract millions of tourists every year. Above all, the ruined city Machu Picchu in Peru, one of the best preserved Inca cities. Majestially, "the city in the clouds is enthroned in the midst of the Andes over the jungle. The pyramid complex of Teotihuacán in the highlands of Mexico is equally impressive. The Mayan site is especially known for its step temple and the large solar pyramid.

But the colonial powers have also left their mark in South America. One of the colonial cities is Cartagena, built in Andalusian style, with numerous palaces and a fortress ring. The historic Cusco, capital of Peru and the Inca Empire at that time, impresses with a mixture of old buildings and ruins as well as modern architecture. In the Chilean Valparaíso, the cityscape is characterized by more than 45 hills, of which you have a wonderful view over the Pacific Ocean and the colorful houses of the city.

Rio de Janeiro is one of the most beautiful cities in the world, not only because of Brazilian culture but also because of its breathtaking landscape. From the famous sugar cane to the long sandy beaches of Copacabana to the rainforest, which reaches into the city, visitors can enjoy a multifaceted cityscape.

The powerful Amazon is the water-richest river on earth and gives its name to the entire tropical rainforest and Amazon region. In this species-rich ecosystem also very special animals live, such as the Amazon dolphins, who assume a pink color in increasing age. In addition, the Amazon is home to some of the Big Five of South America. Not only in Africa are there these famous animals, but South America is proud of its great five. They are both very rare and characteristic of the continent. It's jaguar, ant bear, giant otter, Andean bear and anakonda.

The flora and fauna of the Central American states, such as Costa Rica, Panama and Nicaragua, is colorful and exotic. However, majestic volcanoes and high mountain peaks also rise above the tropical rainforest of the Caribbean. These line up along the Andes and Pacific Fire Rings across the entire west coast of the continent. Thus, the Ecuadorian Quito lies at 2,850 meters above sea level and is thus the highest capital city in the world. Lake Titicaca, with its location at 3,812 meters above sea level, also holds the record as the highest commercial navigable waters on earth.

In the Andes there are more than 99% of all tropical glaciers, a large part of which is located in Peru. Some of Argentina's ice masses reach down to sea level and thus form the eternal ice of Patagonia. The Los Glaciares National Park is known worldwide with the Perito-Moreno Glacier. The glacier tongue flows into a lake and thus shuts it off. The accumulated water empties periodically every two to four years and is a unique natural spectacle.

The Iguazú waterfalls on the border between Brazil and Argentina are no less impressive. More than 250 large and small waterfalls fall into the depth at a length of 2.7 kilometers up to 82 meters.

A nature experience of the other kind can be experienced in the Atacama Desert. The Atacama is the driest desert in the world. In some places, there has been no rainfall for decades. And yet unique animals and plants have settled here as well. Particularly remarkable are the flamingos, which have their habitat on the Salar de Atacama lake. The pink Andean birds are so adaptable that they still breed at heights of up to 4,700 meters and can withstand temperatures up to -30 °C.

Other impressive animals live on the Galápagos Islands. Around 40% of all animal species living there are endemic, that is, they do not occur anywhere else in the world. The most famous animal inhabitants of the archipelago are the giant tortoises, sea lizards and blue-footed oil.

From a culinary point of view, wine and chocolate are part of a trip to Latin America. South America is the world's largest wine exporter to Europe. Especially the slopes of the Andes offer optimal conditions for viticulture. A piece of chocolate fits perfectly with the glass of wine, because numerous cocoa plantations are located in the rainforests of South America. The sustainable cultivation and fair processing of cocoa beans are particularly important in many Latin American countries – and that's what you can taste!
