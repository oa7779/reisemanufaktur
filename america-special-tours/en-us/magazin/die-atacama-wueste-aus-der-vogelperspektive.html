The Atacama Desert from a Bird's View

Take a balloon ride with América Special Tours and Balloons over Atacama and marvel at the Atacama desert in Chile from a bird's eye view.

13.11.2019


The Atacama Desert from a Bird's View

The balloon slowly lifts off towards the sky. The view over the Atacama Desert continues. On the horizon, the glowing fireball rings in bit by bit the dawning day. The wind whistles, the first rays of sun paint over the face. It gets over you the feeling of humility, when you see this majestic landscape in the light of the rising sun.

Desert as far as the eye can see

On a trip to Chile, the Atacama Desert must not be missing. With over 1,200 kilometers, it extends along the Pacific coast across the country borders of Chile and Peru, offering a wide range of panoramic places. Not for nothing is the barren landscape one of the most popular destinations in South America and was already the location for numerous international film productions. A balloon ride offers a fantastic opportunity to experience the Atacama desert from a bird's eye view.

From mid-August to the end of December, balloon rides are always held in the morning hours. At pleasant temperatures, the Atacama Desert shows itself with the rising sun from its most beautiful side. The experience usually lasts between 2 1⁄2 and 3 hours and includes both the pick-up and the return trip to the hotel or guest house as well as a light breakfast before departure. The take-off position is determined on the basis of weather conditions only on the day of the flight. There is no specific route for ballooning: the balloon flies where the wind blows it!

The ride takes about 45 minutes and gives enough opportunity to enjoy the breathtaking panorama in peace. After the balloon ride, each passenger receives a certificate and can review what has just been experienced with a glass of champagne before going back to the accommodations.

Exceptional flower splendor

The Atacama Desert is one of the driest deserts in the world, because the mountains in the east are raining down there and in the west the development of new rain clouds is prevented by the cold Humboldt Stream. But every five to ten years a spectacular natural miracle occurs: The weather phenomenon "El Niño" is causing heavy rainfall in the desert landscape. The seeds of more than 200 different kinds of flowers, which patiently slumbered in the earth, then sprout from the ground and transform the otherwise barren desert into a true sea of colors. Violet, white and yellow flowers, but also various herbs form a colorful flower carpet and show the Atacama desert from a completely different side.
