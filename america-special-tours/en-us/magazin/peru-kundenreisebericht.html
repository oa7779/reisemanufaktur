Travel report of our client Mr. Helmut Rau

Travel report Peru: Our customer experienced unforgettable moments on his Peru round trip with América Special Tours. Read more in our travel blog!

12.07.2018

Travel report of our customer Helmut Rau

Our customer Mr. Helmut Rau already traveled South East Asia with our sister company Asia Special Tours and turned to América Special Tours when planning his Peru round trip. Our travel experts created a multifaceted tour with highlights, such as a high mountain trekking, a rainforest tour and a visit to Lake Titicaca.

Photos © Helmut Rau

Planning

Peru should become our first South America trip, among other things because it offers a great variety. The basic idea contained the elements:
- A culinary experience (cooking course, market)
- Rainforest incl. wildlife observation
- The Inca culture
- Lake Titicaca
- High mountain trekking

The contact with some specialized tour operators was quickly established (among other things also to addresses already mentioned by my brother with good experience). Travel planning and supply creation turned out to be less quickly. The required individuality seemed to be a great challenge. Either the order did not fit (theme height adjustment for trekking), or the cooking course did not correspond to our ideas, or the necessary transfers were not coordinated.
After a very good experience with an equally planned and privately conducted trip to Southeast Asia in 2014 (Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia) with the organizer Asia Special Tours, we turned to their sister América Special Tours and found it. Not only that all the required elements were also accommodated in a sequence that makes sense for travel purposes. The criterion "privately guided" (i.e. always our own guide and driver, which we had already experienced on the Southeast Asia tour as invaluable for the increased experience value) was implemented without any problems. The iterative travel design process increased the anticipation, but also the confidence in the success of a complex journey by always responding promptly to questions and desired changes.
Very well equipped with all travel documents and a very good travel description we started at the end of April 2018 in Frankfurt.

The journey
Lima

With Lufthansa to Bogota and on with Avianca to Lima arrival 1:00 local time – it was planned, but Avianca got us a night at the airport Bogota (problems with the plane and then no more take-off permit) with modest support (not even a water was available). Finally we ended up in Lima at 5:30 a.m. and were finally at the hotel at 7:00 a.m. About 30 hours without proper sleep. Our guide then explained to us that already at 8:30 the driver will pick us up for the cooking class. A little sleep wouldn't have been bad now, but we planned it that way. So take a shower and go to the cooking class.

Afterwards, typical Peruvian dishes were cooked in a small, multinational group. Christian and his crew provided interesting insights into Peruvian cuisine. And of course the prepared dishes were eaten with pleasure.

The afternoon we toured through Lima and its historic old town, before we fell into bed very tired. Because the next morning was 4:30. Unfortunately, the hotel was not able to offer even a small breakfast buffet (note: both problems – Hotel and Avianca – we reported briefly via email to América Special Tours (AST) and immediately received feedback, help and for the further trip a few goodies; great care).

The Rainforest

So get up early today, to the airport (also around this time already the usual traffic chaos in Lima) and off to Puerto Maldonado. Once there, we were welcomed by our guide – as on the whole journey – to the town branch of the lodge, where we left the big luggage and were supplied with small snacks. With the small luggage we went to the bus that took us to the river. By boat (there is no other access) we went to the lodge in the middle of the rainforest (from boat 10 min. walk).

The lodge itself is completely designed according to ecological principles and fitted into the rainforest. Our room had no window to the rainforest, so that smells, noises and the visual impression can have a direct effect on the visitor. And despite being close to nature, there was WiFi at certain times.
The next morning it was time to get up early: 4:00 a.m. to be in the right spot for wildlife watching at the right time: Kaiman, giant otters and co.

The next few days were less refreshing for me. Diarrhoea, nausea – the full program. But especially in such situations, the private form of travel proves to be a great advantage. In the lodge Cesar cared touchingly for me including special tea from some tree bark.

Cusco and the Sacred Valley

Arrived in Cusco, our Elizabeth immediately headed for the nearest pharmacy and helped me to urgently needed pharmaceutical support. That's the only way I could participate in the other program. Especially in such an exceptional situation, it has once again shown how valuable individual care can be.

The next 3 days we were accommodated in a former monastery. Both the special charm of the hotel and its staff were an experience in itself.

From there we started to various excursions in the Holy Valley of the Incas.
The fortifications in Ollantaytambo

Miscellaneous markets

To the old Inca ruin in Moray and to the salt terraces of Maras

And a rather strenuous Andean hike on heights up to 4200m and on old Inca paths.

Machu Picchu

With our excellently organized and omniscient guide Elizabeth, we went via Vistadome through an impressive scenery to Machu Picchu – certainly one of the highlights of our Peru trip. Well thought was the move to visit the Inca ruins only in the afternoon. This may be a little more sweaty – because it's warmer – but there are fewer visitors in the Inca sites.

On the next day in the morning (the regulations say so) the ascent of Wayna Picchu – that is, this distinctive pyramid-shaped mountain on all Machu Picchu images – was on the program. With the instructions of our Elizabeth, when we have to be where, in the luggage and very friendly support of the staff there in bypassing the long line (otherwise we would have been too late at the entrance) the steep steps went up to Wayna-Picchu in perfect weather. After less than an hour and with a drip-wet T-shirt we were able to enjoy the view from above. Just madness – breathtaking.

Lake Titicaca

For the transfer from Cusco to Lake Titicaca, we used the luxury coach. Although 9 hours can be long, we had an unforgettable experience due to the varied design. Various places of worship were approached, explained with expert German tour guide and the fascinating landscape offered the accompanying music.

The next morning the Titicaca lake itself was on the agenda – visit of the Uros, hike on the Llachon peninsula including a typical lunch with the inhabitants on the peninsula.
The speedboat went to the Uros on the floating reed islands. There we were told exactly how these are built, how the life of the Uros is still quite original today. The solar panels on the roof alone for a little light in the evening and for charging the smartphones show that modern civilization leaves its traces there too. We were received incredibly open and friendly and really had fun with the island residents.

The hike then quickly made it clear to us that we were moving at over 4000m above sea level – breathing was much faster. But the reward was breathtaking views of the lake and the islands nearby.

At the end of the hike we got a very tasty lunch. All the ingredients came directly from the garden. Again, the atmosphere was very open and friendly. At the end we were then packed into the usual clothes, which was not only fun for us.

Cordillera Blanca

From Lake Titicaca, the next morning (again early) was a domestic flight to Lima. A day without a fixed program was ahead of us. Unfortunately I didn't come up against the slogan "Shopping!!". Admittedly – in the past 2 weeks, this was rather rare. So a relaxed day of strolling came up.
The next morning then to the bus station, then to drive 9h to Huaraz in a luxury overland bus (full deck chairs!). First a bit tiring along the coast, but then into the mountains up to a pass height of almost 4100m. Orchards alternate with rugged rock walls or picturesque mountain lakes. Arriving in Huaraz, we moved to the hotel San Sebastian – a real pearl. A bit hidden, but centrally located, we found a very lovingly designed hotel with extremely helpful, friendly staff.
Huaraz himself doesn't offer much worth seeing, but the backdrop around it set us for the next few days – the Santa Cruz Trek. Take a long shower and enjoy the comfortable bed before going on the Andes tour the next morning.

With the minibus we went into the Cordillera Blanca. Passing turquoise blue mountain lakes, the highest mountains in Peru, rugged abysses up to 4600 m high. At the starting point at about 3500 m altitude, we were then expected to be carrying animals as well as our Scirocco – our companion horse, which should still provide us with very valuable services.

Then came the sporting part. The path went up, along wild streams and mountain lakes, partly heavily trampled by the Tragtieren, so that walking was quite laborious. In addition, the noticeably thinner air, with me also sniffing and coughing, brought us to our limits in the end. Our guide didn't have his best day for inexplicable reasons, which brought us to the campground (4200m) another detour with 200 hm extra. Petra used the services of Scirocco in the end. Arrived at the tent, our Rolando already had hot tea ready. Down jacket on, because as soon as the sun is gone, it becomes noticeably fresh, and then a simple but absolutely delicious dinner (3-course menu). Early in the cuddly down sleeping bag, which the next day promised to become exhausting.

The next day we wanted to cross the Punta Union Pass – 4750m. The weather was unfortunately not as friendly as the previous day. Thick clouds accompanied us on the partly steep ascent. After the efforts of the previous day, the pass hill demanded everything from us. Happy arriving at the top, we received a fresh gray fur shower, which once again required full concentration for the descent.

A few hundred meters deep and in a much more pleasant climate Rolando prepared us a lunch with potatoes, peas, avocado – simply heavenly. The last climb to the campground in a still thin atmosphere (4200m) was also possible. However, the grazing cows could not do anything with our tents. Several times during the night, we heard that they stumbled over the spanning lines.

The next morning compensated us for the partly unsightly weather the day before. Bright blue sky with however in the morning very fresh temperatures received us while getting up. Gloves were hot. The following descent – once again 7h – was an absolute highlight, as the pictures show.

Arriving at the exit of the national park, we had then brought about 65km in the 3 days. With some melancholy we said goodbye to our accompanying team, without which this unique experience would not have been possible.

Back to Lima, another nice dinner right on the Pacific, and then the flight home to Frankfurt.
What unforgettable 3 weeks were behind us. Not least because of the excellent support we were able to concentrate completely on the impressions that we could take along the journey. Our curiosity about foreign worlds was completely satisfied by our guides.
I'm curious to see where the next trip will take us.
Helmut Rau
