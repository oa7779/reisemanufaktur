Come along to the stormy end of the world! A cruise on Stella Australis is an unforgettable ice and glacier experience – and it’s all luxurious. Following in the footsteps of Francis Drake and Magellan, we will take you from Punta Arenas to Ushuaia into an endless glacier, fjord and mountain world. On the way, you will explore nature on shore excursions and get exciting insights into the wildlife.

1st day on the way towards Tierra del Fuego

An unforgettable journey with the “Stella Australis” or “Via Australis” through legendary waters and unspoiled nature awaits you. After check-in, you will be warmly welcomed by the captain of the ship and his crew. While you get to know your fellow passengers with a welcome drink, the ship departs and sets off for the end of the world: You sail through the mystical waters of the Strait of Magellan towards Tierra del Fuego. Your adventure has begun! Your ship – the Stella Australis – offers its passengers 100 cabins, all equipped with floor-level beds and giving you a view outside into the impressive landscape in each cabin. The cabins are divided into four categories, depending on their deck position. All cabins are equipped with private shower/WC, central heating, air conditioning, telephone for internal calls and 110 volt sockets. In spacious lounges on the expansive decks, you can listen to lectures about the history and flora and fauna of these fascinating regions. The restaurant on board serves regional but also international cuisine. Overnight on the ship.

Day 2 - The journey with the Zodiacs

At dawn, pass the Almirantazgo Fjord until you reach Ainsworth Bay. Here you can see the impressive 40-meter-high Marinelli glacier from a distance. You leave the ship and discover a beaver dam in the middle of the North Patagonian forest on a hike. On the beach you will encounter a colony of rare sea elephants – the largest seals in the world. In the afternoon, travel by Zodiacs to Tucker Island, home to numerous Magellan penguins and cormorants (in April the penguins have already migrated, then alternatively visit Brookes Bay, where you can admire a glacier). Overnight on the ship.

Day 3 – Glacier Avenue

Today you will visit the bay of the Pia Glacier. You will take a small walk to a viewpoint that, in addition to breathtaking views of the fantastic glacier and the water-floating icebergs, also shows you the great variety of regional vegetation. The origin of this glacier lies in the middle of the Darwin Mountains, and it reaches the ocean here in Pia Bay. Then the journey continues to the Beagle Canal with its majestic “Glacier Avenue”: Spain, Romanche, Germany, Italy, France and Holland – these are the names of the ice giants. Emigrants named them 100 years ago in memory of their homeland. Overnight on the ship.

Day 4: The End of the World

Today you reach the Cape Horn National Park – a natural, almost 425 meters high wall in the sea. Cape Horn was discovered on 30 January 1616 by a Dutch trade expedition organized by Isaac Le Maire and led by Captain Willem Schouten. The Cape is named after the port of Hoorn, from which the expedition set sail on 14 June 1615. This is where the Atlantic Ocean and the Pacific Ocean collide. The sea is rough, 255 days a year it rains here – the climate is influenced by the Antarctic. If you spot albatrosses here, according to legend, you will see the souls of the dead sailors who live on in the birds. In the afternoon, leave the ship again for a visit to Wulaia Bay, once one of the most important sites of Native American settlements in the region. You will wander through the North Patagonian forest of Lengas, Coigües, Canelos and ferns to a wonderful viewpoint from which you can wander your view over the bay. Overnight on the ship.

Day 5 Arrival in Ushuaia

In the morning you will arrive in Ushuaia, the largest village on Tierra del Fuego. This is where your cruise ends. On site, you have the opportunity to book optional tours. For example, you can hike through Tierra del Fuego National Park – past forests, mountains and moors – or take a trip to the lakes of Escondido and Fagnano in the north of the national park.
