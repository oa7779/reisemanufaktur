USA Rental Car Trip - The Magic of the South

USA Rental car trip: Experience the charm of the southern states on your individually assembled tour of América Special Tours. Let's get some advice now!

Let yourself be taken on a varied journey through the beautiful southern states of the USA! With the rental car, embark on the Antebellum Trail from Atlanta to the coast to the romantic colonial jewels of Savannah and Charleston. Experience here a southern backdrop as from the picture book! It is also important to explore the beautiful mountain region of the Blue Ridge and Great Smoky Mountains on unique nature adventures. On the ‘Music Highway’ you get to two famous music metropolises: Nashville, the capital of country music and Memphis, the birthplace of blues and rock ́n ́roll. Your further drive will take you through the picturesque Mississippi Delta even deeper to the south. Here, too, historic cities such as Vicksburg and Natchez shine in the splendor of the Antebellum epoch. Visiting a traditional plantation, you’ll feel the charm and elegance of the old south up close. After all, where could your Southern trip end better than in the colorful, lively city of New Orleans? Enjoy the southern hospitality, creative cuisine and unique charm of the southern states – a unique journey between the past and the modern.

1st day Atlanta - The gateway to the southern states

Welcome on your round trip through the beautiful southern states of the USA. Start your tour in Atlanta, the economic metropolis and cultural capital of the Southeast. Upon arrival at the international airport, pick up your rental car and make your own way to your luxurious hotel in Downtown. Often wrongly underestimated, Atlanta has developed into a bustling city with a glittering skyline without losing its southern charm and warm hospitality. Here the past meets modernity. The city looks back on significant historical events – from the American Civil War to the birth of the civil rights movement around Martin Luther King, Jr., the city’s most famous son. Surrounded by forests, expansive green spaces and authentic neighborhoods, the urban oasis surprises your visitors with top attractions, a lively cultural and art scene, first-class shopping and a varied gastronomy from traditional Southern cuisine to international gourmet restaurants. Overnight in Atlanta. 

Day 2 Explore Atlanta

Atlanta’s icons reflect the diversity of the city’s cultural offering – from Coca-Cola to CNN, Martin Luther King, Jr. and Jimmy Carter. The starting point of your exploration tour today is the centrally located Centennial Olympic Park, from which all the main attractions are within walking distance. In the famous World of Coca Cola, over 100 different Coke products can be tried and in the headquarters of CNN a studio tour allows a look behind the scenes of the well-known news channel. The Center for Civil and Human Rights deals with the civil rights movement in the USA, but also addresses current issues such as refugee movements and women’s rights. If you want to delve further into the history of the civil rights pioneer, visit the Martin Luther King, Jr. National Historical Park. After visiting key downtown attractions, the newly laid out Beltline Green Belt invites you to take an extended walk around Downtown and the Midtown Art District. The former railway line is now a popular local recreation area and connects many of the homey, characteristic neighbourhoods and contemporary art projects. Overnight in Atlanta.

Day 3 The Flair of the Old South along the Antebellum Trail

Today you start your car rental round trip and immediately feel the flair of the old south. About an hour east of Atlanta, the Antebellum Trail begins, where historic cities with dreamlike pre-Civil War mansions line up. Your first stop is Madison, one of the most beautiful small towns in America with a southern backdrop as from the picture book. Visit the Heritage Hall Museum House – the windows still show the scratches of the fiancées of former sugarcane and cotton barons who had checked their diamond rings on the window glass for authenticity. Follow the trail to Macon, a vibrant student city and one of Georgia’s music hotspots. Here, too, the unique Antebellum architecture impresses. The Hay House is also called the Palace of the South. Continue on to the coast and finally reach the romantic riverside town of Savannah. Overnight in Savannah.

Day 4 Mystical Savannah

Hardly any visitor will be able to escape the charm of Savannah. The elegant, traditional city is best explored on foot or by trolley bus. The historic quarter consists of 22 picturesque squares and gardens surrounded by ancient oaks, statues and beautiful antebellum villas. The idyllic Bonaventure Cemetery is one of the most beautiful cemeteries in America. Savannah is also the city in the USA with the most lived ghost encounters. On a chilling city tour, you can immerse yourself in the mystical underworld. But the city is not only historically remarkable. The hip and lively student city is constantly reinventing itself. The restored waterfront features trendy boutiques, modern galleries and exclusive restaurants. To try the delicious Southern cuisine, we recommend at lunchtime the restaurant Mrs Wilkes Dining Room - here the food tastes like grandma's. In the afternoon, the American Prohibition Museum takes you to Prohibition time in America. In the adjacent Speak-Easy Bar - as the infamous secret bars were called for this time - an introduction to cocktail art awaits you. Overnight in Savannah.

Day 5 On the Coast to Charleston

How about an extended beach walk or a swim in the Atlantic this morning before continuing your trip? Just 20 minutes outside the city’s gates is Tybee Island, Savannah’s kilometer-long house beach with its own lighthouse and bustling pier. Continue along the coast to the colonial city of Charleston, nestled in scenic hillside and plantation landscapes. The old town with around 900 listed buildings, winding alleys, dark brick houses and tall church towers are explored most stylishly in one of the horse-drawn carriages waiting for customers at Charleston City Market. In addition to the Capitol of West Virginia - whose golden dome is higher than that of the Capitol in Washington D.C. - it is also worth a visit to the Governor's Mansion, a testimony of Georgian architecture. You will continue walking to the port and across the Battery boardwalk, past cannons and monuments from the American Civil War. The historic King Street invites with its exclusive boutiques, souvenir shops, antique shops, restaurants and bars for a final stroll. Overnight in Charleston.

6th Day Time Out in the Mountains – Visiting the Biltmore Estate

Today your journey takes you from the coast to the mountainous region. Your day’s destination, Asheville, is spectacularly located in the middle of the mountains at the foot of the Blue Ridge and Great Smoky Mountains and right on the busiest panoramic road in the USA, the Blue Ridge Highway. Along the way, we recommend a detour to Chimney Rock to enjoy the view from the 96-meter-high granite rock. The entire area is characterised by breathtaking mountains, beautiful lakes and rivers, roaring waterfalls and a colorful ‘Indian Summer’. It is therefore not surprising that the art collector George W. Vanderbilt has chosen this spot of earth as his "Little Mountain Escape". The huge Biltmore Estate is still one of the largest private estates in the US and a prime example of American Golden Age architecture. The former summer residence with its own winery and overnight accommodation invites you to explore and stay. You will not only take part in a guided tour here, but also enjoy the hospitality for which the Vanderbilts were known in your hotel itself. Overnight in Asheville.

Day 7 - The Great Smoky Mountains

There are countless outdoor activities around Asheville, but art and culture lovers will also get their money’s worth in the vibrant student city. Asheville is a special place with a vibrant cultural scene and many art galleries, boutiques and antique shops. The culinary offer also leaves nothing to be desired and is reflected in the many small craft breweries and excellent star restaurants. Take a stroll through the village in the morning before heading out for your nature adventure in the Great Smoky Mountains. It is home to America’s most visited national park, known for its vast biodiversity and untouched wilderness. Nestled in this natural paradise is the luxurious tent camp Under Canvas. Designed with great passion, it offers an eco-friendly way to explore the Great Smoky Mountains without sacrificing authentic nature experiences and comfort. The luxurious glamping tents are equipped with a king size bed, a private bathroom and a terrace with lounge furniture. Enjoy the next two nights under the starry sky in the midst of wild romantic nature – a real Once in a Lifetime experience. Overnight in the Great Smoky Mountains.

8th Day Adventure in Nature

‘The Land of Blue Smoke’ – as the Great Smoky Mountains region is also called due to the blue gray haze hanging over the mountains and forests – enchants its visitors with extensive hiking and mountain biking trails, scenic rivers and lakes and spectacular waterfalls. Today promises you nature experiences á la carte. Choose for yourself how adventurous it may be. From Zipline to whitewater rafting to hikes or jeep tours of various difficulty levels in the Appalachian Mountains, the friendly staff at Under Canvas will be happy to help you organize your personal adventure in the Great Smoky Mountains. In the evening you can relax at the fireplace in front of your tent. Overnight in the Great Smoky Mountains.

Day 9 - The railway town of Chattanooga

Today, continue your journey through the Great Smoky Mountains until you reach the old railway town of Chattanooga. The small town is located in a valley on the shores of the Tennessee River and has much to offer. The main attraction of the city is the Tennessee Aquarium, the largest freshwater aquarium in the world. But the river also invites you to water sports of all kinds and the revived strolling mile Riverwalk lures you with a walk. The city’s landmark is Lookout Mountain, which you can reach with the old, steep Incline mountain railway. Nearby are the beautiful Rock City Gardens with the Lover’s Leap rock outcrop, from where you have a fabulous view of the surrounding landscapes. The absolute highlight is the visit to the underground Ruby Falls. The cave formations and the spectacular waterfall, located deep in the mountain, are accompanied by an impressive light & sound show. Throughout Chattanooga you can admire public art. Numerous restaurants, live music stages and free concerts invite you to an evening in a relaxed atmosphere. Overnight in Chattanooga.

Day 10: In the footsteps of Jack Daniel's whiskey

After your nature adventures in the last few days, cuisine and culture are on the agenda again today. On your way to Nashville, you will enjoy the oak-barreled Jack Daniel’s whiskey. You will stop at the distillery in Lynchburg, where you will learn everything you need to know about the noble drop. Then reach Nashville - Welcome to the Music City! The New York Times, Condé Nast Traveler and Travel + Leisure agree: Nashville is the top destination you simply must have seen. The capital of Tennessee, together with Memphis (Tennessee) and Muscle Shoals (Alabama), forms the so-called ‘Southern Music Triangle’. With over 120 live music stages, the city delights with a lively, varied music scene, but also offers a multifaceted offer for art and architecture lovers in the trendy neighborhoods. There’s also the up-and-coming creative gastro scene to explore – from traditional Southern cuisine to haute cuisine, there’s something for everyone. Try the hot chicken – a specialty from Nashville. Overnight in Nashville.

Day 11 Nashville - The Capital of Country Music

After Nashville, country fans from all over the world and everywhere in the city will meet passionate musicians. Nashville is a city of songwriters who dream of succeeding with their music. Explore the world capital of country music today, let yourself be drifted by live music and visit the interesting museums with a musical reference and many other attractions. How about a visit to the Country Music Hall of Fame or the Johnny Cash Museum? At RCA Studio B, music legends such as Elvis Presley, Chet Atkins and Dolly Parton once recorded their hits. In the evening, put on your own dance shoes, because when visiting a show in the famous Grand Ole Opry you will certainly not be able to keep your feet still. Overnight in Nashville.

Day 12 on the Music Highway to Memphis

Your tour will go into the next round. Drive along the Music Highway to your current day’s destination, Memphis. Memphis is the cradle of rock ‘n’ roll, and the blues has also taken its first steps here. The city’s most famous heritage is Graceland, Elvis Presley’s estate with its final resting place. Important milestones of the civil rights movement of the 1950s and 1960s also took place in Memphis. The National Civil Rights Museum provides illustrative evidence. Visit the Stax Museum, the Rock ́n ́Roll Museum or Sun Studios to discover the background of Memphis’ moving musical history. Ending an exciting day of music, stroll along Beale Street in Downtown Memphis in the evening, where you’ll find numerous blues clubs. Overnight in Memphis.

13th Day Magnificent Mansions in the Mississippi Delta

Today, leave Memphis behind and drive your rental car through the picturesque Mississippi Delta until you reach the city of Vicksburg around noon. The site was once an important setting for a battle in the American Civil War, which can still be traced in the vast Vicksburg National Military Park today. The many historical buildings away from the battlefield also take you into the glorious past of the 19th century. On your further journey, you will arrive just south of Port Gibson on the beautiful Natchez Trace Parkway, a panoramic road that runs through green landscapes and dense forests. The original path system of the Old Trace was used by the indigenous people, hunters and traders. Finally, you will reach Natchez, the oldest European settlement on the Mississippi River. Here you will also find numerous, well-preserved mansions from the Antebellum epoch. Stanton Hall, built in 1857, is considered one of the most beautiful and magnificent residences of that time. Overnight in Natchez.

Day 14 Visiting the Oak Alley Plantation

Today, continue to the capital from Louisiana to Baton Rouge. Throughout Louisiana, you’ll feel the unique mix of nations and cultures. The locals have French, Spanish and African roots, which is reflected in the mixed traditions as well as in the cuisine. The character of the city of Baton Rouge is characterized by its historical plantations, monuments and museums. Do not miss to try the fantastic Cajun cuisine in one of the numerous restaurants. On your further path along the Great River Road, which winds leisurely along the Mississippi River, you will pass time-honored estates and plantation houses. The famous Oak Alley Plantation is called the "Grande Dame" of the plantations, because it still exudes the charm and elegance of the long-disappeared old south. The driveway to the imposing mansion leads through a majestic Eichenallee. After listening to the fascinating stories on a guided tour, move into your private cottage on the property, where a typical regional dinner is already waiting for you. More southern flair is not possible! Overnight in Vacherie.

15th day Exuberant joie de vivre and pleasure in New Orleans

Where better to end your Southern Journey than in New Orleans, full of zest for life? The cosmopolitan city looks back on more than 300 years of history. The Spanish, French, African and Caribbean influences create a mix of distinctive architecture, immersive music and creative cuisine that only New Orleans knows how to serve. On every corner, the timeless city offers an opportunity to discover new things, whether it is a jazz combo or the centuries-old tram. Look forward to a city that never sleeps, where music resounds around the clock from over 400 bars, lively street corners or open-air festivals. But the city has a lot to offer not only musically, but also historically and culturally the guests get their money's worth. Admire the beautiful architecture on a tram or horse-drawn carriage ride. During a cooking class, you will learn more about the preparation of typical Creole food and immerse yourself in the living history of imaginative New Orleans cuisine. End your evening in the French Quarter and stroll through the famous Bourbon Street, where the jazz heart of New Orleans beats. Overnight in New Orleans.

16th Day Farewell from the Old South

Today it is time to say goodbye to the southern states and its warm residents. Behind you lie two exciting weeks full of music, history, impressive architecture and imposing natural landscapes. The charm and hospitality of the old south will surely live on for a long time. At the airport of New Orleans, you drop off your rental car and take your return flight full of new impressions. Good trip home!
