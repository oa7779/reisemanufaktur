Peru Luxury Tour - Enjoyment in the Land of the Incas

Luxury travel Peru: Experience pure enjoyment in the land of the Incas on an individual journey with América Special Tours. Ask now!

Peru is no longer an insider tip in the culinary scene. The Incas were already able to draw on a large repertoire of ingredients when preparing dishes. Over the centuries, the melting pot of Peruvian cuisine attracted very different cultures: The Spanish introduced various livestock, garlic and onions. Chinese migrant workers brought the rice, Japanese recipes for raw fish, African slaves a variety of spices. These diverse influences merged over the years into a wealth of creative dishes. On this trip you will taste the diversity of Peru: dine in award-winning restaurants, experience the flair of a traditional Picantería, roam the markets and taste the national drink Pisco and learn to mix cocktails from it. Of course, in addition to so much cuisine, the culture of the country is not neglected.

1st day Lima: City of Kings

Your culinary journey through Peru begins in the capital Lima, whose old town – legacy of the colonial era – has been a World Heritage Site since 1991. The “City of Kings” carries a dark legacy as the center of the Spanish Inquisition. Due to the geographical conditions, Lima’s sky is also grim: The desert city on the sea is almost always overshadowed by clouds. At the airport, you will be expected by your German-speaking guide on arrival and taken to your hotel in the city. On the way, you will learn first interesting anecdotes about Peru and its capital. Overnight in Lima

Day 2 Lima: Dining Like Bill and Hillary Clinton

In the morning, together with your guide, you will embark on an exploration of the city. First, walk across the Surquillo market. Afterwards you will go on a journey through time through around 500 years of city history. During your walk, you will begin by visiting the Archbishop's Palace and the administrative building of the Catholic Archdiocese. Continue to visit the Monastery of San Francisco. Your journey back in time takes you to the early 20th century: In 1905, the bar “El Cordano” opened. Almost every Peruvian president in the last hundred years has fed here at least once. Through the pedestrian zone of Jiron de la Union you can reach the Hotel El Bolivar, which has been part of the cityscape since 1924. Here you can try Pisco Sour, a typical Peruvian cocktail. Rest a little in the afternoon before a culinary highlight awaits you in the evening. In recent decades, Lima has become a mecca for gourmets. For you, today we go to the restaurant Huaca Pucllana. It is located in the middle of an archaeological site whose ruins are over 200 years old. Not only the view will inspire you: The Peruvian cuisine served here is so delicate that stars such as Mick Jagger, Bill and Hillary Clinton as well as the Spanish king have already arrived here. Overnight in Lima.

3rd day cooking over the rooftops of Lima

After you have already tasted many Peruvian specialties yesterday, you will learn to cook some yourself today. Before that, you take part in a fruit tasting. Your cooking class takes place with a unique view on the roof terrace of a high-rise building. The chef Yurac prepares together with you a multi-course, traditional Peruvian meal, so that you not only bring unique memories from your trip, but also recipes. You can use the afternoon for your own explorations or a digestive walk, before your chauffeur gives you dinner in the evening. Astrid y Gaston – the institution in Lima’s culinary scene. Gastón Acurio was instrumental in ensuring that Peru is now regarded by connoisseurs as an Eldorado for foodies. Together with his German wife, Acurio first ran a French restaurant before turning to the rich culinary traditions of his home country. In the meantime, the chef operates more than 30 restaurants all over the world and has success as a book author and television presenter. They dine tonight in his first restaurant, which serves exclusively tasting menus. For three hours you will enjoy a menu that tells you the history of Peru, its food culture and products. Overnight in Lima.

4th day Lonely beaches and cliffs

Before you fly over the impressive Nazca Lines tomorrow, a guided tour to the Paracas National Reserve is on the program today. It is a terran-maritime protected area with secluded beaches and worth seeing cliffs. At the end of the tour you will stop at the small interpretation center to find out more about the flora and fauna. In the area of marine fossils, you can admire Turritelas (molluscs), which are 35 to 36 million years old. The flamingos living in the reserve can be observed from the viewpoint. Then your chauffeur will take you to your hotel in Paracas. Overnight in Paracas.

Day 5 The Flight Over the Nazca Lines

When planes crossed the region for the first time in the 1920s, the occupants were not badly amazed when from the air suddenly huge pictures revealed themselves, the lines of which were scratched into the desert. Since this World Heritage site can only be truly appreciated from the air, when you arrive in the city of Pisco, you and other guests will board a light aircraft that will take you on a one-and-a-half-hour scenic flight over this fascinating site. After your exciting overflight, a highlight of a completely different kind awaits you today: You will gain an insight into the production of the national drink of Peru: Pisco (a brandy made from grapes) and of course you can also taste it. You will be greeted with a cocktail and then learn about the cultivation of the grapes on a tour of the Tacama estate and the processing of pisco in the distillery. Your visit will end with a tasting of the various Pisco and wine varieties. Overnight in Paracas.

Day 6 Island Adventures

Today you will visit the Ballestas Islands. On the way to the open sea, you will pass the National Reserve, where the emblematic desert figure “El Candelabro” is located. Arriving at the islands, you will have the opportunity to observe sea lions, pelicans, cormorants and penguins in their natural environment. Then your driver will take you back to Lima, from where you will take the flight to Arequipa, the next stop of your journey. Overnight in Arequipa.

Day 7 Arequipa - The White City

Surmounted by high volcanoes is Arequipa, the capital of the region of the same name. Prehistoric tribes and the Inca had already settled here before the region was conquered by the conquistadors in the 16th century. Around the city, which is characterised by colonial architecture, there is a wild stretch of land with snow-capped volcanoes, hot springs, gorges and deserts. Your visit will first take you to the imposing cathedral, rebuilt in Renaissance style after a fire in the mid-19th century, and the Church of Compania y Claustros. However, the most important religious building of the colonial period is the Monastery of Santa Catalina. Due to its vastness, the monastery is also called a “city within a city”. During your exploration of the city, you will have the opportunity to taste some of Arequipa’s specialties. The most famous chocolate brand that originated here is La Ibérica, a family business over a hundred years old that also produces toffee, marzipan and turrón – a sweet made of almonds, honey, sugar and egg. For lunch, head to a Picantería. You can use the afternoon for spontaneous explorations in the “White City” Arequipa. Overnight in Arequipa.

Day 8: The Taste of Cusco

Your journey will take you to Cusco by domestic flight today. The capital of the region of the same name in the Andean highlands (3,400 meters altitude) was once the dominion of the Inca Empire – and for this civilization the “navel of the world”. Numerous ruins testify to the splendor of this deserted high culture – above all, of course, the ruins on the summit of nearby Machu Picchu. In the afternoon, your guide will take you on a walk through the San Blas district with its steep, winding streets. These are lined by whitewashed houses with tile roofs, from whose balconies flower pots in all colors shine. Then visit the center of the Inca Empire, the main square “Plaza de Armas”. There you will find the cathedral, which was built on the ruins of the Inca Palace. Look closely at the painting of the Lord’s Supper. Here the artists have allowed themselves some freedom: The table is set with guinea pigs, for example. Lastly, visit Koricancha, the ancient Inca Sun Temple. Here you will have plenty of time to explore the building, whose walls are said to have been encased in gold and silver before finally making your way to your hotel. Overnight in Cusco.

9th Day Culture and Enjoyment in the Inca Valley

Near Cusco lies the sacred Urubamba Valley of the Inca, a particularly fertile strip of land where numerous archaeological sites have been discovered. This is where your journey leads you today. First, visit the craft market of the town of Pisac, which attracts many tourists. To strengthen, enjoy a delicious lunch at Hacienda Sarapampa. The farm specializes in the cultivation of the Peruvian giant corn (maíz gigante). Explore the farm and learn more about the cultivation and use of this corn variety in Peru. In order to get rid of some of the delicious calories, make a detour in the afternoon to the fortress of Ollantaytambo, which once defended the valley against invaders. For dinner, enjoy a 5-course menu in the ‘Sol y Luna’which stores in its cellar some of the best wines in the world. As an accompaniment to a Peruvian dinner, enjoy wines recommended by the sommelier. Overnight in the Sacred Valley.

Day 10 Machu Picchu and dinner on the luxury train

Today, the highlight of your Peru trip awaits you: Machu Picchu. To reach your current destination, you will first be taken to Ollayntaytambo station, from where you will take the train to Aqua Calientes. Afterwards, a minibus will take you up to the top of the mountain. However, the view of this fabled site against the backdrop of a breathtaking mountain panorama more than compensates for the somewhat more cumbersome journey. Machu Picchu is said to have been built in the fifteenth century under the reign of the Inca ruler Pachacútec Yupanqui. For the descendants of the Incas, the place is still their most sacred place today. At noon you will take a short break from your explorations in the Sanctuary Lodge Restaurant one. In the late afternoon, the shuttle bus will take you back to Aguas Calientes, from where you will return to Cusco in a very comfortable way: you will take a ride on the luxurious Hiram Bingham train, which with polished wood, brass and its heavy armchairs is reminiscent of the sweatman suits of the 1920s. On board, a gourmet dinner will allow you to duly conclude this impressive day. Overnight in Cusco.

Day 11 Farewell to the Land of the Incas

Today, your culinary exploration journey through the land of the Incas is coming to an end. Your chauffeur will take you to the airport and via Lima you will start your journey home.
