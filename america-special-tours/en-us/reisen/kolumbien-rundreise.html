Colombia Travel - From the Andes to the Caribbean

Colombia is a fascinating travel destination that is just being discovered by tourism. On this trip you will experience the multifaceted richness of Colombian nature and culture. As the only South American nation, Colombia borders both the Atlantic and the Pacific. While the Caribbean feeling prevails on the Atlantic coast, the mighty Andes with their impressive mountain landscapes run through the interior. On this trip you will get to know the diversity of Colombia: you will roam the modern metropolis of Bogotá and the magical colonial city of Cartagena. You will explore on foot picturesque villages in the Andes. And you can marvel at the Caribbean islands off the Atlantic coast from the yacht.

Day 1 Arrival in Bogotá

Your journey through Colombia begins in the state capital of Bogotá, the pride of Colombians. Already at the airport you will be expected by your guide, who will accompany you to your hotel in the city, where you can first relax from your long flight. Overnight in Bogotá.

Day 2 Explore Bogotá

On a walking tour of the city you will experience the contrast between the modern Bogotá and the historic old town. From Plaza Bolivar, the colonial “main square” with a cathedral, palace of justice, parliament and town hall, it passes through the narrow streets of the Candelaria – as the old town of Bogotá is also called. In the Museo Botero you will see an important collection of contemporary art and, of course, many works by the famous Colombian artist Fernando Botero. Another highlight is the Gold Museum, which houses the world’s most impressive collection of pre-Columbian gold jewelry. In fine weather, head to the Bogotás mountain, the Monserrate, from which you have an excellent view over the city of eight million. If you want to end the first day with a good dinner, we recommend Harry Sasson. Overnight in Bogotá.

Day 3: Via Zipaquirá to Villa de Leyva

Today you leave the capital behind and head north. In the beautiful colonial old town of Zipaquirá there are numerous buildings from the 18th century. Particularly impressive is the salt cathedral in a mine, whose cloister and chapels are completely carved from the salt rock. At the end of the Way of the Cross you reach the actual cathedral, which consists of a central nave up to 25 meters high and two side naves. Then continue on the main road to Tunja. On the way there is a stop at Sisga Reservoir, where you can treat yourself to a fried trout for lunch. Historically interested people should also make a stop at the Puente de Boyacá, the place where Simón Bolívar won the last and decisive battle against the Spanish colonial rulers in 1819. From there it is only a short way to Villa de Leyva. In colonial times, the village was the “summer residence” of the bishops. The spacious church buildings are excellently preserved and you can walk comfortably through the cobbled streets. Overnight in Villa de Leyva.

Day 4 Around Villa de Leyva

Today you will explore the surroundings of Villa de Leyva. If you like hiking, it is best to start the day with an almost one-hour walk through the hills around the place, from where you have beautiful views of the surrounding area. The first stop of the day is the Paleontological Research Centre. The well-constructed collection of fossils found in the area gives an interesting picture of a mountainous region that was a coastal landscape a hundred million years ago. Afterwards, head to the Monastery of La Candelaria, a 17th century Augustinian monastery. Monks who once lived as hermits in caves have built an impressive monastery here over time. A special highlight are the courtyards of the monastery. Next, make a short stop in Ráquira, a small town that traditionally lives from pottery. In the meantime, there are in the souvenir shops, but also all kinds of handicrafts from all over Colombia to buy. You will visit a pottery and see how the numerous ceramic objects are made. Maybe you also dare to potter yourself. The rest of the afternoon you can stroll through Villa de Leyva. Overnight in Villa de Leyva.

Day 5 - Continue to Barichara

Today’s approximately five-hour journey from the highlands slowly leads to warmer areas. From Villa de Leyva, the road runs only through lush green pastureland to Arcabuco. From there it winds through a narrow gorge, which then opens towards the Río Suárez. On the slopes of the Suárez valley at an altitude between 1000 and 1500 meters above sea level, the path leads via Barbosa to San Gil. On the way, there are always opportunities to stop to visit a traditional sugar mill or try the typical guavanmus. Don’t miss Cuajada con Melao (fresh cheese with sugar molasses) – Caseteja at Oiba is known for this specialty. From San Gil it is still half an hour drive to Barichara. This excellently preserved village was awarded the prize for the most beautiful village in Colombia. High above the canyon of the Río Suárez it lies at 1300 meters above sea level. Overnight in Barichara.

Day 6 Barichara: “The most beautiful village in Colombia”

After breakfast, take an easy hike. Slightly downhill, the path from Barichara to Guane leads to a small sleepy village. Along the way, you will pass by tobacco fields and cow pastures and enjoy the view over the valley of the Río Suárez and the mighty Cordillera de los Cobardes. In Guane you should not miss the small museum full of fossils and relics of the indigenous Guane people. By car, your driver will then take you back to Barichara. In the afternoon, take a tour of the “most beautiful village in Colombia”. The white-limed houses and cobbled streets invite you to explore. In numerous small galleries and craft workshops there is always something interesting to discover. From the simple chapel of Santa Bárbara you have a beautiful view over the village. Overnight in Barichara.

Day 7: The Black Gold of Colombia

Today is all about coffee. After a hike of about one and a half hours through the hills around Barichara, you reach the coffee finca Alfanía. There, coffee is planted and processed according to sustainable cultivation methods. Learn more about cultivation and examine the different stages of maturity. If the degree of ripeness allows, you can even help with the harvest yourself. Use the time to talk to the owners of the finca, they will gladly answer all questions about the topic of coffee. Overnight in Barichara.

Day 8: The Chicamocha Canyon

From Barichara, your driver will take you to the Canyon del Chicamocha today. If conditions permit, you will cross the canyon with a cable car and enjoy a fantastic panorama over the deep gorge. Also from the north side of the canyon there is an outgrowth that will take your breath away. Afterwards, drive across the Mesa de Los Santos plateau to Bucaramanga Airport. Domestic flights take you to Santa Marta on the Caribbean coast. Let the very different flair and the tropical climate of the place affect you. Overnight in Santa Marta.

Day 9 Excursion to Tayrona National Park

Today, take a trip to the Parque Nacional Natural Tayrona, which stretches along 35 kilometers of the Colombian Caribbean coast. Here you can expect beautiful white beaches and small coves. First, though, you’ll wander through the rainforest and along the coast for about two hours until you reach a quiet bay that invites a little cooling. If you are in the mood for a more extended hike, you can extend the tour and take a detour to the ruins of Pueblito, a city of Tayrona natives. Then continue between the mountains of the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta and the Caribbean Sea to your beach hotel. Overnight in Palomino.

10th day of relaxation on the Caribbean beach

Without tour guide and chauffeur

After you have already collected many impressions on your round trip through Colombia, you can use today to relax a little. Walk along the secluded beaches, take trips to the area (booking in advance or spontaneously on site) or just relax in the hammock. Let the soul dangle and enjoy the relaxed atmosphere in the lodge. Overnight in Palomino.

Day 11 - Continue to Cartagena

Today we drive from Santa Marta via Barranquilla to Cartagena. Here you will cross the mighty Río Magdalena, the longest river in Colombia. Enjoy the beautiful view of the sea while driving along the Atlantic coast until you finally reach Cartagena. It is considered one of the most beautiful colonial cities in South America and welcomes them with its imposing ring of fortresses and countless palaces in the Andalusian style. Overnight in Cartagena.

12th day Cartagena de Indias

You start the day with a ride up the hill of La Popa. From here you have a perfect view of the Bay of Cartagena and once the most important colonial port city of the Spaniards in South America. Then visit the fortress of San Felipe. This was built by the Spanish to protect the rich Cartagena from attacks from the mainland. Here, the treasures of the Incas plundered by the Spaniards and the gold and silver treasures from the mines of Peru and Bolivia were also temporarily stored for shipping to Spain. Thus, the city was again and again the object of the desire of numerous pirates. In the winding corridors of the fortress you feel transported back to the time of the pirates. Return to the colonial old town on a relaxing walk. Overnight in Cartagena.

Day 13 With a private yacht through the Caribbean

A private yacht Today, take you to the tropical Islas del Rosario. Small coves with white sand beach and crystal clear water invite you to swim. In consultation with your guide, you can set the daily schedule individually: you can swim, visit the small islands and enjoy freshly prepared seafood or fish on the beach. In the afternoon, return to Cartagena. Overnight in Cartagena.

14 Goodbye to Paradise

Your journey is coming to an end today. In due course, you will be taken to Cartagena Airport to begin your journey home. Even if your holiday is over: The unique memories of Colombia will always accompany you.
