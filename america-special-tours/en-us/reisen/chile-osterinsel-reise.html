Chile — The Magic of Easter Island

Chile Round trip with visit to Easter Islands by South America specialist América Special Tours. Individual and tailored. Now ask me.

8 days · 7 nights

Easter Island is a single mystery: How could people even reach the isolated island in the Pacific about 1500 years ago? Why did they create countless huge stone statues and how did they manage to transport this colossi, erect them and even provide them with stone caps? What events turned the forested island into a vast grassland and, above all, what led to the decline of this fascinating high culture? On this journey, you will embark on a search for traces in one of the most mysterious places on earth and immerse yourself in the myths and mysteries surrounding Rapa Nui.

Itinerary

Your journey through Chile begins in the capital Santiago. Upon arrival, you will be welcomed and taken to your hotel before departing for a first exploration of the city. You will follow the main road, where numerous attractions are lined up, such as the Government Palace La Moneda. It leads directly to the city center. There you will visit the main square with its cathedral and then continue your tour to the Pre-Columbian Museum. Your way through Santiago will take you to Mercado Central, the main market for fish and seafood. Through the Bellavista Artist and Nightlife District, you drive to the viewpoint Cerro San Cristóbal. From here you can enjoy a fantastic view over the seemingly endless sea of houses of Santiago and the impressive backdrop of the nearby high cordillera. A visit to the modern residential areas of Vitacura, Las Condes and the Providencia business and shopping district will round off your visit to the capital. Overnight in Santiago de Chile.

Not many domestic flights take you over 3000 kilometers. Although Easter Island has been part of Chile since 1888, it lies in the midst of the Pacific Ocean – and the mother-soul alone. The nearest inhabited piece of land is over 200 kilometers away. While other places boast of their animal and plant diversity, the volcanic Easter Island is characterized by its species poverty: the secluded location hardly allows seeds to be brought here. Most of the land animals that live here have been introduced. However, the islands are known for one thing in particular: they are littered with hundreds of colossal stone figures - mysterious testimonies of the ancient Polynesian people who settled the island despite its remoteness some 1500 years ago. Up to ten meters high rise the proud faces of the so-called Moai. The mystery of their original purpose has not yet been definitively solved. Overnight in Hanga Roa.

The moai give the archaeologists many puzzles. Probably the people on the island once made about a thousand of these huge stone statues. When, exactly, why and how? – this is the research disagreed. Your guide will take you right in the morning to Hanga Te’e Bay, where you will meet the mighty moais for the first time – and surely feel a similar feeling of awe as the Dutchman Jakob Roggeveen, who was the first European to land here. It is considered certain that the moais were part of ceremonial sites, which mostly consisted of a platform with statues and a forecourt. Scattered across the island, you come across several of these so-called Ahus, such as Akahanga, where you suspect the tomb of the legendary king Hotu Matua – the leader of the first settlers on Easter Island. The next stop of your exploration is Rano Raraku, an extinct volcano with crater lake. There is the gigantic quarry where the moais were made. Afterwards you will have the opportunity to visit more Ahus, including the spectacular 200-meter-long Ahu Tongariki with 15 re-erected moais. After so much culture, you can relax and refresh yourself on the picturesque sandy beach of Anakena. Overnight in Hanga Roa.

Your guide will take you inside the island to the restored Ahu Akivi with its seven rebuilt moais. Why much of the Ahus was destroyed is unclear. Even if some moai earthquakes fell victim, many stone statues were apparently deliberately overturned. Some researchers assume that a change of power made the bird man cult more important and so the old faith was dropped. The ahu in Hanga Poukura, which you will then visit, is an unrestored site that gives a picture of how the ahus were found by archaeologists. To conclude your half-day tour, visit the Tahai ceremonial site near Hanga Roa. Here you will have the opportunity to see three restored Ahus and the only moai that has its eyes reinstated. In addition, researchers have renovated a boat ramp and remains of boat houses here. You can use the afternoon for recreation or for your own explorations. In the evening, a cultural performance with dances and chants awaits you, accompanied by a traditional umu meal – a stew prepared in the ground. Today’s excursion leads to the extinct volcano Rano Kau with its crater lake. Here you have at two viewpoints a breathtaking view over the entire island as well as the crater lake. Afterwards you will visit a site of the cult, which according to some archaeological theories is said to have replaced the ancestral cult around the huge stone figures. Orongo, located on a cliff at the southwesternmost island tip, is a ceremonial village around the bird man cult. When the swallows laid their eggs on the small, offshore islands, the tribal war chiefs met here for a contest about who could bring the first egg to the mainland. He then enjoyed special attention as a “bird man”. When visiting the place of worship, you can not only explore various preserved stone houses and rock drawings – especially with bird motifs – but also have a unique view of the offshore islands to which participants in the competition around the cult of birds once set out. Nearby is the unrestored Ahu Vinapu, whose precise construction is reminiscent of buildings from early Peru – inspiring the theory that the first settlers reached Easter Island not from Polynesia but from South America. Overnight in Hanga Roa.

Today your journey will take you by car to the Poike Peninsula. Here’s your starting point for a hike to Vai a Heva, a rock where an open-mouthed face has been carved to collect rainwater. After a hike to the foot of the Poike volcano, we return to Hanga Roa. Overnight in Hanga Roa.

Today, leave Easter Island behind and return to Santiago de Chile on a domestic flight. Overnight in Santiago de Chile.

Today, your impressive journey to Easter Island is coming to an end. Your chauffeur will take you to Santiago airport for your flight home. Look forward to letting your loved ones share in your adventures in the distance at home.
