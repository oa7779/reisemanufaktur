Mexico Cultural Tour - A Journey Through History

Cultural tour through the history of Mexico with Latin America specialist América Special Tours. Individual and tailored. Ask now and experience for yourself.

On this journey, you will trace the Mexican history marked by the emergence and passing of various advanced cultures – the Maya, Aztec, Mixtec and Zapotek – as well as the arrival of the Spanish conquerors under Hernán Cortés, which ushered in a new era in the country’s history. Fascinating archaeological sites, impressive testimonies of the colonial period and – as a change from so much culture – unique natural beauties await you.

Day 1 Arrival in Mexico City

Your journey through Mexico’s eventful history begins in Mexico City. The metropolis occupied many important functions throughout its history: from 1325 until the beginning of the 16th century, it was known as Tenochtitlan as the capital of the Aztec Empire. After the bloody conquest by the Spanish conquistadors under Hernán Cortés, the legacy of the Aztecs in the city was systematically erased: churches were built on temples and a palace for Cortés on the foundation of the residence of the Aztec ruler Moctezumas. Initially the capital of the province of “New Spain”, Mexico City is the capital after the independence of the country until today. Here you will be expected by your guide when you arrive at the airport and escorted to your hotel in the city. Overnight in Mexico City.

2nd Day Time Travel through Mexico's Capital

Today you will discover the cultural and historical highlights of Mexico City. Together with your guide, you will first explore the historic center, whose impressive colonial buildings and extensive squares testify to the eventful history of the country. You will visit the famous Zócalo, one of the largest city squares in the world and for centuries the political and religious center of Mexico. Around the square are the imposing cathedral and numerous important government buildings. Afterwards, travel along the Paseo de la Reforma to the Museum of Anthropology, where you will have time to learn more about the culture of the Aztecs, of which only a few remains remain in their former capital. Especially the impressive collections of the Aztecs, Maya and Olmecs bring the millennia-old history of the country to life. Overnight in Mexico City.

3rd Day World Heritage Site Teotihuacán

One of the most important archaeological sites in America awaits you today: Teotihuacán. Those who want to start the day particularly spectacularly have the option of experiencing the impressive facility on a balloon ride from the air and enjoying the sunrise over the monumental pyramids. The city is still puzzling archaeologists to this day. Its heyday is estimated to be the middle of the first millennium after Christ, its decline probably followed already in the 7th and 8th centuries. Already the Aztecs found the place to be a ruined city and gave it a mythical meaning. Together with your guide, you will explore the remains of an ancient civilization, its temples, palaces and pyramids. Particularly impressive is the Sun Pyramid in the center of the old city, which with a floor area of 225 by 225 meters and a height of about 63 meters represents the third largest pyramid in the world. On the way back to Mexico City, visit the Basílica de Guadalupe, the most important pilgrimage site in Mexico and throughout Latin America. Year after year, millions of believers make their pilgrimage here to pay homage to the patron saint of the country. Overnight in Mexico City.

Day 4 Coyoacán, Frida Kahlo and Cholula

Before leaving Mexico City, visit the charming Coyoacán district. Cobblestone lanes, colonial architecture and shady squares give the district a very special atmosphere. It is also home to the famous Casa Azul, the birthplace of the world-renowned artist Frida Kahlo, who spent much of her life there. During a visit to the museum, you will get exciting insights into her extraordinary life and artistic work. Then leave the capital and drive towards Puebla. Along the way, stop at Cholula, where the world’s largest pyramid is located by volume. On its overgrown foundation rises today the Sanctuary of Nuestra Señora de los Remedios, from which offers a magnificent view of the surrounding landscape as well as the majestic volcanoes Popocatépetl and Iztaccíhuatl. Then continue your journey to Puebla, one of the most beautiful colonial cities in Mexico. Overnight in Puebla.

Day 5 Explore Puebla

A half-day city tour with your guide brings you closer to Puebla, Mexico’s fourth largest city. The place radiates a unique beauty through its brick houses decorated with tiles, numerous churches, colonial buildings and the impressive backdrop of volcanoes and mountains. The first stop on your walk through the UNESCO World Heritage Site is the Mercado El Parian, a craft market in the middle of the artists’ quarter. Through pretty streets you can reach the Templo de San Francisco, a pretty yellow-washed church, and the Cerro de Guadalupe. This park is located on a hillside and contains the historic fortifications of Loreto and Guadalupe. Finally, you should treat yourself to a little something in one of the traditional confectionery shops on Calle 6 Oriente. Then leave the central highlands and continue your journey through the varied landscapes of southern Mexico. In the evening you will reach Oaxaca de Juárez, one of the most culturally interesting and beautiful cities in the country. Overnight in Oaxaca de Juárez.

Day 6 Monte Albán and Oaxaca

In the morning, visit the archaeological sites of Monte Albán, which are spectacularly located 400 meters above the Oaxaca Valley. Here on an artificially flattened mountain crest was once the capital of the Zapoteks. Together with your guide, you will explore the remains of the city, which was abandoned around 950 AD. Various buildings have been preserved to this day, including an observatory and tombs with interesting murals. Then take a tour of Oaxaca de Juárez, which was once the center of the Mixtec and Zapotek Indian civilizations. When the Spaniards took the place in 1533, Spanish colonial flair spread very soon here. However, the city has preserved its pre-colonial heritage – partly because an active indigenous population still lives here. Together with your guide, you will walk past the Iglesia de Santo Domingo with its magnificent baroque facade, past the house of the reformer and former president Benito Juárez and over some colorful markets. Overnight in Oaxaca de Juárez. 

7th Day Continue to San Cristóbal de las Casas

After breakfast, you will be taken to the airport. A domestic flight with a stopover in Mexico City will take you to Tuxtla Gutiérrez, the capital of the state of Chiapas and the gateway to some of southern Mexico’s most impressive natural and cultural treasures. Here you are already expected and take a private boat trip through the Sumidero Canyon. This is with 13 kilometers in length and partly over 1,000 meters height a breathtaking sight. Here, in the midst of dense vegetation, various water birds, monkeys and crocodiles live. After the tour, your private driver will take you by car to San Cristóbal de las Casas. The pretty colonial town at 2,100 meters in the highlands of Chiapas is surrounded by mountain forests. Many indigenous people of the Mayan ethnic group live in the area. Overnight in San Cristóbal de las Casas.

Day 8 Colonial Heritage and Indigenous Culture

In the morning, your guide will pick you up for a walk through the colonial city, visiting the cathedral with its rich decor and the baroque Dominican church Templo de Santo Domingo, among other things. Here you can stroll through the nearby craft market, where Tzotzil Indians from the area sell their goods. You can learn more about the indigenous peoples of the area in two villages of the Tzotzil – San Juan Chamula and Zinacantán. The tribe has its roots in the Mayan people and has preserved some specific customs and traditions to this day. Overnight in San Cristóbal de las Casas.

Day 9 Over the Agua-Azul Waterfall to Palenque

Today it means getting up very early for you, because you leave the mountains behind and go to Palenque. Along the way, another natural highlight of Mexico awaits you: the Agua Azul Falls. In the middle of a karst landscape are numerous waterfalls, of which Agua Azul is the largest. Smaller but also very beautiful is the waterfall Misol-Ha, behind which lies a cave into which swallows retreat at night. Finally, you will reach Palenque, probably the most impressive Mayan site in Mexico. Nestled in the foothills of the Tumbalá mountains, the ancient city rises above the tropical lowlands that extend to the Gulf Coast. Overnight in Palenque.

Day 10 Palenque: the fertile plain of Maya

Today you will dedicate yourself to the archaeological site of Palenque, which consists of around 500 buildings spread over 15 square kilometers in the rainforest and inhabited by toucans and roaring monkeys. Your guide will show you the highlights of this once important city, including the magnificent Sun Temple and the palace with its labyrinth of rooms and corridors. The city flourished between 600 and 800 AD. Even today, finely crafted reliefs and elegant buildings testify to the high culture that once ruled this fertile land. Then continue your journey to Campeche. Overnight in Campeche.

11th day Pirates and Maya: Campeche and Uxmal

Campeche was founded in 1540 by the Spanish conquistadors on a Mayan city and between the 16th and 18th centuries constituted the main seaport of the Yucatán peninsula. As such, he attracted pirates, so that the Spanish Crown built a powerful fort for the protection of the city. Some of the fortress walls that once surrounded the entire city are still preserved; the ruins give the city something audaciously romantic as a legacy of the pirate era. In the fort of San José you will get a glimpse into the military past of the city. You will also visit two of the bastions and the former residence of the military of the Spanish king. After visiting Campeche, your driver will take you to Uxmal, where a Mayan heritage awaits. In the middle of a hilly karst landscape lie the ruins of a once important city, whose name may be derived from the Mayan word for “three times” – apparently the place was rebuilt and extended again and again. At one of Yucatán’s best-preserved Mayan sites, you can learn a lot about the life of the former high culture. After the visit, continue your journey to Mérida. Overnight in Mérida.

Day 12 Celestún and Mérida

Today, take a trip to the Celestún Biosphere Reserve on Yucatán’s Gulf Coast. The sanctuary is particularly distinguished by its bird wealth, including the pink flamingo, the dwarf tern, the jabiru, the king vulture and the big-blooded goose. You take a boat trip through the river estuaries and can observe the animals in peace. Stop at freshwater springs – take advantage of the unique opportunity for a bath in crystal clear water. In the afternoon, return and discover the colonial splendor of Méridas. Mérida, also known as the White City, is the capital of Yucatán State. The whitewashed houses and traditionally white dressed inhabitants helped the city to this flattering nickname. You will explore the historic center of Mérida, including the magnificent Cathedral of San Ildefonso, built with the stones of ancient Mayan temples. From the beautiful Plaza Principal, shaded by trees, you can take impressive photos of the Cathedral, Palacio Municipal, Casa Montejo and the Governor's Palace. At the end of your tour, you will drive along the historic Paseo Montejo Boulevard, built on the model of the Champs-Élysées in Paris. Overnight in Mérida.

Day 13: The Pyramids of Chichén Itzá

Today you will visit one of Mexico’s most famous historical sites: Chichén Itzá. This ruined site is one of the best restored places of the Maya and was chosen among the “New Seven Wonders of the World”. In the middle of dense jungles lie the impressive Kukulcán Pyramid, some smaller temples and ruins of various buildings. During a walk through the site, let the stories of your guide revive the past in this place. In addition, you will find the remains of ball courts where the Maya once played ball. What exactly did the corresponding rules look like? Archaeologists disagree about this. After the visit, you will have the opportunity to refresh yourself in a typical Yukatec cenote. These natural freshwater limestone fountains already played an important religious and cultural role for the Maya. Then head to Valladolid, a charming colonial town with colorful houses and relaxed atmosphere. Overnight in Valladolid. 
 

14th day of travel home

Your journey through the history of Mexico is coming to an end. Today, your chauffeur will take you in time for your flight home to Cancún. Even if your journey is over – the precious memories will accompany you forever. If you would like to end your holiday in a relaxed way, we are happy to arrange a bathing extension on the Riviera Maya with beautiful beaches – ideal conditions for relaxation!
