Cuba: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate


Cuba is one of the great Antilles and lies in the Caribbean. Tropical savannah climate prevails all over the island. In the south-east, the climate is the driest and hottest in the country, but Cuba is not subject to great temperature fluctuations. As a result, the country is generally considered a year-round destination with consistently pleasant water temperatures. October to February are the cooler months with temperatures of about 10 °C to 25 °C. The warmest months from May to September offer between 25 °C and 30 °C.

The rainy season is from June to October, the dry season from November to April. However, during the period called the rainy season no permanent rain falls but only short tropical showers. The wettest month is June. The Hurricane season is from June to November. There is an increased risk in September and October. Note also the high season from December to April and the holidays in Cuba itself in July and August.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

To enter Cuba, you will need an EU-citizen passport that is valid for another 6 months after your return trip and a tourist card (approx. 22,- €), which allows you to stay for 30 days. (If you book your international flights via us, you will receive the tourist card sent by us). You can get them at the Cuban Consulate or at airports. Since only the tourist card proves the legal entry and it can become problematic in case of loss at the border, you should pay particular attention and always attach to your further valuables. In general, it is advisable to store the original documents in the hotel safe and only carry copies. 25 CUC are payable per person on departure. You will also be photographed on arrival and departure. This is intended to make it easier for Cuban citizens to flee by means of foreign passports. Every Cuban traveler has been obliged since 1 May 2010 to prove on entry a health insurance cover valid for Cuba for the intended duration of stay.


Food/drink

The food in Cuba is quite simple due to the aftertaste of the moderate supply situation. Rice dishes are common, mostly with chicken, pork, lamb or seafood in combination with black beans and often quite greasy. As a side dish, cook bananas or yucca are often served. With Cuba's location, delicacy is also seafood like crayfish. The soups in Cuba are also traditional, for example the garlic soup. The fresh juices from numerous exotic fruits are also particularly enjoyable. Of course, the rum is also worth mentioning, available in a variety of variations and very cheap compared to North America and Europe. The colorful and creative cocktails are sometimes more interesting than the food and far better than what you are used to in Europe.

Electricity

Take an adapter with you. Most new hotels offer 220 volts, but 110 volts are also common.


Photographing/Filming

In general, you can film and photograph freely. Just be careful with military or police facilities, because photography and filming are strictly forbidden here. Also show sufficient respect if you have locals before the lens and in case of doubt prefer to get a permission in advance.


Money/currency

Cuba operates with two currency units. On the one hand, the Cuban Peso (CUB), this is the national currency for the Cuban population and on the other hand with the Peso Convertible (CUC), this is the tourist currency. EUR 1 corresponds to 1.16 CUC. U.S. dollars are no longer common since 2004 and are not worthwhile for exchange on site due to the high fee. You have the best acceptance with Visa – and Mastercard. However, it is best to check with your credit institution in advance for security.


Health/vaccination

You do not need any vaccinations to enter Europe. However, come from a risk area within South America, e.g. Yellow fever must be demonstrated. Vaccination of diphtheria, hepatitis A, B and tetanus is recommended. Cuba is considered malaria-free, but due to the Dengue fever transferring Aedes mosquito, measures for personal mosquito protection are to be taken. Find out about uncertainties at a tropical institute in your area. Be careful about tap water (and ice cubes) and buy this always packed for safety. Enjoy fish and meat only well cooked by. Keep flies away from your food. Wash your hands as often as possible with soap, but always after the passage to the toilet and before eating. Do personally needed medications necessarily in sufficient amount with yourself as pharmacies are often not sufficiently supplied. Urgently recommended is also to take out a foreign health insurance. For tourists there are special medical practices where you pay cash with CUC. Cholera vaccination may be useful depending on the travel profile. Individual advice from a tropical or travel physician is recommended.

Government/Politics/Economics

Country name: Republic of Cuba

State form: Socialist Republic, one-party rule of the Communist Party of Cuba

Country: Area 110,860 km²

Capital: Havana (2.1 million) Inhabitants)

Population: 11.2 million

Religion: Mainly Catholics, however, the majority of the population is officially without religious affiliation

Language

The official language is Spanish. However, the Cubans have a not very clean debate, do not be surprised if you still have some communication problems after a course. Nevertheless, Cuban Spanish is often easier for Europeans to understand than the dialects spoken in Spain. Since English is not very widespread outside Havana and the tourist areas, it is recommended to acquire some basic knowledge. But with the Cubans as a very friendly people you will have no difficulty in finding a connection.


Telephone/Internet

Germany/Austria/Switzerland: 0049/43/41
Cuba code: 0053

In Cuba, the state telephone company "Etecsa" is responsible for everything. With mobile phones brought with you, you will automatically be switched to the roaming partner "Cubacel". Telephone calls to Europe are about 4.50 EUR. An alternative is to rent a mobile phone in Cuba, or make calls abroad via the hotel WiFi.

Tips

In Cuba, there is a very low monthly wage, so every local is extremely grateful for the tip of tourists and most of them also depend on it. The Cubans themselves also like to tip when they go out for dinner. If you are satisfied with your tour guide and the driver, they are of course very happy about a tip. It is best to hand over the money in separate envelopes, perhaps even with a small thank you note. Your hotel will certainly help you get the envelopes.

Depending on the satisfaction, the permanent and also local tour guide receives about 3-5 CUC per guided day and also the chauffeur receives 2-5 CUC per day for his work. The luggage carrier is also pleased with a small attention of 1-2 CUC. Please note that these are recommendations.

In restaurants 10% tip is appropriate. Often, however, these are already included, which is why a look at the bill is worthwhile. You can either leave your tip on the table or hand it directly to the waiter.

Behavior

As a socialist country, Cuba pays particular attention to its locals. Generally, the locals are friendly and accommodating towards tourists. Just be careful about critical statements. In order to prevent a robbery, you only carry as much cash with you as you really need and do not carry valuables too openly to the show. The pass should also stay in the hotel safe, here is a copy. Don't hit the strings and heed the laws. Unlike the rest of Latin America, Cuba is taking sanctions very accurately. "Above all without or even "FKK" are not welcome in Cuba.

Car hire
 

In Cuba there is right-wing traffic. The traffic rules are international and the road quality is passable to good. Travelers heading towards the east of the country have to expect that the roads in the east are much worse than the west of Cuba. In the capital, there is an impenetrable traffic chaos during professional transport. Since street lighting in rural areas is often weak and potholes often lurk on the roads, it is recommended to plan trips so that you arrive at your destination before dusk. Signs and signposts are rather rare in Cuba, especially off the main roads. A good road map is absolutely advisable. We recommend smartphone owners, already before travel, since in Cuba the Internet connection is very limited to load an offline map or navigation app on their mobile phone, e.g. MapFactor Navigator or MAPS.ME.

The maximum speed on thoroughfares is 80 - 100km/h, although the average speed is 80 km/h and 50 km/h on secondary roads. Tourists are allowed to drive in Cuba for up to 90 days with a valid European driver's license, but it is also recommended to carry the international driver's license.

All car rental companies in Cuba are state-owned. América Special Tours is only an intermediary. The rental agreement is concluded directly between the customer and the Cuban car rental company. Insurance is not included in the price and must be paid directly to the rental car company when taking over the car in Cuba. The car rental companies also reserve the right to change the category of cars in the short term, as there are often bottlenecks in Cuba. When receiving the car, a "guarantee deposit", the amount of which depends on the car rental company or category, is required in the form of a credit card print or in cash. Without the deposit, it is not possible to borrow the rental car in Cuba. The credit card print will be destroyed when the car is returned, which will be refunded in cash. If the car is returned to another location, we recommend that you not deposit the deposit at the place of delivery in cash but use the credit card instead.
