Mexico Country information

Mexico Country Information: Find out everything you need to know about the country for a trip to Mexico. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Mexico: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate


Mexico is mostly part of North America, while a small southern part belongs to Central America. The location between Caribbean, Atlantic and Pacific as well as the mountains at altitudes of over 5,300 meters ensure a high variety of different climatic conditions. Especially the dry months from October to May are popular with holidaymakers. During this period, however, warm clothing is necessary for the visit in higher locations and city tours. In very high altitudes even snow can fall.

In addition, the tourist peak season must be considered from December to January and in the week east, during which many Mexicans travel. The northwest of Mexico has a subtropical climate with humid winters and marked temperature differences between the seasons. Here it warms up to 35°C in the summer (maximum even up to 40°C). In winter, it cools down to about 10°C.

On the island Holbox in front of the Yucatan peninsula you can dive and snorkel with whale sharks from June to mid-September (better still from July to August). During this period, the largest fish in the world are attracted by plankton wealth. A clear dry season and a rainy season are to be distinguished from the south. The dry season runs from the end of November to the beginning of June.

Mexico City at 2,300 meters has about an average temperature of 15 °C per year, lowest temperatures of only 5°C are normal in January. During the rainy season, the high humidity can be quite exhausting and in some parts of the country it can become very hot. Tropical cyclones can occur between June and November. Instable weather can be found mainly in September and October, and even the foothills of a hurricane can cause bad weather.

In the past, seaweed on the Mexican coast has increased (Sargazo==References====External links==* Official website* Official website* Official website Sargazo is a periodic phenomenon that usually occurs on the Mexican Caribbean coast from April to October. It is influenced by the tides so that the level of arising can vary from day to day. It is important to know that seaweed does not affect the whole country, because the Pacific coast and also parts of the Mexican Caribbean coast (e.g. Isla Mujeres, Cozumel) have been spared from seagrass. The hotels and resorts respond to Sargazo by investing in barrier systems and boats that transport the seaweed or keep it at a distance. Staff at the hotels also work hard daily to clean the beaches, so that several times a day the seaweed is picked up.

Entry, Customs & Visa

As an EU citizen, you do not need a visa to enter and stay for a maximum of 180 days, but a passport valid 6 months after your return, as well as a tourist card that you will receive from a Mexican consulate or airline during your arrival. This must be returned upon departure. Keep the card safe. The export of gold (except gold jewellery), antiques, archaeological finds, cacti, corals and other protected animals is not permitted.


Food & Drink

Especially common in Mexico are tortillas, they are filled with different ingredients and with a different name (Taco or Quesadilla==References== The filling is very variable from minced meat to chicken or on the coast fish and seafood. Most of the dishes are lime juice, chili sauce (salsa) and fresh herbs (with coriander in dominant amount). The famous Chili con Carne, which is always associated with Mexico, comes from the south of the USA contrary to this belief. At the most, at the US border or in tourist locations, you will find this court.

Electricity

Mains voltage 110 volts (sometimes 125), US flat plug. The best way to get an adapter for your electrical appliances is in advance.


Photographing and filming

Since films in Mexico are more expensive, you should definitely cover yourself with them in advance. Opposite memory cards, these are significantly cheaper on site. In museums, photography is only allowed for a fee. In the case of native Indians, you should first collect their consent. In the case of military or police facilities, additional caution is required when photographing.


Money and payment on site

The abbreviation for the peso ($) is a bit misleadingly the same as for the US dollar, so pay attention to what currency is meant in the store. (as of September 2019: one euro equals about 21.5 pesos). Common credit cards are accepted at larger tourist resorts or higher-quality restaurants and shops. Meanwhile, you can withdraw from most ATMs using the credit or EC card and cash.


Health

There are no vaccination requirements for entering Mexico. Malaria prophylaxis is recommended, prevention of typhus and hepatitis is recommended. In any case, you should have sufficient sunscreen and insect preservatives with you and take personal mosquito protection measures into account, e.g. long covering clothes or application of repellents to uncovered skin areas. Standard vaccinations according to the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute for Children and Adults should be reviewed and completed. Depending on the travel profile, it can be useful to carry an emergency medication (standby) with chloroquine, which should be discussed with a tropical or travel physician before traveling. Due to the Zika virus infection, pregnant women and women who want to become pregnant are recommended to take personal mosquito protection measures in any case.

Government/Politics/Economics

Country name: United Mexican States, Estados Unidos Mexicanos

State form: Presidential Republic

Country: 1,964,375 km². (5 1⁄2 times the size of the Federal Republic of Germany)

Capital: Mexico City (25 million) Inhabitants)

Population: 129 million, mainly mestizos, approximately 12 million Indigenous, 62 ethnic groups and 4 to 5 million Mexicans of European origin.

Religion: 82.7% Roman Catholic, 7.5% Protestant and other Christian communities, 2.4% other religion, 4.7% without religion, 2.7% not reported

Language

It is spoken Spanish or one of the numerous indigenous languages. ==References====External links== Cancún can also handle English. In large hotels, German is also spoken in part. Only in less touristy places English is not widespread. Together with Guatemala (the stronghold of the language schools throughout Latin America), Spanish in Mexico is considered to be the most accurate and understandable. It is always recommended to acquire some knowledge before the trip – the locals are happy and you will be easier to do.


Telephone/Internet

Germany/Austria/Switzerland: 0049/43/41

Mexico code: 0052

With telephone cards, almost everywhere in the country can speak from telephone booths. One minute to Europe costs about 20 pesos, one local call about 1 peso. Most European mobile phones don't work in Mexico. The best way to find out is with your provider in advance. A prepaid card from Mexico eliminates the charges for incoming calls. Prepaid cards are more expensive, but also save all roaming fees. One more advantage: you get your new number at home. Text messages are always cheap.


Tips

If you are satisfied with your tour guide and the driver, they are of course very happy about a tip. It is best to hand over the money in separate envelopes, perhaps even with a small thank you note. Your hotel will certainly help you get the envelopes. Depending on the satisfaction, the permanent and also local tour guide receives approximately 5-10 USD per person and per guided day and also the chauffeur receives 3-5 USD per person and per day for his work. Please note that these are recommendations. The propina (= Spanish for tipping) is very important for many Mexicans, as it is an elementary part of income. In restaurants, it's a good idea to tip 10% to 15%. In tourist centers, the servicio already advertised on the invoice, but you can also ask again. Depending on your preferences, you can leave the money directly on the table or hand it over to the service staff. If you want to make sure that the tip really arrives with your friendly waiter, it is recommended to give the tip in cash and not to include in the card payment. Luggage carriers and room staff are given an attention of about 3 to 5 USD. Also with any other service provider you can leave a little. In general, if the service was satisfactory, be grateful. Only in the case of taxi rides, a tip is not usual, the amount is rounded up at most.

Behavior

The Mexicans are very warm, hospitable and extremely polite people. This behaviour is expected in return by foreign tourists to a certain extent, i.e. one should know and apply the most important phrases in Spanish for example. Directness through Si or No is avoided and many phrases and sneezings are used. It's not being pushed. Topics such as political problems or the like should be treated with caution, since some locals might feel attacked by this in their national pride. The understanding of time here is a completely different one than in Germany. Bring with you sufficient patience and let the serenity drive you. When invited, you'd better be half an hour late than early. By the way, alcohol consumption in public is strictly prohibited! Also pay attention to your style of clothing, because the outer appearance is very important to the well-kept Mexicans.

Time difference

The time difference to Central Europe is -7 hours in summer and winter, depending on the time change sometimes also -8 hours.


Domestic Flights & Trains

In Mexico, unfortunately, there are often short-term changes in domestic flights. We ask for your understanding. Of course, we will inform you about this in good time and rebook you to the best possible alternative if major changes are made.


Car hire

In Mexico, there is legal traffic. The larger roads in the country are subject to charges, but also in good condition.
On Mexican country roads, caution is required against crossing cattle and in villages from waves of soil. There is an impenetrable traffic chaos in the larger cities during professional transport. The maximum speed on motorways is 110 km/h, on highways 70 km/h and in villages 40 km/h. Tourists are allowed to drive in Mexico for up to 90 days with a valid European driver's license, but it is also recommended to carry the international driver's license.

The fuel costs in Mexico are much cheaper than in Europe. However, they vary greatly, so it is advisable to inform yourself beforehand about the price situation. Unlike Europe, Mexico has only two types of petrol. Magna (87 octan), which is marked green at the petrol stations and Premium (93 octan), which is red and slightly more expensive. When you take over a rental car, find out which gasoline your car needs. It's not self-refuelling, it's a tanker who takes care of everything. You can either specify how many liters you want to refuel or how many pesos. Always make sure that the pump is zero before it fills up. The tanker should always be rewarded with 5 to 10 pesos tip.
