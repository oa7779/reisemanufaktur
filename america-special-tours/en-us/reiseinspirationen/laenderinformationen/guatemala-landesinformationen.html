Guatemala Country information

Guatemala Country Information: Find out everything you need to know about the country for a trip to Guatemala. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Guatemala: Country information A-Z


Best time/climate

The best travel time for Guatemala is the dry season, i.e. the months October/November to May. Especially at altitudes it can be very pleasantly warm and dry, ideal for trekking tours and animal watching. Also tours are recommended during this time at the altitudes. In May, however, it can be very hot in low-lying areas (tierra caliente). During the rainy season, floods and landslides can occur, especially in the high mountain regions, which are quite difficult to travel. On the other hand, the rainy season is particularly popular with surfers or for whitewater rafting. However, during these months there is also the danger of tropical cyclones. In recent years, floods and mudslides in particular have had catastrophic consequences.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

EU citizens are exempt from visa requirements. A passport is required, which is valid for at least six months when entering the country. EU citizens can stay in Guatemala for a total of 90 days per calendar year. Upon entering the airport you will receive an entry stamp including the date of entry. Make sure that you also get a date of validity. This is particularly important when traveling longer than 30 days. Especially when entering from neighbouring countries across the country, care must be taken to collect the entry stamp in order to avoid difficulties in leaving. Children need a children's passport.

Food & Drink

Since Guatemala is a multicultural country, there are many ways to eat and drink: from chic and expensive restaurants to the typical "mercados" in almost every village. In addition, there are many European-style cafés and fast restaurants. Even with the same quality, prices can vary depending on the restaurant. Tortillas, flat flakes of corn and water, baked on a comal, are served with each meal. Tamales are steamed in corn leaf, sometimes with meat. Paches look like tamales, but they're made of rice. Enchiladas with chicken or cheese filling as well as tacos (thin tortillas with meat, herb and avocados) are adaptations from Mexico. Typical sauces are the salsa picante from chili peppers, which stands on the table in small bottles and the chirmol, a tomato sauce. The green or white Güisquil is a delicious vegetable, as well as the red Camote (sweet potato) and also the Yucca. Just as important as the corn is for the Guatemalans, the black beans (frijoles) are also served at almost every meal. The typical breakfast consists of scrambled eggs, frijoles, tortillas and baked bananas (plátanos fritos). Of course, the guacamole, an avocado paste with lemons must not be missing. A lot of chicken is eaten and it is preferable to the mostly tough beef (carne asada). The Mojarra is a delicious fish, like the crayfish (cangrejos). A delicious snack is the ceviche, a salad of raw seafood with a spicy lemon- vinegar sauce. Guatemala likes to eat sweet, so the cakes and small pieces are wonderful delicacies.

Fruits are available in this country all year round: apples, bananas, pineapples, papaya and oranges can always be bought. Mangoes are offered from February and are also eaten immature with salt. The Maracujas, Mameys and Zapote are popular. Finally, the Gautemaltecs enjoy Kakik, Pepian, Fiambre, Caldo de mariscos, Tapado, Rice and Beans as well as various types of fish and seafood.

Typical drinks are atoles made from corn, rice and cooking bananas. Horchata was taken over by the Spaniards and is a cool drink made of sweetened rice with cinnamon. The soft drinks (refrescos) are made from fruit. Unsurpassed are the delicious mixed drinks made from fruit with milk or water (liquados). The local beers (Gallo, Cabro, Monte Carlo and the dark Mozo) are well edible. There are good wines only from Spain or South America, the local wines are made of fruit and very sweet. White rum is also produced in Guatemala and the Centenario from Zacapa is one of the best rum species in the world

Electricity

The current voltage is 110 volts. Different plug types are used, so taking a travel adapter is necessary.


Photographing/Filming

In Guatemala it is possible to photograph without restrictions almost everywhere. Only in some churches and military facilities or similar one should urgently ask for permission. When photographing people should always be asked beforehand. Above all, the photographing of the indigenous Mayan population, especially in rural areas, should be omitted. Children should also not be photographed.


Money/currency

The currency in Guatemala is called Quetzal (Q) and is named after the coat of arms bird of the country. One quetzal is 100 centavos. There are 10, 20, 50 and 100 Quetzal notes in circulation. At present, 1 euro is about 9 quetzal. All over Guatemala, major credit cards are accepted, except Maestro. The country is now equipped with a good network of ATMs, which can be empty or broken. Taking small amounts of money into Quetzal and US dollars is therefore always advisable. In addition, only small changes are accepted in small towns or shops, because on large bills often cannot be issued at all. Otherwise, money in Guatemala is changed almost exclusively to banks. Exchange offices are unusual.


Health/vaccination

There is a certain risk of malaria, chikungunya or dengue. Bend it best with sufficient mosquito protection and suitably protective clothing. If you are unsure, you can get detailed advice from a tropical doctor before your trip. In principle, you do not need vaccinations to enter European territories. However, regular standard vaccinations should be reviewed. Vaccination against hepatitis A and B is also useful. Yellow fever and typhus vaccination are also recommended. Such evidence shall be provided when entering from a risk country. In any case, it is recommended to observe all-day and consistently personal mosquito protection measures. Depending on the travel profile, it can be useful to carry an emergency medication (standby) with chloroquine for the prevention of malaria disease in individual cases, whose selection should be discussed with a tropical or travel physician before the trip.

Language

The official language in Guatemala is Spanish. In addition to Spanish, other languages are spoken in Guatemala. The largest number of speakers is Quiché, the language of the Quiché Indians who live in the highlands of Guatemala. The Mam language of the Mam Indians and Cakchiquel, which is spoken by the people of the same name, follows. A total of 22 Mayan languages are spoken in Guatemala. Spanish in Guatemala differs in many ways from Spanish (Castilian) spoken in Spain. For example, "you" are said to be ustedes (instead of vosotros). And while in Spain the c is usually pronounced like an English th (as in the English word through), in Guatemala (and throughout Latin America) the c is spoken like a sharp s (as in sea).


Telephone/Internet

For telephone calls abroad, the respective country pre-elections apply. For Germany this is the 0049, for Switzerland the 0041 and for Austria the 0043. Guatemala has the national code 00502. When calling with the mobile phone high roaming charges can arise. It is recommended to use public card phones or to communicate in internet cafés over the Internet. Emergency numbers: Police 120, Fire Brigade 123, Red Cross 2381-6565, Tourist emergency call 1500


Tips

Hotels and restaurants do not charge a service fee, so staff should be rewarded with a tip of 10% as this is often an important merit. Guides and related bus drivers should also be rewarded for their work.

Behavior

There are currently about 17 million people living in Guatemala. The "Indigenas", as the descendants of the Maya call themselves, make up about 50% of Guatemala's population today. In the various regions of the country there are 23 indigenous ethnic groups, which are economically, culturally and socially distinct from the rest of the population. The Indígenas have retained much of their traditional culture even after five centuries of massive influences by Europeans. Above all, women still often see traditional colorful clothing. Apart from the Indígenas and the Ladinos, there is a small minority of Garífunas on the Caribbean coast. Most Guatemalans who come into contact with tourists are open-minded and like to talk, even if the communication does not always work optimally due to lack of language skills. But with a few pieces of Spanish and with the help of hands and feet you can find nice and open dialogues in the Guatemalans, which often show a great interest in Germany. As in all Latin America, the Guatemalans live in a culture of the moment. Long-term planning and punctuality are not always on the agenda. Flexibility and quick response to the situation are, on the other hand, important.

Time difference

The time shift in Guatemala to Central European Time (CET) -7 hours. The transition from summer time to winter time is not at the same time in Guatemala as in Europe. Therefore, there is a time difference of -8 hours.
