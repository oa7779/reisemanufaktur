Nicaragua Country information

Nicaragua Country Information: Find out everything you need to know about the country in Central America for a trip to Nicaragua. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Nicaragua: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate


The most pleasant time to travel for the majority of Nicaragua is the dry season from November to April, with it can become very hot towards the end of the dry season, usually at the end of April/beginning of May, and the water levels in the lakes are decreasing.

For hiking and camping, the tourist high season (December to March) is recommended, especially along the border with Costa Rica. Nicaragua can be divided into three major temperature zones: the mountain country to the north, the coastal area with Managua, León and Granada and the central lowlands with the Caribbean coast. In the coastal areas of the Atlantic and Pacific, temperatures range from 21°C to 32°C.

The coastal area of the Pacific and parts of the interior can be called the tropical dry zone of Nicaragua, the annual precipitations in the rainy season from May to August and from September to October almost 2,000 mm (for comparison: Berlin 570mm). Thus, the coastal area is the most fertile region for agriculture. If you want to travel on this, the dry season is best suited, which starts in December and ends in January. The best travel time for the Isla de Ometepe is in November to April. The average temperature is about 27 to 30° Celsius.

Entry, Customs & Visa

In addition to a valid passport for entry, which is still valid for at least six months, EU citizens need an online entry application. This must be requested at least 7 days before entry on the electronic portal of the State of Nicaragua (https://solicitudes.migob.gob.ni/). The scheme is designed to speed up the entry process for tourists. Information on the itinerary, as well as travel documents are requested and checked. The applicant must print out the processing number and the confirmation code or otherwise store it in order to present it on entry. Of course, we are happy to do this process for you. Upon entry, the purchase of a tourist card (currently US$10 plus an entry fee of US$2) is additionally required, which permits a stay of up to 90 days per calendar year. Upon departure, the US$2 fee will also be charged. As a rule, both are already included in the price of air tickets. Short-term changes possible, possibly price differences when entering the country. Children need a children's passport.


Food & Drink

Nicaragua's national cuisine is good and healthy, versatile and very tasty. The basis of a meal consists of rice and beans as well as corn. A lot is also cooked with fish, meat and bananas. You should definitely try "ceviche", a cocktail of fish and seafood prepared as cold soup, and the numerous fresh fruit juices "jugos", often made from exotic fruits that we do not know in Europe. International cuisine is also offered in the big cities and many hotels. In addition, you should definitely e.g. "Gallo Pinto" (a typical breakfast made of rice and red beans), which is also eaten as lunch or dinner, and "Vigorón" (a salad made from white cabbage, tomatoes, palm hearts and fried pork litter) served on a banana leaf.

Electricity

The current voltage is 110 volts. In Nicaragua the plug type A (two-pin flat plug) is mostly used, so it is necessary to take a travel adapter for Swiss, German and Austrians.


Photographing/Filming

In Nicaragua it is possible to photograph without restrictions almost everywhere. Photographing and filming of facilities, vehicles and personnel of the military and police forces without permission is not permitted. The same goes for prisons. With people you want to photograph, you should ask for permission beforehand.


Money and payment on site

The Nicaraguan currency is the Córdoba Oro, commonly called Cordoba. There are 1000, 500, 100, 50, 20 and 10 Córdoba Oro notes and coin money. One Euro has a value of about 37 Cordoba, one Swiss franc about 33.75 Cordoba (price from September 2019). Also in Nicaragua you get Córdoba at every ATM. Credit cards (Visa and Mastercard) are accepted in many hotels, supermarkets and restaurants, but occasionally there may be liquidity issues.


Health

There is a certain risk of malaria, chikungunya or dengue. Bend it in any case with sufficient mosquito protection and suitably protective clothing. If you are unsure, get detailed advice from a tropical doctor before your trip. In principle, you do not need vaccinations to enter European territories. However, you should have regular standard vaccinations checked for your protection. Vaccination against hepatitis A and B is also useful. Yellow fever and typhus vaccination is also recommended.

No compulsory vaccination is required for direct entry from Germany. Upon entry from a yellow fever area (including Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, French Guiana, Guyana, Panama, Paraguay and Peru), yellow fever vaccination is to be demonstrated for all travelers from the age of one year onwards. Managua and the centers of the big cities are considered free of malaria. In the rest of the country there is a medium to low risk of malaria. Depending on the travel profile, a standby medication with chloroquine can be useful, whose intake should definitely be discussed with a tropical or travel physician before the trip.

Language

The national language in Nicaragua is Spanish. The native language of the indigenous population, on the other hand, is the ancient Inca language Kichwa. In better hotels, tourist centers and at the airport you can also get on well with English. However, the possibilities of contacting the local population are considerably simplified if you can communicate at least a little in the national language. Even if the pronunciation is wrong and the vocabulary is low, people there reward every attempt to communicate with you and encounter one with a lot of warmth, kindness, kindness and helpfulness.


Calling & Internet

For telephone calls abroad, the respective country pre-elections apply. For Germany this is the 0049, for Switzerland the 0041 and for Austria the 0043. Nicaragua has the national code 00505. When calling with the mobile phone high roaming charges can arise. International calls are better done on the Internet.


Tips

If you are satisfied with your tour guide and the driver, they are of course very happy about a tip. It is best to hand over the money in separate envelopes, perhaps even with a small thank you note. Your hotel will certainly help you get the envelopes

Depending on the satisfaction, the permanent and local tour guide or driver guide receives approximately US$2- US$5 per guided day/per person and also the chauffeur receives US$1- US$2 per day for his work. Please note that these are recommendations.

Luggage carriers and room staff should also be rewarded with a small financial extra (US$1). Taxi drivers, on the other hand, do not expect a tip, the fare should be determined before driving.

In most restaurants, 10% tip is already included in the bill. However, if you are particularly satisfied with the service, the staff is pleased about an additional dose. If you want to make sure that the tip really arrives with your friendly waiter, it is recommended to give the tip in cash and not to include in the card payment


Domestic Flights & Trains

In Nicaragua, unfortunately, there are often short-term changes in domestic flights. We ask for your understanding. Of course, we will inform you about this in good time and rebook you to the best possible alternative if major changes are made. For local flights, an airport fee of US$1.50 will be charged, which is not included in the flight price (subject to change).

Behavior

In general, the Nicaraguans are very friendly and cordial people with a very great sense of celebration. Family and kinship play an absolutely central role in social, economic and political relations. Among young people, the family is still regarded as the most important area for support and promotion. If you are traveling to Nicaragua, don't forget one thing: the Nicaraguan understands something else by time than the German. On your Nicaraguan holiday, try to do it the same way to the locals and tackle everything a bit more relaxed, maybe you can even take some of this quiet mentality back to Germany.
