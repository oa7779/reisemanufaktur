Chile: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate

Chile has an enormous extent of 4,200 km from north to south connected with a width of just 180 km. Linked to the extreme altitude differences by Andes and coastal cordilleras, there are many different climate zones. Another aspect is the Humboldt Stream, which flows along the entire Chilean coast, provides low water temperatures and thus makes Chile a rather unsuitable country for bathing holidays.

Skiing On the other hand, in the Andes you can do very well and best from June to September. On the Easter Islands Subtropical climate prevails with temperatures from 18 °C to 28 °C. Most rain falls from June to August, otherwise this is a year-round destination. In Chile you will also find the driest region of the world – the Atacama Desert, with an average of one millimeter of rainfall per year, protected by the Andes and the coastal mountains.

Northern and Central Chile has a Mediterranean climate with clear differences. The almost precipitation-free summer is 18°C, while in winter it cools down to about 7.5°C. The driest months are July and August. It is therefore recommended to target your holiday in the period October to April.

The further we move towards South This is where oceanic climate prevails with summer temperatures of max. 16°C.

In Chilean part of Patagonia It is humid and cool with evenly distributed rainfall and temperatures between 4 and 12 °C. Travel in this area best in the months of December to February.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

Travelers from Germany, Austria and Switzerland receive a 90-day tourist visa free of charge at the border. You must cancel the copy of the form until you leave. Furthermore, you need a passport or children's passport, which must be valid 6 months after return, as well as air tickets for the next or second flight. Return trip.
 

For a trip to the Easter Island it is necessary to complete an online document and carry it printed out with you, as the flight to the Easter Islands can otherwise be denied (https://ingresorapanui.interior.gob.cl/In addition, you may need to show the hotel voucher you receive from us or our local partners. Items you need for personal use can of course be imported duty-free. However, fresh foods (milk products, fruit and vegetables, meat and sausage products) as well as plants and seeds are strictly prohibited. Also on the accidentally undeclared, carried apple in hand luggage can get high fines to you.

Food & Drink

Chile's cuisine has been changing for several years. Where in the past only monotonous rice dishes were found with beef, chicken or fish, more and more cooks are experimenting with the country's wealth of various ingredients. Above all, the incredible diversity of marine animals from the over 5,000 km long coast is outstanding. Nevertheless, a proper portion of meat must not be missing, so beef and chicken still dominate the menus. In the sheep breeding region Patagonia there is also delicious lamb meat. A very original dish from southern Chile is the Curanto. It is sometimes still prepared in a hole in the ground with hot stones. Ingredients are e.g. Shells, pork, sausages and potatoes. If it is to be a brisker dish, the Empanada typical in all of South America is recommended, which is also eaten in this country. During dessert, you should remember that the Chileans generally like it very sweet. Without manjar, a sticky paste made of boiled condensed milk, no pastry can be done. A souvenir of German immigrants is the kúchenes.

Also known is the Pisco Sour, a tasty aperitif based on clear grape brandy from northern Chile. Not to be forgotten are the Chilean wines, at the same time excellent and inexpensive, now the country has become the fifth largest wine exporter in the world! The fashion grapes Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot are strongly represented in the red wines and Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc in the white wines.

Electricity

The mains voltage in Chile is 220 volts, 50 Hertz AC. However, European plugs do not fit anywhere, so take an adapter with you or buy it cheaply on site.


Photographing/Filming

In general, you can photograph and film freely. However, you should nevertheless proceed with sensitivity and ask people for their consent beforehand. In the case of police or military facilities, as well as religious festivals, caution should be exercised. The photo material here is more expensive than in Germany, best cover yourself with sufficient utensils before.


Money/currency

The currency in Chile is the Peso (CLP), abbreviated as the US dollar. The best way to collect money in the larger cities is by using the Redbanc chain's ATMs. At best rates you will be charged a withdrawal (maximum of about €250 per day) of between €3 and €5. You can generally pay with the usual credit cards, but you will have to expect higher bank charges for cash withdrawals from your credit card. However, some German banks have contractors in Chile, which can facilitate the transfer of larger amounts. If you want to exchange money on site, you should do so in exchange offices instead of in banks. Never on the road! In hotels and at the airport, the courses are also usually worse than in currency exchange offices.


Health/vaccination

Special vaccinations for entry are not required. However, vaccination against tetanus, diphtheria, typhus and hepatitis A and B is recommended. A valid vaccination against yellow fever no longer requires even when traveling to the Easter Islands. We recommend taking personal mosquito protection measures into account. Chile is malaria-free and a (low) dengue fever risk lurks only on the Easter Islands. Long clothes, impregnated mosquito nets and insect protection, protect you from mosquito bites.


In large, rather dirty markets or generally street stalls you should be careful with the food, as otherwise you run the risk of victims of Chilenitis, the local variant of Montezuma's revenge, ==References== Also be careful with raw fish and seafood. When traveling to the Andes, it is important to ensure adequate acclimatisation.

Language

The language of communication is Spanish. In Chilean Spanish, syllables and endings are often swallowed, and there are many expressions only used there. Therefore, understanding is not so easy right away, if you only have basic knowledge. There are also languages of the indigenous minority such as Mapuche. The English language is not very good for most inhabitants. For this there are relatively many Chileans, especially from the middle and upper classes, who speak German because their ancestors come from Germany or they have learned the language in one of the numerous German schools.

Chile has a modern telephone network with several providers. In most cases, hotels or public telephone stations have a contract with a company anyway. Those who make a lot of calls are well advised with a prepaid telephone card, available at every kiosk. Internet cafés can be found especially in the big cities on every corner. Shopping malls and café chains also offer Internet access. Mobile networks quickly collapse in remote regions. In cities, for example, you can buy a prepaid phone.


Tips

If you are satisfied with your tour guide and the driver, they are of course very happy about a tip. It is best to hand over the money in separate envelopes, perhaps even with a small thank you note. Your hotel will certainly help you get the envelopes.

The permanent and also local guide receives, depending on satisfaction, around 10 USD per guided day/person and also the chauffeur receives about 5 USD per day/person for his work. Please note that these are recommendations.

In restaurants 10% tip is appropriate, but many restaurants include the tip directly into the bill. If this is not the case or if the service was particularly outstanding, a tipping is very welcome. You can leave them on the table, but it is more common in Chile to hand them directly to the waiter. If you want to make sure that the tip really arrives with your friendly waiter, it is recommended to give the tip in cash and not to include in the card payment.

Behavior

The Chilean people are generally very open and hospitable to tourists. In the south of Chile, the lake region, lives a larger number of German-born inhabitants. Many Chileans are familiar with German culture and the descendants of immigrants are fully integrated – so here you can enjoy an advantage in meeting locals. Of course, as a traveler, it is still important to show sufficient appreciation and respect for customs. A smile, accommodating and curiousness are generally enjoyed. At a greeting men shake hands with each other or knock on each other's shoulder. Among women or among different sexes one gives oneself a breathed "kiss" on the cheek. In comparison with older people, a lot of attention is paid to the Siezen (usted), whereas otherwise it is generally more quickly duped.
