Bolivia Country information

Country information Bolivia: Find out everything you need to know about the country for a trip to Bolivia. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Bolivia: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate


Since Bolivia is an Andean state, the land lies at over 1,500 meters above sea level, the highest peaks of the Andes reach in Bolivia to about 6,500 meters up. Accordingly, the respective climate depends above all on the altitude.
Overall, the rainy season falls nationwide in the months from November to April. The best travel time is therefore in the months May to October with long dry phases. In the High Andes there is a high mountain climate with precipitations that fall as snow all year round. 
Quite different in the lowlands in the north with their tropical climate. The temperatures are over thirty degrees all year round and it rains a lot.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

EU citizens are exempt from visa requirements, they only need a valid passport for entry, which is valid for at least six months when entering the country. EU citizens can stay in Bolivia for a total of 90 days per calendar year free of visas, with multiple entry and exits allowed. When entering international airports, you will receive an entry stamp including the date of entry. Care should be taken to ensure that a validity stamp is also listed. This is particularly important when traveling longer than 30 days. Children need a children's passport.

The best way to avoid taking Mate de Coca (Coca-Tee) as a souvenir to Germany. Although acquisition, possession, consumption and export are legal under Bolivian regulations, the German customs authorities consider that the attempted importation of Coca-Tee is contrary to the German narcotics law.

With the hot drinks there is not quite so special coffee, but a varied range of tea, especially the cocatee, which you should have tasted at least once. The "Mate de Coca", in which Coca leaves are poured with hot water, also helps against the altitude sickness, which affects many travelers due to the geography of the country.

For breakfast, there is "Api", a sweet tea consisting of poured maize, lemon, cloves and cinnamon.
Another interesting Bolivian drink is Chicha – a sour corn beer. In Bolivia, however, there are also other good beer varieties, some of which are strongly influenced by German. The best brands are Sureña, Potosina, Huari or Pacena. Wine drinkers also find good varieties in Bolivia, e.g. the Kohlberg or La Concepción brands.


Food & Drink

Most dishes of Bolivian cuisine consist of potatoes and rice, corn or other vegetables as well as a piece of meat, often beef or chicken. Potatoes, corn and quinoa have already been cultivated by the Incas here and remain an integral part of the cuisine until today, as they are also most commonly grown by farmers. The potatoes are available in several hundred different varieties, often using the same variety for typical dishes. Also popular are "Chunos, potatoes that have been air dried before consumption.

In Bolivia you will find many delicious juices, beers, wines or tea varieties. The freshly squeezed fruit juices are particularly popular, which are available in many restaurants but also on numerous street stands. All possible tropical fruits are mixed with water or milk.

Electricity

Power supply 220 volts. Electric appliances don't need an adapter for sockets.


Photographing/Filming

In Bolivia it is possible to photograph without restrictions almost everywhere. Only in a few churches it is not allowed. When photographing people should always be asked beforehand, but in most cases it is gladly allowed. In smaller places, look for photo accessories such as memory cards or other. Therefore, cover yourself with enough material before the trip.


Money/currency

The Bolivian currency is Bolivian. One boliviano corresponds to 100 centavos. Currently, banknotes of 10, 20, 50, 100 and 200 bolivianos are in circulation in Bolivia. In addition, there are coins to 10, 20 and 50 Centavos, as well as 1, 2 and 5 Bolivianos. You can also pay US $ in many places in Bolivia.
You can exchange money in Bolivia – but get a much better price for US $ than if you want to exchange €. So it's recommended to bring US $ in smaller denominations. Avoid $100 grades. You can exchange in banks or exchange offices (casas de cambio). Not uncommon are cash exchange (Cambistas) on the street. These can of course be very practical outside the regular opening hours and live off the business. They usually pay a course similar to the exchange offices. But even here there are black sheep, so be careful and exchange only small sums in manageable denominations.


Health/vaccination

Yellow fever vaccination is strongly recommended to all travelers before traveling to the designated yellow fever endemic areas. Upon entry from a Yellow Fever Endemic Country, entry to Yellow Fever Endemic Areas in Bolivia and when leaving for a Yellow Fever Endemic Country, a yellow Fever proof of travelers from the completed 12th month of life is required. A timely vaccination must be carried out 10 days before entry. Standard vaccinations according to the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute should be tested. Due to the Zika virus infection, pregnant women and women who want to become pregnant are recommended to take personal mosquito protection measures in any case. For the Chinkungunya and Denguevirus, we also recommend carrying long clothes and using mosquito protection throughout the day to prevent mosquito bites. There is a medium to high risk of malaria in certain rural and border areas. The larger cities (Santa Cruz, Sucre, Cochabamba, Tarija) are malaria-free. Depending on the travel profile, chemoprophylaxis (tablet intake) can be useful.

In locations over 2,500 meters, it is possible that the altitude sickness occurs due to too fast ascent to the height, which is usually perceived only after 24 hours or longer stay in height. Warning symptoms may be sleep disorders, headache, dizziness, vomiting and increasing air distress. If the symptoms do not disappear by a break or an overnight stay, should be descended below a height of 2,000 meters. Due to the pronounced altitude of La Paz (airport 4,070 m, downtown 3,600 m), symptoms of altitude sickness may occur after arrival in the first few days. Diamox is a medicine used against altitude sickness. Not to be underestimated is the extreme intensity of solar radiation in height - especially ultraviolet radiation, which must be taken into account with appropriate skin protection measures.

Government/Politics/Economics

Country name: Plurinational State of Bolivia

State form: Presidential system with bicameral parliament

Country:  1,098,581 km²

Capital: Sucre, approx. 285,000 inhabitants

Government seat: La Paz, approx. 800,000 inhabitants

Population: Approximately 11.3 million 

Religion: Catholics 78%, rest of the population belongs to Protestant church, Mennonites, Islam or Bahaitum, strong influences of natural religions

Language

The official language in Bolivia is Spanish. However, this Spanish differs from the Spanish spoken in Spain. In Bolivia, the c as a sharp s pronounced and not as in Spain as an English thIn addition, Quechua, Aymara, Guaraní and 33 other indigenous languages are considered official national languages. In the cities, mostly Spanish is spoken and in the rural areas, mostly Indian languages mean.


Telephone/Internet

Calling to or from and within Bolivia is now very well possible. The code from Germany, Austria and Switzerland to Bolivia is 00591, from Bolivia to Germany it is 0049, from Austria 0043 and from Switzerland 0041.
If you want to make a phone call within Bolivia, you have only three different departamentos codes:
La Paz, Oruro, Potosí: 02
Pando, Beni, Santa Cruz: 03
Cochabamba, Tarija, Chuquisaca: 04

Emergency numbers: Police: 110; Fire department: 119; Ambulance: 118.

The Internet is best used in hotels, restaurants or cafés. Also for calls to the home, it is recommended to carry out these via the hotel WiFi.


Tips

If you are satisfied with your tour guide and the driver, they are of course very happy about a tip. It is best to hand over the money in separate envelopes, perhaps even with a small thank you note. Your hotel will certainly help you get the envelopes.

Depending on the satisfaction, the permanent and also local tour guide receives approx.5-10 USD per day/person guided and also the chauffeur receives 2-5 USD per day/person for his work. Please note that these are recommendations.

In restaurants 10% tip is appropriate, because in Bolivia the service fee is not included in the bill. Although you can leave your tip on the table, it is more common in Bolivia to hand it directly to the waiter. If you want to make sure that the tip really arrives with your friendly waiter, it is recommended to give the tip in cash and not to include in the card payment.

Behavior

Since Bolivia is a Catholic and traditional country, you should be careful to cover your shoulders and knees when visiting sacred places, such as churches. In the larger cities, it is recommended to simply store the valuables in the hotel safe. Distinctive, valuable jewelry is not appropriate. In general, it is recommended to travel in Bolivia with the low-priced taxes. Always meet the people with due respect , adjust your behavior to local customs and respect the traditions. Most importantly, Bolivia's population is called Campesinos (farmers), and not Indians.
