Argentina Country information

Argentina Country Information: Find out everything you need to know about the country for a trip to Argentina. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Argentina: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate

As the second largest country in South America, Argentina has an enormous north-south extension of about 3,700 km. The east-west expansion is at least around 1,400 km. Thus one cannot assume a uniform determining climate; there are almost all possible climate zones of the earth.

North: With subtropical climate, the north of Argentina is basically a year-round destination, with it can become very hot in the south summer (January to March) of the northeast. April to September are the most pleasant months when temperatures are lower and it is generally drier. In the tropical region around the Iguazú waterfalls, you can expect year-round rainfall and quite high temperatures. In the northwest, the Andes, the climate is extremely dry. However, the few rainfalls fall in summer with temperatures of up to 27°C, depending on altitude. In winter there are maximum temperatures around 10°C.

Centre/Buenos Aires: The climate can best be described as Mediterranean. In Buenos Aires, the spring and autumn months are recommended, as it becomes hot and sultry here in the southern summer. However, the temperatures in the colder months are still around 15 °C and do not fall below 7°C at night. So it is also possible to visit winter without any problems.

South/Patagonia/Fireland: East Patagonia is mostly in the rain shadow of the Andes and is therefore very dry, as the rainfall coming from the west already rains before. It is quite different in Tierra del Fuego, where the barrier effect of the Andes falls away. In contrast to the rest of the country, however, the temperature fluctuations are extremely low between about 6°C and 14°C. Travel most meaningfully between December and February, as it gets very cold in the south winter there. In general, however: warm clothes!

Mountains of Argentina: Here is between mid-June and mid-October ski season. In July and August the ski resorts are often fully booked.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

EU citizens are exempt from the visa requirement, they only need a valid passport that is valid for at least six months when entering the country. A return flight or further flight ticket is sometimes required. The tourist visa, available free of charge upon entry, covers 90 days. Children need a children's passport.

Food & Drink

The food alone is worth a trip to Argentina! For breakfast you will find delicious pastries, such as the typical Criollos (small biscuits made from puff pastry). The most popular food is the asado (in the restaurant "parrillada"), with various grilled types of beef, spicy sausages and offal. As a vegetarian, it is generally a little harder because Argentina is considered the big meat country. However, there are vegetarian and vegan restaurants in big cities. Fish can also be found in good restaurants on the menu, but only on the coast really popular. Also highly recommended are the specialities from the north such as Iocro (spicy stew, meat, corn and spices), empanadas (filled dough bags) and Humita (cooked maize with paprika in leaves). Last but not least, make sure that everything is a good trace sweeter than you are used to. In any case, try the tasty Dulce de Leche (very sweet caramel cream)- No, no, no! As for the ice in the heladerias: As for the ice in the heladerias in the context of: do not let the frutas al agua escape – they have nothing to do with our water ice! 

The Argentine wine, one of the best in the world, is also famous. It is cultivated primarily in the west of the country. Many brands are recommended, such as: San Felipe, Trapiche and Postales del Fin del Mundo. A typical fashion drink has been since the 1990s Fernet con Coca (also Fernando). This herbal liqueur, originally from Italy, consumes the majority of world production in Argentina. The Argentine coffee is usually very strong and is usually sweetened equally strongly. Café con leche is a normal milk coffee, while Café cortado a coffee with a shot of milk. Al revés (reverse) or lagrima (d.: tear), means that the milk content exceeds the coffee content. National drink of Argentina is the Mate, a herbal tea, which is drunk with a tube from a calabash. The Mate drinking is a true ritual in which all members of a group drink from a single mate cup.

Electricity

Mains voltage is 220 volts. It is best to buy an adapter for brought electrical appliances in your home country.

Filming/Photography

In smaller places, look for photo accessories such as memory cards. Therefore, cover yourself with enough material before the trip or in Buenos Aires. 

Money/currency

The official currency in Argentina is the Argentine Peso. Since Peso and Dollar are abbreviated ($), you should always check which currency it is, generally traded in Pesos. With EC bank cards, as well as German credit cards and the PIN you can withdraw from ATMs Pesos, but with some banks only up to 700,-- ARS per single transaction per day and with high commission. It is recommended to only change money in Argentina (most practical dollars), preferably in Buenos Aires, as the price level is higher in the south of the country. Problems arise more often when it comes to cashing travel checks. Moreover, the strong inflation currently prevailing in Argentina is not to be forgotten.

Health/vaccination 

Yellow fever vaccination is recommended, especially when it comes to the Iguazú waterfalls for you. Coming from Argentina, yellow fever vaccination can also be required if you continue to travel to a third country. However, no vaccination must be presented upon entry from Europe. In addition, vaccinations against hepatitis A, long-term stay or special exposure are also recommended for hepatitis B, typhoid and rabies. Standard vaccinations according to the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute should also be tested. Dengue, Chikungunya and the Zika virus infection occur (very rarely) in the jungle areas. We recommend that you wear long clothes and use mosquito protection to prevent mosquito bites, especially during the day when the Aedes mosquito is active. Argentina was officially declared malaria-free by the World Health Organization in 2019.

Car hire

In Argentina, there is right-wing traffic. Transport rules are very similar to European ones, but they are often not taken seriously. In large cities hectic traffic chaos prevails during professional transport, while the rural roads are lonely and empty. On the country roads you have to expect regular toll stops. Most roads in Argentina are in good condition. Tourists are allowed to drive in Argentina for up to 90 days with a valid European driver's license, but it is also recommended to carry the international driver's license.

Language

The official language is Spanish with clear differences to the standard Spanish. In Buenos Aires and other tourist centers, the most important English services are available, otherwise Argentinian English skills – especially in the countryside – are limited. Grammatical difference to Castilian Spanish, for example, is the "du-form": you use the pronoun "Vos" instead of "Tú" . Thus, the form Tu puedes in Argentina becomes Vos podés. However, the particular form in the Castilian standard language is understood without problems. Some expressions in Argentinean Spanish have a different meaning to traditional Spanish – here you should be careful. One example is the castilian verb coger ("take it from us"), which, on the other hand, is a vulgar expression for sexual intercourse in Argentina and is therefore replaced by "tomar" or "agarrar". In rural regions, the languages of indigenous peoples ("pueblos indígenas"), such as Quechua, Mapuche, Mapudungun and Guaraní, are spoken.

Tips

If you are satisfied with your tour guide and the driver, they are of course very happy about a tip. Depending on the satisfaction, the permanent and also local tour guide receives approximately 10-20 USD per guided day and also the chauffeur receives 5-10 USD per day for his work. Please note that these are recommendations. In restaurants 10% tip is appropriate, as in Argentina the service fee is not included in the invoice. Although you can leave your tip on the table, it is more common in Argentina to hand it directly to the waiter. If you want to make sure that the tip really arrives with your friendly waiter, it is recommended to give the tip in cash and not to include in the card payment.

Behavior

The behaviours are generally quite similar to those in Europe, and they are particularly similar to those in the Mediterranean. All over the country, you stick to "Ladies First." The Argentinians are always tempted to be nice and friendly to their fellow human beings, so it can happen, for example, as foreigners, that one is invited to the "Asado" in a pub. In a group of people, even after a short time, profound conversations can be possible once confidence has been established.
