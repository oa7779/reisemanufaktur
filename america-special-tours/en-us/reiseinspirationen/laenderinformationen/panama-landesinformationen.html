Climate and clothing

The Republic of Panama is located in the center of the western hemisphere and borders the Caribbean Sea to the north, Colombia to the east, the Pacific Ocean to the south and Costa Rica to the west. It forms the link between Central and South America with a width of just 80 km at the narrowest point in the country.

All year round there is a tropical, very hot climate on the coasts and in Panama's lowlands and you can look forward to a holiday at 29°C. If you travel inland, temperatures will get a little fresher due to the increasing altitude. Rainfall is usually high, with differences between the Caribbean where there is virtually no dry season and the Pacific where there is a dry season from December to mid-April.

Entry, Customs & Visa

You do not need a visa to enter Panama for a stay of up to 180 days. Your passport must be valid for at least three months upon entry. You also need a valid travel ticket, as well as a valid residence permit for the country of the next trip. In some cases, a tourist visa alone is not sufficient. In addition, sufficient financial resources must be made credible. This means either 500,- US-$ in cash or the availability of the corresponding sum on the credit card account (detectable by credit card statement).

When entering Panama from Costa Rica by land, care should be taken to ensure that both the Costa Rican exit stamp and the Panamanian entry stamp are affixed in the passport, as there may be considerable difficulties in leaving or checking roads without an entry stamp in the passport.

 

Food & Drink

In the course of history, immigrants from various nationalities came to Panama and left their mark on Panama's cuisine. This is how the "comida típica" was created, a mixture of indigenous, Spanish, Italian, Caribbean and Chinese cuisine.

Panama is blessed with fertile soil, plenty of sun and sufficient rain, which is why you can find lots of fresh vegetables and fruits on the plates. In addition to the well-known varieties such as tomatoes, onions and corn, cook bananas or flour bananas (platanos), manioc (yuca) or yams are also eaten here. Look forward to the wide selection of fruits, such as mango, papaya, pineapple and coconuts that this country has to offer! Fresh fish and seafood can also be found in Panama in abundance and exert their influence on the Panamanian cuisine.

In the morning the Panamanians start with a hearty breakfast, which may seem somewhat unusual for the European stomach. You prefer baked liver (hígado) with glazed onions, some beef (bistec), sausages (salchichas) or eggs (huevos). In addition, there are fried cooking bananas (patacónes) or manioc slices (yuca) or traditional corn meal flakes (tortillas). "Empanadas are also a popular breakfast dish. These are corn flour bags filled with meat, cheese or vegetables.

At lunchtime there is mostly seafood or meat with rice (arroz), mashed potatoes (pure de papas) and beans (frijoles). In addition, the sweet, baked cooking bananas (maduros) are usually served.

Also popular is the "Ceviche", raw marinated fish in lime juice, with onions and pepper or the "Sancocho", a sharp stew with poultry, pork or beef and various root vegetables, which also dthe National Court of Panama.

You should also try the so-called "Tamales". Here, vegetables or meat are stuffed into a side of the May with olives and raisins and then wrapped in a banana leaf to be heated in a cooking pot.

In general, the inhabitants of Panama like to eat chicken, which is also reflected in many dishes, such as the popular "arroz con pollo" (rice with chicken).

In between, the "chichas" (fruit juices with water or milk) are particularly recommended for refreshment. They are available in well-known flavours such as mango, pineapple or papaya, but also in exotic varieties with tamarinde (tamarindo), ox-herzepfel (Guanábana) or tree tomato (Naranjillo). The drink "chicha arrroz con piña", a rice milk drink with pineapple, cinnamon and corn juice, is also foreign to the European palate.

Electricity

The sockets in Panama supply the current with a voltage of 110 volts. If this does not happen automatically, adjust your electronic devices to the lower mains voltage using a voltage converter. It is also recommended to take an adapter with you.

Photographing and filming

In general, the inhabitants of Panamás are very photo-friendly and especially children like to pose proudly in front of the camera. Nevertheless, it is always welcome to ask the people you want to shoot briefly for their permission. This is especially true when photographing indigenous people. In museums there is a general photo ban in Panama, as the government wants to ensure that the exhibited historical objects are not duplicated and exhibited in other countries.

Money and payment on site

The official currency in Panama is the Balboa (PAB) and is tied to the US dollar at a ratio of 1:1. The current exchange rate (as of October 2019) is: 1 Euro = 1.11, 1 Swiss franc = 1.01.
The Balboa exists only in the form of coins; otherwise it is paid with dollar bills. Credit card payments with VISA or Mastercard are usually accepted in larger sites. However, it is recommended to carry US dollars in cash. The use of bank cards such as the Maestro card does not always work without problems.

Health

No compulsory vaccinations are provided for when entering Germany, Austria and Switzerland. However, those from a yellow fever area (e.g. Sudan) needs proof of a valid yellow fever vaccination. However, vaccination is strongly recommended for all travelers from 9 months of age before traveling to an area where yellow fever occurs in Panama. These include the regions east of the Panama Canal: the territories of Emberá and Kuna Yala, the province of Darién and the areas east of the Panama Canal in the provinces of Colón and Panama. For travelers east of the Panama Canal (Panama City, the direct vicinity of the Panama Canal, Pearl Islands, San Blas) vaccination is not necessary.

However, there is a very low risk of malaria, dengue and chikungunya fever. Depending on the travel profile, a standby medication with chloroquine can therefore be useful, whose intake should be discussed with a tropical or travel physician before the trip. Protect yourself with appropriate clothing, insecticides and a mosquito net. Due to the Zika virus infection, pregnant women and women who want to become pregnant are recommended to take personal mosquito protection measures in any case. We recommend to check and complete the standard vaccinations according to the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute for Children and Adults. Travel vaccinations are also recommended for hepatitis A, long-term or special exposure vaccinations, including hepatitis B, rabies and typhoid.

Diarrhea is much more common among Panamans. However, due to appropriate food and drinking water hygiene, most diarrhea diseases can be avoided. Drink only water of safe origin and no tap water. If there is no bottled water available, preferably use filtered, disinfected or boiled water to rinse and brush your teeth. In addition, it is recommended to peel and cook your own food and keep flies away from the food.

Throughout the year, the disease leptospirosis can be transmitted occasionally by water contaminated with rodent excretions. The bacterial infection usually runs like a mild, grippal infection, but in rare cases it can also lead to serious involvement of the liver and kidneys. In the event of expected exposure, a drug-related prophylaxis with doxycycline may be considered in individual cases after careful risk assessment by a travel or tropical physician.

Overall, healthcare is often not comparable to European technical and hygienic standards, especially in rural areas. The cost of medical treatment as well as medications must usually be paid on site immediately in cash or by credit card.

Language

Because of the colonial past, Panama's native language is Spanish. The inhabitants of this beautiful country tend to speak very quickly and vaguely. However, if you try to shine with a little Spanish as a foreigner, this is usually very positive and the Panamanian tries to speak more clearly and more slowly. In some parts of Panama, the capital or touristic Bocas des Toro, English is understood and spoken. In rural areas, however, English skills are rare for the population. Some indigenous tribes continue to talk in their native language. Patois, a Creole language from the Caribbean with English roots, is spoken along the Caribbean coast.

Telephone/Internet

Panama has a wide-ranging telephone network. To use it, they buy a SIM card from any provider for their mobile phone and can use it, depending on the card, to make phone calls, text messages or use the Internet. In most cities and in the tourist areas of Panama, almost every hotel and also most restaurants have a Wi-Fi connection that they provide to their guests. It is also recommended to make long-distance calls to the home country via Skype or Facetime.

Tips

If you are satisfied with your tour guide and the driver, they are of course very happy about a tip. Depending on the satisfaction, the permanent and local tour guide receives about 2-3 USD per guided day and also the chauffeur receives 2-3 USD per day for his work. Please note that these are recommendations. In restaurants 10% tip is appropriate, as in Panama the service fee is usually not included in the bill. However, some restaurants include the service fee of "propina" already in the bill, which is why a close look is worthwhile. Give your tip to the waiter directly in the hand or leave it in the paybook. You can also pay for the card payment. However, if you want to make sure that the tip really arrives with your friendly waiter, it is recommended to give the tip in cash and not to include in the card payment.

Time difference

The time zone in Panama is the east coast of the United States (GMT-5) all year round and is seven hours behind us during summer time and six hours during winter time.


Behavior

The inhabitants of Panama are of a very warm, friendly and open nature. A warm "buenos dias" or in the afternoon a "buenas tardes" is put to the welcome, accompanied by a smile. You'll see: that does wonders! The inhabitants of Panama are aware of the beauty of your country, are rightly proud of it and do not call it "the bridge of the world, heart of the universe." Religion also plays an important role, especially in rural areas, and church services are numerous. They are usually held a little looser than in Europe, provided with food breaks and serve the village community, besides the practice of religion, also social exchange.

The choice of clothing in Panama is conservative or freaked out, depending on the region, only bathing clothes are frowned on outside beach regions, so women "above without stopping is even forbidden.

Remember, the Panamanian is different from Europeans. On your Panama holiday, try to do it the same way to the locals and take everything a bit more relaxed, maybe you can even take some of this quiet mentality back home with you.


Domestic Flights & Trains

Unfortunately, short-term changes in domestic flights are often occurring in Panama. We ask for your understanding. Of course, we will inform you about this in good time and rebook you to the best possible alternative if major changes are made.


Car hire

For Latin American conditions Panama has a good road network and a rental car with four-wheel drive is not necessary here. The main traffic routes are Carretera Interaméricana, which is also called Panamericana and Carretera Transístmica, which connects the coastal city of Colón with Panama City. However, especially on side roads and along the entire Caribbean coast, the road quality leaves something to be desired and with its countless potholes and cracks invites you to drive carefully and slowly. In the case of professional transport, traffic chaos prevails on the main roads, as well as on the roads of the larger cities, which must be avoided. During the Rush Hour, Monday to Friday from 5 a.m. to 7 a.m., the "Puente de Americas" is only in the city and from 4 p.m. to 7 p.m. only to travel out of town to relieve the traffic.

Please also note the strict belt duty, which is strictly controlled in the otherwise relaxed country. The maximum speed on motorways is 100 km/h, on highways 80 km/h and in villages 40 km/h. Tourists are allowed to drive in Panama for up to 90 days with a valid German driver's license, but it is also recommended to carry the international driver's license.
