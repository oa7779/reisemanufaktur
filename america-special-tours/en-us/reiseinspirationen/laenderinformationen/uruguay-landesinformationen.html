Uruguay Country information

Uruguay Country Information: Find out everything you need to know about the country in Central America for a trip to Uruguay. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Uruguay: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate

Uruguay is located on the east coast of South America and consists mainly of flat land. There are only a few elevations, which are then maximum about 450 meters high. The best time to travel is November to March.

The months of January to April are particularly suitable for a bathing holiday, as the water temperature is at least 21°C. There is no rainy season in Uruguay, but it rains most often in April. July is the coldest month in Uruguay at 11.1°C.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

EU citizens do not need an entry visa for a stay of up to 90 days when entering with a valid passport. Children need a children's passport. The import and export of foreign and national currency is permitted up to the equivalent of US$10,000. Items for personal use may be imported duty-free, and electronic devices often have a precise control of value and quantity. Herbal and animal products (including seeds, fruits etc.) may not be imported.

Quickly in between you eat Chivito, a kind of Uruguayan fast food that comes along as burgers with lots of vegetables and a small thin slice of beef loin, or empanadas, a delicious and inexpensive food to take away. These are hand-sized chicks with meat or cheese, which you will find in every bakery shop. Gramajo is available in many bars. This dish consists of fried potatoes, eggs and ham. Sweet desserts, the main ingredient of which is usually Dulce de leche, form a universally used milk caramel that occurs in Alfajores (= cake sandwiches). In Uruguay one likes to drink Yerba Mate, where the tea is cooked in large bottles and passed on within the group. Everyone then drinks from the bottle with a special straw (bombilla). For the mate you buy the ingredients in the supermarket and simply create it yourself. A very popular soft drink in Uruguay is the Clericó. This is a brew of white wine, sparkling wine and fruit juice with small pieces of fruit. Uruguay is also known for its good wines, especially those obtained from the Tannat grape.

Food & Drink

The Uruguayan dinner is a prime meal and contrasts strongly with the rather spartan breakfast and lunch. The Uruguayan cuisine has many European influences, with Spanish seafood and Italian pizza and pasta dishes dominant. However, it is also agricultural and works with beef in all its forms. For vegetarians, Uruguay will therefore be a challenge. You have to rely on the ravioli, which is almost everywhere. But also for other culinary lovers the food in the country has its pitfalls, because it is by and large very rich in fat and poor in spices. The typical national dish in the country is Asado, which means grilled meat or animal offal over an open wood fire. Asado should be tried at least once on the Mercado del Puerto in Montevideo.

Electricity

Power supply of 220 V, 50 Hz; an adapter is recommended.


Photographing/Filming

People should be asked before photographing them. For your own safety, be careful not to present an expensive camera to everyone.


Money/currency

The Uruguayan peso is the currency of Uruguay. His ISO code is UYU. The abbreviation is urug. Peso and the symbol is $U or even only $. 1.0 EUR equals 35.06 UYU. A peso is divided into 100 centesimos. Banknotes are valued at 2,000, 1,000, 500, 200, 100, 50 and 20 NU$; coins in denominations of 10, 5, 2 and 1 NU$ as well as 50, 20 and 10 Centésimos. The euro and the US dollar are recognized as means of payment in Uruguay and can easily be changed to the local currency at the exchange offices. Hotel and car rental invoices can also be paid in US dollars or euros. Smaller purchases are better done with the local currency, which usually achieves a better price.


Health/vaccination

We recommend that all-day consistent use of personal mosquito protection measures due to the Zika virus infection and the Dengue fever risk be taken into account. Yellow fever vaccination is not required when traveling to or from Uruguay to a third country. Standard vaccinations according to the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute for Children and Adults on a trip should be reviewed and completed. Hepatitis A is recommended as a travel vaccination, and hepatitis B is recommended for long-term stays.

Language

The official language of Spanish in Uruguay is largely influenced by the Italian population. On the Brazilian border, however, Portuguese influences become noticeable, here you will find the language Portunol, a mix of Spanish and Portuguese. English is one of the modern economic and educational languages and is therefore also represented by many people in Uruguay as a second language. In Uruguay dialects are rather less common.
For example, while in Spain the c is usually pronounced like an English th (as in the English word through), in Uruguay the c is spoken like a sharp s (as in sea). However, the pronunciation of ll (as in llamo) and y between vowels (as in oye) is particularly typical as a German sch or a French j.

Tips

Waiters get about 10% of the bill as a tip. Shoe cleaners, suitcase carriers, porters, maids and other servants expect a few notes or coins because they live off tipping.

Behavior

Uruguay is the most European country in South America. People, mostly descendants of Spanish, Italian or Polish immigrants, still look very European. Many are tall and light-skinned. Uruguay is and has always been a country of migration, each family has its own immigration history and ancestors from Europe. Correspondingly friendly and open-minded, one is facing newcomers. Those who are invited to eat should try the food they are offered. With a private invitation in the evening it is fine to arrive about 30 minutes after the agreed time, otherwise foreign visitors are usually expected to show on time. As a guest gift are suitable e.g. Flowers. As everywhere in South America, Uruguay also places great emphasis on a well - maintained exterior. In churches and finer restaurants, short trousers and overly generous clothing are therefore inappropriate. You give your hand to welcome him. One conversation usually begins with the question of the condition of the other. The usual forms of courtesy in Western Europe also apply here, and good behavior is a matter of great importance. You should always show respect for older people in particular.
