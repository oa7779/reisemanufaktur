Costa Rica: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate

Costa Rica is located between the Atlantic Ocean and the Pacific Ocean in Central America. The climate is tropical according to the latitude with small seasonal differences. However, the Cordillera provide a difference in climate between the Caribbean and Pacific coasts.

North Costa Rica is dry between January and April, the temperatures depend heavily on the altitude. In the mountains you should adjust to temperatures up to 10°C at night.

The rainy and dry season also changes in Central Costa Rica This is where rainfall reaches about 2,000 millimeters a year.

The ideal travel time for Costa Rica is the Dry seasonIn the months of December to April it is quite safe to expect bathing weather. But even during the rainy season, the weather is usually nice at least in the morning. During the rainy season between May and November (with July and August generally a little drier), travel is a bit difficult, as roads are not passable and numerous restaurants and hotels are closed. Nevertheless, there is no permanent rain here but only tropical showers in the afternoon. Especially much rain is to be expected during the months of September and October.

Each year there are different Turtle species to lay eggs on the beaches of Costa Rica.

At the Caribbean Coast are the dates for this:

  • Leather turtles (April to November)
  • Real horseback turtles (May to October)
  • Unreal tortoise turtles (May to August)
  • Tortoises (June to September)

The sea turtles can be observed particularly well here in the Tortuguero National Park.  

At the Pacific coast The following turtles can be observed during egg laying:

  • Leather turtles (December to March)
  • Soup turtles (October to March)
  • Oliv-Bastard turtle (all year round, especially between September and December)
  • Real horseback turtles (May to October)

Suitable protected areas include Santa Rosa National Park, Guanacaste National Park, Ostional Protected Area and Las Baulas Marine National Park.

For Divers December to March are ideal, while Surfers excellent waves during the rainy season.

At the Caribbean Coast Costa Rican it is rather humid all year round. Here the average annual precipitations are 3,500mm.The sun shines most frequently here in March and April, the least hours of sunshine await you in July. On average, the maximum annual temperatures are between 25 and 27 °C. At night it is hardly cooler than 20°C.

At the Pacific coast it can get warm in the north quite 40°C. Most precipitation falls in June and September and then back into November. Further to the south on the coast it is a bit cooler, but in particular much damper.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

You will need a passport to enter, which is valid for at least 6 months after your return. Should your document be worn out or illegible, it is recommended to have it renewed, otherwise the authorities may refuse entry. A visa is not required for a tourist stay of less than 90 days. This applies to citizens from EU countries and Switzerland. In addition, it must be demonstrated that sufficient funds are available for the stay (100 USD per month). In addition, all tourists must carry a return or onward ticket within the permitted time of stay.

 

Food & Drink

Everyday dishes are e.g. the Gallo Pinto, a mixture of: Rice, black and red beans, coriander and onions. It is eaten either as a breakfast dish with eggs or as a main dish with fish or meat. In addition, salad, fried pancakes and some vegetables are served. The many juices or milkshakes made from ripe exotic fruits, e.g. Mango, Papaya, Guanábana, Maracuya, Melon are not to be thought away! So is the delicious coffee!
For Europeans, the Pejibaye is unknown – a palm fruit. They are cooked and usually eaten with mayonnaise.

Electricity

The mains voltage is 110 volts, you need an adapter with US flat plug.

Photographing/Filming

Those who like to photograph analogue should take as many films as possible from Germany: The country offers a huge selection of motifs. Of course you can also buy film material on site, but these are expensive and usually only available in low sensitivity levels (100 and 200 ASA). Rarely do you find such in ordinary supermarkets or shops. Batteries can normally be bought in supermarkets. Especially in the rainforest it is quite dark in the shade of the tall trees. Travelers photographing analogue should use at least 400 ASA movies, better are 800 ASA films or even higher sensibilities. The higher the focal length of the lens, the more important it is to use a tripod – unless you are very trained in photographing with a quiet hand without a support frame.

Money/currency/ ATMs

Costa Rica's currency is the Colón (with 100 Céntimos), which is subject to strong inflation. Prices are therefore expressed in USD, since it has almost become the second currency and is the basis for exchange rates. Currency exchange is only available in banks and authorized hotels (there it's faster!) as well as travel agencies and casas de cambio possible. Credit cards (Amex, Visa) are widely used. Replacement of Colones in USD is only possible up to 50 USD.

Health/vaccination

No compulsory vaccinations are provided for when entering Germany, Austria and Switzerland. However, those from a yellow fever area (e.g. Sudan) needs proof of a valid yellow fever vaccination. Costa Rica is largely free of epidemic diseases, the medical care in the capital is excellent. Malaria and dengue fever are at risk in coastal areas and regions below 600 meters. Protect yourself with appropriate clothing, insecticides and a mosquito net. The waterworks warn of drinking water outside San José. Therefore: Avoid ice cubes and dodge on mineral water.

Government/Politics/Economics

Country name: Republic of Costa Rica, República de Costa Rica

State form: Presidential Government

Country: 51,100 km² (comparable to Lower Saxony)

Capital: San José, about 355,000 inhabitants)

Population: 4.7 million, with around 94% descendants of European immigrants, about 5% Africans, Asians and indigenous people; share of foreigners: 9%

Religion: Catholic (state religion, approx. 76.3%) Evangelical (13.7%) other (10%)

Language

In Costa Rica, you can communicate relatively well with English. Only away from tourist areas it can rarely happen that you do not get to the destination with English. Often even restaurants have English menus. In the Caribbean, the locals themselves speak an English dialect that Patois. However, mastering a few sentences in Spanish is always an advantage to make it easier to get in contact with the locals.

Telephone/Internet

Code to Costa Rica is 00506
National code to Germany / Austria / Switzerland 0041/43/49
Public telephones can be found anywhere in the country, in hotels, restaurants and shops. You use telephone cards (converted from € 0.75 to €4.50). In Costa Rica there are no prefixes. Three minutes to Europe cost around €1.50, from the hotel it is about 30% more expensive. Most providers have roaming contracts with the Costa Rican provider ICE. Prepaid tickets are available for tourists (at the ICE desk in the arrivals area of the airport and in ICE offices) converted about 4,- €. When renting a mobile phone, 100 to 200 USD security deposit is required.

Tips

In the restaurant the service is included in the bill, therefore Costa Ricans usually do not give a tip. However, tourists are expected to do so more frequently. Roommaids should receive about 300 Colones per night, luggage carriers 200 Colones per piece of luggage.

 

Behavior

Respect and kindness is a matter of course in Costa Rica. In the country, however, the class consciousness is still relatively strong, according to which the peasants and simpler workers automatically subordinate themselves to the higher rank. There is a great deal of emphasis on the professional title, so it is also used in the address. Costa Rica is Catholic, gives the Church a high rank and has a strong faith. But you are open to new opinions and respect them. Depending on the region, the choice of clothing is conservative to freak out. Only bathing clothes are frowned upon outside of beach regions, as well as staying on the beach "topless". In general, people are as varied as their nature, it is important to observe carefully and respectfully adapt, one will appreciate their accommodacy!
