Brazil Country information

Brazil Country information: Find out everything you need to know about the country for a trip to Brazil. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Brazil: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate

Brazil is a country with a huge north-south expansion and from east to west. Large parts are in the tropics, so the climate is mostly tropical or subtropical. That's why Brazil is a year-round destination depending on the region.
All year round, the Northern coastal areas Maximum temperatures of over thirty degrees, but a steady wind blows. From July to December the least rain falls here, accordingly this period is recommended.

If you use the Amazon region you want to visit, you should plan in the period from June to October. Rain falls here all year round, which is limited to one or two hours per day during the dry season. The same applies to travel through the Pantanal. It is recommended to travel here between the end of April and September, as large amounts of precipitation fall in the rainy season from October to April.

Direction South and in the area around Rio de Janeiro, the climate is subtropically temperate; there are significant temperature differences during the year. From November to March, the temperatures reach about 30 °C during the day and drop to a pleasant 20°C at night. In the period from December to February, however, the coast is frequented by numerous local holidaymakers. The southern winter from May to September is dryer and less crowded (except July, when Brazilians have school holidays), the maximum daily temperatures here are 20 degrees.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

Citizens of the European Union do not need a visa to enter Brazil if the stay is of a tourist nature and they do not want to stay for more than 90 days. You must fill in an entry card, which is usually given to you on the plane. You must keep the lower section of the map carefully in order to return it on departure. The passport must be valid for six months when you arrive. Since the agreement between the EU and Brazil has been in force, citizens of the Schengen area can enter or pass through Brazil for (business) travel, or stay here for up to three months during a six-month period. As a whole, tourists are only allowed to spend 180 days in Brazil per calendar year. In the plane you will receive a customs declaration before your arrival, which you will submit to the customs officials after receiving the suitcases. Gifts up to 500 US$ can be imported duty-free, in cash the upper limit is 10 000 R$. Officials are very careful with computer technology. When returning to an EU country, duty-free imports are permitted: goods up to a value of 430 euros, 200 cigarettes, two litres of wine and one litre of spirits.

Food & Drink

Meat is an important component of Brazilian food, fish and shellfish are also given a major role in rivers and the sea. Popular side dishes are rice, black beans, farofa (a manioc flour), potatoes and pasta. An inexpensive way to eat is the Comida a kilo. At a buffet you fill your plate, put it at the checkout on a scale and pay according to the weight of the food. The selection of fruits is also fantastic – many people have never heard of it in Europe. Also the Brazilian coffee can be seen, especially the Cafezinho after dinner! Of course, the national drink must not be missing – the Caipirinha with Brazilian rum, lemons, sugar cane schnapps, cane sugar and ice cream.

Electricity

The power supply is not quite uniformly regulated in Brazil and is between 110 and 220 V. It is best to carry a universal adapter.


Photographing/Filming

In general, you can photograph and film easily. In some regions, however, be respectful of the locals and respect their freedom. In rural areas or on religious occasions in particular, restraint is required. In case of doubt, simply address the people directly and obtain permission. No shyness - the Brazilians are a friendly people and will most likely agree. A special case is the photography of Capoeira dancers or the famous Baianas in Salvador da Bahia. Here a fee is often demanded immediately after snapping a photo. So be safe and just ask!


Money/currency

Since the introduction of the Real (BRL), the exchange rate has not been subject to any major fluctuations. The biggest real note is the blue 100-real note, but more often in circulation are the brown 50-real notes. The small notes of 20, 10, 5, and 2 should always be included. The 1 real coin is similar to the 1 euro coin, and there are also two different 50, 25, 10 and 5 cent coins. Due to the strong Brazilian real travel in Brazil has become comparatively expensive. Especially in the southeast, prices are comparatively high.

Common international credit cards are accepted everywhere in the country. Cash withdrawals with German credit cards or with EC-cards are possible at correspondingly marked machines (including Citibank, Banco24Horas, Banco do Brasil, Bradesco). However, many travelers report difficulties. Therefore, it is recommended to ask the bank before leaving if cash withdrawals are possible in Brazil and not rely solely on a supply of Brazilian currency to ATMs. V-Pay cards are not accepted abroad outside Europe. In the case of failed cash withdrawals at ATMs, preferably the bank receipt is to be kept because sometimes there is still a burden on the domestic account.

 

Health/vaccination

No vaccinations are required for entry to Brazil, but yellow fever vaccination and malaria prophylaxis is recommended if you travel to the appropriate areas. Vaccination is also recommended for visiting the Iguaçu waterfalls. In addition, the proof of vaccination protection coming from Brazil on entry is required when traveling to certain third countries.

In addition, of course, a sufficient insect protection must be observed. Prevention against hepatitis A and B is also recommended. Standard vaccinations should be checked and completed on a trip according to the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute for Children and Adults. Due to Zika virus infection and dengue fever, the consistent use of personal mosquito protection measures throughout the day should be taken into account. Depending on the travel profile, it can be useful to take an emergency medication for the prevention of malaria disease in individual cases, whose selection should be discussed with a tropical or travel physician before the trip.

Government/Economy/Policy


Country name: Federal Republic of Brazil (República Federativa do Brasil)

State form: Presidential Federal Republic

Country: 8.5 million km², 47% of the area of South America

Capital: Brasília, 450,000 inhabitants

Population: 208 million, growth 1.1% p.a.; ethnic diversity, about 800,000 indigenous people

Religion: Mostly Roman Catholic, about 68%

Language

Brazilian Portuguese is spoken in Brazil. Emphasis is placed on "Brazilian" because it differs substantially from the Portuguese spoken in Europe. If you already have previous knowledge, pay great attention to your language usage because many expressions that are quite common in Portugal can have estimating or even insulting meanings in Brazil. In the tourist hot spots you will also get along with English, but it is really recommended to acquire at least a few expressions in advance, as it is not far from English and other foreign languages. If you have Spanish skills, it will be an additional support for understanding, as the languages are similar in some places.


Telephone/Internet

Internet cafés are widespread in Brazil. The costs range from 1,50 € to 2,50 € per hour. However, hotels usually have Internet options themselves. Even in more remote areas such as the Pantanal or the Amazon, you have some (slower) internet access. To use your mobile phone and purchase a Brazilian SIM card, you actually need a tax number (CPF). However, almost all network operators now have the possibility to buy a SIM as a foreigner on presentation of their passport. In Brazil, mobile networks are organized regionally. In order to call a Brazilian mobile phone, the respective regional code must therefore also be chosen. Brazil: 0055 Germany: 0021 + 49/41/43


Tips

If you are satisfied with your tour guide and the driver, they are of course very happy about a tip. For tour guides or driver guide 5-10 US dollars per day, for a pure chauffeur a little less, is considered the benchmark. The hotel page is also pleased with a small attention of 2-3 Real. Please note that these are recommendations. Most restaurants include the tip (10-15%) already in the bill. With particularly good service, the waiter is happy about an additional gift. However, this should never be left on the table when leaving the restaurant, but should always be given directly to the service staff.

Behavior

Brazilians are usually very open and also speak very freely about sensitive political issues such as corruption. As a tourist, it is always advisable to take an always polite attitude and not to speak provocatively, as otherwise one can cause irritation. The Brazilians are also open in terms of acceptance. For example, as regards tolerance towards homosexuals, they have a clear advantage over their neighbouring countries. Nevertheless, there are of course very conservative areas here too, especially in the north. Be aware that racist acts are very serious offences in Brazil, which are punished in a clear case – with up to eight years in prison. Therefore, be mindful and respectful. Wangen-Küssen (Gemeinde ist nur die zwischen Handbezieh der Wangen) is well known in Brazil and not unusual even at the first encounter, but one shakes hands among men rather, the same applies on formal occasions. In general, there is much more body contact in conversations than in Europeans. Standing close together, holding each other's arm, even hugging is normal among not very familiar persons.

Time difference

In Brazil, there are four time zones. In many parts of the country, the time of Brasília (- 4 hours) is valid. Depending on the change in summer time, the time difference in the different regions of Brazil is between 3 and 6 hours.
