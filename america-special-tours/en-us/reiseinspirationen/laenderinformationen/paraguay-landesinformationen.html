Paraguay Country information

Paraguay Country Information: Find out everything you need to know about the country for a trip to Paraguay. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Paraguay: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate

The South American Spring (September to December) is a particularly good time for Paraguay trips. The temperatures then always move around 25°C during the day. In summer (December to March) the thermometer can rise to above 40°C. The autumn (April to June) often gives the country changing weather and high humidity. Since Paraguay has both tropical and subtropical regions, precipitation is quite different throughout the year. Although there are no pronounced rainy seasons as well as pronounced dry seasons, the frequency and intensity of the rains can be very different depending on the region and season.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

EU citizens are exempt from the visa requirement, they only need one passport for entry, which is valid for at least six months when entering the country. For a stay of up to 90 days, German nationals only have to present a valid passport, the "International Entry Card" issued by the airline prior to landing and the form with the affidavit that no plants or animal food or foreign currency in excess of USD 10,000 will be imported to obtain the entry stamp.
Travelers traveling by land must pay particular attention to the border crossings of Ciudad del Este/Foz do Iguaçu (Brazil) in the east, Encarnación/Posadas (Argentina) in the south and Puerto Falcón (Argentina opposite Asunción) in the west of the country, that their passport is issued by:
the Paraguayan immigration authorities will be stamped with an entry stamp. Otherwise, a penalty of about €37,- is payable for checks on cross-country roads and when leaving. Children need a children's passport. The import and export of foreign and national currency is permitted up to the equivalent of US$10,000.

Food & Drink

Paraguay's cuisine is simple, but very tasty, a successful mix between the strong that the European knows and appreciates and tropical or subtropical dishes combined with typical, like manioc and corn, that can hardly be thought of as a side dish. The cuisine Paraguays is also very healthy, seasoned with fresh ingredients such as garlic, chili peppers and limits. There's not much food in circulation, canned foods. Specialities such as ‘Chipas' (corn bread with egg and cheese), ‘Sopa Paraguaya' (paste of ground maize, cheese, milk and onions), ‘Soo-Yosopy' (soup of corn flour and beef chop), ‘Albondiga' (soup with meat dumplings), ‘Boribori' (soup with diced meat, vegetables and small corn meal dumplings with cheese) are worth a taste. "Palmitos" (palm hearts), "Surubi" (a fish of the Río Paraná) and the local beef taste particularly delicious. The national drink is ‘cana', which is obtained from sugar cane and honey. Sugar cane juice is known as "mosto". "Yerba Maté, a soft drink, is drunk by almost all the inhabitants of the country.

People should be asked before photographing them. For your own safety, be careful not to present an expensive camera to everyone.


Money/currency

The Paraguayan currency is the Guarani. There are banknotes for 20,000 PYG, 10,000 PYG, 5000 PYG and 2,000 PYG as well as coins for 1000,- 500,- 100, PYG, 500 PYG, 100 PYG and 50 PYG. In addition, there are also small coins, the Centimo, of which 100 represent the value of a PYG. However, due to their low value they are hardly in circulation, usually therefore it is rounded to 50 PYG. In practice, however, high-quality goods or services are almost always traded in US dollars, but they are the unofficial currency used in Paraguay and are also paid out at many ATMs if desired. A US dollar is between 5,500 and 6000 PYG. National and foreign currencies can be entered and exported without restrictions. Credit cards are accepted almost everywhere in the country. An exception here are cheap hotels. It is recommended to take along US dollar notes in small bills.


Health/vaccination

As of the end of January 2018, entry from the Brazilian states of Bahía, Espírito Santo, Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo as well as departure there has to be accompanied by an international yellow fever card at passport control. A yellow fever vaccination is also medically useful when planning a stay, especially in the southern parts of the country. Due to the risk of Zika virus and dengue fever diseases, we recommend taking personal mosquito protection measures into consideration in any case. There is a low risk in the border provinces of Canindeyú, Alto Paraná, Caaguazú. The rest of the country and cities are considered free of malaria.

Language

As in other Latin American countries, Spanish is considered the official language in Paraguay. Guarani, the native language of the indigenous people, has recently been officially recognized. By the way, in German we use some words originally from the Guaraní. These include the words Jaguar, Pineapple, Maracuja, Nandu and Piranha. Most Paraguayans thus speak a mixture of both languages, with hardly any of these languages in pure form. It may even happen that you speak Spanish better than 40% of the population. English is less understood and spoken. German, on the other hand, is more widespread and is one of the most important foreign languages in Paraguay. As in the whole of Latin America, in Paraguay, c is spoken like a sharp s (as in sea). In addition, instead of tu (for "du") vos is said. It's common in Paraguay, it's hardly needed.

In restaurants and hotels, the tip should be between 5-10% of the invoice fee. Taxi drivers are tipped a bit if you have a lot of luggage with you.

Behavior

Today, the people living in Paraguay are more than 90 percent descendants of the Spaniards and the indigenous population who emigrated between the 16th and 18th centuries. The second-named ethnic group is composed of different tribes, with the Guarani forming the dominant group. Their language and culture still dominates Paraguay's everyday life. The rest of the population consists of Europeans immigrated in the past, especially Slavs and Germans, as well as Asians. Especially in the 1960s, Japanese ethnic immigrants immigrated.
The landlocked state is located far away from abandoned tourist trails and therefore Paraguayan culture, topography and history are rather less well known, but Paraguay is increasingly becoming more accessible and hospitable to tourists. The capital Asunción is a clean, compact, beautiful and Latin American colonial city. The inhabitants of Paraguay are extremely polite to tourists, so for example there is nothing unusual at home to be invited to dinner, especially when there is a football game to see. Paraguayans are very positive, helpful people and extremely alien friendly and tolerant. This calm attitude is also very noticeable in everyday life: everything needs its time. "Tranquilo" and "mañana" are magic words that you hear again and again. The "tranquilidad" (separateness) with which the locals master every even more precarious situation is admirable on the one hand, and on the other hand rather need to get used to it for Europeans. Nothing's in a hurry, and most of it will sort of work out. A friendly patient smile helps in many situations rather than getting angry.
Perhaps we could take a look at the Paraguayans, because they are among the happiest nations in the world.
