Peru Country information

Peru: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate 

The significant north-south expansion as well as the long coastline of the Pacific Ocean and the great differences in altitude between sea level and the more than 6,700 meters high Andes ensure very different climatic conditions in Peru.

If you are planning a holiday on the southern Pacific coast, you should focus on the months of December to April, as the remaining months have strong fog and cloud. Further south, towards Lima, temperatures range from 20°C to 26°C in the warmest month (February) and between 13 and 17°C in the coolest month (August). Due to the Humboldt Stream, precipitation and air heat are increasing slightly towards the north. Temperatures on the northern coast are about 20°C at night and at around 30°C during the day.

In the Andes, the climate varies according to altitude. Differences are tierra caliente (up to 1,000m) with an annual average temperature of 25°C, tierra templada (up to 2,000m) with temperatures between 15 and 20°C and the tierra fria (up to 3,500m) with hot days and cold nights and an annual average temperature of 12°C. At altitudes above 5,000m, temperatures do not rise above the zero degree limit.

If you are planning trekking tours in the Andes, you should choose the dry months there between May and September, otherwise rains make some roads and paths impassable.

East of the Andes is the rainforest with year-round rainfall, which are always short, violent and predictable. From time to time, the El niño phenomenon leads to unusual weather conditions with strong heat and many precipitations. There is a constant average temperature of about 25°C.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

As a tourist and EU citizen, you do not need a visa for a trip of up to 90 days per half-year. Your passport must be valid for another 6 months after your return. When entering the country, an entry card with the length of stay, to be completed beforehand, shall be issued, which must be kept for the duration of the stay and re-submitted upon departure. It is essential to ensure that the entry date is documented by a stamp of the border authority in the passport and on the entry card. Incoming tourists must occasionally present travel or return tickets.

The taking of Coca tea leaves as souvenir is not recommended, since the attempted importation of Coca tea constitutes a violation of the German narcotics law.

Food & Drink

The great cuisine in Peru varies from region to region. In coastal regions, Ceviche is recommended (raw seafood) marinated with lime juice, red onions and parsley, served with sweet potatoes and corn cobs. In the mountain regions you will find exotic meat such as guinea pigs and alpaca. Of course, you also get "normal" meat such as. Cattle or chicken. For Peruvian food, rice and potatoes are required. Often, small chopped red onions are served with lime juice. Some delicious appetizers are e.g. Palta rellena (avocado stuffed with chicken or shrimp and vegetables) or papa a la huancaina (boiled potatoes with egg and olives and poured with a paprika sauce). ==References====External links==*Official website Suri (a maggot) or Lagarto (Kaiman). A particularly typical drink is the Mate de Coca (dried coca leaves poured over with hot water). In markets you get lots of fresh fruit juices. Not to mention the Pisco Sour, consisting of pisco, a grape brandy, mixed with lemons and beaten protein.

Electricity

In Peru you have a voltage of 220 volts. In any case, you need a US plug adapter.


Photographing/Filming

Publicly accessible squares and streets can be photographed or filmed, with the exception of "sensitive institutions" such as banks, police and police stations and military installations. Not all locals want to be photographed, ask the people, ask for permission and be respectful. The people in Peru are very friendly and open and will in most cases react the same way.


Money/currency

The currency of Peru is the Peruvian Sol (abbreviation: PEN). However, in most cities the American dollar is accepted as a means of payment, so you can also take cash with you in dollars. More and more often even the euro is accepted as a means of payment in tourist centers. In many places you can also pay with your credit card (e.g. Visa and Master). However, always check with your bank in advance for fees. ATMs can be found in all major cities and tourist locations.


Health/vaccination

If you are traveling from Bolivia or other affected countries to Peru, you will need a valid yellow fever vaccination. When traveling to the designated yellow fever endemic areas of Peru, in particular for the entire Peruvian Amazon area and the areas east of the Andes below 2300 m, all travelers from the completed 9th month of life will have a timely vaccination, i.e. 10 days before entry, strongly recommended. Coming from Peru, vaccination may be required when traveling to a third country.
When visiting jungle areas, you should consider malaria prevention in advance. In addition, vaccination against tetanus, polio and hepatitis A and B is advisable. In case of uncertainty or more detailed information, it is best to provide information in a tropical medicine institute. It is recommended to check and complete standard vaccinations according to the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute for Children and Adults on a trip. There is a low risk of malaria throughout the country under 2000 m, especially in Ayacucho, Junín, Loreto, Madre de Dios, San Martin, Puerto Maldonado and Iquitos. Lima, Cuzco, Machu Picchu, the Andean highlands, the coast in the south of Lima are considered malaria-free. Ica and Nazca. Depending on the travel profile, an emergency self-medication can also make sense in individual cases a chemoprophylaxis, whose selection should be discussed before the trip with a tropical or travel physician.
Another important health aspect is the height and the associated thin air when visiting the Andes. After an acclimatization time, fatigue and limpness disappear again, but headaches, shortness of breath and nausea are signs of an imminent altitude sickness, which only helps to move rapidly into deeper areas. A sufficient fluid supply of two to three litres a day, with great physical exertion, is important to prevent this. Do not take too big, heavy meals. Drinking coca tea or chewing leaves is also an effective method for locals.

Government/Politics/Economics

Country name: Republic of Peru, República del Perú

State form: Republic

Country: 1.285 million km², 3,079 km coastline

Capital: Lima with approx. 9.9 million Population (with the port of Callao)

Religion: 81.3% Roman Catholic, 12.5% Protestant, 3.3% other religions, 2.9% non-denominational

Language

The official language of Spanish is spoken by about 70% of the population. In addition, about 40 to 60 indigenous languages are common, especially Quechua with a 24% share of the language and the slightly less widespread Aymara. English is widely understood in tourist centers.


Telephone/Internet

Germany/Austria/Switzerland: 0049/43/41

Peru code: 0051

The big telephone companies are called Telefónica Perú and Entel. For both, you can purchase telephone cards for public phones. One hour to Germany, Austria or Switzerland costs about 10 USD. Cell phones only work in Peru if they are equipped with triband technology. With prepaid cards that you can purchase in supermarkets, gas stations and kiosks, you have the opportunity to make cheap local calls. Wi-Fi is available at airports, shopping malls, cafes, restaurants and hotels.


Tips

If you are satisfied with your tour guide and the driver, they are of course very happy about a tip. It is best to hand over the money in separate envelopes, perhaps even with a small thank you note. Your hotel will certainly help you get the envelopes.

Depending on the satisfaction, the permanent and also local tour guide receives approximately 10-15 USD per day/person and also the chauffeur receives 2-5 USD per day/person for his work. Please note that these are recommendations.

The luggage carrier is also pleased with a small attention of 2 USD. Tips on taxi rides are not common.

In restaurants of the upper price range, a service surcharge of 10% is often charged. In simpler restaurants, you can decide for yourself whether you reward the service with an extra supplement. If you want to make sure that the tip really arrives with your friendly waiter, it is recommended to give the tip in cash and not to include in the card payment.

Behavior

The locals are already well used to tourists, especially in heavily frequented destinations. But even in more remote areas, people usually meet travelers warmly. Although there are no religious or other taboos in Peru, care should be taken to respect the locals. Especially in churches you should not wear short trousers or skirts. Older people are given more respect. It is even sometimes colonized within the family. Among peers or younger people, however, they tend to be duped often and faster. At the welcome a kiss on the right cheek is common.
