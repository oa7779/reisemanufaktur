Ecuador Country information

Ecuador Country Information: Find out everything you need to know about the country for a trip to Ecuador. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Ecuador: Country information A-Z

Best time/climate

Ecuador is located in the North South America and directly at the equator. The Andes, which are up to 6,000 meters high and the Amazon, make it a very interesting destination all year round. The coast is dry from June to November, with a persistent high fog often forming. During the rainy season from December to May, however, traveling is just as easy. It divides the dry-hot southern coastal section around Guayaquil and the wet-hot part in the northern area around Esmeraldas. The average temperature is 26 °C.

For the Andes is recommended as a travel time June to December, with the rainy season starting in November. The driest months are June to September. However, these months are also subject to significant daily temperature fluctuations. April is the rainiest. In the lower altitude regions it is called a subtropical climate, while in the somewhat higher areas there is a spring-like climate and it is rather cold in the high mountains. A stay in the Amazon is most useful from August to November, as the rainy season continues from January to September. There is a humid climate with the highest rainfall values in the country. The average annual temperatures are 26°C. The air humidity is around 90 % for the whole year.

The Galapagos Islands Visit between January and July in the rainy season. With the exception of occasional tropical rain showers, it is warm and sunny during this time with clear sky and a pleasant water temperature of 23 °C. The dry season from July to December is often more difficult and the water temperatures lower with lots of wind. In the highlands of the islands it becomes rather humid, while on the coast it is hot and dry. The cold Humboldt stream from the south is responsible for this. Often you also have to deal with Garúa, fine drizzle, which is the result of tropical hot air and strong clouds of the mountain country.

Entry and customs regulations/visa

Citizens from the EU countries and Switzerland need a passport or a children's passport for entry, which is valid for at least 6 months after return. A visa is not required for a tourist stay of up to 90 days. Upon entry, you will receive a customs declaration which you will have to hand out upon departure. A minimum amount of money for staying in the country may be checked. In addition, all tourists must carry their passport or a copy of it, a return and/or onward ticket within the permitted period of stay. It is also recommended to carry a copy of the entry stamp and insurance. The same applies to resident foreigners (i.e. also German residents), in which case proof of statutory or private health insurance must be provided. For the visit of the Galapagos Islands, the presentation of a return flight ticket and hotel reservation for the planned duration of stay has been required since 5 February 2018. For the visit of the Galapagos Islands, the authorities, in the name of environmental protection, are particularly strict and demand not only return tickets but also hotel reservations for the scheduled stay.

Food & Drink

A lot of rice is eaten in Ecuador. That's enough chicken, fish or seafood. Everywhere the coriander, for the central European palate may need some getting used to. A typical dish with coriander, for example, is the traditional ceviche (sea fruits in lemon sauce). The selection of fruits is spectacular, so the selection of juices is also spectacular. However, there are also differences in quality here, such as in cheaper restaurants where there is a risk of only getting a completely sugared lemonade. Ecuador is also a pronounced "supplement". Nowhere in the world is as much consumed as here. From the popular chicken soup (caldo de gallina) to strong fish soups (sopa de pescado) to tasty potato hut cheese soups with avocado (locro de queso). But generally avoid buying food on the street, here a sensitive stomach can get sick quickly.

Electricity

The current voltage in Ecuador is 110 V. Mechanically you also need a plug with two flat contacts (like US adapters).


Photographing/Filming

In Ecuador, the freedom of panorama applies: everything you can photograph from public transport routes may be photographed without special permission. However, you should be more careful with police officers and military facilities. Indigenous groups of people can sometimes not be photographed for religious reasons. In general, of course, it is a matter of courtesy to obtain permission before photographing people. They are always happy about a small tip. You will notice that you have much more friendly faces in front of the camera afterwards. Digital cameras and accessories are easily available in medium to large cities and sometimes even in smaller towns. For your safety, you should not display expensive camera equipment too openly.


Money/currency

The official currency in Ecuador has been the US dollar since 2000, which is the most common currency in the world. Sucre In the case of the United Kingdom. It is best to change directly in Germany and take off further money on site at the vending machine. This can be easily done with your Visa and/or Mastercard or Maestro EC card. The best way to find out about the fees is by using your bank in advance. Go to your safety only to ATMs within bank branches.


Health/vaccination

Vaccination is not required for entry into Ecuador. In general, diphtheria/tetanus/poli vaccination is recommended. In the coastal and Amazon region, vaccination against hepatitis A and B as well as yellow fever and malaria prevention is recommended. Although no evidence of yellow fever vaccination is required when entering Germany directly, all travelers are strongly advised to use yellow fever vaccination from the 12th month of their life. From Ecuador, vaccination may be required when traveling to a third country. When entering from a affected region, for example. Brazil is required to demonstrate yellow fever vaccination. Due to the Zika virus infection, pregnant women and women who want to become pregnant are recommended to take personal mosquito protection measures in any case. There is a medium to low risk of malaria in the eastern and western lowlands, as well as in the east of the Amazon region. There is no risk of malaria at altitudes (above 1500 meters), in cities and the Galapagos Islands. Depending on the travel profile, a standby medication with chloroquine or at most in individual cases also a chemoprophylaxis can be useful, the selection of which should definitely be discussed before the trip with a tropical or travel physician. In any case, the consistent application of personal measures to minimize mosquito bites makes sense.

Language

In Ecuador the official language is Spanish. In addition, there are still some languages of the Indian population, which have different variants of the Quechua are.
Understanding with English is getting better, but it can happen, especially outside the big cities, that you don't get very far. It is therefore advisable to appropriate some expressions in advance. In addition, people are delighted to be welcomed in the native language. But even with a few pieces of Spanish and an understanding with hands and feet, you will be happy to continue.

Behavior

In Ecuador you will find an open, friendly and open people. If you show respect for the locals, you will also be shown respect. It is easy to get information and help. If you want to take a taxi, let your hotel help you and pre-arrange the fare. The Galapagos Islands are subject to stricter general codes of conduct. This is one of the few places in the world where you can still experience untouched nature, and that should remain so. For example, do not bring plants and/or animals from the mainland to the islands or to another island as each has its own sensitive ecosystem. Do not touch animals – sea lion babies or bird cubs will otherwise be rejected by their parents. The visitor beach resorts and marked paths must not be abandoned. More information is available from your local nature guide.
