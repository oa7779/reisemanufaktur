Colombia Country information

Colombia Country Information: Find out everything you need to know about the country for a trip to Colombia. The experts of América Special Tours will be happy to advise you!

Colombia: Country information A-Z

A brief overview


Country name: Republic of Colombia, República de Colombia

State form: Presidential democracy

Country: 1.138 million km²

Capital: Bogotá, just under 8.4 million Inhabitants

Population: 49,4 million Inhabitants with 3.0% indigenous population, 18% Afro-Colombians, 49% mestizos, 30% white.

Religion: Catholic (about 80%); increasing Evangelical (about 20%)


Climate and clothing

The capital Bogotá is a year-round destination as well as the Caribbean coast. Even in the "raintime" it only comes to short showers – it never rains several days at a time. The rainy months are mainly November and May and April. It is always advisable to have a poncho or rain jacket with you during this time if you are surprised by a tropical shower. In the "summer" months of December to March and July/August, the weather is dry and travel is all the more pleasant. Please note the holidays of Colombia from mid-December to mid-January and mid-June to mid-July, as hotels etc. are more frequently crowded here.

Entry, Customs & Visa

For a tourist stay of up to 90 days you do not need a visa in Colombia, it suffices the passport with a validity of another 6 months after return. Upon entry, the presentation of a return or further flight ticket may be required. At the border crossing points or at international airports, the duration of the stay is determined by the local passport stamp. Passengers should therefore ask the border officials to approve a period of stay which covers the desired period of stay, by submitting letters of invitation, air tickets or similar. Travelers wishing to enter Brazil or abroad are currently required to demonstrate yellow fever vaccination. In the case of customs checks, you should ensure that you have a receipt for any items such as emeralds or gold jewellery. An export ban exists for original cultural goods.

Food & Drink

The cuisine of Colombia is very regional and has many differences. Extraordinary exotic specialities are, for example, roasted ants or guinea pigs, which in some regions are considered a delicacy. Beef, whether steamed or fried, as well as chicken are popular foods. Fish is also offered in the sea regions. This is usually prepared in coconut milk and served with coconut rice. In Europe, coffee is often combined with Colombia – the Colombians themselves, on the other hand, tell you that the coffee in the country does not taste very good because the good coffee is completely exported. The presence of fresh fruits is overwhelming: in the supermarket there are up to five different mango varieties or six different banana varieties. You should definitely try fruit juices prepared with water or milk!

Electricity

The voltage in Colombia is 110 volts. Take an adapter with you for safety.


Photographing and filming

Colombia offers you an incredible wealth of motifs: landscapes, flora and fauna. Photographing is not a problem unless otherwise stated. However, you should exercise caution in military or police facilities. The locals usually like to be photographed, but you should proceed with respect and ask for permission for safety. Most people are happy about a small tip. Some ethnic groups, however, e.g. Afro-Colombians and indigenous people, for example, are generally not photographed for religious reasons. In general, be careful not to photograph unknowing drug deals or delicate demonstrators, as they hate to land on pictures. Also make sure to not present an expensive camera to everyone. Meanwhile, in all cities you can easily get camera accessories. In the best case, cover yourself with enough equipment in advance to avoid wasting precious travel time looking for a memory card.


Money and payment on site

Official means of payment in Colombia is the Colombian peso. One Euro corresponds to 3771 Pesos (as of December 2019). To take it with you from Germany, either Euro or US Dollars are recommended. These can be easily changed in banks and exchange offices. You should avoid trading on the street.


Health 

In the case of cardiovascular problems or asthma, it is strongly recommended to first discuss with the family doctor, because the altitude in the Andes, the associated low oxygen content of the air as well as the very large temperature gradients between day and night (up to 20°C) represent a fairly high burden for the human body. There is a certain risk of malaria or dengue in the jungle regions. Bend it best with sufficient mosquito protection and suitably protective clothing. If you are unsure, you can get detailed advice from a tropical doctor before your trip. In principle, you do not need vaccinations to enter European territories. However, you should have regular standard vaccinations checked for your protection. Vaccination against hepatitis A and B is also useful. Yellow fever and typhus vaccination is strongly recommended. Such evidence shall be provided when entering from a risk country. Travelers wishing to enter Brazil or abroad are currently required to demonstrate yellow fever vaccination. There is a low risk of malaria in the whole country below 1,600m, a very low risk in rural areas in the north. Malaria-free are the big cities, S. Andres and Providencia.


Domestic flights

In Colombia, unfortunately, there are often short-term changes in domestic flights. We ask for your understanding. Of course, we will inform you about this in good time and rebook you to the best possible alternative if major changes are made.

Language

For Latin America, the formula is: "If you speak English, open your doors. If you speak Spanish, your hearts open up to you, and you speak very clear Spanish in many parts of the country. Only in the Caribbean are letters often swallowed and in Medellín there are also some small differences in pronunciation. In Bogotá you will find a relatively pure Spanish. In the Andes and Amazon, Indian languages such as Quechua are also spoken. In Leticia, Portunhol is spoken, a Creole language from Spanish and Portuguese, as this town forms a unit with the Brazilian Tabatinga.


Calling and Internet

Germany/Austria/Switzerland: 0049/41/43

Colombia code: 0057

Communication in Colombia is relatively well developed. Colombia's landline works very well, even in the smallest village. In the cities you will find common telephone cards. Unfortunately, coin and card phone cells are often damaged. However, there are private telephone offices in each city. Even small shops offer some telephone services. Internet cafes can be found everywhere. The main mobile operators are Claro, Movistar, Comcel and Tigo. In most cases, Claro is the best choice. If you buy a SIM card, you can save expensive roaming fees. Outside the large cities and especially in the nature parks there is no universal Internet and mobile phone connection.


Tips

Normally, you give the waiter about 10% of the bill as a tip. From time to time, the servicio is already included in the final amount at about 10%. The waiters in the restaurant usually ask if you want to include the 10% In pronounced tourist centers a tip is expected. Luggage carriers should politely give a 5000 - 10000 (1-3 Euro) pesos. If you are satisfied with your driver, he is happy about 10,000-20,000 pesos (3-6 €) per day. Depending on your satisfaction, you can also leave between 30,000 and 50,000 pesos per day. Shoe cleaning boys on the streets usually live off tipping and expect about 1-2 dollars. Please note that these are recommendations. Taxi drivers, on the other hand, are not tipped.

Behavior

Colombians are a friendly and open-minded people. They rejoice at every traveler visiting their country, as they still struggle with the former bad reputation of the country. At the first contact with Colombians, it is best to talk about the many beautiful things that the country has to offer outside of the typical prejudices.

Time difference

At Central European time, Colombia has a difference of -7 hours during summer time. During the winter time, the time difference is only -6 hours.
