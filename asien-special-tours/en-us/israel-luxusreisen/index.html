A 13-day private journey, made your own

From the Mediterranean
into the desert.

A sample itinerary for a 13-day private journey, from the coast through Galilee and the Dead Sea to Jerusalem and the Negev. Designed for you and your chosen companions, it includes private transfers and a dedicated guide if you wish. Day numbers are for reference only; the route, overnight stays, and experiences can all be tailored to you.

Your route at a glance

Three landscapes.
Designed just for you.

From the Mediterranean and its historic port cities to Galilee and the Sea of Galilee, then the Jordan Valley, the Dead Sea, Jerusalem, and the desert. These are suggestions for your private journey, not fixed group stops. We will shape the experiences, pace, and guiding around your preferences. Confirm travel times, opening hours, and access locally and in light of current conditions.
  1. DAY 01

    Arrive in Tel Aviv

    After you land at Ben Gurion Airport, a private transfer will take you to your hotel in Tel Aviv or Herzliya. Keep the first day unhurried: settle in, check in, and—depending on your arrival time—enjoy the sea air along the promenade.

    Ways to make the day your ownTake a short walk along the Tayelet, admire the White City's Bauhaus architecture, or enjoy dinner in the Neve Tzedek neighborhood.

    OvernightTel Aviv or Herzliya

  2. DAY 02

    The coast: Caesarea, Haifa, and Akko

    In Caesarea, Roman history meets the Mediterranean: its theater, colonnaded streets, and aqueduct evoke the ancient port. Farther north, Haifa looks across the bay to the terraced Bahá’í Gardens; in Akko, lanes, caravanserais, and Crusader halls tell the story of a richly layered old city. Travel by private vehicle, at a pace set with your driver and guide.

    Ways to make the day your ownOr spend the day in Tel Aviv and Jaffa instead of traveling north. Consider a private Bauhaus walk, time at Carmel Market, or a visit to Jaffa's harbor. The route is yours to choose; these alternatives are not intended to be combined in one day. Visits to the Bahá’í Gardens depend on opening hours and access rules.

    OvernightHaifa on the coastal route · Tel Aviv for the city day

  3. DAY 03

    Nazareth and the Sea of Galilee

    Nazareth's Basilica of the Annunciation and winding old town shape the city center. Continue to the Sea of Galilee, where the shoreline brings religious traditions, agriculture, and everyday life together. A private guide can add context at the sites you choose, without rushing from stop to stop.

    Ways to make the day your ownShape the day around your interests: perhaps Capernaum, Tabgha, or the Mount of Beatitudes. If you chose the Tel Aviv alternative on Day 2, we can adjust the route and overnight plans accordingly.

    OvernightSea of Galilee or Upper Galilee

  4. DAY 04

    Safed and the Golan Heights

    Safed (Tzfat) is a historic center of Jewish mysticism and Kabbalah. Its artists' quarter, synagogues, and stone lanes bring religious history and contemporary crafts together. On the Golan Heights, basalt landscapes, scenic overlooks, and agricultural communities shape the scenery.

    Ways to make the day your ownChoose what appeals to you: a private winery visit, a stop at Mount Bental, or a seasonal walk in Banias Nature Reserve. Trails and access depend on weather and local conditions; for a more relaxed day, focus on either Safed or the Golan.

    OvernightSea of Galilee or Upper Galilee

  5. DAY 05

    A free day by the Sea of Galilee

    After exploring the north, enjoy a day with no set plans. Lakeside villages, calm water, and the Jordan Rift Valley invite you to slow down. In cooler winter weather, this is an ideal day for a relaxed private drive or a leisurely lunch.

    Ways to make the day your ownKeep the day entirely free, or arrange a boat trip, a private visit to Tiberias, or a short lakeside walk, subject to weather and availability. Your driver and guide can tailor the day locally; there is no fixed group schedule.

    OvernightSea of Galilee or Upper Galilee

  6. DAY 06

    Beit She’an, Qasr el-Yahud, and Ein Gedi

    Beit She’an's theater, colonnaded street, bathhouses, and mosaics form one of the country's most striking Roman-Byzantine archaeological sites. Continue through the Jordan Valley to the baptism site at Qasr el-Yahud, then south past Qumran to the Dead Sea. At Ein Gedi, freshwater springs and oasis vegetation meet the desert.

    Ways to make the day your ownThe archaeological site at Qumran offers insight into the ancient settlement and the nearby area where the Dead Sea Scrolls were found. Start early for this day of longer drives, and check opening hours and access to the baptism site. If needed, move Beit She’an or Qumran to another day.

    OvernightDead Sea, Ein Bokek area

  7. DAY 07

    Masada and time to unwind at the Dead Sea

    The fortress of Masada rises on an isolated rocky plateau above the desert. Its palaces, cisterns, and fortifications reveal how this dramatic setting was used. The cable car makes the ascent easier; hiking routes depend on weather and operating hours. Leave the afternoon for the Dead Sea and time to unwind.

    Ways to make the day your ownConsider sunrise at Masada, subject to operating hours and access, or a shorter walk in Ein Gedi Nature Reserve. Swim only at designated beaches that are open to visitors; do not dive or get the water in your eyes or mouth.

    OvernightDead Sea

  8. DAY 08

    Across the Judean Desert to Jerusalem

    The ascent from the Jordan Valley to Jerusalem brings a striking change in scenery, from salt flats and cliffs to the Judean hills. The Mount of Olives offers sweeping views of the Old City and Temple Mount. If time allows, follow the viewpoints and historic gardens toward the Kidron Valley.

    Ways to make the day your ownVisit the Garden of Gethsemane and the Church of All Nations at the foot of the Mount of Olives, or add another scenic overlook. Wear comfortable shoes in the Old City; religious sites may have specific visiting hours and dress requirements.

    OvernightJerusalem

  9. DAY 09

    Jerusalem's Old City and New City

    Explore the Old City at your own pace with a private, expert guide: the Jewish Quarter, the Western Wall, the Cardo, and the lanes of the four quarters. The Via Dolorosa and Church of the Holy Sepulchre lead through sites central to Christian tradition; beyond the walls, the New City offers markets, museums, and contemporary urban culture.

    Ways to make the day your ownChoose what interests you and fits your schedule: perhaps Mahane Yehuda Market, the Israel Museum and Shrine of the Book, or a walk through Yemin Moshe. Access to religious sites and the Temple Mount follows current visitor rules; hours may vary on Friday evenings and Saturdays.

    OvernightJerusalem

  10. DAY 10

    Negev landscapes on the way to Tel Aviv

    Travel south from Jerusalem through the Negev before returning to the Mediterranean coast. Open landscapes, ravines, and desert towns offer a striking contrast to the city. To arrive in Tel Aviv without rushing, keep the number of stops limited.

    Ways to make the day your ownBe'er Sheva makes a convenient urban stop; the Nabataean towns of Avdat and Mamshit require a longer detour. Makhtesh Ramon lies much farther south and is best planned as a separate excursion, with additional driving time or an overnight stay.

    OvernightTel Aviv

  11. DAY 11

    Tel Aviv and historic Jaffa

    Tel Aviv brings together Bauhaus modernism, creative neighborhoods, and Mediterranean life. In the south, Jaffa's historic harbor, stone lanes, and coastal views reflect both ancient trade routes and the city's communities today.

    Ways to make the day your ownShape the day around what you enjoy: a private Bauhaus walk, time at Carmel Market, an unhurried stroll through Neve Tzedek, or a private culinary experience with a local expert.

    OvernightTel Aviv

  12. DAY 12

    A free day by the Mediterranean

    One final day without a set sightseeing schedule: sleep in, walk along the beach, revisit a favorite place, or simply unwind. In winter, the sea may be better suited to long walks than swimming.

    Ways to make the day your ownVisit galleries, design shops, and cafés; take a short trip to nearby Herzliya; or enjoy a special dinner. Request restaurant reservations and transfers for New Year's Eve well in advance.

    OvernightTel Aviv

  13. DAY 13

    Departure from Ben Gurion Airport

    A private transfer takes you from Tel Aviv to Ben Gurion Airport. Your pickup time depends on your flight, traffic, and recommended check-in time; allow extra time for airport and security formalities.

    Ways to make the day your ownIf you have a late flight and enough time, enjoy a relaxed breakfast or a short walk near the hotel. Avoid sightseeing farther afield unless you have a reliable plan to return.

    OvernightTransfer to Ben Gurion Airport

This itinerary is inspiration, not a booking or feasibility guarantee. Before planning, confirm transfer times, opening days, holiday access, and current travel advisories.

Winter and holiday considerationsShorter days, possible rain, and short-notice closures may affect your itinerary. Around Christmas and New Year's, confirm opening hours and reserve restaurants and transfers well in advance. Transportation, shops, and visiting hours may vary on Shabbat and religious holidays. Avoid desert hikes during heavy rain because of flash-flood risk, and follow current official travel advice.

The details, thoughtfully considered

Your questions.
Clear answers.

Do you offer group tours?

No. We create private journeys tailored to you—never group tours, shared transfers, or group excursions. Your route, hotels, and experiences are shaped around your preferences. Private transfers and a dedicated guide can be arranged, subject to availability. Whether you are drawn to design, cuisine, the sea, the desert, or history, you set the pace and priorities.

How flexible is my journey?

Before you book, we design your journey together. Once you are traveling, we can consider changes as availability, local conditions, and the security situation allow. Confirmed services may incur change or cancellation fees; we cannot promise unlimited flexibility.

Can we travel from the U.S., Austria, or Switzerland?

Yes. You are welcome to contact us in English or German. Let us know your country of departure and, if helpful, your time zone. We will discuss international flights, arrival times, and private transfers with you. You may share your planning budget in EUR or USD; we will confirm the proposal currency and booking terms together.

What does a bespoke luxury journey cost?

There is no set price. Your trip's length, season, room category, private guiding, and experiences all shape the cost. The ranges in our inquiry form are planning guidelines per person and exclude international flights. We will prepare a detailed proposal after discussing your wishes.

What should I know before planning a trip to Israel?

The security situation, entry requirements, and availability can change at short notice. An inquiry confirms neither that a trip can operate nor that a booking has been made. Before making plans, review current government travel advisories and the specific terms of your trip. German Federal Foreign Office travel advice · U.S. travel advisory
