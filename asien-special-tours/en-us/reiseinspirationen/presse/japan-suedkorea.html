Japan and South Korea – tradition meets modernity

Munich – 16 April 2019. Japan and South Korea are not only worth a visit for cherry blossoms. This trip to Japan and South Korea from Asia Special Tours takes you to two countries where modern skyscrapers and ancient temples compete all year round, offering an insight into both worlds.

Flashing neon signs next to traditional archways – in Japan and South Korea, tradition and modernity are often only a hand-width away. Both countries are currently experiencing a particularly high number of visitors on the occasion of the annual cherry blossom season. In fact, the destinations offer a successful mix of adventure and relaxation in every season, while those interested in the 15-day travel idea In this context, it is important to be aware of the need for a better understanding of the situation.

The journey begins in South Korea's capital Seoul. Against the backdrop of modern skyscrapers, travelers will discover the city's districts, visit traditional houses and cook under guidance an authentic Korean lunch. Passing the garden of morning silence, we go to the village of Hanoe, a UNESCO site that provides insight into the original village life in South Korea. Discoverers will experience a journey into the past in the following days. In Guryongpo they see the old Japanese construction of the 1930s and the oldest star observatory in Asia and in Gyeongju they discover the temple architecture of the early Silla Empire.

As a contrast program we return to the city of Busan, where a boat tour as well as a visit to the UN memorial and the peace park are due. The international market, which has existed for 60 years, awaits you in Busan's colorful city center. After all, it's time to say goodbye to South Korea.

With modern means of transport we continue to Japan: The ferry leads to Fukuoka, the famous high-speed train Shinkansen to Hiroshima. After a detour to the island of Miyajima, whose red Torii gate is a popular postcard motif, we go to the cultural capital Kyoto. A tea ceremony, famous temples and an overnight stay in a Japanese Ryokan serve the traditional side of the trip. The following time in Tokyo is again in the focus of modernism. From the street food tour to the Tsukiji outdoor market to the Geisha district, there is nothing in the world's largest city that doesn't exist.
