Day 1 Arrival in Uzbekistan

Welcome to Uzbekistan! When you arrive in Tashkent, you will already be welcomed by your German-speaking tour guide. Together with your private chauffeur you drive to the hotel, where you can arrive in peace. Overnight in Tashkent.

2nd day city tour of Tashkent

Tashkent is still shaped by Soviet architecture – and a place where Europe and the Orient merge. Enjoy a discovery tour through the capital of Uzbekistan, which will bring you closer to the ancient squares and buildings, but also the modern part of the 2,000-year-old city. The visit begins with a visit to the Historical Museum in the morning. There you will learn all about the Silk Road town of Tashkent and its moving history of conquests by Genghis Khan and Timur. Afterwards you will visit the Navoi Theater Square as well as the monument of the same name and the Abdulkassin Medrese from the 16th century. Afterwards you will experience a ride on the metro, the first subway in the whole of Central Asia. Overnight in Tashkent.

Day 3 Tashkent – Urgench – Khiva

After an early breakfast, you will be taken to the airport to fly on to Urgentsch and finally reach Khiva. Make yourself a little fresh at the hotel and then take a trip to the old town of Itchan-Kala (UNESCO World Heritage Site) - there you will feel like you are in a dream from 1001 nights. You will visit the Juma Mosque, Khodja Medrese, Pahlavan Mahmoud Complex and Tash Hauli Palace, a masterpiece of Oriental architecture. It consists of various buildings with courtyards and is surrounded by a wall with several battlements. In the afternoon, visit the highlight of your tour today: the beautiful Khan Palace Nurullah Bai. Overnight in Chiwa.

Day 4: Chiwa – Ayaz Kala – Chiwa

Not only the oasis city of Chiwa has a lot to offer for culture and history lovers. You can convince yourself of this during an excursion into the area when you head to Ayaz Kala. The fortress from the 3rd or 4th pre-Christian century is located on an extensive excavation field. From here you have a good view of the vast desert landscape. Explore the ruins of Ayaz Kala, Kirkkiz Kala and Toprak Kala – Toprak Kala is probably the most archaeologically studied excavation site in the region. Residents were forced to leave their facilities when the Kuschana empire collapsed, and Turkish attacks destroyed the irrigation facilities. In the afternoon you will return to Khiva with many new and impressive impressions. Stroll through the streets again and let the oriental flair affect you. Maybe you already discover one or the other souvenir for the loved ones at home. Overnight in Chiwa.

Day 5 Chiwa – Darwaza (Turkmenistan)

To get to Kunja-Urgentsch, cross the border to Turkmenistan today. The city is located on the edge of the Karakum Desert and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2005. It was for several centuries one of the most important trading centers in Central Asia and the magnificent capital of the choresmic empire. You will visit, among others, the 12th-century Fachriddin Mausoleum, a funeral mosque and the 62-meter-high Gutluk Temir Minaret from the 15th century – the highest in Central Asia today. The buildings date back to the period of the Choresmic Renaissance (14th century). century. Afterwards, drive through the desert to Darwaza. Because of the gas burning in this area, rich in natural gas, the area is also called “Gateway to Hell”. You will stay in tents next to the crater and enjoy a typical barbecue in a very special atmosphere in the evening. Overnight in tents.

Day 6 Darwaza – Ashgabat

Today your journey will take you through the desert to Ashgabat. On the way you will visit Erbent, a small village. If you feel like it, you can talk to the friendly locals with the help of your guide and learn more about their everyday life and their lives. Overnight in Ashgabat. 

7th day explore Ashgabat

Turkmenistan's capital is located in an oasis in the Karakum desert. Unlike the name “lovely city” suggests, Ashgabat has evolved from a Russian military base. Today, the city is especially famous for Turkmen craftsmanship. Convince yourself in the carpet museum of the skills of the locals. Furthermore, you will visit the impressive Independence Square, the dreamlike Mosque Chesreti Omar and the National Museum, which includes 200,000-year-old stone tools and finds from Altin Tepe, Merw and Nisa. If you are still in the mood, then visit the excavation sites of Nisa – the former capital of the Parthians – which are now part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site. The archaeological remains vividly illustrate the influences of cultural influences from Central Asia and the Mediterranean world. Overnight in Ashgabat.

Day 8 Around Ashgabat

Today you will take an excursion to the Kopet-Dag mountains; there you will visit the settlement of Nochur. The families here live mainly from silk embroidery and are happy to show you their sophisticated craftsmanship. Nearby there is an underground lake: the emerald green Kov-Ata Lake is filled with up to 37 degrees of hot thermal water and invites not only to admire, but also to swim. On the way back to Ashgabat, visit the Geok Depe Mosque. You will also visit the beautiful and largest mosque in Central Asia: the Turkmenbashi Mosque, which awaits you in the sun white and golden shining and especially overwhelms with its interior. Overnight in Ashgabat.

Day 9 Ashgabat – Mary

After the splendor of the Turkmenbashi Mosque, today you will find a – no less impressive – ruined city: Merw. Your chauffeur will take you to the airport, from where you will fly on to Mary, also a UNESCO World Heritage Site. From the city you can reach the old ruins of the oasis town of Merw after only 30 kilometers. Over the centuries, the city has been the target of countless attacks and looting by the Parthians, but also by Alexander the Great, Genghis Khan and Tamerlan. The reason: Merw was one of the most important trading centers along the Silk Road. Overnight in Mary.

Day 10 - Through the desert to Bukhara (Uzbekistan)

Today you travel back to Uzbekistan via the city of Türkmenabat and along the Silk Road through the Kyzylkum Desert to Bukhara. Many people think desert landscapes would be boring and monotonous – but you will discover that this seemingly dead landscape also offers interesting impressions. Drive along the Amudarja River, the life-giving stream of Central Asia. Along the way, your guide will tell you a lot of interesting things about the country and people. So you can look forward to a varied ride. Overnight in Bukhara.

Day 11 - Brilliant Bukhara

Bukhara is one of the oldest cities in Central Asia and was once one of the most illustrious commercial centers on the Silk Road. It owed its former wealth to the location on the edge of an oasis, in the middle of the Kyzylkum Desert and developed into an important cultural and spiritual center. Even today, the atmosphere of bygone times is noticeable when you stroll through the narrow alleys of the old town with its dreamlike parks, mosques and wide squares. Get to know the city by visiting the Ark Fortress, Bolo Hauz Mosque and the 9th and 10th century Samanid Mausoleum. In the afternoon, your guide will take you to Poj Kalon with the Mir-I-Arab Medrese and the 46-meter-high Kalon Minaret, built in 1127 and one of the city’s landmarks. You can also expect the arched market halls as well as the famous Lyabi Hauz ensemble with the Nodir Divanbegi Medrese. Overnight in Bukhara.

12th day Places from 1,001 night

After breakfast, continue your sightseeing tour in Bukhara and visit the home of the merchant Chodsha-Jev – he was considered one of the most respected citizens of the city. Afterwards you will visit the Choir Minor Medrese with its impressive turquoise blue minarets. The afternoon is at your disposal. How about a coffee in the middle of the serene atmosphere on Lyabi-Hauz-Platz? Or stroll over one of the bazaars. Then you could meet Hodja Nasreddin, the Till Eulenspiegel of the Orient, whose cunning humor lives on today in numerous fairy tales. Overnight in Bukhara.

Day 13 Bukhara – Chess Risabs – Samarkand

Today’s destination is the legendary city of Samarkand. Along the way, make a stop at chess risabs. The name Chessrisabs means “green city”. It is not only a UNESCO World Heritage Site, but also historically significant as the birthplace of Tamerlane. You will visit the impressive Ak Saray Palace, which according to stories was built by Amir Temur and served as his summer residence. Let yourself be impressed by the majolikes and cut mosaics that cover the facade with countless motifs. However, the historic city holds even more sights such as the Kuk-Gumbaz Mosque or the tomb of Tamerlan’s son: Yakhangir, who fell off the horse at the age of twelve and died. Afterwards, continue your journey to Samarkand. Once here, you will be taken to your beautiful hotel. Overnight in Samarkand.

Day 14: Stunning Samarkand

Another UNESCO World Heritage site is waiting to be explored by you. The visit to Samarkand – one of the oldest cities in the world! – begins at the famous Registan Square, which is surrounded by the beautiful Ulugbek, Shir Dor and Tilla-Kori medres. Afterwards you will visit the Gur-Emir Mausoleum. Overnight in Samarkand.

15th day Award-winning wines cost

You haven't seen everything yet! Today your guide will continue with you the sightseeing of the breathtaking Samarkand. Visit the Bibi Khanim Mosque, whose construction was commissioned in 1399 by the conqueror Tamerlan after his victorious return from India. Of course, you also have enough time to stroll along the bazaar. Afterwards you will visit the Theatre of Oriental Costumes, the Ulugbek Observatory and the main excavation sites of Afrosiab. The highlight of the day is probably the dead city of Shah-I-Zinda, one of the oldest places of worship in Central Asia, which consists of 20 mausoleums. Afterwards you will take part in an Uzbek wine tasting in the Chowrenko wine cellar. Overnight in Samarkand.

Day 16 Back to Tashkent

Your journey will take you back to the capital Tashkent. Along the way, you can enjoy a special treat: at the Jizzakh honey market, people from nearby villages meet to offer their products. From May to June there is flower honey, in the following two months you can try “cotton honey” – a variety that is collected during the cotton bloom. Also with tasty apples, pears, plums and kaki you can stock up here. However, you will search for potatoes and onions in vain – everyone grows them themselves. In Tashkent, the Museum of Applied Art is on the program. Built in traditional Uzbek style at the beginning of the 20th century, it houses approximately 4,500 exhibits. Overnight in Tashkent.

Day 17 Tashkent – Kokand – Rishtan – Ferghana

In the morning, you will travel via the picturesque Kamtschik Pass to the Ferghana Valley. Along the way, visit Kokand and its imposing Chudojar Khan Palace, the impressive Dzhami Mosque, Kamol Kazhi Medrese and the two necropolises of Daman-Chachon and Madirakhan. Later you will visit the city of Rishtan, famous for its fine ceramics. Arriving in Ferghana, you can check in directly at your hotel. Overnight in Ferghana.

Day 18 Ferghana – Margilan – Kuwa – Tashkent

First of all, explore the city of Ferghana. Afterwards, your guide will take you to a silk factory in Margilan, where you will learn more about silk breeding and processing. From here you will travel to Kuwa, where Buddhist monuments are currently being excavated. In the afternoon you will travel back to Tashkent. Overnight in Tashkent.

19th day Departure

Today, unfortunately, we say goodbye. With 1001 new impressions and experiences in your luggage, you will be taken to the airport of Tashkent, from where you will start your journey home. The beautiful memories will always remain for you and often beautify everyday life.
Depending on the departure time, we recommend the Late Check-out. So you do not have to leave your rooms at 12 o'clock, but can use them for as long as possible before your departure (subject to availability).
