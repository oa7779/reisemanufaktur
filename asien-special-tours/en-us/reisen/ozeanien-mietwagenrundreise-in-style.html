Natural treasures for an entire continent: New Zealand unites on an area that is significantly smaller than Germany, as diverse landscapes as hardly any country on earth - from misty mountains to smoking volcanoes, wide coasts, deep fjords and dense rainforests. On the west coast of the south island, wild beaches, glacier-covered mountains and bush landscapes meet in the immediate vicinity. As a self-driver, you are flexible and have time to concentrate on the highlights of New Zealand thanks to pre-booked accommodation. You do not have to do without first-class accommodation: We have the most beautiful hotels and lodges for you in the program!

Day 1 Arrival in Auckland

Kia Ora! Today you reach Auckland. This starts your rental car tour on the North Island. Upon arrival at the airport, your driver will take you from the airport to the hotel. Your room stands out 14 The clock is available. Use the rest of the day for relaxation or explore Auckland if you are still enterprising despite jet lag. Auckland is considered a melting pot of European, Asian and South Pacific cultures as well as indigenous Maori heritage. This variety brings with it a wealth of unique restaurants and shopping opportunities. Auckland’s waterfront location has earned the city the nickname “City of Sails”. Do not miss the imposing Sky Tower, located in the heart of the city. Of a height of 328 It is the highest tower in the southern hemisphere. In addition to the silhouette, these buildings shape dozens of dormant volcanic cones around Auckland. Overnight in Auckland.

Day 2 Auckland - Coromandel Peninsula (ca.) 150km

Today you take over your rental vehicle and drive from Auckland on Highway 1 south to the Coromandel Peninsula. Along the coast of the Firth of Thames is the Township of Thames, reminiscent of the gold rush that broke out here in 1867. By 1900, the city had about 19,000 inhabitants. From Thames, it’s a short drive across the beautiful Coromandel Ranges Mountains to Pauanui’s posh beach. The Coromandel Peninsula is characterised by a mountain belt of volcanic origin . The contrast between beaches and lush green cultivated land gives this area a special scenic appeal. Overnight in Whangamata.

Day 3 Coromandel Peninsula - Rotorua (ca.) 190 km

From Pauanui towards the south, past other picture book beaches, we head to Tauranga - one of the largest ports on the North Island. They cross the kiwi plantations around Te Puke and come to Rotorua. A volcanic lunar landscape, hot springs, seething mud ponds and a vibrant Maori culture: Rotorua is a fascinatingly foreign piece of earth. And an unstable one: the sulphur smell over Rotorua proves that the earth's surface is only very thin at this place. Even today, it can happen that in the front garden of the residents the earth breaks open and a new boiling pool is created. Overnight in Rotorua.

Day 4 Rotorua - Hot Springs, Sky-High Geysers and Maori Culture

Rotorua in the heart of the North Island, located on the lake of the same name, is rich in Maori culture and known for its bubbling, geothermal activities. Millions of years ago, volcanic eruptions created a fantastic landscape of mountains, lakes and steaming earth. Experience dramatic geothermal energy here, recognizable by the typical sulfur smell. You will have the opportunity for a variety of activities such as a visit to Wai-O-Tapu Thermal Wonderland, Waimangu Volcanic Valley and Te Puia Geysers. There is also an important indigenous center, where you can watch Maoris carving their ornate carvings and experience traditional village buildings. You will also learn a lot about New Zealand’s nature here, for example in Rainbow Springs with its kiwi breeding station. Overnight in Rotorua.

5th day Rotorua - Lake Taupo - Tongariro National Park (ca.) 180 km

Go to Taupo today. North of Taupo is Huka Falls, a dramatic river landscape. Lake Taupo is a crater lake that was once formed by a massive eruption of the Taupo volcano. The water is larger than Lake Constance and similar to this a popular holiday destination, especially for anglers of trout. Enjoy the relaxed atmosphere on the shore of the lake. On the east bank of the lake, continue on to Turangi and on to Tongariro National Park. Overnight in Tongariro National Park.

6th Day Crater Landscape Mordor - Tongariro National Park - Wellington (ca.) 330 km

The Tongariro National Park contains three still active volcanoes, which are venerated as sacred by the Maori in several places of worship in the area. The wise foresight of Maori chief Te Heuheu Tukino laid the foundation for the establishment of the fourth oldest national park in the world. Lava casts flowed from several eruption channels for thousands of years. In October 1996, there were still heavy ejections. The barren crater landscape also served as a location for "Mordor" in "The Lord of the Rings". Along the Kapiti coast, head south to Wellington. Wellington is not only the seat of government, but also the artistic and cultural highlight of the country – it houses a variety of art galleries and interesting museums. With more than 300 bars, cafes and restaurants, a lively scene and many culinary highlights await you. Overnight in Wellington.

Day 7 Wellington - the capital of New Zealand

Worth seeing is the surroundings of Parliament with the former government buildings, of which the oldest – from 1876 – is considered the second largest timber construction in the world. Opposite is the current government. Architecturally striking is the modern cultivation, known as the “bee basket”. For a glimpse into the country’s culture, visit Te Papa, New Zealand’s National Museum, which conveys much to know about the country through its innovative and highly interactive exhibition. In addition, you should not miss a ride in the capital by cable car – the historic tram that goes up to the Botanical Garden. From here there is also a beautiful view of the city and its port. Overnight in Wellington.

Day 8 Through the Cook Water Road to the south (approximately). 30 km

With the ferry you cross over to the south island. First, you will pass through the Cook Water Road. Then glide through the Marlborough Sounds, an area of “drunken” valleys created by the geological subsidence of a central mountain range. The original mountain range is still evidenced by steep mountain ridges, which today spring out as narrow peninsulas between deep sea arms. The ferry trip is an experience! You can drop off the rental vehicle in Wellington and pick up another vehicle in Picton. Or take the vehicle on the ferry. Please indicate which variant you prefer. Overnight in Blenheim.

9th Day On a glass of wine to Blenheim

Blenheim in the Marlborough plain is known for its wine. Cultivation in this region began in the 1970s, when “Montana Wines” was looking for new land in Auckland. The composition of the earth, high sunshine duration, a long autumn and a cold winter are excellent conditions for a first-class wine, especially Sauvignon Blanc. Overnight in Blenheim.

Day 10 Blenheim – Nelson (approximately). 115 km

Make your way to the Nelson Tasman region with its lively coastal towns full of bustling cafes, charming boutiques and cool art galleries. The area is rich in spectacular scenery. There are three national parks just 90 minutes from the city. Overnight in Nelson.

Day 11 Nelson and Abel Tasman National Park

Nelson is the city with the most sunshine hours in the country. This is where New Zealand’s first street café opened, and many alternative artisans have settled. Fifty kilometers of impressive coastal landscapes, golden yellow beaches, fascinating animals – this is the Abel Tasman National Park. Overnight in Nelson.

Day 12 Nelson – Hokitika (ca.) 350 km

Drive down the west coast and cross the Paparoa National Park with its cliffs, limestone canyons and wild, secluded beaches. Along the way, we recommend stopping at Punakaiki Pancake Rocks and Blowholes, where the wavering sea has shaped amazing shapes from the rocks. Overnight in Hokitika.

Day 13 Hokitika - Franz Josef (ca.) 135 km

Continue along the west coast. This remote area has a wild romantic landscape with huge rainforests, bubbly waterfalls and snow-capped mountains. If you wish, you can walk along the Franz Josef Glacier a glacier valley path along the mighty Waiho River. Or do you prefer a helicopter flight over the glacier? Overnight at the Franz Josef Glacier.

Day 14 Franz Josef Glacier – Wanaka (ca.) 285 km

Fox Glacier is located in Westland National Park. As in nearby Franz Josef, the glacier stretches down here far into the valley – at an altitude of about 300 meters above sea level – and thus borders directly on the edge of the rainforest. Today, leave the glacier region in a southwestern direction along the Haast River to Central Otago, past Lake Wanaka, until Wanaka. Overnight in Wanaka.

Day 15 Wanaka – Queenstown (ca.) 115 km

Wanaka is something like the quiet little sister of Queenstown: here, too, the area invites for walks, hikes and bike rides, but the city itself is quieter. In front of the dramatic scenery around Wanaka with its impressive mountain and hill landscapes, some of the exterior shots of the epic film trilogy “Lord of the Rings” take place. Continue on to Queenstown, the adventure city of New Zealand. The self-proclaimed adventure capital of the world lives up to its name. Queenstown offers a wide range of adrenaline-charged pastimes: bungee jumping, jet boat riding or paragliding boost the hormone balance! Overnight in Queenstown.

Day 16 Welcome to Queenstown

The self-proclaimed adventure capital of the world lives up to its name. Queenstown offers a wide range of adrenaline-charged pastimes. Bungee jumping, jet boat riding or paragliding get the hormone balance going! But even if you prefer to go a little slower, you will get your money's worth here. The surrounding mountains can be explored on scenic routes, various golf courses invite you to a game. Overnight in Queenstown.

17th day Queenstown - Milford - Te Anau (ca.) 405 km

Today you will drive to Te Anau, the gateway to Fjordland National Park - New Zealand's largest and still largely untapped national park. Hardly any landscape embodies the term wilderness better than fjordland. In the west, 14 fjords penetrate deep into the primordial forests of a rugged Alpine world like overlong sea tongues. In the immediate vicinity, snow-capped mountain peaks of up to 2700 meters elevate over alpine grass mats. In the middle of this fascinating landscape lies Milford Sound, in whose crystal clear water is reflected the over 1000 meters towering Mitre Peak. From Te Anau, take a trip to Milford Sound. On the most beautiful mountain road in New Zealand to Milford, you will pass the Mirror Lakes and the dark Homer Tunnel and even encounter Kea parrots on a stop with some luck. You will then reach the landmark of New Zealand and the most beautiful end of the world, the Milford Sound. Here you have the opportunity to take a boat trip with lunch on Milford Sound. Overnight in Te Anau.

Day 18 Te Anau – Invercargill (ca.) 160km

Head to Invercargill, the southernmost city in the country. Local specialties include bluff oysters and blue cod, and the museum hosts an exhibit on New Zealand's sub-Antarctic islands. It’s a short trip to Bluff to see the Stirling Point signpost, which shows the distance to the South Pole and other parts of the Earth. Overnight in Invercargill.

Day 19 Invercargill – Dunedin (ca.) 245 km

Going north, we recommend Catlins, New Zealand's "hidden jewel". The Catlins region is an area of great contrasts and natural beauty with an abundance of wildlife. From magnificent coastal cliffs and headlands, long curved beaches to vast, temperate rainforests, waterfalls and hilly, farmland - the Catlins are a fascinating place off the beaten path. Overnight in Dunedin.

20th day of Victorian flair in Dunedin

The university town of Dunedin, New Zealand’s oldest city with Scottish heritage, boasts a unique combination of cultural riches, impressive architecture and modern lifestyle. Dunedin has a justly deserved reputation as one of the best preserved Victorian cities in the Southern Hemisphere. Architecturally outstanding is the massive Flemish Renaissance railway station (built 1904 - 1906) with its impressive mosaics. Dunedin is home to some of New Zealand’s top fashion designers, has many lively cafes and bars and not to forget: the steepest street in the world! Overnight in Dunedin.

Day 21 Dunedin - Mount Cook (ca.) 330 km

Drive across Moeraki to see dozens of spherical boulders scattered on the windstorm beach before reaching Oamaru. Then inland over rugged hills, tall farms and stunning turquoise lakes before reaching Aoraki Mount Cook National Park. A region that magically attracts mountaineers, hikers and landscape lovers. Overnight at Aoraki Mount Cook Alpine Village.

Day 22 Mount Cook – Christchurch (ca.) 325 km

This morning, you may want to take a Tasman Glacier Tour or cruise before heading to Christchurch via the Canterbury Plains. Overnight in Christchurch.

23rd day Farewell from New Zealand

For a hundred years, Christchurch was characterised by beautiful gardens and parks as well as impressive architecture – much of it reminds of England. Unfortunately, an earthquake in 2011 fell victim to many of the city’s treasures. Work is still being done on reconstruction. From the Ports Hills you have a beautiful view down to the harbor. Then take a walk through the Botanical Garden with its majestic old trees or Hagley Park. Not far from the airport is the International Antarctic Centre, which lets you explore life in one of the most extreme places on earth in many ways. With the rental car you end your unique round trip through the land of the long, white cloud, as the Maoris call their home.
