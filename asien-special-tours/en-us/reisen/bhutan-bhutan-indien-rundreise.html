Bhutan and India Journey - The Golden Triangle and the Dragon Kingdom

Bhutan and India Travel: Experience the Golden Triangle and Dragon Empire with Asia Special Tours. Ask for individual travel now.

Two fascinating countries await you: At the beginning of your journey, explore the magnificent buildings of northern India – above all the fairytale-like Taj Mahal – and stroll through metropolises where history dating back thousands of years meets modern urban bustle. A contrast to this is the silence of the secluded Himalayan Kingdom of Bhutan. In the midst of beautiful mountain landscapes, you will visit tranquil places, impressive monasteries and get exclusive insights into everyday life in the Land of the Thunder Dragon.

1st day Arrival in Delhi

Your journey through India and Bhutan begins in Delhi, the subcontinent’s historic capital. Upon arrival at the airport, you will already be expected and escorted to your hotel. Delhi is the first cornerstone of the Golden Triangle – the triad of tourist sites of particular interest in northern India. The other two stations are Agra and Jaipur. Before continuing your journey to the Kingdom of Bhutan, you will spend a week exploring the highlights of this Golden Triangle. Overnight in Delhi.

Day 2 The highlights of the capital

After a leisurely breakfast, you will embark on an exploration tour of the Indian capital. Your day starts in Old Delhi with a visit to Chandni Chowk – the largest wholesale market in Asia. On the way, you will pass the imposing Red Fort. Then visit the Raj Ghat – a simple memorial in honor of Mahatma Gandhi. Afterwards you will travel to New Delhi, the part of the city characterized by British architecture. Here is the first time a visit to the Qutb Minar on the program. Afterwards, visit the Humayun Tomb, the first garden tomb of the Indian subcontinent. During the drive through the “Imperial Delhi” you will see the India Gate, the Parliament Building, the Secretariat and the former Viceroyal Palace. A special experience awaits you at the end of the magnificent Gurudwara, the Sikh Temple. Here all people – regardless of religion or origin – receive a warm meal every day in the so-called “Lang-ar”. If you want, you can help in the large community kitchen, where volunteers around the clock prepare simple but nutritious food such as chapati (flat bread), dal (lenses) and vegetables for thousands. Overnight in Delhi.

3rd day onwards to Agra

Today you continue your journey towards Agra. After your arrival, you can treat yourself to a short rest at the hotel. From your room, you can take a first look at one of the most famous buildings in the world: the Taj Mahal. However, this magnificent building awaits you only tomorrow morning. This afternoon you will dedicate another World Heritage site in Agra: the Red Fort. Three generations of Mughal emperors celebrated their power and glory in this palace city on the banks of the Yamuna. In the middle of the 17th century Emperor Akbar laid the foundation stone for the red sandstone fortress. Once upon a time, the palace city consisted of 500 buildings. Within the 2.4 kilometer long protective wall, behind an eleven-meter-deep castle moat are the representative palace buildings, mosques and gardens. Overnight in Agra.

Day 4: The Fairy Tale Taj Mahal

If early in the morning the fog still hangs over the water basins of the garden and most tourists turn around in bed again, the best time to visit the Taj Mahal is. In incomparable perfection and beauty, this “most expensive proof of love ever” still shines today in white marble – especially in the early hours of the morning before the onslaught of tourists. 20,000 workers built it in 22 years. Not only from the outside, the building rightly impresses millions of tourists every year. The walls inside are decorated with precious gems and mosaics. After the visit, continue your journey to the last corner of the Golden Triangle. On the way to Jaipur, visit the ruined town of Fatehpur Sikri. With red sandstone bricks, the Grand Mogul Akbar had his capital built here in the 16th century, but it had to be abandoned shortly after completion due to a lack of water. Nevertheless, it has been preserved almost ghostly to this day with the great mosque and all palaces. You can walk through an intact, supreme city: multi-storey palaces, gardens, a marketplace, a waterworks, countless covered corridors, stairs and terraces testify to Akbar's architectural masterpiece.<span style="font-family:&quot;Calibri&quot;,sans-serif;font-size:11.0pt;line-height:120%;">In the evening you reach Jaipur, the capital of Rajasthan.</span>Overnight stay in Jaipur.

Day 5: The Magnificent Fort Amber

In the late 19th century, the city was painted in shades of pink in preparation for the visit of the British king – in Rajasthan’s tradition, this is the color of hospitality. In the morning, visit Fort Amber. By jeep you drive up into the courtyard of the palace. You will visit the public audience hall, the magnificent Ganesha Gate and the Spiegelpalast. The building is considered one of the most beautiful of its kind in Rajasthan. From the galleries and terraces you have a wonderfully wide view over the valley and the mountain world of the Aravalli chain. On the way back to town, stop at Hawa Mahal – the “Palace of the Winds”. The filigree facade of red sandstone with its countless windows once served the ladies of the court to observe the bustle on the street unnoticed. Then visit Jantar Mantar, an impressive 18th century astronomical observatory. In the late afternoon, take a walk through the old town and immerse yourself in the lively everyday life of Jaipur. Overnight in Jaipur.

Day 6 Return to Delhi

In the morning you will return to Delhi, where the rest of the day is available for spontaneous explorations of the historic city. In the late afternoon, a tour of Old Delhi awaits you. As soon as the sun sets, Chandni Chowk shows up from a whole new, atmospheric side. The tour starts at Sisganj Gurudwara, a Sikh temple, and continues to the nearby Sunehri Masjid, which impresses with its exciting history. A short stop at Kanwarjis – a legendary confectionery store – should not be missing. Then stroll through the bustling alleys of Ballimaran, past traditional bakeries and chai stands. The walk ends at Jama Masjid, surrounded by sparkling bazaars that are open until late into the night and fascinate with their colorful bustle and exotic fragrances. Overnight in Delhi.

7th day flight to Paro

Leave the hustle and bustle of the Indian capital behind you: in the Himalayan Kingdom of Bhutan, you can expect peace and the fascinating expanse of the Himalayas instead. Just arriving is an experience: by plane you will cross some of the highest peaks in the world before your plane lands at Paro, Bhutan’s only international airport. In the evening you can get first impressions of the mountain kingdom while visiting a market here. Overnight in Paro.

8th day hike to the "Tigernest"

Today, take a hike through Bhutan’s impressive mountain world. Your destination is the famous “Tigernest Monastery” (Taktshang), which – 900 meters above the valley – nestles against the rugged rock faces of the Himalayas. The name comes from a legend that the second Buddha, Guru Padmasambhava, flew here to meditate on the back of a tigress. On the way back, visit a small village to get a glimpse into the everyday life of the mountain kingdom. Try the local wine here. Overnight in Paro.

9th day Visit to the Kila Gompa nunnery

Without tour guide and chauffeur

Today, take a winding road up to the Chelela Pass at 3,810 meters – one of Bhutan’s highest passes. The route runs through dense forests of blue pines and rhododendron. On clear days, from here you have a magnificent view of the snow-capped peaks of Chomolhari and Jichu Drake. Take a break here to enjoy the fresh mountain air and take a little walk. Afterwards we continue to the remote Kila Gompa nunnery, which is located on a steep mountain slope below the pass. In the secluded silence of this spiritual retreat live Buddhist nuns who dedicate their days to study, prayer and meditation. Kila Gompa is one of the oldest nunnery monasteries in Bhutan and was founded as a meditation site in the 9th century. After the visit, descend to the meeting point with your vehicle. Along the way, enjoy a picnic lunch among the pine trees. The rest of the day in Paro is yours – use the time for your own explorations or to rest in your hotel. Overnight in Paro.

Day 10: Travel to the capital

After a leisurely breakfast, you will be driven to Thimphu, the capital of the kingdom. Upon arrival, visit the impressive Buddha Dordenma statue perched high above Thimphu on a hill. At 51.5 meters, it is one of the largest seated Buddha statues in Asia and offers a magnificent panoramic view over the city. Then visit the National Memorial Choir – a spiritual landmark initiated by the third King of Bhutan, Jigme Dorji Wangchuck, to commemorate peace and prosperity. After his early death, it was completed in his honor in 1974. In the evening – if time permits – a visit to the magnificent Trashicho Dzong fortress is on the agenda. It houses the throne room of the king as well as important government offices and also serves as the summer residence of the supreme abbot. On working days, you may be able to witness the hoisting of the national flag and the solemn change of guard. Overnight in Thimphu. 

Day 11 Exploring the Bhutanese Capital

Today is marked by one of the most unusual capitals in the world. City bustle or traffic chaos you will look for here in vain. To start, visit the Museum of Traditional Textile Art and Changangkha Lhakhang, a temple perched like a fortress on a mountain ridge above the city. Afterwards you will visit the Institute of Zorig Chusum, known as the “painting school”. During the tour of the workshops, you will experience up close how the students are taught in painting, wood carving, embroidery or metalworking. Another highlight is a visit to a traditional paper factory, where paper from the bark of the Daphne tree is made entirely by hand. Afterwards, you will head to the “Simply Bhutan Museum”, an interactive open-air museum that brings you closer to traditional life in the Kingdom in a lively way. Here you will learn how to distill the local brandy macaw, get to dress in traditional clothes and listen to the chants of Bhutanese women building mud houses. You will also visit the Royal Textile Academy in the heart of Thimphus – a modern museum and training center that displays both Bhutanese and international textiles. In the connected weaving school you will observe how young people are trained in the art of weaving, yarn dyeing and fashion design. Overnight in Thimphu.

Day 12 Via the Dochula Pass to Punakha

After breakfast, leave the capital behind and continue your journey to the northeast. Along the way, you will cross the Dochu-la Pass at 3,088 meters above sea level, where an impressive array of 108 small stupas, a large main stupa of Bhutan and waving prayer flags marking the highest point of the road awaits you. On clear days, a spectacular panoramic view of the majestic peaks of the eastern Himalayas opens from here. Afterwards, the journey goes down to the village of Lobeysa (also called Metshina). Upon arrival, take a walk through the village and surrounding rice fields to Chhimi Lhakhang, a hilltop temple below Metshina. This temple is dedicated to the Lama Drukpa Kuenley, also known as the “divine madman”. Especially among women who have difficulty getting pregnant, the temple enjoys great popularity. Then continue on to Punakha. Overnight in Punakha.   

Day 13 Guest with a local family

Let the day go on calmly. After a leisurely morning, you will leave for a farmhouse where you will prepare lunch together with the resident family. Not only can you learn more about the country’s culinary traditions, but you can then get an intimate insight into the life of the Bhutanese over lunch together. After saying goodbye to your hosts, leave for the Dzong of Punakha. Until 1955, the country was ruled from here. The unique monastery fortress is beautifully located at the confluence of the rivers Po Chu and Mo Chu and is popularly called the “Palace of Happiness”. Overnight in Punakha.

Day 14 Returns to Paro

After breakfast, drive back to Paro, from where you will return home tomorrow. On the way through small villages you can observe the rural bustle: many farmers offer their products on the roadside, so that you can taste fresh apples, peaches and pears at a stopover. After their arrival, you can rest in their hotel. After lunch, take a trip to the northern end of the valley to visit the National Museum-um. The collection includes ancient Bhutanese works of art and artifacts, weapons, coins, stamps and a small natural history exhibition. Afterwards, visit Kyichu Lhakhang – a temple built in the 7th century and one of Bhutan’s oldest and most sacred shrines. Overnight in Paro.

15th day traveling home via Delhi

After spending two weeks exploring the highlights of India and Bhutan, your break from everyday life is drawing to a close today as your driver takes you to Paro Airport, from where you will take your flight home via Delhi.
