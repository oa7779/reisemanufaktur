Bonsai, Bamboo & Zen - a trip to Japan's most beautiful gardens

Japan Travel - Bonsai, Bamboo & Zen: A trip to Japan's most beautiful gardens awaits you with your Japan specialist Asia Special Tours. Ask now!

Itinerary & services

English itinerary text is an offline machine translation of the German original for this team preview. Please confirm travel details with your advisor; the German source remains available through the language switch.

Japan is a paradise for garden and park lovers: from centuries-old pleasure walking gardens of the high society of the Edo and Meji period, to beautiful temple gardens and tiny tea gardens to magical temples, you will find everything your heart desires here. Let yourself be taken on a journey through the history of Japanese garden architecture, one of the most fascinating in the world. As with other Japanese arts, the emphasis is on simplicity, naturalness and silence. A Japanese garden reflects the beauty of the Japanese landscape with its rocks, mountains, weathered trees and roaring waterfalls. While some gardens actually imitate existing landscapes, others create their own images. During your trip, you will visit some of the most beautiful and unique gardens in the country and learn a lot of exciting things from your guide about the peculiarities of Japanese garden architecture.

1st day Arrival in Japan

Welcome to Japan! Your journey begins in the state-of-the-art metropolis of Tokyo, where you will be welcomed and warmly greeted by an English-speaking assistant. This accompanies you to the vehicle available for you, which brings you to your hotel. The rest of the day is at your disposal: time to make your first explorations on your own in this breathtaking world metropolis. Overnight in Tokyo. 

Day 2 A garden like a poem: the Rikugi-en

Tokyo is undoubtedly one of the most vibrant metropolises of today – a city that never comes to rest, illuminated by shining neon lights and animated by a steady stream of people. But between the impressive skyscrapers there are also quiet retreats in the form of enchanting gardens and parks. The tour starts at Koishikawa Botanical Garden, right next to the prestigious University of Tokyo. As a center for botanical research, this garden offers a diverse variety of plants and a peaceful atmosphere. Continue to Koishikawa Kōrakuen, a historic garden near Tokyo Dome. Characterized by Confucian influences, he was created in 1629 by Yorifusa, a feudal lord of the Edo period. The next stop will take us to Rikugi-en, a hidden jewel in the heart of a residential area. Its name, “Garden of the Six Poem Forms”, refers to the classical Waka poetry, whose poetic beauty has been artfully integrated into the landscape. The end of the tour is Asakusa, the heart of the historic Shitamachi district. Once known as the secret retreat of the geishas, it is now home to Senso-ji, Tokyo’s oldest and most impressive Buddhist temple. In the surrounding alleys, numerous traditional shops give the district its unique charm. Overnight in Tokyo.

 

Day 3 Tokyo's Oases of Rest

Today is again dedicated to Tokyo’s gardens. This city of contrasts, known for its impressive modern architecture and lively streets, reveals numerous green oases that combine history and silence. First, the tour takes us to Hamarikyu Garden, a traditional Japanese garden that once served as a private villa of a powerful feudal lord in the Edo period. Especially famous for its seasonal flowers and the Nakajima-no-Ochaya tea house, this garden invites you to pause and enjoy a cup of tea. The next stop is the Eastern Gardens of the Imperial Palace. The 21-hectare site once housed the walls of Edo Castle, parts of which are still preserved today. The garden, which used to be the castle’s second defence circle, impresses with its magnificent Aza-leen and hydrangeas. Then make your way to Shinjuku Gyo-en, one of Tokyo’s largest and most popular parks. Opened to the public in 1903, this former imperial garden combines elements of the French formal garden, the English landscape garden and the traditional Japanese garden. The crowning conclusion is the Meiji Jingu, Tokyo’s most famous Shinto shrine. The shrine is a popular destination for traditional Shinto weddings lined with around 120,000 trees. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 4 Day Day Trip to Kamakra Temples

Today you will leave the metropolis of millions behind you together with your guide to Kamakura, a charming place southwest of Tokyo on the fascinating Sagami Bay. Between the 12th and 14th centuries, Kamakura was Japan’s political center and numerous historical shrines and temples still remind us of this formative period. The Kotoku-in Temple houses the imposing “Great Buddha,” one of the most significant depictions of Buddha Amitabha, deeply rooted in Japanese culture. Another outstanding sanctuary is the Hase-dera, famous for its over nine meters high Kannon statue made of solid wood. This temple is part of the Bando Sanjusankasho pilgrimage route, which is dedicated to 33 temples. These are dedicated to the goddess of mercy, Kannon. The end of the day is a stroll through Komachi Street, where numerous small shops and inviting restaurants line up. In the afternoon you will return to Tokyo relaxed. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 5 - Go to Kanazawa

Without guidance

Today we continue to Kanazawa, which is located on the coast of the Japanese Sea. In the 17th century, this beautifully preserved town was one of the most prosperous regions in Japan and a center of art and culture. Upon arrival, you will still have the opportunity to discover the city in peace on your own. For example, how about a walk through the traditional Higashi-Chaya district, which delights with its unique flair of bygone times. Overnight in Kanazawa.  

Optional: 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art
Be enchanted by this extraordinary museum, known not only for its advanced architecture but also for its ever-changing exhibitions. A highlight of the collection is Leandro Erlich’s “Swimming Pool”. In this unique piece of art, you will have the opportunity to immerse yourself in the water and appear as if you were underwater.

Day 6 The Kenroku-en: one of the most beautiful gardens in Japan

Look forward to more beautiful gardens today. First, visit the Kenroku-en Garden, which is one of the three most magnificent gardens in the country. This garden is home to Japan’s oldest water fountain, as well as charming tea houses and an imposing villa once inhabited by one of Kanazawa’s wealthiest families. Just a short walk away is the magnificent Kanazawa Castle. Continue on the path to Gyokusen-en, a quiet garden whose winding paths and old trees will transport you to another time. This garden was built in the 17th century and has been maintained by the same family for four generations. The Tea House in the Garden, one of the oldest in the country, invites you to enjoy a cup of green tea and traditional Japanese sweets amidst this magnificent backdrop. Finally, your guide will take you to the old town, to the former house of the Samurai Nomura. The elegant tatami rooms, separated by traditional sliding walls, convey a sense of calm and grace. On leaving the veranda, you will also be welcomed here by a classic Japanese garden. Overnight in Kanazawa.

Day 7 Charming Okayama

Without guidance

Your next destination is Okayama, a city that delights with its rich cultural heritage and history. Here you will find the impressive Krähenburg, whose striking black facade is one of the most famous landmarks in the region. Likewise, the historic merchant district of Bikan attracts, whose well-preserved buildings and picturesque alleys invite you to stroll. In addition, Okayama is considered a gateway to the fascinating art islands in the Seto Inland Sea, which delight art lovers from all over the world with their extraordinary museums and art installations. After arriving at the hotel, you will have time to relax, explore the area on your own or enjoy regional cuisine in one of the traditional restaurants. Overnight in Okayama.

 

Day 8 of the Koraku-en – a special place of scenic beauty

Together with your guide you will discover Okayama and the nearby Kurashiki today. Stroll through the charming Bikan and Canal district, whose traditional homes and small shops give the area a very special character. The Tsurugatayama Park delights with the majestic “Great Glycine of Achi”, a national treasure that is probably between three and five hundred years old. At the end of May, the glycinia blossoms in an enchanting pink splendor and gives the park a magical atmosphere. In the afternoon, continue to Okayama if you feel like visiting the Ohara Art Museum. Japan’s first museum of Western art features works by world-famous artists such as El Greco, Matisse and Picasso. Are you looking for something quieter? Book a 30-minute cruise on a traditional Kawabune, a riverboat on the Kurashiki River. In Okayama, your guide will take you to the impressive Krahenburg. A short walk across the Tsukimi Bashi Bridge will take you directly to the Korakuen Garden, one of Japan’s three most beautiful gardens. With many new impressions, you will return to your ryokan in the evening. Overnight in Okayama.

Day 9 - Continue over Himeji to the old imperial city of Kyoto

Without guidance

This morning you make your way to the old imperial city of Kyoto. On the way there is the opportunity of a stopover in Himeji. The bright white Himeji Castle is one of the most famous and beautiful sights in Japan and impresses with its majestic presence. The Koko-en Garden, which boasts nine unique gardens, exudes the charm and aesthetics of the Edo period. Let yourself be fascinated by the harmoniously landscaped areas, the bamboo forests and pine trees that make this garden a true masterpiece of nature and art. Then continue your journey and reach Kyoto, a city famous for its fabulous cultural heritage. Kyoto is home to around 2,000 temples and shrines, including 17 UNESCO World Heritage sites that reflect Japan’s history and spirituality in an impressive way. Overnight in Kyoto.

 

Day 10 Zen Gardens and Golden Pavilions

It is not for nothing that Kyoto is regarded as the epitome of Japanese culture. For over a thousand years the imperial court lived here and even today the numerous palaces, shrines and gardens make the city a cultural center. After breakfast, meet your guide in the foyer of your hotel and set off for a city tour together. Your first destination is the oldest botanical garden in Japan. Here you have the opportunity to marvel at 120,000 plants from 12,000 different species. In addition, an impressive winter garden awaits you. Then continue on public transport to the golden pavilion, the famous Kinkaku-ji Temple. Once built as a pleasure garden of a Sho-gun, the gold leaf building is majestically reflected in the adjacent pond and offers a breathtaking sight. Just a few minutes away is the Ryo-an-ji Temple, world famous for its Zen stone garden. If time permits, a walk to the Toji-in Temple will round off the day. Its idyllic pond garden with ornately arranged rocks and trees offers another look at the harmonious garden art of Japan. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 11 The Naka Shin-en – a filming location of the film “Lost in Translation”

Are you ready for more highlights? Then head to the elegant Ginkaku-ji Temple at the foot of the eastern mountains of Kyoto, also known as “the Silver Pavilion”. Originally built as a shogun villa, today it stands out for its white sea of sand and the hill of sand in the form of the sacred Mount Fuji-san. Along the philosopher’s path, stroll among cherry trees along a quiet canal. At the end of the trail, one of Kyoto’s largest temples, the Nanzen-ji Temple from the Meiji period, is waiting for you. The Zen temple complex is home to some rare tree species that display a special foliage in autumn. Next, your guide will take you to Murin-an, a prime example of a Meiji era pleasure walking garden. The Heian Shrine is also worth a visit. The highlight of this shrine is definitely its four gardens. Perhaps one of the gardens sounds familiar to you: the Naka Shin-en was broadcast in the film "Lost in Translation". Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 12 Mystical Bamboo Forests and Zen Gardens

Your journey begins in one of the most picturesque places in the city: the magical bamboo forest of Arashiyama. Here the bamboo rods rise close to each other so high in the sky that the view of the sky is almost completely obscured. The sunlight nevertheless finds its way through the dense canopy and immerses the forest in a very special, almost unreal atmosphere. In the Arashiyama district, visit Tenryu-ji Temple, arguably Kyoto’s most beautiful Zen Temple and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Afterwards, a train ride will take you to the southeastern part of the city, where you will discover more outstanding Zen gardens. The Tofukuji Temple impresses with its monumental dry landscapes that surround the Abbot's quarter. Just a few minutes walk away, you can reach Komyoin Temple, a true oasis of silence. This small, intimate garden is used by locals as a retreat to stay inside and admire nature in its full beauty. Each season adds a unique poetry to this place, making it the perfect conclusion to your journey of discovery through Kyoto’s masterful gardening art. Overnight in Kyoto.

13th day Farewell to Japan

Today ends your beautiful journey through Japan. In the past few days you have collected numerous impressions of Japan’s masterful garden and landscape architecture that will remain in your memory for a long time. With these magical pictures and experiences, you will be on your flight home. Your driver will take you to the airport in Osaka for your return flight. We wish you a pleasant journey home, goodbye!
