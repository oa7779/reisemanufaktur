Natural and hiking paradise South Korea

Do you love nature, beautiful landscapes and enjoy hiking? This trip for nature lovers leaves nothing to be desired. Come with us on a journey to the most beautiful national parks in South Korea and enjoy their scenic beauty on hikes that match your fitness level. Also, shipping on the beautiful Chungjoho Lake and seeing hundreds of stalactites and stalakmites in the massive Gosugul Cave will make your heart beat faster. During this trip, however, you will not only enjoy the dreamlike landscapes of South Korea. The visit to the village of Hahoe and an overnight stay in a traditional Hanok guest house will also give you authentic insights into the life of the inhabitants of the “water-ridden village”. Deep into the everyday life of a Buddhist monastery, immerse yourself in the Haeinsa Temple. Pray like a monk, meditate like a monk and dine like a monk – for one day this will also be part of your life. Many more spectacular impressions you return from the “Land of Morning Silence” after one and a half weeks.

1st day start of your journey

Welcome to South Korea! Your journey through the tiger state begins in the capital Seoul, where you will be welcomed and warmly greeted. Use the rest of the day to relax a little from your flight or make your first explorations in the Asian metropolis. Seoul has a long historical tradition as the capital of various dominions, dating back to pre-Christian times. Thus, the place among modern skyscrapers houses rich cultural treasures from several millennia. Overnight in Seoul.

Day 2 A city full of contrasts: multi-faceted metropolis Seoul

Today you will experience up close in what a fascinating field of tension between history and modernity Seoul moves. Their first destination is the Gyeongbokgung Palace (“Palace of Bliss”), which was once the main palace and residence of the royal family. On foot you will continue to the Samcheongdong district, which has its very own charm. Small cafés, individual shops, antique shops and art galleries paired with the surrounding nature create a beautiful scenery that invites you to dream and linger. In the Hanokdorf Bukchon you will dive deeper into the past of the city. It is home to hundreds of traditional houses called “Hanok” in Korean. It is busy in the market of Gwangjang, Korea’s oldest traditional market. From traditional goods to culinary delicacies, you will find a diverse offer here. In the original city center of Seoul, you will end the impressive first day. By car, travel to Bugaksan, located north of Seoul, the highest of the four mountains that surround the city’s former center. From here upstairs you get an overall impression of the city. You can also see the ancient walls of Seoul’s city fortress, which runs along the mountain ridge. Overnight in Seoul.

3rd day Legendary Seoraksan Mountains

In the morning you leave for the legendary mountains of Seoraksan. Seoraksan National Park is undoubtedly one of the most beautiful landscapes South Korea has to offer. From green coniferous forests rise peaks, crowned by unique rock formations. It is Korea’s third highest mountain range and known for its breathtaking views, hidden temple complexes and varied hiking routes. In particular, you can now convince yourself of the beauty of the latter. Depending on your mood, you can either be carried up to the summit from the cable car and hike from there comfortably to the Gwongeumseong fortress, where you have a fantastic view over the rustic mountain landscape. After seeing your fill, take the cable car down again and walk to the nearby Sinheungsa Temple with its 10-meter-high bronze Buddha statue. Then you will be taken to your hotel. Overnight in Gangneung.

Day 4 "The Five Mountains" of the Odaesan Mountains

Your hike today will take you to the Odaesan Mountains, whose name (in English “The Five Mountains”) is derived from the five plateaus – Manwoldae, Jangnyeongdae, Girindae, Sangsamdae and Jigongdae. The Odaesan Mountains is considered a sacred place next to the sacred mountains Geumgangsan, Jirisan and Hallasan and was designated as a national park in 1975. The trail leads from the car park about one and a half kilometers to the Sangwonsa Temple and from there another one and a half kilometers steeply up to the Birobong summit, the highest peak of the mountain range. From here above you can enjoy a fantastic panoramic view. Once back downstairs, head back to Gangneung and visit the Jungang Market, one of the most representative traditional markets in the province. It is divided into two main parts: traditional market and underground fish market. Afterwards you will visit the Ojukheon residence, which is part of the national treasure. At the end of the day, you will dedicate yourself to a part of Korea’s current history. You will visit Tongil Park, the Reunification Park. There you can visit the North Korean Navy submarine, which was stranded in 1996. You will then be taken to your hotel. Overnight in Gangneung.

Day 5 Hike in the Sobaeksan Mountains

The destination today is the Sobaeksan Mountains, which was declared the 18th National Park of South Korea in 1987. After Jirisan and Seoraksan, it is the third largest mountainous national park in the country. Your hike will take you, among other things, to the temple of Guinsa, which is located below the summit of Yeonhwabong. The temple was founded by a monk who first built a small hut where he practiced asceticism. After revealing the truths of the universe to him, he erected the temple of Guinsa. After your hike, you will be taken to your hotel in Danyang. Overnight in Danyang.

Day 6 Stalactites and Stalakmites of any shape and size

Continue to the Cheongpung Cultural Heritage Complex in Jecheon, where you’ll find a variety of cultural heritage and artifacts from the area. During a cruise on Lake Chungjuho – an artificial reservoir that connects Chungju and Danyang – you will enjoy the beautiful landscape from the water and let a fresh breeze blow around your nose. From the water it goes underground when you visit one of the most famous caves in South Korea. The limestone cave Gosugul is famous for its extraordinary beauty and has been declared a national monument. The interior has a temperature of 15°C throughout the year, which also makes it an ideal habitat for around 25 different animal species. With a little luck, for example, you can observe different bat species. An immovable natural spectacle are the approximately 120 different forms of stalactites and stalakmites. An impressive sight like you have probably never seen before! With an overnight stay in the traditional Hanok guest house Rakkojae, you will end your eventful day. Situated in the middle of the historic village of Andong, it offers you an excellent opportunity to fully immerse yourself in the lives of the people of that time. Overnight in Andong.

Day 7 Traditional village life in Hahoe

The morning will be dedicated to exploring the village of Hahoe, which is one of South Korea’s UNESCO sites and convinces by preserving Korean architecture and culture. It owes its name “village surrounded by water” to the river Nakdong, which winds around the village. Then head to the nearby Buyongdae cliff, from which a spectacular panoramic view of the entire Hahoe village and the surrounding river landscape opens up. You can get a glimpse of Confucianism while visiting the Confucian school Byeongsan Seowon. 
Finally, leave Andong – the home of Confucianism in Korea – behind and travel to Daegu, where you will immediately visit Yangnyeong Market, one of the most important markets for medicinal herbs and ingredients for alternative medicine treatments. More interesting information about alternative healing methods can be found in the Yangnyeongsi Museum of Alternative Medicine. There are more than 420 artifacts and 360 different alternative medical applications on display, including very rare and highly toxic medical products. After visiting the museum, you will be taken to your hotel. Overnight in Daegu.

8th day Buddhist monk for a day

Today you can relax: have breakfast in peace and make the morning according to your personal preferences. Around noon you leave for the Gayasan Mountains. Their goal is the temple Haeinsa, which was founded in 802 by the monks Suneung and Ijeong. To this day, in the midst of the idyllic nature at the foot of Mount Gayasan, monks lead their ascetic life here. The temple houses 15 cultural monuments and about 200 private art treasures, which have been included in the UNESCO World Heritage Site. Among them are the famous Tripitaka Koreana, with over 80,000 printing blocks the most comprehensive collection of Buddhist writings in Southeast Asia. Here you have the unique opportunity to exchange your life for that of a monk for one day. Upon arrival at the temple, you will be registered for your stay at the temple. Throughout the day, you will learn (together with other guests) to relax your body and mind through meditation and prayer processes. Furthermore, you can expand your taste world through the temple kitchen and learn more about the history of the temple. Overnight at Mt. Gayasan.

9th day walking in a dreamlike nature

Well fortified after a vegetarian temple breakfast, explore the Gayasan Mountains, whose many peaks are all over 1,000 meters high. Particularly beautiful to behold in spring are the cherry blossom and azalea-lined streets that lead to the Haeinsa Temple. You will have the opportunity to admire the beauty of the mountains on a hike. Depending on your mood, you can choose between two routes. Both routes are easy to moderate and also easy to hike by families. On your way, you will discover the most diverse plants that line your path – including ginger, pines and wild flowers. After your hike, you will be driven to your hotel in Gyeongju – the former capital of the Silla Empire. Overnight in Gyeongju.

Day 10: Enchanting Namsan Mountains

Today, too, a beautiful walk awaits you. As you wish, you can choose between a three- and a fifth-hour route. From the Samneung valley you hike up towards Sangsabawi. On your way, you will pass through some relief carvings and statues until you reach the Sangseon-am-Klause. From here you can enjoy a beautiful view over the valley with good visibility and, with a bit of luck, hear monk songs from a distance. Continue past the rock formation Badukbawi and along the mountain ridge to Sangsabawi. Here you make turns and follow back the same path. If you opt for the longer route, continue hiking from Sangsabawi to the summit of Geumo-san (468 meters) and Yongjangsaji, where you will see a stone carved image of Buddha and a three-story stone pagoda. Your descent is towards the village of Yongjang, from where you will be taken to your hotel in Gyeongju. Choose the shorter route, then visit the Gyeonju National Museum and Tumuli Park, where the burial mounds of 23 rulers of the Silla period rise. With a walk through the nearby Gyerim forest, densely covered with old elms and pastures, you can end the day relaxed. Overnight in Gyeongju.

Day 11 Discovering Legendary Cultural Treasures

Your day begins with two UNESCO World Heritage sites that will surely impress you. The Bulguksa Temple represents the temple architecture of the ancient Silla Empire at its best. On the temple grounds are other national treasures such as the main stairs with 33 steps, which represent the 33 steps for enlightenment. The famous Seokguram Grotto, which you will visit afterwards, was dug into the mountain wall of Tohamsan Mountain in the 8th century. Inside it is a Buddha statue overlooking the East Sea. After the visits, you will be taken to Gyeongju station and take the high-speed KTX train back to Seoul. Upon arrival, the rest of the day is at your disposal to explore the multifaceted metropolis once again on your own. Overnight in Seoul.

12th day Farewell to South Korea

The time until the planned airport transfer is at your disposal. Unfortunately, your time out from everyday life is already coming to an end. Your driver will take you to the airport for your return flight. We wish you a good journey home!
