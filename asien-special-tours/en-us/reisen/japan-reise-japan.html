Japan Tour - In the Land of the Rising Sun

Japan Round Trip: This exclusive round trip shows you Japan’s most beautiful sides, now ask for Asia Special Tours and experience for yourself!

Fascination Japan: in hardly any other country are the contrasts between ancient traditions and hypermodernism as ubiquitous as here. Centuries-old traditions merge with state-of-the-art technology, huge skyscrapers rise behind filigree temples and idyllic garden landscapes offer oases of peace in the midst of lively activity. This trip brings you closer to some facets of this fascinating country and you will not be able to help but fall in love. Surely this will not be your last trip to the “Land of the Rising Sun”!

1st day Arrival in Japan

Irasshaimase and welcome to Japan! Your journey begins in Tokyo, where you will be welcomed and warmly greeted by an English-speaking assistant. This accompanies you to the vehicle available for you, which brings you directly to your hotel. The rest of the day is at your disposal: time to make your first explorations on your own in this breathtaking world metropolis. Overnight in Tokyo.

2nd day temples, gardens and stroll over the Tsukiji outdoor market

Today you can look forward to a day full of varied impressions of the exciting metropolis Your guide awaits you in the foyer of your hotel and will lead you first to the Tsukiji market. Even though the world-famous fish market has been relocated to the island of Toyosu, the Tsukiji outdoor market with traditional shops and world-class sushi restaurants remains a lively hub for visitors and locals to enjoy delicious food and buy local specialties. Stop at one of the restaurants for a super-fresh sushi breakfast. Well strengthened, leave the market behind and make your way to Hamarikyu Park, a true oasis in the middle of a sea of skyscrapers. The perfect place to dangle your soul over a cup of tea while the guide tells you something interesting about the Japanese tea tradition. Continue on to the Asakusa district. Visit Sensoji Temple, Tokyo’s oldest Buddhist temple. Your guide will explain to you the various temple customs and customs. It is also home to the oldest geisha district in the city. The visit to Meiji Shrine will round off your day impressively. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 3 Tokyo – colorful and crazy

Today, an exploration tour of the colorful and crazy Tokyo of modern times awaits you. You’ll meet your guide again in the foyer of your hotel and head to the Akihabara district, known for its electronics stores and anime culture decorated with colorful neon signs. Continue to Ameyoko, a street market with hundreds of merchants and market cries promoting traditional sweets, among other things. Here you should definitely try a Japanese crepe. Now make your way to the modern glitter district of Shibuya. Here you will make a photo stop at the world-famous Shibuya Crossroads, considered the busiest crossroads in the world. Finally, your guide will take you to the entertainment and amusement district of Shinjuku. Known especially for the busiest train station in the world, Shinjuku is indeed the hub of various institutions and industries in Japan – from government offices to pubs and bars, from quiet park to lively red light district, from fine boutiques of top fashion brands to alternative trendy shops, from Michelin-star restaurants to unique themed restaurants. Overnight in Tokyo.

4th day of free time in Tokyo or the Temples of Kamakuras

Without guidance

Explore the capital of Japan on your own today or take a day trip to Kamakura, which is located southwest of Tokyo on Sagami Bay. Kamakura was Japan's seat of government between the 12th and 14th centuries. Especially from this period you will find numerous shrines and temples in the city. The Kotoku-in Temple houses the “Great Buddha”, one of the most important depictions of Buddha Amitabha. Another famous sanctuary is the Hase-dera with its over nine meters high Kannon statue made of solid wood. It is one of the 33 temples on the Bando Sanjusankasho pilgrimage route dedicated to Kannon, the goddess of mercy. Also visit Komachi Street, where you can find a variety of shops and restaurants. Return to Tokyo in the afternoon. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 5 The Mountains and Volcanoes of Hakone

Without guidance

Today we continue to the Hakone region, which is located in the middle of the mountains and volcanoes of the Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park. The residents of Tokyo like to come here to escape the hustle and bustle of the capital. Your overnight stay is something very special today: you are a guest in a ryokan. The typical Japanese inns were already built in the Edo period (1603 – 1868). Today you can find them especially outside of larger cities. Staying here is especially worthwhile to experience Japanese guest friendship. Among the highlights of a stay at Ryokan are meals. Tonight you will enjoy a typical Japanese Kaiseki dinner. At the end of the day, you should treat yourself to a relaxing bath in the hotel’s onsen. Overnight in Hakone.

6th Day Discover Hakones Highlights

Without guidance

Spend today exploring Hakone. You can take a tour of the mountains with the Hakone Tozan Switchback train and enjoy a magnificent view of surrounding valleys. Afterwards, you can take the cable car to Mount Owakudani, past sulphur smoke plumes, hot springs and rivers. Upstairs on Mount Owakudani, one of Hakone's culinary specialties awaits you: Kuro-Tamago, an egg hard-boiled in the hot sulphur springs. If you believe the myths, eating it gives you seven more years of life. Downhill, it is best to take the gondola cable car to Togendai. Then take a boat trip over the Ashi-no-ko, a crater lake that offers you beautiful views of the surrounding peaks. Hakone is home to many museums and parks. One of them is the Hakone Open Air Museum, it is a harmonious balance between nature and art. Various indoor exhibitions include a wide selection of Picasso’s masterpieces. Overnight in Hakone.

Day 7 "Typical Japanese" - experiencing a traditional tea ceremony

After a final dip in the hotel’s Onsen, you’ll leave Hakone and travel on to Kyoto – Japan’s cultural capital. For over a thousand years the imperial court lived here, and even today the numerous palaces, shrines and gardens make the city a cultural center. Unlike many other cities, Kyoto has been largely spared war damage. As a result, the city was able to preserve its unique cultural heritage and is not for nothing still considered an epitome of Japanese culture today. A very special highlight awaits in the former imperial city: They dedicate themselves to Japanese traditions and take part in a tea ceremony. After arriving in Kyoto, you will go on your own to the private residence of a Tea Ceremonial Master, where today you will be introduced to the high art of tea ceremony under expert guidance. This can truly be called an art that requires years of practice and dedication. You will learn about the close link between the rituals and Zen Buddhism, the meaning of each gesture during the ceremony and, of course, how the tea is prepared and served correctly. You are also welcome to try yourself in the correct preparation of the tea. Afterwards, the rest of the day is at your disposal. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 8: The most beautiful sides of Kyoto

Today’s tour with your guide begins with a visit to the famous Fushimi Inari Shrine in Kyoto. The richly decorated building is dedicated to the goddess of rice, and thousands of orange torii gates stretch up the mountainside in long tunnels. Continue by train to Sanjusangendo. In the world’s longest wooden building, an exhibition of 1001 life-size golden Buddhist statues awaits you. A taxi will then take you to Nishiki Market, known as “Kyoto’s Kitchen”. Try to your heart’s content, but keep some appetite for lunch at a nearby restaurant. After lunch, Nijo Castle is on the agenda, a 17th century palace. The building is known for its ornately painted walls and the “nightingale floors” that squeak quietly under the steps. The conclusion is the Nanzenji Zen Temple. This peaceful place is famous for its Zen stone garden. After an eventful day, say goodbye to the guide and return to the hotel or explore the city on your own. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 9 In the footsteps of the ancient capital Nara

In the morning, meet your guide at your accommodation and leave Kyoto for Nara – Japan’s first permanent capital (710–784) before moving to Kyoto. Even today, impressive temples, shrines and historical sites tell of Nara’s glamorous past. The most important attractions are located around the sprawling Nara Park, where tame deer – venerated as sacred messengers of the gods in the Shinto tradition – roam freely among visitors. A special highlight is the visit to the imposing Todaiji Temple, one of the largest wooden structures in the world. Inside is the “Daibutsu”, the largest bronze Buddha statue in the world. On the way to Kasuga Taisha Shrine, famous for its thousands of stone and bronze lanterns, you will immerse yourself in an almost mystical atmosphere that makes this place so special. In the afternoon, return to Kyoto. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 10: Farewell to Japan

The time until the planned airport transfer is at your disposal. Breakfast in peace, take a last walk through the area or buy some more souvenirs for your loved ones at home. Then your time out from everyday life comes to an end. Even if your holiday is over, the precious memories, beautiful photos and exciting stories of your trip will accompany you forever. Look forward to letting your friends and family share your experiences in the distance at home. A shuttle bus will take you in time for your return flight to Osaka Airport. This ends your beautiful trip to Japan with Asia Special Tours. We wish you a good journey home. Goodbye!
