Day 1 Arrival in Uzbekistan

Welcome to Uzbekistan! When you arrive in Tashkent, you will already be welcomed by your German-speaking tour guide. Together with your private chauffeur you drive to the hotel, where you can arrive in peace. Overnight in Tashkent.

2nd day Culinary delights: try traditional Lagman

Take advantage of today to get to know the capital of Uzbekistan. First, visit Khasti Imon Square with the beautiful Barak Khan Medrese, Tillya Sheikh Mosque and Kaffal Shashi Mausoleum made of simple brick masonry. Here you can take a look at the Quran handwritten by the third Caliph Osman (644 – 656). Afterwards you can visit the Kukeldash Medrese, which still serves as a Koranic school today and take a look at the Museum of Applied Art. For lunch, try Lagman: Meat, Vegetables and Noodles – the perfect introduction to your stay. The chef will also show you how to make the special noodles, which can be up to 20 centimeters long. In the afternoon, continue your exploration in the modern part of the city and visit the Independence Square, the Tashkent subway with its ornately designed metro stations and the huge Chorsu bazaar, whose Oriental smells and spices will transport you to the fairy tales from 1,001 night. In the evening you will fly to Urgentsch, where you will be picked up at the airport and taken to Khiva. Overnight in Chiwa.

3rd Day Cooking with an Uzbek family

After breakfast, you will be completely dedicated to exploring Chiwa. The small oasis town in the middle of the desert has preserved the fairy-tale atmosphere of an ancient, oriental city to this day. You will visit the old town of Khiva – Itchan Kala – with the surrounding Kunya Ark fortress, the beautifully decorated Islam Khodja Minaret and the Pakhlavan Mahmoud Mausoleum. You can also enjoy the Mohammed Amin-Khan and Allakuli-Khan medres, as well as the Kalta Minor minaret. In the afternoon, you will be visiting an Uzbek family who will show you how to bake the traditional bread that cannot be imagined without a meal in Uzbekistan. In addition, you can look over the shoulder of your hosts while preparing the dish Shiwi Oschi, which is one of the womb foods of the Uzbeks. It consists of green noodles, fried meat from lamb or beef and vegetables and is served with sour cream. Of course, you can also try. Lunch is served at a typical restaurant, and in the evening you will be served Tuchum Barak (paper bags filled with eggs). Overnight in Chiwa.

Day 4 - Through the desert to Bukhara

An interesting ride ahead of you today. On an ancient caravan road, cross the Kyzylkum Desert, cruising the Amudarja River to reach the city of Bukhara. There are always opportunities for short stops along the way – you will find that this apparent wasteland is full of life! In Bukhara you can get a first impression of this breathtaking city during a walk.. You can enjoy your dinner at Labi-Hauz-Platz, where many locals also meet, and taste a typical Bukhara shashik. Traditionally, calf or mutton meat is grilled on an open fire. Before that, among other things, it is seasoned with coriander and placed in wine vinegar for a day. Overnight in Bukhara.

5th day Bukhara with all your senses

Bukhara is one of the oldest cities in Central Asia and was once one of the most illustrious commercial centers on the Silk Road. Even today, the atmosphere of bygone times is still felt. Visit the most important sights of this magical city from 1,001 night. Your full-day exploration tour will take you to the Old Town, where you will visit the mausoleum of the former Samanid ruler Ismael Samani from the 9th and 10th centuries, the Choir Minor Mausoleum and the beautiful Bolo Hauz Mosque. Other highlights are the Ulugbek medrese, the gigantic Ark citadel in the plan shape of the constellation “Great Chariot” and the Poi Kalon complex, which consists of a minaret, a mosque and the beautiful Miri Arab medrese. In the three overcoupled bazaars you can experience the daily activity up close. Finally, the complex Labi Hauz invites you to linger: several tea rooms are grouped around the water basin “Hauz”. Anyone who has been to Uzbekistan must have tried the national dish Plov – that is exactly what you do today with an Uzbek family. Plov comes in about 90 variants – depending on the occasion, the preparation can vary and there are also regional differences. Overnight in Bukhara.

Day 6 - Drive to the legendary Samarkand

After you have fortified yourself at breakfast with, among other things, fresh kaymag (Uzbek cream), today awaits you a day full of crafts and culture. On your way to Samarkand, stop at Gijduvan, famous for its ornate ceramics. The ceramics from Gijduvan are particularly beautiful to look at due to the elaborate ornaments and combinations of geometric figures. In the schools you can watch the ceramic production live. Take the opportunity to buy a traditional piece of Bukhara region before arriving in Samarkand. Along the way, you will also see Navoi and the ruins of the Rabbat Malik caravanserai. For dinner, enjoy Kazan Kebab, a stew of potatoes, lamb and coriander. Overnight in Samarkand.

7th day Noble drops in the wine cellar Chowrenko

Your tour of the UNESCO World Heritage-listed city begins in the famous Registan Square, which is surrounded by the impressive Ulugbek, Shir Dor and Tillya-Kari medres. The Gur Emir Mausoleum, where the conqueror Timur and his grandson are buried themselves, shines in old splendor after complete renovation. Afterwards you will visit the huge Bibi Khanum Mosque, which bears the name of one of the women of Timur and was to become the largest mosque in the Eastern world at that time – after all, it is still one of the largest mosques today. On the Siab Bazaar you can browse for unique souvenirs. Slowly hungry, fortify yourself, depending on the season, with Schurpa – a spicy meat soup – or Samsa – pies filled with pumpkin – for the afternoon program. This will take you to the observatory of the famous astronomer Ulugbek. After the visits, we have arranged a wine tasting in the prestigious Chowrenko wine cellar for you, whose wines are even award-winning (if available). For dinner today, taste Mastawa – a meat soup with rice and various vegetables – or Damlama (steamed vegetables with meat). Overnight in Samarkand.

Day 8: More places from 1,001 night

You haven't seen everything yet! Today you can look forward to the excavation site Afrosiab and the Shah-I-Zinda tomb street with its numerous artfully designed mausoleums. While cooking manti together with an Uzbek family, you can once again experience the Uzbek family life up close. Exchange with your hosts as you taste your self-cooked lunch. Manti are pasta bags filled with lamb or beef, onions and vegetables, served with chopped herbs, pepper and sour milk. In the afternoon, make your way back to Tashkent. At a farewell dinner in a local restaurant, you can end the evening comfortably and review the impressions of the last few days. Finally, try again a typical Uzbek dish: the filled dough bags Chuchwara. Overnight in Tashkent.

Day 9 Farewell to Uzbekistan

Today, unfortunately, we say goodbye. With 1001 new impressions and experiences in your luggage, you will be taken to the airport of Tashkent, from where you will start your journey home. The beautiful memories will always remain for you and often beautify everyday life.
