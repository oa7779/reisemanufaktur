Bhutan fascinates: Here in the Himalayas, the clocks seem to run slower. The preservation of traditions and the protection of the rich natural resources occupy a high priority in the country. And even if the kingdom is slowly opening up to modernity, you will still find in Bhutan an untouchedness that has become rare in our globalized world. This journey takes you to a land of deep spirituality, vibrant traditions and breathtaking natural beauty.

1st day Arrival in Paro

Your tour starts in Paro, the only international airport in the country. In good weather, you will have a breathtaking view of the Himalayan Mountains up to Mount Everest when you land. However, people with a fear of flying could find little pleasure in this: Due to the towering mountain peaks around the manoeuvring field, taking off and landing is considered so difficult that only a few specially trained pilots are allowed to fly to Paro. After your landing, you will already be expected and brought to Thimphu by your driver. The capital is located at an altitude of about 2320 meters in a fertile valley on the banks of the river Chhu Thimphu. It is one of the smallest capitals in the world and – next to the seat of government of the island state of Palau – the only capital without traffic lights. Once the government had installed traffic lights. However, after the population rebelled against the devices, police officers now regulate traffic – the well-being of its citizens is capitalized on in the country of gross social happiness. Upon arrival at the hotel, you can use the rest of the day for initial explorations in Thimphu. The central location of your hotel is ideal for this! Overnight in Thimphu.

2nd day capital without hustle

Today is marked by one of the most unusual capitals in the world. City bustle or traffic chaos you will look for here in vain. You will start your visit with a visit to the Museum of Folk Culture. Inaugurated by the Queen Mother in 2001, the museum aims to bring the country’s “modern” youth closer to the culture of their ancestors and preserve ancient customs and traditions from disappearing from everyday life. Further insights can be gained by visiting the Institute of Traditional Arts and Crafts, which will introduce its students to the 13 traditional arts and craft forms of Bhutan in a six-year course of study. Boys and girls learn the traditional painting and drawing techniques here and create clay, slate and woodwork. Afterwards, visit the nunnery Thangtong Dewachen. Your sightseeing tour will take you from here to commemorate the Third King, Jigme Dorje Wangchuck, a memorial to peace and prosperity. The paintings and statues inside offer a deep insight into Buddhist philosophy. Afterwards you will visit a factory for the traditional production of paper. The day ends with a visit to Tashichhodzong, the “Fortress of the Glorious Religion”. Overnight in Thimphu.

Day 3 Over the Dochula Pass to Gangtey

After breakfast, leave Thimphu and head towards Gangtey. The route takes you at an altitude of 3050 meters over the breathtaking Dochula Pass. The ridge is lined with countless colorful prayer flags that flicker in the high wind. With a clear view, you will have an outstanding view of the surrounding peaks of the Himalayan Mountains. Upon arrival in Gangtey, visit the Crane Center and Gangtey Monastery. Gangtey is home to the rare black-necked cranes that migrate from the Tibetan plateau to escape the harsh winter. During the winter months, an estimated 200 to 300 cranes live in this valley. Afterwards, visit the 16th-century Gangtey Goempa Monastery, which sits on a hill above the valley floor making it a valley landmark. It is the central seat of the Pedling tradition of Buddhism in West Bhutan and was built as a center of spiritual teaching. If you want to explore the beautiful valley further on foot, the Gangtey nature trail is recommended. Overnight in Punakha.

4th day onwards to Bumthang

Today, a full-day drive to Bumthang awaits you, the length of which is compensated for by the beauty of the passing nature. They pass fertile rice terraces, deep valleys and small farms. The settlement is becoming sparse, but the vegetation is becoming denser. From April to May, colorful rhododendron and magnolias bloom here. You may encounter Yakherds who have not yet been driven into the high mountain plains. Via the Pele Pass, which is located at an altitude of 3300 meters and is flanked by prayer flags, you will continue to Trongsa. From a distance you can catch a spectacular view of the imposing Dzong of Trongsa, which perched sublimely on a mountain ridge. Finally, via the Yotongla Pass, which extends at an altitude of 3400 meters, you reach the next stop of your journey. Here you will stay in a cozy lodge in the middle of an orchard, whose architectural style is based on Dzong architecture. Overnight in Bumthang.

5th Day White Beer and Swiss Cheese in Bhutan

The Bumthang region in central Bhutan comprises four mountain valleys, Choekhor and Chumey, Tang and Ura. The first two are mainly agricultural, in Tang and Ura livestock breeding. Your tour begins at the temple Jampay Lhakhang, which is said to have been built in the 7th century. It is one of 108 temples that, according to legend, were built in just one day by the Tibetan King Songtsen Gampo to “pin” a giant demon on the ground forever – she wanted to stop the spread of Buddhism. Then visit Kurjey Lhakhang, a monastery where the remains of Bhutan’s first three kings rest. The last sanctuary of your visit today is Tamzhing Lhakhang, which you can reach via a suspension bridge. After so much spirituality, you return to worldly realms: you visit a cheese factory and a brewery. While cheese is on the traditional diet of the Bhutanese, behind the “Red Panda Brewery” is a Swiss emigrant who brought the white beer to the country. Fritz Maurer came to the country in the mid-sixties – due to a quirky job advertisement: The King of Bhutan was looking for a cheesemaker for a year. Overnight in Bumthang.

Day 6 Excursion to the Tang Valley

The Tang Valley is the most remote of the four valleys of Bumthang. On the meadows graze sheep and in higher altitudes you can also discover yaks. In this touristy still little developed region you take a hike today. On your way, you will pass one of Bhutan’s most important pilgrimage sites: Mebartsho, the “Flaming Lake”. In reality, this is a gorge through which a roaring river winds. Visitors let small lights swim on the water surface as offerings. From here, continue your walk to the 16th-century Palace of Ogyen Choeling. The attached museum offers a good insight into the stately life before 1950 overnight in Bumthang.

Day 7 Excursion via Trongsa to Punakha

Today, return west from the remote Bumthang region. Along the way, make a stopover in Trongsa where you can visit the museum, housed in a former watchtower. Here you can learn about the culture, religion and customs of the country and admire a variety of interesting objects such as religious artifacts and traditional garments. After a total of about eight hours, you reach Punakha towards evening. Overnight in Punakha.

Day 8 Returns to Paro

After breakfast, you will return via the Dochula Pass to the starting point of your trip to Paro. Along the way, enjoy a traditional lunch at a farm over 300 years old. You will also visit the Shrine of Kyichu Lhakhang, a complex consisting of two identical temples built on a small ledge. Around the sanctuary, countless colorful prayer flags blow in the wind. Overnight in Paro.

Day 9 hike to "Tigernest"

Today, take a hike through Bhutan’s impressive mountain world. Your destination is the famous “Tigernest Monastery” (Taktshang), which – 900 meters above the valley – nestles against the rugged rock faces of the Himalayas. The name comes from a legend that the second Buddha, Guru Padmasambhava, flew here to meditate on the back of a tigress. The place is a famous pilgrimage site for Buddhists and one of the most sacred places in the Himalayas. Once you have arrived at the top, enjoy the dreamlike view of the valley in front of you. Overnight in Paro.

Day 10: Farewell to Bhutan

Today your journey through the fascinating Kingdom of Bhutan ends. Many precious memories richer you will take your return flight.
