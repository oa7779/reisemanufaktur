East India Journey to Four of the "Seven Sisters" - Magic between Monasteries and Jungle

East India trip to four of the "Seven Sisters" - magic between monasteries and jungles of Asia Special Tours. Enquire here and consult individually.

Itinerary & services

English itinerary text is an offline machine translation of the German original for this team preview. Please confirm travel details with your advisor; the German source remains available through the language switch.

Are you adventurous and would like to discover India away from the tourist trail? On this journey for explorers, you will experience beautiful and exotic places in the little-traveled East of India. Enjoy the sight of the snow-capped peaks of the Himalayas, of green jungles and magnificent nature reserves. Get acquainted with Buddhist monasteries and the centuries-old heritage of indigenous tribes and experience the fascinating wildlife of Assam. This journey takes you through four of the so-called “seven sisters” who are only connected to the rest of India by a narrow land corridor. The northeastern provinces of Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya and Nagaland are full of magic, giving you a glimpse of India as it is hidden from most travelers. If simple accommodations and sometimes long travel times are in order for you, you will be delighted and happy to think back on this unique trip for a long time.

1st day Arrival in India & further east

Namaste and welcome to India! Your journey begins in Kolkata, from where you will immediately travel by domestic flight via Bangladesh to the extreme east of the country. Your destination is the city of Shillong, located at 1,500 meters above sea level, in the state of Meghalaya, the “House of Clouds”. Hardly touched by tourism, Meghalaya fascinates with its gentle hillside landscapes full of forests, pastures, rice fields and fields. There you will be welcomed at the airport and driven to your hotel overlooking Lake Umiam. The rest of the day is at your disposal. Overnight in Shillong.

2nd day Market driving, music & archery: multi-faceted shillong

After breakfast, visit the Donbosco Center For Indigenous Culture (DBCIC), Asia’s largest museum of indigenous culture, and familiarize yourself with the culture of the people living here. Later, while walking through the Iewduh market, you will gain first insights into the daily life of the locals. Iewduh literally means "great market" and the scale of Iewduh honors its name. Here you get everything the heart desires. Keep your camera at hand, because the busy and colorful hustle and bustle also delivers fantastic photo opportunities. After the hustle and bustle, head to Dylan’s Café for a delicious lunch of hearty American dishes or local specialties. The café is dedicated to the famous musician Bob Dylan, who is worshipped here like a god. Well strengthened, watch a competition in traditional archery, the Tir, which is one of the favorite sports of Khasi, one of the three largest ethnic groups in the region. Before the competition begins, bets are made on how many of the storeys hit the target. This sport has almost completely displaced horse racing in Shillong. Overnight in Shillong.

Day 3 The Sacred Forest of Mawphlang

Meghalaya is about half covered by forest, of which almost the entire area does not belong to the state, but municipalities of the matriarchal Khasi ethnic group. These are the sacred forests of Meghalaya, where, according to local tribal belief, a deity takes the form of a tiger or leopard lives. All life here is under their strict protection and even the picking of a leaf or taking a stone is forbidden. The most famous of these forests is the sacred forest of Mawphlang, about an hour southwest of Shillong. This one may seem inconspicuous to the uninitiated, but together with a local Khasi guide, you reveal his secret. Near the entrance, which almost seems like a portal to another world, stand huge megaliths that mark the border of the sacred forest. They were built in memory of the dead and have served as sites for thanksgiving and blessing ceremonies since ancient days. Once you enter the forest, you will discover an amazing network of plants and trees. Some of them, who are probably already more than 1,000 years old, are full of ancient wisdom. There are many medicinal plants here, some of which can even cure diseases such as cancer and tuberculosis. Overnight in Shillong.

Day 4 - Continue to Guwahati

Your journey will now take you to Guwahati, which is located in the Indian state of Assam. After checking in at the hotel, visit one of Guwahati’s main attractions, Kamakhya Temple. The original temple dates back to the 10th century but was destroyed by Muslim invaders and rebuilt in the Assamese style in the 17th century. It is located on the Nilachal hill about eight kilometers west of the city and is an important sanctuary for the followers of the Shakti cult, which places female deities at the center of worship. Non-Hindus are prohibited from entering the temple interior. The temple is dedicated to the local deity Kamakhya, a bloodthirsty manifestation of the goddess Durga. Until 1832, human sacrifices were offered to the goddess on the occasion of the annual Ambubachi festival in July, which volunteered and were venerated as saints after her death. Animal sacrifices are still common today. Overnight in Guwahati.

Day 5 Colonial Charm at Wild Mahasheer Tea Planters Bungalow

The next destination on your journey is the city of Balipara, also located in Assam, which you can reach after about five hours of driving. After checking in at the hotel, you can arrange the rest of the day according to your expectations. Your accommodation for tonight is the Heritage Hotel Wild Mahasheer Tea Planters Bungalow at the Adabari Tea Estate, originally owned by the Assam Tea Company. The 19th-century property is a historic building characterized by its beautiful Victorian architecture. The four individual bungalows are spread over a 22-hectare plot surrounded by tea gardens, trees and flowers. Enjoy the tranquility interrupted by the buzz of insects and chirping of birds. The spacious rooms are furnished stylishly in colonial style and offer a beautiful view of the garden. The Wild Mahasheer Tea Planters Bungalow owes its current name to one of the most resistant fish in the world: the Mahseer, whose catch was long reserved for the Indian Maharajas. Representatives of this species also frolic in the nearby Bhoroli River. Overnight in Balipara.

Day 6 Orchids and Buddhist Spirituality in Bomdilla

A new day, a new goal. After breakfast, make your way to Bomdilla, which is about 2,400 meters above sea level in the Indian state of Arunachal Pradesh – the northeastern corner of India. The small town is known for its cool climate, apple plantations, friendly people and fantastic views of snow-capped Himalayan peaks. Along the way, visit the Tipi Orchid Center, which houses over 7,500 orchids (April and May blossoming season). After checking in at your accommodation, pay a visit to the Buddhist monastery, which is one of the main attractions in the area. If time permits, you can then take yourself on a stroll over the market into a world of colors and fragrances. Overnight in Bomdilla.

7th day Stunning panorama on the way to Tawang

Today is a travel day ahead of you. Your destination is the city of Tawang, which houses the largest monastery in India and the second largest monastery in the world after Potala Palace in Tibet! Today's ride leads first along the river Dirang and on a climb to the passes of Sela. On a clear day, the view is breathtaking all around. On this famous pass lies snow most of the year. In between, you can stop again and again to enjoy the fantastic view. Overnight in Tawang.

Day 8 The Monasteries of Tawang

This day is dedicated to exploring the mountain town of Tawang. The ancient Tawang Monastery, located at 3,500 meters above sea level on a grail near the border with Tibet, is the main attraction of the state. The 17th-century monastery is the center of the spiritual life of the “yellow hats”, one of four sects that formed during the introduction of Buddhism in Tibet, and was once founded on behalf of the fifth Dalai Lama. The walls have been reinforced in the past to ward off attacks from Bhutan. In the huge library many precious writings are kept. But the greatest treasure is a ten-meter-high Buddha figure, which was transported in individual parts from Tibet across the Himalayas. Other interesting stops of your visit are the Women's Monastery (Ani Gompa) and the Monastery of Urgelling. In Urgelling, the sixth Dalai Lama was born. Enjoy the special atmosphere in this place and come to rest. Alternatively, you can visit a village of Monpa, a local Buddhist tribe that practices the special art of Tibetan Thangkha painting. In addition, the Monpa are very knowledgeable in carpet weaving, weaving and wood carving. Overnight in Tawang. 

9th Day Viewed Guest in Dirang: Black-necked Cranes from Siberia

Due to the fascinating beauty of the picturesque mountain landscape, you drive back to Dirang via the Sela Pass. The place is nestled between pretty valleys, apple plantations and sheep farms. In winter you can watch black-necked cranes from Siberia with a bit of luck. After check-in at the hotel, the rest of the day is freely available to you. Take a walk around the area or relax in your hotel. Overnight in Dirang.

Day 10 Back to Balipara

There is another day of travel ahead of you today. From Dirang, head back to Balipara, where you’ll check in for two more nights at the Wild Mahseer Tea Planters Bungalow. Overnight in Balipara. 

Day 11: Various Birds in the Nameri National Park

Your day starts early with a trip to the Nameri National Park at the foot of the Himalayas. The sanctuary is home to elephants, leopards, bison and horse deer. However, the park is particularly famous for its large number of bird species that you can observe here. Experience the tranquility and morning freshness of the forest during a jungle walk in the park. With a bit of luck, you will spot rare white wing ducks that live here in quite large numbers. In addition, the park is home to Weisswangen partridges, furchenhorn and Nepalhorn birds, malay tree and amur falcons, grayhead and smaller sea eagle, various woodpeckers, pirole and many more. Back at the hotel you can rest a little and fortify with a delicious lunch. In the afternoon, visit a nearby village of the Garo tribe, who are very skillful weavers. If you wish, you can also try on the traditional garo robe. In the evening, return to your hotel. Overnight in Balipara.

12th day Jeep Safari in Kaziranga National Park

Today, leave Balipara behind and drive to Kaziranga. Along the way, you can admire the breathtaking landscape of Assam – the region is one of the most biodiverse in the world. The Kaziranga National Park covers an area of 430 square kilometers, which is dominated by high grasslands, forests and marshes. This idyllic national park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Kaziranga is home to more than 75% of the population of the large Indian rhinoceros and provides a unique habitat for many animals. Your accommodation for the next two nights is Diphlu River Lodge, located on the edge of the National Park, which blends beautifully into the countryside. Here you will enjoy an incomparable intimacy with nature. The total of 12 reet and bamboo-covered cottages are inspired by the simple architecture of the Mising ancestral houses and furnished with handmade fabrics and furniture. Diphlu River Lodge is committed to responsible tourism, always putting nature, wilderness and local communities first. After check-in, take a guided Jeep safari (together with other guests) and immerse yourself in the fascination of the wilderness and its inhabitants. Overnight in Kaziranga.

Day 13 Varied Day in Kaziranga National Park

Nature is calling! Spend the whole day exploring the park and its rich wildlife on guided jeep safaris, hiking safaris or elephant rides (together with other guests) from very different perspectives. At dusk, you will finally return to your lodge. Overnight in Kaziranga.

Day 14: Travel to Jorhat

From the jungle we continue today to Jorhat. There, after check-in at the hotel, the rest of the day is freely available. Overnight in Jorhat.

Day 15 Insights into Life on Majuli

Well strengthened, take a trip to Asia’s largest river island today. With a simple, local ferry you cross the mighty Brahmaputra to the island of Majuli. Since the island is still off the tourist routes, in addition to the inhabitants living mainly in small villages, many animal and plant species find a safe habitat. Arriving on the island, you will take a first exploration trip. Particularly interesting are the monasteries or Sattras, which were already created in the 15th century on Majuli. Here a unique form of Vaishnavism is practiced, which calls for equality of all and non-violence towards humans and animals. You will visit the Dakshinpaat Sattra, where the monks lead a celibate life, as well as the Natun Samaguri Sattra, whose monks are allowed to marry – an interesting contrast. The Natun Samaguri Sattra is also known for making masks. Together with your guide, you will also visit villages of the Mishing, the largest ethnic group on Majuli Island. Women of the Mishing folk group are great weavers. Look over their shoulders as they work and watch the artful fabrics emerge. Overnight in Jorhat.

Day 16: Nagaland

The final destination on your trip is the city of Kohima in the Indian state of Nagaland. The multitude of tribes are collectively referred to as “Naga” and differ by their language, partly their hairstyle and especially by the large colored cloths. Kohima is located in the south of Nagaland at an altitude of 1,444 meters above sea level and is the capital of the state. After arriving at the hotel, you can arrange the rest of the day according to your expectations. Our tip: If you are traveling to Kohima during the popular Hornbill Festival (end of November – mid-December) and want to treat yourself to a real highlight once again as a crowning conclusion, we recommend that you spend the nights in Kohima at the dreamlike Kohima Camp Nagaland. The luxury camp, built especially for the period of the festival, is located in the middle of green forest landscapes. The tents are equipped with a romantic four-poster bed and tasteful furniture, offering breathtaking views of the mountain panorama all around. In addition to visiting the festival, village walks organized by the camp are also recommended, giving you the opportunity to immerse yourself in the culture of the Naga.

17th day Explore Kohima and the surrounding area

Together with your guide, you will explore the most interesting sides of Kohima today. Worth seeing are the well-kept soldier cemetery overlooking the city, Kohima Cathedral, whose architecture contains many elements of traditional Naga houses, and the State Museum, which displays a rare collection of artifacts from each Naga tribe. Located twenty kilometers west of Kohima, Khonoma village will then give you a glimpse into the traditional life and history of the Angami who belong to the Naga tribes. The small village has been able to retain its traditional charm to this day. Also the nature around Khonoma with dense forests and lush green mountains is overwhelming. Quite rightly, Khonoma is called the “green village”. During your visit, you should also visit the surrounding rice terraces where up to 20 different types of rice are grown. Finally, return to Kohima. Overnight in Kohima.

18th day Farewell to India

Today we say goodbye to the mysterious East of India. To get a lot more impressions of the fabulous cultural and natural treasures that the northeast provinces of the country have in store, you will be taken to the airport and take your onward or return flight via Kolkota.
