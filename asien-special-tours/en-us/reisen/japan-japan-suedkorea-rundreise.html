Japan and South Korea - Tradition meets modernity

Japan South Korea Tour: The combination of tradition and modernity makes this Japan and South Korea trip from Asia Special Tours an unforgettable experience.

Itinerary & services

On this trip you will get to know the most beautiful sides of the two fascinating countries. Explore the modern metropolises of Seoul and Tokyo, admire magnificent temples, shrines and palaces, be carried away by the colorful bustle of the markets, sleep in style in the traditional accommodations Hanok and Ryokan, and get unique insights into the life of the locals. Of course, culinary experiences should not be missing on your trip, because Japanese cuisine in particular is rightly one of the most popular worldwide. Get a glimpse of how Korean liqueurs are made, try the freshest sushi at the famous Tsukiji Fish Market, and dive into Tokyo’s street food scene on an evening tour of traditional Japanese pubs. In Seoul, you can prove your talent at a private cooking class. An all-round successful journey through two exciting countries, on which you will inspire in particular their diversity and the friendly people.

1st day Arrival in South Korea

Welcome to the Land of Morning Silence! Your journey begins in Seoul, where you will be welcomed and warmly greeted by your German-speaking guide. Arrive in peace and explore the surroundings. The rest of the day is at your disposal. Overnight in Seoul.

Day 2: Experience Seoul with all your senses

After breakfast, it goes on culinary for you! The morning is entirely dedicated to South Korean cuisine – it is particularly diverse and imaginatively colorful! Together with your guide, you will head to the O-Me Cooking Lab, where you will conjure up a delicious dish under guidance. To buy the necessary ingredients for your cooking class, first visit the market, which offers a variety of products and is full of colors and fragrances. Then turn the purchased ingredients into a tasty typical dish. Of course, your instructor is at your side and explains the individual steps. Afterwards you can enjoy your authentic, delicious lunch. So well fortified visit the Gyeongbokgung Palace (in English: “Palace of Bliss”), which was once the main palace and residence of the royal family. On foot you will continue to the Samcheongdong district, which has its very own charm. Small cafés, individual shops, antique shops and art galleries paired with the surrounding nature create a beautiful scenery that invites you to dream and linger. At the end of the successful day you will visit the Hanokdorf Bukchon. It is home to hundreds of traditional houses called “Hanok” in Korean. Overnight in Seoul.

Day 3 A cup of tea in the “Garden of Morning Silence”

Today you leave for Seoul. You will travel to the province of Gyeonggi-do to visit the Garden of Morning Silence. The garden is one of Korea’s largest botanical gardens and enjoys great popularity. From the promenade surrounded by a refreshing pine forest to the 330,000 square meter flower garden, the Arboretum shows all the diversity of the plant world. After a cup of tea in this magical setting, head to the Sansawon brewery. In the in-house museum you will learn more about the production of various Korean liqueurs. Afterwards, together with other guests, you can try yourself in the art of liqueur distillery. Overnight in Seoul.

Day 4 Traditional Village Life in Hahoe

It’s time to leave Seoul behind. The fast train takes you to your next destination: Daegu. Once there, your guide will first take you to the Medical Market for Herbs. The Daegu-Yangnyeongsi (Medical Market) is an important herbal medicine market, which was already considered a flourishing hub for traders and travelers from all over the country and its neighboring countries during the Joseon Dynasty. After a short walking tour, continue to Andong, where you will visit the Confucian School of Byeongsan Seowon. You will get an authentic insight into traditional life in the village of Hahoe. It is one of South Korea’s UNESCO sites and convinces by preserving Korean architecture and culture. It owes its name “village surrounded by water” to the river Nakdong, which winds around the village. You will end your eventful day with an overnight stay at the traditional Hanok guest house Rakkojae (simple but authentic accommodation). Situated in the middle of the historic village, it offers you a unique opportunity to fully immerse yourself in the lives of the people of that time. Overnight in Andong.

Day 5: A Journey into the Past

A new day, a new goal. On your way to Gyeongju, stop at the Jukdo market, which has now become one of the largest traditional markets in the entire Gyeongsangbudo province. In Guryongpo, time seems to have stopped more than 100 years ago. Travel back in time to a South Korea once occupied by Japanese colonial rulers. As you stroll along the approximately 500-meter-long “Guryeongpo Street of Contemporary Cultural History”, you will find buildings of Japanese construction in the 1930s. Back in the here and now you continue your journey to Gyeongju. In the middle of the city center, the tombs of 23 rulers of the Silla period rise in Tumuli Park. Here you can not only walk among the artificial, green hilltops, but also visit one of the gigantic burial mounds. Inside it are more than 11,000 grave goods as well as the crowns of the king. In the immediate vicinity is the Cheomseongdae Observatory, the oldest existing star observatory in Asia. After this impressive journey back in time, you will be taken to your hotel with all modern amenities. Overnight in Gyeongju.

Day 6 UNESCO World Heritage Site in Gyeongju

Your day begins with two UNESCO World Heritage sites that will surely impress you. The Bulguksa Temple represents the temple architecture of the ancient Silla Empire at its best. The temple site contains other national treasures, such as the main staircase, which represents the 33 steps to enlightenment. The famous Seokguram Grotto, which you will visit afterwards, was dug into the mountain wall of Tohamsan Mountain in the 8th century. Inside it is a Buddha statue facing the East Sea in the position of Bhumisparsha Mudra. In the afternoon you will reach Busan, which you will first get to know by water. Sit back and let some of Busan’s attractions such as Oryokdo Island and Nurimaru APEC House on Dongbaek Island pass by during your cruise. Afterwards you will be taken to your hotel. Overnight in Busan.

Day 7 A city with many facets: fascinating Busan

You will visit the UN Memorial and Peace Park. These honor the UN soldiers from 16 nations who participated in the Korean War (1950-1953) and died in battle. Then you will visit the cliffs of Taejongdae, whose surroundings are characterized by a rocky coastline. With a clear view, you can look up to the Japanese island of Tsushima! In Busan city center you will visit the Gukje market (in German: "international market"), which looks back on a 60-year history. Here, from clothing to fresh food from the country, you also get Korea’s well-known street food. In the late afternoon, visit the Choryang Ibagu-gil Trail. Residents built their houses on the top of the hill, creating the small neighborhood and the Ibagu-gil. At the top of the hill, from Sanbokdoro Street you can enjoy a great view over the entire city center of Busan. The perfect backdrop to review the impressions collected during the last few days in South Korea. But your journey is far from over! Overnight in Busan.

Day 8 Tto man-na-yo South Korea and konnichi wa Japan

Today you will fly to Japan and it will be called "Tto man-na-yo" (in English: "Goodbye"), South Korea. After a short flight you will reach Japan, the land of the rising sun. At Fukuoka Airport, you will be welcomed and warmly greeted by an English-speaking assistant. This accompanies you to the vehicle available for you, which will take you to the station for your onward journey with the super fast Shinkansen to Hiroshima. The port city, whose roots date back to the 16th century, is known worldwide mainly because of the atomic bombing on 6 August 1945. But you will also feel that the city has resurrected after the tragedy, almost like “phoenix from the ashes”. Today it is considered one of the most relaxed cities in Japan. At the same time, life pulsates here on the extended strolling miles, in res-taurants and art museums. The inhabitants are extremely friendly and Hiroshima welcomes its visitors in the most cordial way. Overnight in Hiroshima. 

Day 9 - The island of Miyajima

Without guidance

During your stay in Hiroshima, you should not miss a trip to the island of Miyajima. These can be reached from the mainland after a short ferry ride. On the island, which has long been considered sacred, trustworthy macaques and other tame wild animals frolic in original forests. However, the island is particularly well known for the Itsukushima Shrine. Some parts of the Shinto shrine are built on pillars, so they seem to float in the water at high tide. In particular, the red tori gate, the landmark of the complex, is a prime example of this. After an extensive exploration of the island, return to Hiroshima. Be sure to visit the Peace Museum and learn about the background and effects of the nuclear attack on Hiroshima. Here, memorabilia and testimonies of victims were compiled. In addition, the institution is involved in the international peace movement. Overnight in Hiroshima.

Day 10 Typical Japanese: experiencing a tea ceremony

On board the Shinkansen you will continue your journey. Your destination is Kyoto - Japan's cultural capital. For more than a thousand years, the Imperial Court lived in Kyoto, and today the numerous palaces, shrines and gardens make the city a cultural center. Unlike many other cities, Kyoto has been largely spared war damage. As a result, the city was able to preserve its unique cultural heritage and is not for nothing still considered an epitome of Japanese culture today. A very special experience awaits you here this afternoon: dedicate yourself to Japanese traditions and take part in a tea ceremony. After arriving in Kyoto, you will go on your own to the private residence of a Tea Ceremonial Master, where today you will be introduced to the high art of tea ceremony under expert guidance. This can truly be called an art that requires years of practice and dedication. You will learn about the close link between the rituals and Zen Buddhism, the meaning of each gesture during the ceremony and, of course, how the tea is prepared and served correctly. You can also try yourself in the right preparation of the tea and of course try it. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 11: The most beautiful sides of Kyoto

After breakfast, meet your guide in the hotel foyer and explore some of the most important attractions together. You start with the Fushimi Inari-Taisha. The famous Shinto shrine is dedicated to the goddess of fertility, rice and success. Particularly impressive is the way up into the wooded hills, lined with thousands of bright orange torii gates. Afterwards we will take the train to the Sanjusangendo Temple. A truly unforgettable sight awaits you inside: 1,001 golden, life-sized Kannon statues lined up in the longest wooden hall in Japan. A short drive will take you to the Nishiki market. The covered shopping street lures with traditional shops, cooking kitchens and specialty shops. But beware of snacking – lunch is already waiting in a nearby restaurant. In the afternoon, visit the impressive Nijo Castle, former seat of Sho-gun Tokugawa. A subway ride will take you to the last highlight of the day: Nanzenji Temple. After a day full of impressive discoveries, say goodbye to your guide and have the opportunity to customize the evening. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 12 Majestic Fuji

Without guidance

In order to get an impression of Japan's beautiful landscape, your journey will now take you to Hakone. The Hakone region lies in the middle of the mountains and volcanoes of Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park on the shore of a lake. After arriving, spend the rest of the day exploring Hakone with your included transport pass. This includes a large number of different transport options in the surrounding area. During the day, you may also have the opportunity to catch a view of Mount Fuji. Of course, this is only possible on days with cloudless skies. Your overnight stay is something very special today: you are a guest in a ryokan. The typical Japanese inns were already built in the Edo period (1603 – 1868). Today you can find them especially outside of larger cities. Staying here is especially worthwhile to experience Japanese hospitality. Among the highlights of a stay at Ryokan are meals. Tonight you will enjoy a multi-course Kaiseki dinner. At the end of the day, treat yourself to a relaxing bath in private onsen before bed for rest on your futons. Overnight in Hakone.

Day 13: The metropolis of Tokyo calls

After a morning swim in private onsen, a final train ride will take you to the modern metropolis of Tokyo. After arrival, you will still have time to explore the city a little independently. Towards evening you will meet your guide for the next few hours. On a street food tour, you will now be introduced to the culinary diversity of Japan. Tokyo offers a huge offer. Whether street food, small Japanese pubs or luxurious star restaurants: the gastro scene really leaves nothing to be desired. Not only Japanese specialties are offered, but also Western and exotic food. Together with your guide, you will embark on a stroll through the street food scene of Tokyo. You will look behind the scenes of hidden izakayas - small Japanese pubs, restaurants and bars. Of course, you will not return to your hotel hungry. Overnight in Tokyo.

14th day temples, gardens and stroll over the Tsukiji outdoor market

Today you can look forward to a day full of varied impressions of the exciting metropolis. Your guide will wait for you in the foyer of your hotel and will be the first to take you to Tsukiji Market. Even though the world-famous fish market has been relocated to the island of Toyosu, the Tsukiji outdoor market with traditional shops and world-class sushi restaurants remains a lively hub for visitors and locals to enjoy delicious food and buy local specialties. Stay at one of the restaurants for a super-fresh sushi breakfast! Then leave the market behind and make your way to Hamarikyu Park, a true oasis in the middle of a sea of skyscrapers. The perfect place to dangle your soul over a cup of tea while your guide tells you interesting things about the Japanese tea tradition. In Asakusa, visit the Sensoji Temple, Tokyo’s oldest Buddhist temple. It is also home to the oldest geisha district in the city. The visit to Meiji Shrine will round off your day impressively. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 15 Farewell to Japan

The time until the planned airport transfer is at your disposal. Unfortunately, your time out from everyday life is already coming to an end. Even if your holiday is over – the precious memories, beautiful photos and exciting stories of your trip will accompany you forever. Look forward to letting your friends and family share your experiences in the distance at home. Your driver will take you in time for your return flight to the airport. This ends your beautiful journey through two impressive countries with Asia Special Tours.
We wish you a good journey home!
