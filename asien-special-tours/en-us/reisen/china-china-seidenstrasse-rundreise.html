Along the legendary Silk Road through China

This journey takes you on the legendary Silk Road, which has connected Orient and Occident for thousands of years. Get to know the Chinese part of the Silk Road comprehensively. You will travel on historical trails, discover ancient oasis cities, magnificent buildings and visit important Buddhist art treasures. In addition, this trip holds dreamlike landscapes for you. Experience the fascination of Silk Road up close!

1st day Arrival in Beijing

Welcome to China! Your journey begins in Beijing, the exciting capital of the “Middle Kingdom”. At the airport, your personal guide is already waiting for you and driving you to your hotel. The rest of the day is at your disposal. As a political and cultural center of the country, Beijing can look back on a 3000-year history, which is reflected in a globally unique cultural heritage. Thanks to the 2008 Olympic Games, China’s capital, often dismissed as a giant village, has transformed into a modern world city. Just as the emperors once built their city as an image of the universe, China’s rulers of the 21st century created their capital as an image of a new, powerful China. Take the time to take a look around. Overnight in Beijing.

Day 2 Beijing after your Gusto

Without tour guide & chauffeur

Explore the metropolis today on your own. Do not miss a visit to the Forbidden City with the Imperial Palace. This magical place was the seat of the Chinese emperors of two dynasties – the Ming and the Qing – and for half a millennium was inaccessible to those who did not belong to the court. The crowd is as overwhelming as the architecture, the colorfully painted palace halls, the pagoda-like roofs. Afterwards you can stroll over the Tiananmen Square, which is 500 meters wide and 880 meters long, the largest square on earth. The summer palace is also extremely worth seeing. The complex is a rare masterpiece of Chinese horticultural art that will enchant you. The Summer Palace on the West Mountains near Beijing is the only surviving imperial garden. In it, the perfect dream of Chinese garden architecture is realized: an ensemble of palaces and temples, pavilions and pagodas with a total of 3,000 rooms on 290 hectares. There is even a white marble ship to admire. One feels how the Chinese emperors and their court lived. Overnight in Beijing.

3rd day With the night train to Dunhuang

In the morning you will be taken to the airport to fly to Lanzhou. Once there, take a round trip to get to know the city a little closer. In the evening, continue your journey and take the night train to Dunhuang. Overnight on board.

Day 4 The Oase City of Dunhuang

In the early hours of the morning you will reach the pretty oasis town of Dunhuang, which in its heyday developed into one of the richest cities in Asia with over 100,000 inhabitants. Even today, Dunhuang is one of the most important stops along the Silk Road, thanks in particular to its main attraction: the famous Mogao Grottoes. Together with your guide, you will visit the grottoes that are among the Buddhist treasures of China. Today there are still 492 caves, more than 45,000 square meters of mural painting and 2,450 colored Buddha statues! The design of the caves required a period of more than 1,000 years – it stretched over several dynasties. For this reason, Dunhuang is rightly referred to today as the miracle on the edge of the Gobi desert and a cultural and artistic treasure trove of China. No less impressive are the sand dunes of the Taklamakan Desert, which reach almost to the city of Dunhuang. The sliding down of the sand dunes creates an unmistakable sound that gives the dune area its name: "Singing Sandberg". Here is also the Mondsichelsee, which owes its name to its unmistakable form. A fascinating miracle of nature. Overnight in Dunhuang.

5th day Bizarre clay formations in Yardang National Park

Today you will visit the Yardang National Park, a yardang that is an erosion form of sediment that is reminiscent of a table. For the most part, they owe their appearance to the wind, which has done its job over several million years. Translated from Uyghur, Yardang means “hills of clay”. Let your imagination run wild and you will recognize figures in the formations – for example lions or camels. Almost 400 square kilometers, the national park is large and offers an unreal expanse worth exploring. The most notable attraction is the so-called ghost town – in this area the yardangs resemble an abandoned city. Take your time, there is a lot to see! Overnight in Dunhuang.

Day 6 - Via Liuyuan on to Turfan

After breakfast, you will be driven to Liuyuan, where you will take the fast train to Turfan. Upon your arrival in the evening, you will already be expected and taken to your hotel. The fertile oasis of Turfan is located at the foot of Bogda-Shan (5,445 meters, the sacred mountain of the Uyghurs). The vibrant business life on the bazaars and the colorful mixture of peoples make the stay an experience. The Turfan Sink offers a unique scenic panorama: Dry Hot Desert, deep gorges, picturesque mountains and green wooded oases. It is both the deepest and hottest region of China. The mountains around the sink prevent the penetration of cool winds, and so in the hot summer months temperatures can rise to 47.5 degrees Celsius. Overnight in Turfan.

Day 7 Around Turfan: Ruins of Jiaohe & underground canal system

Today is dedicated to exploring Turfan and its surroundings. About 10 kilometers west of the city lies the long-established and abandoned town of Jiaohe in the Han period. The former garrison town played a major role in the Tang, Song and Yuan dynasties on the Silk Road but was completely destroyed by Genghis Khan’s armies. However, its structure can still be guessed well from the large ruin field. The spectacular rocky plateau on which Jiaohe lies was enclosed by two rivers, but today they have dried up. The place owes its name to them “Jiaohe”, which in Chinese means “winding rivers”. On the way back from Jiaohe you will visit the Karez water canals, which are a testament to the diligence and intelligence of the peoples living here in antiquity. It is also known as the “Great Wall of the Underground”. The Karez systems are the life source of Turfan. Or vice versa: Without Karez, there would be no Turfan culture. They transport melt and spring water from the foot of Tianshan to Turfan, providing the city with the vital water. Fascinating, isn't it? With many new impressions, you will finally return to your hotel. Overnight in Turfan.

Day 8 More ruins and tombs at Turfan

Less well-preserved but larger are the ruins of Gaochang, an ancient oasis town on the edge of the Taklamakan Desert. As Jiaohe once founded as a garrison city, Gaochang developed into a cosmopolitan city that was both a trading center and a stopping point for Silk Road merchants. After the city was destroyed in the early Ming era by the Mongols in the fire of war, the buildings are unfortunately hardly recognizable today. Nevertheless, the visit is worthwhile. A few kilometers further northwest, in Astana, is the cemetery of the ancient city of Gaochang. During the systematic excavation from 1959, some bodies mummified in the dry desert climate were found, which had been wrapped in silk and buried together with objects such as ceramics, wood carvings, coins and documents identifying the acquis. Finally, the Enmin Minaret is waiting for you, 44 meters high, built of bricks and decorated with many geometric patterns in the typical Uyghur style. Imam Imin Goja had this building built – his son Sulaiman had the work continued after his death in 1777. It is therefore often referred to as the Sulayman Mosque. Overnight in Turfan.

9th day Painting Lake Bostan

Your journey will take you to Korla today. Along the way, stop at Lake Bostan, known for its rich fish fauna. Of course, you can make as many photo stops as you want throughout the journey. Overnight in Korla.

Day 10: Continue to Kucha

A new day, a new goal. On the drive to Kucha, explore the ruined monastery town of Subashi. It is the capital of the ancient Guici Empire and lies at the foot of Tianshan. Afterwards you can visit the second largest mosque in Xinjiang, the Kuqa Mosque. This mosque with green bricks has no Chinese influences, but is completely in the traditional arabesque style. Overnight in Kucha.

Day 11 The Thousand Buddha Grottoes at Kizil & the Tianshan Grand Canyon

The small oasis town is a Uyghur place with an interesting history. Until the 8th century, when Kucha came under Chinese rule, it was an independent kingdom and closely associated with India. Already in the 4th century, Kucha was an important Buddhist center and developed into the nucleus of the spread of the religion originally from India in China. Today, Kucha is above all a stop on the long way to Kashgar, which is worthwhile not only because of the bazaar atmosphere of the old town, but especially thanks to the Thousand Buddha Grottos near Kizil, 70 kilometers west of Kucha. The Buddhist caves were carved into the overhanging rocks and steep mountain walls from the 5th century onwards and are the oldest Buddhist caves in China. 235 of the grottoes have defied the horrors of the desert and its destructive power to this day. Once you’ve had enough of the beautiful murals, make your way to the Tianshan Grand Canyon, whose highest peak towers 2,048 meters high. At certain times of the day, the sun dips the mountains into an intense red. Then they look like they are glowing with heat – a fascinating sight. Overnight in Kucha.

Day 12 Through the Taklamakan Desert to Hotan

On your way to Hotan, you will also travel through the Taklamakan Desert, the second largest and most hostile sand desert in the world. The fascinating and varied desert landscape is guaranteed no boredom. Along the way, stop for the breathtaking view of the Mazartagh ruins. Overnight in Hotan.

Day 13 Colorful Market Running in Hotan

The oasis city of Hotan was once the most important Buddhist cultural center on the southern Silk Road route before Islam reached China in the 9th century. The former capital of the Kingdom of Yutian, like most cities on the Silk Road, was temporarily annexed to the Chinese Empire. For centuries, jade, silk and carpets from Hotan have been considered the finest in China. Production is still going on today. Before you reach Hotan, visit the ruined city of Malikewat, which is about 30 kilometers south of the city. From the once so important center of Buddhism stand today only dilapidated walls. Far more colorful and lively, on the other hand, is the local market, where colorful fruits and vegetables pile up, spices exude beguiling smells and the merchants loudly advertise their goods. Let yourself be drawn into the bustle and taste a variety of local specialties (optional) at the stalls.The rest of the day you can freely design. Overnight in Hotan.

Day 14 Through the Stone Desert to Kashgar

The journey continues to Kashgar. The journey leads you through a stone desert past wild camels and fertile oases. Poplars were grown in many places to deter desert sand and allow the cultivation of cotton, tomatoes and melons. Along the way, visit the Altunluk Mausoleum, a 15th-century tomb complex of the royal family. Finally, you will reach Kashgar: a commercial center with vibrant business life and a hub of the various Silk Road routes. Overnight in Kashgar.

Day 15 Unique nature experience on the way to Tashkurgan

Before you get to know the busy city of Kashgar, which has retained its charm despite modernization, take a trip to Tashkurgan, the last place in China. Behind it rises the 4,800-meter-high Khunjerab Pass, the gateway to Pakistan. Along the way, Lake Karakul, located at the entrance of the Pamir Mountains at an altitude of 3,600 meters, offers you a unique experience of nature. From here you have an excellent view of the ice-capped peaks of the Muztagata and the Kongur, which are the second and third highest peaks of the Pamir Mountains. In the afternoon you will arrive in Tashkurgan. Overnight in Tashkurgan.

Day 16 - The "Stone City" of Tashkurgan

Tashkurgan translates from Tajik “stone city” and refers to the more than 1,300 meters long stone wall. The city was built on the remains of an old fort. You can quietly visit the site of the Stone City before returning to Kashgar. Overnight in Kashgar.

Day 17 Kashgar, the Silk Road hub

Today you will discover Kashgar, the hub of the Silk Road. Here the northern and southern Silk Roads met – and still meet today. This great oasis was once the last stop before the arduous crossing of the Karakorum and Pamir mountains. Together with your guide, you will visit the Id-Kah Mosque, which is considered the largest mosque in China and can hold around 7,000 people. The main gate flanked by two minarets looks like marzipan confection with its bright bricks. Afterwards you will drive to the tomb of the fragrant concubine, who was brought to Beijing as a little girl by Qing Emperor Qianlong, because the emperor was beguiled by her scent. You can use the rest of the day to explore Kashgar a little further on your own. Interesting, for example, is the lively Uyghur bazaar, which is divided into specialized areas, such as stalls for hats, musical instruments, carpets and iron goods. Part of the area consists of mud brick walls and courtyards with tiny restaurants and tea houses that invite you to linger. Overnight in Kashgar

18th day flight to Urumqi & boat trip on the Sky Lake

After breakfast, you will be driven to the airport from where you will continue by domestic flight to Urumqi. Upon arrival, break with your guide and chauffeur to Lake Tian Chi, located 50 kilometers southeast of Urumqi. The so-called “Sky Lake” is located at an altitude of 1,900 meters at the foot of Bogdo Shan. It is up to 90 meters deep and shines in crystal clear, jade-green shimmering water, whose banks are lined with pastures and cypresses. Sit back and enjoy the waters on a boat trip. Overnight in Urumqi.

19th Day Goodbye to China

For today, the Provincial Museum in Urumqi is still on the program. You will then be taken to the airport, from where you will travel home or onwards with many lasting impressions of life along the Chinese Silk Road.
