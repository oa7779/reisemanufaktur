Natural treasures for an entire continent: New Zealand unites on an area that is significantly smaller than Germany, as diverse landscapes as hardly any country on earth - from misty mountains to smoking volcanoes, wide coasts, deep fjords and dense rainforests. On the west coast of the south island, wild beaches, glacier-covered mountains and bush landscapes meet in the immediate vicinity. As a self-driver, you are flexible and have time to concentrate on the highlights of New Zealand thanks to pre-booked accommodation.

Day 1 Auckland - Coromandel Peninsula (ca.) 150 km

Take over your rental vehicle today and drive south from Auckland on Highway 1 to the Coromandel Peninsula. Along the coast of the Firth of Thames is the Township of Thames, reminiscent of the gold rush that broke out here in 1867. By 1900, the city had about 19,000 inhabitants. From Thames, it’s a short drive across the beautiful Coromandel Ranges Mountains to Pauanui’s posh beach.
The Coromandel Peninsula is characterised by a mountain belt of volcanic origin . The contrast between beaches and lush green cultivated land gives this area a special scenic appeal. Overnight in Whangamata.

Day 2 Explore the Coromandel Peninsula

Today you can take time to explore the Coromandel Peninsula with all its contrasts: white beaches meet lush green cultural land, rough rock cliffs on rolling hills overgrown by millennia-old jungle. From the beach you can easily explore the area by kayak – various islands, coves and sea grottoes are interesting excursion destinations. Particularly spectacular is Cathedral Cove, a bay from which some bizarre limestone formations tower. The surf erosion impressively washed out the soft chalk cliffs. You can also take a trip to Hot Water Beach. Here, about two kilometers below the surface of the earth, a hot layer of rock is located, which makes it possible to dig a private hot water pool at low tide. However, the region offers much more than beautiful beaches: in the north, a mountain range hosts various charming hiking trails. At Tairua you will also have the opportunity to admire the rare Kauri trees, which are up to 2,000 years old, up close. Overnight in Whangamata.

Day 3 Rotorua (approx.) 200 km

From Pauanui towards the south, past other picture book beaches, we head to Tauranga - one of the largest ports on the North Island. They cross the kiwi plantations around Te Puke and come to Rotorua. A volcanic lunar landscape, hot springs, seething mud ponds and a vibrant Maori culture: Rotorua is a fascinatingly foreign piece of earth. And an unstable one: the sulphur smell over Rotorua proves that the earth's surface is only very thin at this place. Even today, it can happen that in the front garden of the residents the earth breaks open and a new boiling pool is created. Overnight in Rotorua.

Day 4 Rotorua - Lake Taupo - Tongariro National Park (ca.) 180 km

Go to Taupo today. North of Taupo is Huka Falls, a dramatic river landscape.
Lake Taupo is a crater lake that was once formed by a massive eruption of the Taupo volcano. The water is larger than Lake Constance and similar to this a popular holiday destination, especially for anglers of trout. Enjoy the relaxed atmosphere on the shore of the lake.
On the east bank of the lake, drive to Turangi and on to Tongariro National Park. Overnight in Tongariro National Park.

Day 5 Tongariro National Park – Wellington (approximately). 330km

The Tongariro National Park contains three still active volcanoes, which are venerated as sacred by the Maori in several places of worship in the area. The wise foresight of Maori chief Te Heuheu Tukino laid the foundation for the establishment of the fourth oldest national park in the world. Lava casts flowed from several eruption channels for thousands of years. In October 1996, there were still heavy ejections. The barren crater landscape also served as a location for "Mordor" in "The Lord of the Rings". Along the Kapiti coast, head south to Wellington. Wellington is not only the seat of government, but also the artistic and cultural highlight of the country – it houses a variety of art galleries and interesting museums. With more than 300 bars, cafes and restaurants, a lively scene and many culinary highlights await you. Overnight in Wellington.

Day 6 Through the Cook Water Road to the south (approx.) 110 km

With the ferry you cross over to the south island. First, you will pass through the Cook Water Road. Then glide through the Marlborough Sounds, an area of “drunken” valleys created by the geological subsidence of a central mountain range. The original mountain range is still evidenced by steep mountain ridges, which today spring out as narrow peninsulas between deep sea arms. You can either take the rental car on the ferry or drop off at Wellington and pick up a new car in Picton. Please let us know which variant you prefer. Accordingly, we select the vehicle rental company. Continue on to Nelson for your pre-booked accommodation. Overnight in Nelson.

Day 7 Explore the surroundings of Nelson

Today you have time for your activities around Nelson. The region around Nelson offers scenic beauty as well as many cultural and culinary highlights. It is a center of crafts and thanks to its high number of hours of sunshine, it is a fertile wine-growing area with numerous wineries where you can taste in particular Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Noir and Riesling. Overnight in Nelson.

Day 8 Punakaiki - Fox Glacier (ca.) 460 km

The journey continues along the west coast to the southern Alps. As a natural weather barrier, the high mountain ridge is responsible for one of the most extreme precipitation regimes in the country. Above the snow line, large masses of ice collect, forming fast-flowing valley glaciers. The approximately 12 km long Fox and Franz Josef glaciers - unique in temperate latitudes - slide down to an altitude of 300 m above sea level. The coastal road leads you in a southerly direction between Tasman Lake on the right and the Southern Alps on your left. You will drive through the rocky and wooded Buller Gorge and drive through one of New Zealand’s most beautiful and impressive panoramic streets with its villages Punakaiki, Greymouth and Hokitika. Overnight at Fox Glacier.

Day 9 Fox Glacier – Wanaka (ca.) 286 km

Fox Glacier is located in Westland National Park. As in nearby Franz Josef, the glacier extends here far into the valley – at an altitude of about 300 meters above sea level – and thus borders directly on the edge of the rainforest.Leave today the glacier region in a southwesterly direction along the Haast River to Central Otago, past Lake Wanaka, up to Wanaka. Overnight in Wanaka.

Day 10 Wanaka – Queenstown (ca.) 115 km

Wanaka is something like the quiet little sister of Queenstown: here, too, the area invites for walks, hikes and bike rides, but the city itself is quieter. In front of the dramatic scenery around Wanaka with its impressive mountain and hill landscapes, some of the exterior shots of the epic film trilogy “Lord of the Rings” take place. Continue on to Queenstown, the adventure city of New Zealand. The self-proclaimed adventure capital of the world lives up to its name. Queenstown offers a wide range of adrenaline-charged pastimes: bungee jumping, jet boat riding or paragliding boost the hormone balance! Overnight in Queenstown.

Day 11 - A day in Queenstown

Bungee jumping, jet boat riding or paragliding get the hormone balance going! But even if you prefer to go a little slower, you will get your money's worth here. The surrounding mountains can be explored on scenic routes, various golf courses invite you to a game. Overnight in Queenstown.

Day 12 Queenstown - Milford - Te Anau (ca.) 173 km

Today you will drive to Te Anau, the gateway to Fjordland National Park - New Zealand's largest and still largely untapped national park. Hardly any landscape embodies the term wilderness better than fjordland. In the west, 14 fjords penetrate deep into the primordial forests of a rugged Alpine world like overlong sea tongues. In the immediate vicinity, snow-capped mountain peaks of up to 2700 meters elevate over alpine grass mats. In the middle of this fascinating landscape lies Milford Sound, in whose crystal clear water is reflected the over 1000 meters towering Mitre Peak. From Te Anau, take a trip to Milford Sound. On the most beautiful mountain road in New Zealand to Milford, you will pass the Mirror Lakes and the dark Homer Tunnel and even encounter Kea parrots on a stop with some luck. You will then reach the landmark of New Zealand and the most beautiful end of the world, the Milford Sound. Here you have the opportunity to take a boat trip on the Milford Sound (optional). Overnight in Te Anau.

Day 13 Te Anau – Dunedin (ca.) 290 km

Today you will continue to Dunedin, the impressive Scottish founded university city. A landmark of this city is the Railway Station, which should not be forgotten when visiting. Dunedin also has the steepest road in the world. Once upon a time, Dunedin was the richest city in the country: Already in 1863 there were already gas-lit streets and in 1879 the tram for transport to higher parts of the city was built according to the model of the Californian cable car. Overnight in Dunedin.

Day 14 Dunedin – Christchurch (ca.) 362 km

Drive through Palmerston and Oamaru in the North Otago region. You might like the inland route over Omarama, Twizel, Lake Tekapo and Lake Pukaki, a spectacular turquoise glacier lake. Stop at the Church of the Good Shepherd on Lake Tekapo for one of New Zealand’s most famous picnic spots (ca.) 550 km to Christchurch. Continue on the Canterbury Plains, the longest lowland route in New Zealand, to Christchurch. Overnight in Christchurch.

Day 15 Christchurch & Departure

Today you return your rental car and get ready for the return or onward journey. Depending on the destination, you will still have time to explore Christchurch. For a hundred years, Christchurch was characterized by beautiful gardens and parks as well as impressive architecture – much of it reminds of England. Unfortunately, an earthquake in 2011 fell victim to many of the city’s treasures. Work is still being done on reconstruction. From the Ports Hills you have a beautiful view down to the harbor. Then take a walk through the Botanical Garden with its majestic old trees or Hagley Park. Not far from the airport is the International Antarctic Centre, which lets you explore life in one of the most extreme places on earth in many ways.
