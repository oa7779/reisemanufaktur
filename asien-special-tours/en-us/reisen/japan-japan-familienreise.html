Japan for the whole family

Japan Family Travel: Whether small or large, on this individual Japan trip from Asia Special Tours the whole family gets their money’s worth. Ask now!

Itinerary & services

English itinerary text is an offline machine translation of the German original for this team preview. Please confirm travel details with your advisor; the German source remains available through the language switch.

Japan - a country of diversity: whether small or large, no one is guaranteed to get bored here. On this varied family trip, you will not only get to know the main attractions of the country, but also see many more facets that remain hidden to most visitors. For example, visit a Japanese school and learn to draw the world-famous manga figures. Immerse yourself in the secret world of ninjas and gain insight into their fighting techniques and mysterious abilities. In Kanazawa, you will either learn to make traditional Japanese sweets or make your own chopsticks; in the former imperial city of Kyoto, you will experience a traditional tea ceremony or relax with a Zen meditation. The culmination is a street food tour in Osaka, where you explore the city in the most delicious way possible.

Day 1 Welcome to Japan

Your journey begins in Tokyo, where you will be welcomed and warmly greeted by an English-speaking assistant. This accompanies you to your available private vehicle, which will take you to your exclusive hotel. The rest of the day is at your disposal to make your first explorations on your own in this breathtaking world metropolis. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 2 Tokyo as it Lives and Lives

Look forward to a day full of varied impressions. Depart in the morning to Tsukiji Market. Even though the world-famous fish market has been relocated to the island of Toyosu, the Tsukiji outdoor market with traditional shops and world-class sushi restaurants remains a lively hub for visitors and the local population to enjoy delicious food and buy local specialties. After visiting the market, continue to Hamarikyu Park, a historic Japanese garden from the Edo period known for its picturesque tea houses and ponds. Today it serves as a quiet resort for locals and tourists, offering fantastic views and nature. Afterwards we will continue to Asakusa. Here you will visit the Sensoji Temple, Tokyo’s oldest Buddhist temple. It is also home to the oldest geisha district in the city. For lunch, head to one of the neighborhood’s traditional restaurants. Try the tempura, for which Asakusa is known. The Meiji Shrine, surrounded by around 120,000 trees from 365 different species, makes you forget that you are in the largest city in the world. Overnight in Tokyo.

3rd day Bento course and the island of Odaiba

Now that you’ve eaten Japanese a few times, it’s time to try your hand at Japanese cuisine. Under guidance, you will learn how to prepare and make a traditional Japanese bento box. However, this Bento box is anything but ordinary. She is also known as Chara-Bento, a creative form of eating art that transforms traditional Bento into a depiction of the famous animation character Totoro. As part of this half-day course, you will learn all the skills and techniques necessary to create this delicacy. You can take the Bento box home as a souvenir. Of course, there is still time to exchange ideas during the subsequent meal. For the afternoon, we recommend a trip to the artificial island of Odaiba (optional), which attracts countless attractions. To end the day, a ride on the Ferris wheel is a good idea. As soon as the darkness settles over the city, the Rainbow Bridge, a suspension bridge over the port of Tokyo and connection to the city center, shines in the most beautiful colors of the rainbow. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 4 The “Coole Japan”

Today, you will dedicate yourself to the neighborhoods known as the center of Japanese pop culture and also referred to as “Cool Japan”. Your first destination is a Japanese school where you will not only learn a lot of interesting things about the history of the manga, but also learn to draw yourself in the style of the world-famous Japanese cartoon. Continue to Nakano Broadway, a four-storey shopping center dedicated solely to manga and anime culture. From Nakano, take the train to Harajuku and stroll along Takeshita Street, which is very popular with young people. Then head to Akihabara, a neighborhood known for its electronics stores decorated with colorful signs. More recently, the district has established itself primarily as a center of anime, manga, video game and cosplay culture. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 5 The colorful world of instant noodles in Yokohama

Your guide will pick you up at the hotel in the morning and take you to Yokohama. Located just 30 minutes from Tokyo, the city is the second largest and one of Japan’s most international cities. Nevertheless, Yokohama has not lost its tradition and is a relaxed alternative to the strident Tokyo. Young Japanese especially appreciate the selection of extraordinary museums to explore here. There is the Hara Model Railway Museum, home to the world’s largest model railway exhibition. A visit to this museum is like a small trip around the world. The Cup Noodle Museum, the world’s first gastronomic theme park, offers exciting insights into the history of instant noodles. In various workshops, you can even put together your own instant noodles. At the Noodle Bazaar you can taste noodles from eight different countries. Not far away is Yamashita Park, Japan’s first coastal park. You may have the opportunity to see one of the giant cruise ships arriving at the pier. The tour ends with a short boat ride from the park to Yokohama Train Station, from where you’ll take the train back to Tokyo. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 6 The mountains and volcanoes of Hakone

Without guidance

It’s time to leave Tokyo behind and so you’ll take the train to Hakone. The Hakone region lies in the middle of the mountains and volcanoes of Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park on the shore of a lake. The residents of Tokyo like to come here to escape the hustle and bustle of the capital. Your overnight stay is something very special today: you are a guest in a ryokan. The typical Japanese inns were already built in the Edo period (1603 – 1868). Today, these are found especially outside of larger cities. A stay here is especially worthwhile to experience Japanese hospitality. Among the highlights of a stay at Ryokan are meals. Tonight you will enjoy a multi-course Kaiseki dinner. At the end of the day, treat yourself to a relaxed bath in private open-air onsen before you rest on your futons. Overnight in Hakone.

Day 7 With the pirate ship over the Ashi-no-ko lake

Without guidance

Spend today exploring Hakone with your included transport pass. This includes a large number of different transport options in the surrounding area. You have the opportunity to take a tour of the mountains with the Hakone Tozan Switchback train and enjoy a magnificent view of the valleys at your feet. Then take the cable car standing in the Guinness Book of Records up to Mount Owakudani, past sulphur smoke plumes, hot springs and rivers. Up on Mount Owakudani, one of Hakone’s culinary specialties awaits you: Kuro-Tamago, an egg hard-boiled in the hot sulphur springs. Downhill, it is best to take the gondola cable car to Togendai. Be sure to take a boat trip over the Ashi-no-ko, a crater lake that offers you beautiful views of the surrounding peaks. Overnight in Hakone.

Day 8 - Go to Kanazawa

Today we continue to Kanazawa, which is located on the coast of the Japanese Sea. In the 17th century, this beautifully preserved town was one of Japan’s most prosperous regions and a center for art and culture. Upon arrival, you will have some time to explore the city on your own. Overnight in Kanazawa.

Day 9 Visiting the “City of the Samurai”

Your first destination is the Kenroku-en Garden, one of the three most beautiful gardens in Japan. Nearby is Kanazawa Castle, an absolute must-see in the city. Around noon, stroll through the Omicho Market, known throughout Japan for its fresh seafood and delicious sushi. Your guide will take you to a restaurant, where you’ll fortify yourself with a delicious lunch for the afternoon program. Here you have two options to choose from. On the one hand, you have the opportunity to visit Morihachi, a candy shop that has existed for almost 400 years. On the other hand, there is the opportunity to visit the studio of a master of gold leaf crafts. This will show you how to make your own chopsticks. With your very personal souvenirs in your pocket, you will finally visit the former house of the Samurai Nomura, which is located in the old town. It is known for its elegant tatami rooms, separated with sliding walls. The highlight can be reached by stepping outside on the porch into the garden – a complete Japanese garden with all the typical characteristics. Overnight in Kanazawa.

Day 10 True Japanese: Tea Ceremony in Kimono

You will continue by train to Kyoto – Japan’s cultural capital. For over a thousand years the imperial court lived here, and even today the numerous palaces, shrines and gardens make the city a cultural center. Unlike many other cities, Kyoto has been largely spared war damage. As a result, the city was able to preserve its unique cultural heritage and is still considered an epitome of Japanese culture today. Here is another highlight waiting for you. Upon arrival at the hotel, you will meet your guide and from there make your way together to one of Kyoto’s typical wooden houses (Machiya), which has been beautifully restored. Then you can try on a traditional Japanese kimono under expert guidance. Dressed in style Japanese, you will now be introduced to the high art of tea ceremony. You will learn about their close connection with Buddhism, the meaning of each gesture during the ceremony and of course how the tea is prepared and served. Afterwards you will get a short, fascinating introduction to the paper folding art, the origami. The rest of the day is free to discover Kyoto. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 11: The Secret World of Ninja and Her Sword Dance

Today, a little adventure awaits you. Ninja, also known as Shinobi, were spies and assassins in Japan. You will enter the secret world of the ninja and get a glimpse of her fighting techniques and mysterious abilities. After arriving at the meeting point, slip into a ninja’s traditional training clothes. To calm the mind and focus all the senses, start as the ninja did with a meditation exercise. Then you will be introduced to the combat exercises and surprise techniques. In the late afternoon, meet with a local expert who will introduce you to samurai culture. The lifestyle of the samurai and the traditional Kenbu swordfight have always exerted a great fascination. After an impressive show, slip into traditional clothes and marvel at the arts of a samurai kenbu master. In a small trial course, you can then prove your talent for sword dance. Finally, you will receive a "Samurai Certificate", which will remind you of this impressive experience for a long time. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 12 Per rickshaw through the bamboo forest

At the beginning of the day, ride your English-speaking rickshaw driver through the fascinating Arashiyama district. Your tour starts at the iconic Togetsukyo Bridge, one of Arashiyama’s most striking landmarks. Enjoy the view of the bridge and the calm Katsura River before exploring the famous bamboo forest. Then get into a traditional rickshaw. You will also pass the Nonomiya Shrine, which plays a central role in the “Sage of Genji” – the world’s first novel. The two-hour rickshaw ride ends at Togetsukyo Bridge, where you’ll say goodbye to your driver and return to Kyoto city center. There you will meet your guide and visit the impressive Nijo Castle. Afterwards you will stroll through the Nishiki market, known as “Kyoto’s cuisine”. Here you have the opportunity to taste local specialties. The tour ends with a walk to the Teramachi shopping street, which is lined by hundreds of shops – a great opportunity to browse and souvenir shopping. Your German-speaking guide will show you interesting places and give you recommendations for lunch before saying goodbye. An individual visit to the Kyoto International Manga Museum is optional afterwards, and then return to your hotel. Overnight in Kyoto.

13th day Kyoto according to your mood

Without guidance

After the fun-filled exploration tours with your guide, today is free to explore Kyoto as a family on your own. We are happy to offer you the two excursions listed below.On request, of course, we also organize further excursions and activities according to your wishes. Overnight in Kyoto.
Optional: Classic city tour in Nara
With a private guide, discover the cultural highlights of the former imperial city of Nara, including Todai-ji with the great Buddha, Isui-en Garden and Kasuga Taisha Shrine. Encounters with the tame deer in Nara Park and a walk through the historic district of Naramachi round off the excursion.
Optional: Culinary excursion to Lake Biwa with farm tour and mochi production
At Lake Biwa, you will get to know traditional country life with a farming family and make Japanese mochi towels yourself. A homemade chicken sukiyaki lunch and idyllic landscape make this excursion a special treat.

Day 14 Last stop: Osaka

Climb once again in the Shinkansen, which will take you to Osaka, the culinary capital of Japan. After arrival, you will have some time to explore the city independently. In the late afternoon, your evening whirlwind tour of Osaka awaits you. Your English-speaking guide will take you on a “food tour” where your taste buds will get their money’s worth. There is no city where you could get to know the extravagant and colorful world of Japanese food better. The city’s motto is “kuidaore” or “food until you drop.” Your first stop will take you to a street food stand where numerous traditional Japanese snacks are waiting for you. For example, how about “Menchi Katsu”, a fried pie made of minced meat? “Sake”, the national drink, invites you to be tried. After tasting a selection of Japanese wines, your tour will take you to a restaurant specializing in the preparation of Wagyu beef. Let the evening end in a small Tempura restaurant. Strengthened you stroll through the district with its countless small bars and immerse yourself in Osaka’s nightlife. Overnight in Osaka.

Day 15 Farewell to Japan

Today your journey through Japan ends. Enjoy a lot of impressions and experiences, and your driver will take you to the airport in time. Come home well!
