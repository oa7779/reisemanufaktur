Nature journey along the Silk Road

China Silk Road Journey: Experience a unique nature journey along the Silk Road with Asia Special Tours. Ask them in a moment.

Follow one of the most famous trade routes in the world and discover the fascinating landscapes along the Chinese Silk Road. Between spectacular deserts, hidden lakes, colorful rock formations and millennia-old cultural treasures, you will experience the entire diversity of Northwest China. The massive dunes of the Badain-Jaran Desert, the “Singing Dunes” of Dunhuang and the colorful Danxia mountains form the impressive backdrop of this extraordinary journey. Be inspired by the vastness, silence and mystique of these landscapes and immerse yourself in the history of a trade route that connected East and West for centuries. The crowning conclusion is Jiayuguan, the former gateway to the Silk Road and western terminus of the Great Wall.

Day 1 kick-off on the Yellow River

Welcome to China! At the airport you will be warmly welcomed by your guide and driven to your hotel. Your journey begins in the provincial capital of Lanzhou, which is located in a basin on the upper reaches of the Yellow River. Once a major station along the Silk Road, today it is an important transport hub between northwest and central China. Upon arrival, take a stroll along the Yellow River and visit the Yuan Dynasty White Pagoda as well as the Iron Bridge built by a German company in 1907, which remains the city’s landmark to this day. For lunch, taste the famous hand-drawn Lanzhou beef noodles. Overnight in Lanzhou. 

2nd Day Stone Forest on the Yellow River

Today you take an excursion to the impressive landscape zone of the stone forest on the Yellow River, which you reach after a two-hour journey by car. First of all, you get an overview of the vast landscape zone on a shuttle bus (together with other guests). Then take a boat trip on the Yellow River and admire the extraordinary rock formations that rise on both sides of the river. On the east bank, a hike leads through a picturesque gorge, whose imposing rocks and bizarre rock forms always open up new perspectives. Finally, you have the opportunity to take the cable car up to a viewpoint. From there you can enjoy a spectacular panoramic view over the Yellow River and the striking rock landscapes. Overnight in Lanzhou.

Day 3 After Zhangye and the Matisi Grottoes

In the morning, take the fast train to Zhangye. Then take a trip to the impressive Matisi Grottoes, located on the west bank of the Mati River in the northern foothills of the Qilian Mountains. The cave complex comprises around 70 Buddhist cave temples with more than 500 statues and valuable murals. Among the most famous facilities are the Thousand Buddha Caves and the Gold Pagoda Monastery. The temples scattered over a vast area blend harmoniously into the impressive mountain landscape and provide a fascinating insight into the religious and cultural history of the region. The complex owes its name to the Mati Monastery, the “Horse Hoof Monastery”. According to legend, a divine horse left a hoofprint in the rock here, which is still venerated today. Overnight in Zhangye.

Day 4 - Wonders of Colour in the Danxia Geopark

Today you can look forward to one of the most spectacular natural wonders in China. The colorful landscape of the Danxia Geopark near Zhangye seems almost surreal and makes even photographs seem unreal. The red sandstone formations, whose name Danxia literally means “red rock”, form a unique hill and mountain landscape reminiscent of solidified waves. Depending on the incidence of light, the layers of rock shine in a wide variety of red, orange and yellow shades and look as if they have been painted artistically. The characteristic rock formations are among the most beautiful examples of Danxia landscapes found in various regions of China. During a hike through the Geopark, new perspectives on this fascinating work of natural art open up again and again. Do not forget your camera, because the photo opportunities are among the most impressive in the country. Overnight in Zhangye.

Day 5 Fascination with the Gobi Desert

Today, a real adventure awaits you. After breakfast, you will first take the car to Youqi. From there, continue your trip by jeep to the fascinating Badain-Jaran Desert, part of the Gobi desert system and known for its spectacular landscapes. Huge sand dunes, some of which are among the highest in the world, rise from a seemingly endless sea of dunes. A special natural phenomenon are the numerous lakes, which lie like green and turquoise oases between the golden sand mountains and form an impressive contrast to the barren desert landscape. During the journey, magnificent views of this unique scenery open up again and again. After a relaxing lunch break at one of the idyllic lakes, continue your journey through the desert and finally reach the secluded Badain-Jaran Monastery. The Lamaist monastery was built in 1755 and nestles picturesquely on a lake at the foot of mighty dunes. The peaceful atmosphere of this spiritual place is an impressive conclusion to a day full of extraordinary nature experiences. Overnight with a nomadic family.

6th Day Sacred Lakes and High Sand Peaks

In the morning, take the Jeep past the “Sacred Lake” Yingderithu deep into the fascinating dune landscape. Their destination is the highest sand peak in the world, whose enormous dune rises about 430 meters above its surroundings. The climb requires some endurance, but the view from above compensates for all efforts: in front of you spreads an impressive panorama of golden dunes and glittering lakes, which lie like oases in the expanse of the desert. Then continue your journey to Lake Northu, the largest lake in the Badain-Jaran Desert. The deep blue water forms a fascinating contrast to the mighty sand mountains and gives the landscape an almost surreal beauty. In the afternoon, say goodbye to this unique desert world and travel by car via Zhangye to Jiayuguan. Along the historic Silk Road, you can expect the next exciting discoveries. Overnight in Jiayuguan.

Day 7 At the End of the Great Wall

Majestically, the fortress Jiayuguan rises against the backdrop of the vast desert landscapes of northwest China and forms the first highlight of your day. As the westernmost pass of the Great Wall, it marked for centuries the gateway between the Chinese Empire and the countries of Central Asia. Afterwards you will visit the so-called “hanging wall”. It owes its name to its steep course along the mountain slope, as a result of which it seems to float almost above the terrain from a distance. This section of the Great Wall was built as an extension of the defense system at Jiayuguan Pass to the north. Construction began in 1539. Of the original 1.5 kilometers, about 750 meters are still preserved today. The partially steep ascent is rewarded with magnificent views of the surrounding landscape and the fortress. Overnight in Jinyuguan.

8th day Buddhist treasures: the Mogao Grottoes

A new day, a new goal. You follow the course of the historic Silk Road through impressive sand dunes, barren stone deserts and surprisingly green oasis landscapes to Dunhuang, the former junction of the north and south route of the Silk Road. While caravans used to take several weeks for this route, today you can easily reach the oasis city within a few hours. Nestled between the Gobi and Taklamakan Deserts, Dunhuang looks back on more than 2,000 years of history. Here you will visit the famous Mogao Grottos, which are among the greatest cultural treasures of China. The unique cave temples document the development of Buddhist art, architecture and spirituality over centuries. Of the once around 1,000 grottoes, 492 caves are still preserved today, which extend on several levels along a rock face. Inside are more than 45,000 square meters of colorful murals and around 2,450 Buddha statues. The work was created over a period of more than a thousand years. The Mogao Caves are considered the most important and best preserved Buddhist cave temples in the world and were declared a World Heritage Site by UNESCO in 1987. Overnight in Dunhuang.

Day 9 Singing sand dunes and the ghost town

Before sunrise, drive out to the famous Singing Sand Dunes that rise just outside the gates of Dunhuang. They owe their name to the mysterious sound that arises when sand avalanches slide down the steep dunes. Experience how the first rays of sun dip the sand landscape into warm shades of gold, orange and red and create a fascinating play of colors. Later, take a trip to the Yardang National Park, one of China’s most extraordinary landscapes. Over millions of years, wind and erosion have formed bizarre formations from the sedimentary rocks. The term “Yardang” comes from Uyghur and means “hills of clay”. Over an area of around 400 square kilometers, a landscape of impressive expanse and almost surreal beauty stretches. A special highlight is the so-called ghost town, whose formations modeled by the wind are reminiscent of the silhouettes of an abandoned metropolis. Take your time to let this fascinating natural backdrop affect you, because behind each formation lies a new perspective and a new history. Overnight in Dunhuang. 

Day 10: Farewell to China

Today we say goodbye. After days of adventure along the legendary Silk Road, you will be taken to the airport and board your return or onward flight. In your luggage, not only souvenirs travel, but also unforgettable memories of spectacular desert landscapes, thousands of years old cultural sites, Buddhist sanctuaries and the fascinating oasis cities of northwest China.
