The condiment is in contrast – every now and then when traveling. Imagine once in your life seeing the world from above, from the geographical roof that guards our earth, so to speak? Nature, grandeur, silence, spirituality. And then: gigantism, creativity, growth without taboos. On this journey, these contradictions complement each other. After visiting the vibrant Chinese mega-metropolis of Beijing and the old imperial city of Xi’an with the gigantic terracotta army, you will experience the autonomous region of Tibet, which will enchant you with its magical monasteries. The huge country also fascinates with its unique landscapes. As everywhere else in Tibet, the country shapes its people and so it is not surprising that Buddhism is at home here. The silence of Tibet invites you to pause and dwell.

1st day Arrival in Beijing and first explorations: the Summer Palace

Welcome to China! At Beijing Airport, you will be met and taken to your hotel. Arrive in peace, make yourself fresh and put your legs up. You have already noticed that Beijing is flirting and vibrating at the airport and on the way to the hotel. Before you turn to this side of the mega-city, the first thing waiting for you is a rendezvous with the relaxed side of the city. You will visit the Summer Palace, a true masterpiece of Chinese horticulture that will enchant you. The Summer Palace on the West Mountains near Beijing is the only surviving imperial garden. In it, the perfect dream of Chinese garden architecture is realized. A masterful ensemble of palaces and temples, pavilions and pagodas with a total of 3,000 rooms on 290 hectares. Let the mood affect you, then you will feel how the Chinese emperors and their court have lived. Overnight in Beijing.

2nd day Imperial magnificent buildings

Are you ready? Today we finally head to the center of Beijing! Their first destination is the Temple of Heaven from the Ming period (1368 to 1644). The highlight of this large complex is the round hall with its triple stepped blue roof. The building is meant to embody the cycle of time: the four main pillars stand for the seasons, the twelve inner pillars for the months and the twelve outer pillars for the double hours of a day. Continue to the Forbidden City. This magical place was the seat of the Chinese emperors of two dynasties – the Ming and the Qing – and for half a millennium was inaccessible to those who did not belong to the court. Today, the crowd of visitors is as overwhelming as the architecture, the colorfully painted palace halls, the pagoda-like roofs. Also impressive are the powerful names of the magnificent buildings: “Palace of Heavenly Purity” is called one temple-like complex, the “Gate of Highest Harmony” another, another gate in turn pays homage to the “divine military art”. In the Square of Heavenly Peace, finally, the clumsy life reigns. It's actually the largest place on Earth. Overnight in Beijing.

3rd Day Once standing on the Great Wall

Get ready for a powerful encounter. The Great Wall is waiting for you! To experience them far away from the tourist crowds, travel to Gubeikou’s Great Wall, some 130 kilometers from Beijing. This section was once built along a mountain range to protect the gateway to the capital from the Mongols and Manchurs. Along with the sections at Shanhaiguan and Juyongguan, Gubeikou was central to defense against incursions from the north. Of the wall with its watchtowers, fortresses and other military buildings are today mainly ruins left, because this section was not restored. It is therefore still beautifully authentic and surrounded by a fantastic landscape. The view of the curved wall winding over the ridges of Yanshan is simply breathtaking! Explore this gigantic structure on a two- to three-hour hike and enjoy the view. After this unique experience, you will return to Beijing. Overnight in Beijing.

4th Day Imperial Xi'an

This morning you will be taken to the train station, from where you will depart by express train to Xi'an. The former capital of eleven dynasties was once the cultural, economic and political center of the Middle Kingdom. Today it is the seat of government of Shanxi Province and the largest city in northwest China. After a short break in the hotel, while strolling through the old town you can admire the bell and drum tower. The 2.5-ton bell, which dates back to the 15th century, can now be admired in the northwest corner on the stone base. In the evening, visit the modern district of Grand Tang Everbright City. It is especially impressive after dark when spectacular light shows, ornate installations and lively historical performances illuminate the streets. Here, traditional architecture blends with cutting-edge technology, immersing you in Xi’an’s historical heritage. For dinner, we recommend tasting the regional speciality Jiaozi – delicious pasta bags with a variety of fillings. Although Jiaozi are popular throughout China today, they have their origin and perfect preparation in Xi'an. Overnight in Xi'an.

Day 5 The Emperor's clay warriors & bike ride on the city wall

Look forward to another cultural highlight of your trip to China: the Terracotta Army. Immediately in the morning, the trip goes to Lintong, about 35 kilometers east. There are the famous terracotta warriors in line. Discovered by chance in 1974, the gigantic tomb at the foot of Li Mountain was built by the first emperor Qin Shihuangdi. So far, archaeologists have excavated around 7,200 figures of soldiers on foot and horseback. Remarkable: All these earthen warriors are individually designed, no two are alike – an artistic masterpiece! It represents a complete army of that time. Since the troops have been deployed in battle order, their localization can be approximately reconstructed. Therefore, it is certain that there are still other sculptures on the huge site, which has only been exposed to about a quarter so far. Back in Xi'an, you still have time to cycle along the imposing city walls. It is the only fully preserved city wall in all of China. Overnight in Xi'an.

Day 6 With the Qinghai Express on the roof of the world

Now it’s time to leave Xi’an behind and embark on a completely different adventure! With the express train you first travel from Xi'an to Xining. Here, your private guide will pick you up and take you to the Qinghai Express, which travels via Golmud to Lhasa. These are breathtaking 21,5 Hours of train ride, where you should fight for every minute of wakefulness – the backdrop is just so great. 1.956 The kilometer: The 814 The kilometer-long section from Xining to Golmud has already been 1984 put into service, to the remainder 1.142 Miles to Lhasa runs the train since 2006Because very few people can do it: they have a bed in the four-bed compartment of the 1Class. We recommend that you provide yourself with snacks and drinks to your taste before departure. Overnight in the Qinghai Express ()4he compartment.

Day 7: Lhasa, “Place of the Gods”

Do you know that? Sometimes you just don’t want to arrive. This is the way you are going today. In the morning, your train will reach the Northeast Tibetan region of Amdo. From here, the journey leads through deserted expanses, usually at over 4,000 meters altitude. With a vertex of 5,072 meters, the Tibetan Railway is the highest railway line on earth. You will cross the 4,837-meter-high Kunlun Pass and the first Yangtze Bridge, passing the highest railway station in the world (Tanggula at 5,068 meters) and the highest tunnel (4,905 meters). The train can reach a top speed of 160 km/h on the route from Xining to Golmud, and 120 km/h from Goldmud to Lhasa. If you get off in Lhasa in the evening and are welcomed by your personal guide, you will probably need something to come down from the speed and experiential noise. Overnight in Lhasa.

Day 8: Tracing the Faith: The Barkhor Pilgrimage

Today you can start the day in peace. A leisurely walk along the famous Barkhor pilgrimage route awaits you, which is the spiritual center of Lhasa. The trail runs around St. Jokhang Temple, the most important sanctuary of Tibetan Buddhism. The Jokhang Temple, built in the 7th century, houses the precious Jowo statue – one of the most sacred Buddha depictions in Tibet. As you walk through the temple, you can soak up the deep spiritual atmosphere and admire the ornate statues, paintings and carvings that grace this historic building. The devotion of the pilgrims who visit the temple gives this place a special spiritual power. Overnight in Lhasa.

9th Day Seat of the Tibetan God-Kings & Philosophy in Tibetan

At the sight of Potala Palace, the viewer automatically shudders: The Dalai Lama’s winter residence rises majestically on 13 floors over 100 meters into the sky. Behind the facade, 999 rooms are to be hidden. Hundreds of believers are already on their way early in the morning and are constantly turning their prayer wheels. The murmur and shuffling of thousands of steps sounds in the corridors, in the air waves heavy incense. Immeasurable riches open up: richly decorated relic stupas, perfect bodhisattvas and Buddhas, elaborate ceiling carvings and tower-high shelves with writings from distant centuries. Finally, you get to the roof, where an overwhelming panorama of the mountain ranges around and on the city opens up. After this goosebump experience, drive to Koster Sera, just outside Lhasa. The former state monastery was founded in 1419 by students of the Gelugpa Order and formed a monastic city populated by several thousand monks. Even today, it is known throughout Tibet for its excellent academy. Every day, young monks gather for “discussion lessons” to discuss theological questions with ritualized gestures and loud clap. An extremely interesting experience. Overnight in Lhasa.

10th Day Beauty in White & Summer Residence of the Dalai Lamas

This morning you will continue your introduction to the spiritual world of Tibetans by visiting one of the most important monastic sites in Tibet. The large, white structure of the Drepung Monastery looks like a pile of rice from a distance – that also means its name. In its heyday, Drepung Monastery was the largest monastery in the world with more than 10,000 monks. Today it is only 600, but due to its always well-maintained substance, it is considered one of the most important cultural assets in Tibet. Here are grave stupas of three Dalai Lamas. Speaking of the Dalai Lama, then visit the Dalai Lama’s former summer palace, called Norbulingka, set in the middle of a vast garden. It was built in the second half of the 18th century on the instructions of the 7th Dalai Lama and served as a summer residence for him and his reincarnations. Inside it is a spacious reception room, the walls of which are covered with beautiful paintings from recent history. Also admirable is the breathtakingly beautiful throne of the Dalai Lama. In addition, you can admire picturesque chapels, meditation rooms, the large reading hall and the private rooms of the Dalai Lama and his family. Overnight in Lhasa.

Day 11: On the Friendship Highway to Gyantse

On the Friendship Highway, which leads to Kathmandu, your journey will take you on to Gyantse. The journey takes you through one of the most spectacular and impressive landscapes in the world. Along the way, a detour will take you to a viewpoint near the sacred Yamdrok Lake, where you can picnic and get some legs. The turquoise blue lake, set in many color shades, is one of the four sacred lakes of Tibet and offers a truly spectacular sight. Via the Karola High Pass, which is over 5,000 meters high, you can finally reach the small town of Gyantse. More than in other cities, ancient Tibet has been preserved in Gyantse with its white houses and colored window cladding. Here you will visit the monastery complex Palkhor Chöde, an 18 religious and residential building in the old town. Impressive is especially the Kumbum, the only walk-in relic shrine that has survived the times. From the simple gods on the lower levels, one comes up to the Most High, the origin of all being. Whoever celebrates the stupa clockwise and shows his honor to the deities in each chapel of the four floors, can increasingly purify his consciousness during the ascent – at least faith. Overnight in Gyantse.

Day 12: Tashilunpo Monastery: the seat of the “Buddha of infinite light”

On the next leg on the Friendship Highway, travel on to Shigatse. There you will visit with the monastery Tashilunpo a significant attraction of Tibet. The monastery with the golden roofs represents the upscale Tibetan architecture and architecture. It attracts thousands of Buddhists every year, with around 600 monks, including as many as 100 novices, the monastery is one of the most important religious centers in Tibet. Tashilunpo is also the residence of the Panchen Lama. He is considered a reincarnation of Amitabha, the Buddha of Infinite Light, and is the highest Tibetan dignitary after the Dalai Lama. We also recommend visiting the free market, known for its Tibetan specialties – browse and buy a piece of Tibetan craftsmanship. Overnight in Shigatse.

Day 13 Visiting the "green goat"

In the morning you leave Tibet and fly on to Chengdu. The capital of China’s southwestern Sichuan province is China’s gateway to Tibet. The great past of the city is clearly noticeable in the numerous, well-preserved temples to this day. And yet the city seems young and alive. Here the old and the new China have come together harmoniously. Your private guide will pick you up at the airport and take you to your hotel, where you can refresh yourself briefly. Then, together with your guide, make your way to one of the many tea houses that shape the face of the city. Here you can enjoy the Chinese national drink in peace. Strengthened, it continues to Chengdu’s most important Daoist temple, the Qingyang Gong. Like many Daoist temples in China, it is rarely visited by tourists. The name translates as “temple of the green goat” or “temple of the green goat” and refers to the last words Laozi, the founder of Daoism. Accordingly, all who understood his teachings could find him in the market of the green goat. Afterwards, stroll a bit through the old town of Jinli with its beautiful small shops in traditional style that invite you to browse. Overnight in Chengdu.

Day 14: Cutty Panda Bears & the Great Buddha by Leshan

Would you like to see the cute panda bears, the living landmark of China, up close? Today you will have the opportunity to do so. The highly endangered species suffers from increasing urban sprawl and thus the destruction of its habitat. Several breeding stations for panda bears have been set up for protection in China, one of them about ten kilometers north of Chengdu. The breeding and research station for panda bears in Chengdu regularly has success in raising pandababys in captivity. Much to the delight of visitors. In a completely different way, however, fascinating is your next goal. You will drive to Leshan, where you will find the largest Buddha statue in the world ever struck from a rock: 28 meters wide and 71 meters high. Buddhist monks carved them into the rock face over a period of more than 90 years. The head of the giant figure alone is 14.7 meters long, 10 meters wide and provided with over 1,000 helical hair knots. During a boat trip on the Min River, you can admire the mountain with the Buddha statue in all its glory. At the end of the eventful day you will finally return to Chengdu. Overnight in Chengdu.

Day 15 Farewell to China

Even the most beautiful journey has to come to an end. Surely you have packed your suitcases with many great impressions. You can arrange the time until departure according to your ideas. Then you will be taken to the airport for your home or onward flight. We wish you a pleasant flight!
