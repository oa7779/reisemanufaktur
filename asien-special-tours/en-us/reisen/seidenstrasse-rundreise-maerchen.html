1st day Arrival in Tehran

Welcome to Iran. At Imam Khomeini International Airport in Tehran, you will be welcomed by your guide. Together you drive to your hotel. Depending on the arrival time, you can start with first sightseeings or rest a little at the hotel. Overnight in Tehran.

Day 2 Discoveries in the Capital

After a leisurely breakfast, you will embark on a city tour together with your personal guide today. A visit to Saadabad Palace (the first summer residence of the Qajarian kings) will allow you to share in the magic of the Orient. The extensive complex comprises 18 palaces: the white and green palaces are particularly worth seeing. The white palace is the largest with 54 rooms and 5000 square meters. In the green palace you can still admire the noble equipment of the Shah period. The Niavaran Palace is also worth a visit. Mohammed Reza Shah built it in the 60s in a modern style. At the end of the day you will stroll over the Grand Bazaar, which with its length of about 10 km and 30,000 shops and workshops is one of the largest in the world – almost a city within a city. Overnight in Tehran.

Day 3 Visit to the Pilgrimage City of Qom

Today you continue your visits to Tehran. Visit Golestan Palace (UNESCO World Heritage Site), Archaeological Museum (National Museum) and Museum of Jewelry and Carpet. Then leave the capital and continue on to Kashan. On the way, stop at the pilgrimage town of Qom. In the metropolis of Shia Islam, you will have the opportunity to feel the atmosphere in the sanctuary Fatima Masumeh. She belonged to the prophet family and the tomb, which was erected in her honor from 819 onwards, has been further expanded over the years, as well as lined with tiles and mirror mosaics. In the late afternoon you will reach the city of Kashan, on the desert edge of the large salt lake. Overnight in Kashan.
Note:: With Except for the Golestan Palace and the Jewel Museum, all museums in Tehran are closed on Mondays. The visits will be adjusted according to your travel date. Non-Muslims in Qom are not allowed access to the interior of the shrine, only an outside visit is possible. 

Day 4: Kashan's Citizen Houses

In no other Iranian city is there such a high density of citizen houses. Because Kashan was a city of rich merchants thanks to the carpet trade, rose water and ceramics production. Its spacious construction, which includes water basins and its own hammams, and the rich furnishings of the rooms are absolutely worth seeing and give an insight into the life of the Iranian bourgeoisie. Particularly magnificent is the house Boroujerdi with a typical Iranian garden. In addition, you will see the Sialk settlement mound with traces of settlement from the 7th millennium BC and the fin garden. It is said to be the oldest existing garden in Iran (from the 6th century BC). The car continues towards Isfahan. Along the way, visit the “red village” Abyaneh, whose mud-brick houses nestle on a slope. During a walk you can hear the villagers speak an ancient Persian dialect. A small mosque with a blue dome also invites you to visit. Then finally you reach the charming city of Isfahan – welcome in 1001 nights. Overnight in Isfahan.

5th day Cultural metropolis of Isfahan

A full day of climax is waiting for you! A visit to the cultural metropolis of Iran is best started at Imam Khomeini Square. It is the second largest place in the world – after Tiananmen Square in Beijing. Around him you can see the imposing Imam Mosque, the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque (also called Women's Mosque, since ladies were also allowed) and the Alighapoo Palace. Its name means "High Gate", because the palace forms the entrance to the palace district, which is located west of the square. Afterwards you will stroll comfortably over the legendary bazaar, where you can try your luck in trading and haggling. Let the day end relaxed! How about a visit to an enchanting tea house? Overnight in Isfahan.

Day 6: Further visits to Isfahan

After an extensive breakfast, we continue with Isfahan, because you can not get enough of this city. Together with your guide, you will visit the historic Friday Mosque, a 1,000-year-old union of Islamic architecture and art. You will also visit the Christian Armenian Vank Cathedral in the district of Djolfa built by Shah Abbas the Great for Armenian settlers. After a break, we will head to the Audience Palace of the Kings, also known as the Forty Pillars Palace. An evening walk on the ancient bridges on the Zayandeh River rounds off the eventful day. Enjoy the special atmosphere on the river and mix with the locals. Overnight in Isfahan.

Day 7 In the desert city of Yazd

A new day, a new destination: you continue your journey towards Yazd. The journey continues along the desert edge. On the way, in Meybod you will visit Narin Castle and the renovated Karawanseri Robat with the cold store and a ceramic workshop. You will also pass the city of Nain, which is located on an important side vein of the former Silk Road. Worth seeing are the Friday Mosque and a traditional merchant’s house, which today houses an ethnographic museum – the House of Pirnia. Passing impressive mountain landscapes, it goes all the way to Yazd, one of Iran’s oldest cities and built in traditional clay construction. Overnight in Yazd.

Day 8: Following in the footsteps of the Zoroastrians

This day is first of all marked by the teachings of Zarathustra, a pre-Islamic religion based on three pillars: thinking good, speaking good and doing good. There are currently about 20,000 Zoroastrians living in Iran, many of them in Yazd. They travel to the “Towers of Silence”, where formerly open funerals of followers of the Zoroastrian faith took place. Then you will see the new Zoroastrian fire temple. It is said that the fire in its sacred interior has been burning for 1500 years. Back in Yazd, you’ll see the wind towers as well as the Friday Mosque with the tallest minarets in the entire country. Furthermore, you can marvel at the dome building with the grave mausoleums. Among other things, Sezed Rokn o Din, the builder of the Friday Mosque, is buried here. After a break, take a walk through the old town to Alexander’s prison and the sanctuary of the 12 imams. You should not miss a visit to a Zurkaneh, a so-called “powerhouse”. Here, the Iranian men practice in traditional martial arts exercises. Among other things, heavy wooden clubs are used as devices and the exercises are partly combined with dance-like inserts. Overnight in Yazd.

9th Day Old Persian City of Residence Pasargadae

After breakfast, leave Yazd and leave for Shiraz. Enjoy the varied ride and let yourself be driven by your impressions. In Abarkouh, visit the clay-built and basement cold store as well as the Friday Mosque. After a rest under the over 4000 year old lonely cypress of Abarkouh, the journey continues to Pasargadae, the residence capital of the founder of the Achaemenid Empire, Cyrus the Great. There you will visit the extensive complex and get an overview of this historic place. In the evening you will reach the magical city of Shiraz. Overnight in Shiraz.

Day 10 Persepolis & the Rock Tombs of Naqsh-e-Rostam

Today you take a trip to the Old Persian Persian (UNESCO World Heritage Site), the ancient Achaemenid capital of the Persian Empire, first built in 520 BC by Darius I. Even today, the ruins of the former palace city bear witness to its former splendor. After a detailed visit, you will travel to Naqsh-e-Rostam around noon and visit the necropolis of the Achaemenids, which can be seen in the form of rock tombs on the steep mountain slope. You will also visit the rock reliefs of Naqsh-e-Rajab. Then you will return to Shiraz, where you will take a walk in the park of the Koran Gate. Early in the evening, visit the garden with the grave mausoelum of Hafez, a well-known Iranian poet. Incidentally, Goethe has quoted some verses by Hafez in his work West-Eastern Divan (1819). Overnight in Shiraz.

Day 11 City of Roses and Nightingales

Many people associate Shiraz with two things: it is the city of poets and the city of gardens and parks. Start your visits today with a walk to the impressive Karim Khan Fortress, in the center of the city. Parallel to the fortress walls are the Vakil Karim Khan Zands residence buildings. The Vakil complex with the Vakil Mosque and the Vakil Bazaar is one of the most famous urban complexes of Islamic architecture in Iran. The domed Vakil bazaar is one of the most beautiful in the country and still offers a rich range of spices, textiles and crafts. The Shah-e-Cheraq Shrine is the third most important Shia sanctuary in Iran. The dome is built in bud form, typical of Shiraz, and the interiors are decorated with magnificent, sparkling mirror mosaics. In the Nasr-ol-Molk mosque, especially the colorful stained glass windows are worth seeing and in the Nasr-ol-Molk house the precious interior decoration from the Qajaren period. Of course, visit some of the city’s numerous gardens such as the Narenjestan Garden with the Zinat-ol-Molk House and the Eram Botanical Garden of Shiraz University. Let this impressive day end peacefully with a tea in one of the numerous tea houses. Overnight in Shiraz.

Day 12 Departure from Shiraz

Khoda hafez and goodbye! Today, unfortunately, it is time to say goodbye to this fascinating country. You will be picked up at your hotel and taken to the airport. With 1001 new impressions in your luggage, you'll start your journey home.
