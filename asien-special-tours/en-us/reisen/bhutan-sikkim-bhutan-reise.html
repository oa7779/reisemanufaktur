Bhutan and Sikkim Journey - In the Heart of the Himalayas

Bhutan and Sikkim Travel: Travel to the heart of the Himalayas with Asia Special Tours. Ask now and experience your individual journey.

Itinerary & services

English itinerary text is an offline machine translation of the German original for this team preview. Please confirm travel details with your advisor; the German source remains available through the language switch.

In the heart of the Himalayas, explore the fascinating testimonies of two kingdoms. Via the metropolis of Calcutta you penetrate into the former kingdom of Sikkim and into increasingly remote areas of the Himalayas. Along the way, you’ll see lush green tea plantations, the ruins of ancient palaces and venerable Buddhist monasteries. Finally, you will reach Bhutan, the last Buddhist kingdom on earth. Due to its remoteness from the outside world, the Bhutanese have preserved many traditions, so you will feel like traveling not only another country, but a different time.

1st day Arrival in Kolkata

Your journey into the heart of the Himalayan Mountains begins south of the mighty mountain range in the Indian metropolis of Kolkata, a city that is crowded, vibrant and rich in cultural heritage. Before the silence of the Himalayan Mountains captivates you, you will experience the hustle and bustle of an Indian city. During British colonial rule, Kolkata was known as the jewel of the East and until 1911 the capital of the country. Upon arrival, you will be expected at the airport and escorted to your hotel. Built in Victorian style, Oberoi exudes a luxurious ambience and impresses with excellent service and unique architecture. After a short rest break, you will embark on a first exploration of the city. You will start your tour at the Victoria Memorial, built entirely of white marble. Then walk over the colorful flower market. Your visit ends with a visit to the Order of Mother Teresa, the missionaries of charity. The Order serves the poorest of the poor and maintains homes for the dying, leprosy and AIDS sufferers and homeless people. In the evening, try the regional cuisine at a traditional Bengali dinner. Overnight in Kolkata.

2nd day onwards to Darjeeling

Today you leave the hustle and bustle of Calcutta behind and fly north to Bagdora. From here, you are only separated by an approximately two-hour drive from Darjeeling, which extends over 2000 meters over several kilometers along a steep mountain slope. Green hills, covered by plantations, characterize this landscape in the foothills of the Himalayan Mountains. Numerous tea gardens produce tea here with different character and aroma. Darjeeling, the capital of the region of the same name, enjoys worldwide fame due to its high-quality tea. The city attracts not only with its pleasant climate but also with the breathtaking panorama of the adjacent Himalayan mountains. Already on the way here you will visit a tea plantation and taste a cup of the famous Darjeeling directly at the place of origin. Overnight in Darjeeling.

Day 3 A ride on the "Toy Train"

Already at breakfast in your hotel you will enjoy a spectacular view of Kanchenjunga, the third highest mountain on earth. Watch the changing play of colors as the light of the morning sun is reflected in the snow-capped slopes of the rock massif. Then there is a trip to Ghoom. The Buddhist monastery, whose colorful facade can already be seen from a distance, lies at an altitude of almost 2500 meters and has worth seeing murals, old texts and a huge gilded statue of Maitreya Buddha. Here the friendly novices allow you a glimpse into your life. Don’t miss a trip on the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway in Darjeeling: to this day, the passenger train with the brocade-related seats is pulled by a steam locomotive through the beautiful mountain panorama of Darjeeling. It was established in Darjeeling after Tenzing Norgay and Edmund Hillary were the first people to climb Mount Everest to promote mountaineering as a sport. You will also visit the center of Tibetan refugees. Here you can watch Tibetan craftsmen make traditional items such as carpets, woolen boots, wood carvings and leather goods. Overnight in Darjeeling.

Day 4 Beer and Bollywood

In the morning you leave for the extreme north of India. Here lies the state of Sikkim, which was once an independent kingdom. Your ride will take you through tea plantations and further along the river Rangit. Along the way, make a stop at Dansburg Brewery, owned by famous Bollywood star Danny Denzongpa. Here you can convince yourself that the region produces not only the finest tea but also good beer. Finally, you reach Pelling. At a height of 2150 meters, the small town lies in the middle of beautiful nature against the background of the Kanchenjungas. Upon arrival, you will take an approximately one-hour hike to Sanga Choeling, one of the oldest monasteries in Sikkim. Overnight in Pelling.

Day 5 The Ruins of the Kingdom of Sikkim

In the morning, visit Phemayangtse Monastery, home of the Nyingmapa Order. After a short drive, you will reach the ruins of Rabdentse, the remains of the capital of the Sikkim Kingdom. From here, the rulers administered their mountain kingdom from 1670 to 1814, which was only incorporated into India in 1975. However, the second capital of the empire – Rabdentse – was already destroyed by Nepalese Gurkha troops in the early nineteenth century. From the remains of the former royal city you have a beautiful view over the valley and the Kanchenjunga mountain range. You round off the day with a walk in the Temi Tea Garden, which is known for the high quality of its tea. Then continue your journey to Ravangla. Overnight in Ravangla.

Day 6: Insights into life in the monastery

After breakfast, head towards Gangtok, capital of the former kingdom and current state of Sikkim. Along the way, visit the Buddhist Monastery of Rumtek, the largest monastery in the country with magnificent views of the city of Gangtok, as well as a nunnery. Your guide will be happy to help you with the translation if you want to know more about life in the monastery. For lunch, try the traditional cuisine of the Kingdom of Sikkim before drifting a little while visiting the market. Overnight in Gangtok.

7th Day Traditional Craftsmanship

Today is dedicated to the capital of the former kingdom of Sikkim. After visiting the monasteries of Enchey and Dro Dup Chen, you will get an insight into the craftsmanship of the region. You will visit the Institute of Traditional Arts and Crafts, where, among other things, handmade paper, wood carvings and bags are sold. In the afternoon, take a walk outside the city through the Saramsa Garden. The park is full of colorful orchids and various fruit trees. Overnight in Gangtok.

Day 8: Continue to Kalimpong

Today, after a relaxing morning, your journey will take you to Kalimpong, a quiet place at 1250 meters altitude in eastern Himalaya. Already the untouched landscape on the way there will inspire you. Kalimpong is famous for its orchid and tree nurseries: oaks, maples and chestnuts stretch as far as the eye can see. Overnight in Kalimpong.

Day 9 Exploring Kalimpong

The small town was once a flourishing trading center between Tibet and India. Through missionaries she underwent a clear Scottish influence for a long time. In addition, Tibetans and Nepalese settled here over time, and when visiting the place you will always come across the numerous cultural influences that unite in Kalimpong. You will visit a Tibetan school, an establishment where glass noodles are made using traditional Chinese techniques and the Scottish University Mission Institute. After lunch at the Roerich Museum, learn about Kalimpong’s peculiar connection with Western culture, art and religion. Overnight in Kalimpong.

Day 10: Travel to the Bhutan border

Today you will continue your journey along tea gardens to Chalsa, located on the edge of a nature reserve just a few kilometers from the border with Bhutan. Your ride will take you through tea plantations that stretch like green carpets as far as the eye can see. The fascinating beauty of nature compensates for the slightly longer ride. In the afternoon you will arrive at your hotel in Chalsa. End the day here with a bath in the pool or enjoy a soothing treatment with traditional healing techniques in the hotel's own naturopathy center. Overnight in Chalsa.

Day 11 Arrival in Thimphu

After eleven days in India, you cross the border to Bhutan today. At Phuntsholing you will drive through a typical Bhutanese border gate into the last Buddhist kingdom on earth. From here it is still almost 180 kilometers to the capital Thimphu. On your approximately six-hour journey to the next stop of your trip, you will already gather many impressions of this very special country. Overnight in Thimphu.

Day 12 Thimphu

Thimphu is located at an altitude of about 2320 meters on a hill in a fertile valley on the banks of the river Chhu Thimphu. It is one of the smallest capitals in the world and – besides the seat of government of the island state of Palau – also the only capital without traffic lights. City bustle or traffic chaos you will look for here in vain. For this you can find a lot of peace in the streets and alleys. On your tour of the city today you will visit the Textile Museum, where you can admire, among other things, antique garments of the royal family. From the temple castle Changangkha Lhakhang high above the city you have a beautiful view into the valley. Back in the heart of the city, visit the Institute of Traditional Arts and Crafts. During a painting lesson together with the students you will have the opportunity to try out some of the techniques yourself. In addition, you can explore the outside grounds of the Institute of Traditional Medicine or be impressed by the unique architecture of the fortress-like monastery Trashi Chhoe Dzong. The complex has served as the seat of government since 1952 and also includes the King’s Throne Room. Overnight in Thimphu.

Day 13 About the Dochula Pass to Wangdue Phodrang

In the morning, take a trip to Tango Monastery. After you have covered part of the distance by car, the path continues on foot through picturesque oak forests. In spring, the rhododendron is in full bloom here. The monastery dates back to the 15th century and was founded by a Tibetan lama. Today a monastic school is housed here. At a traditional ceremony, the monks bless you for a long life. In the afternoon you will continue your journey to Wangdue Phodrang. Your ride will take you through the village of Oesepang, which houses an experimental potato farm, and the village of Hongtso, where many Tibetans have settled, as well as the Dochu La pass, flanked at over 3000 meters by Bhutanese chantens and prayer flags. From a viewpoint, you have a wonderful view of the eastern Himalayan mountains on clear days. In the evening, visit the ruins of the Dzong by Wangdue Phodrang. When it was destroyed in a fire in 2012, the whole country mourned one of its most important historical buildings. Overnight in Wangdue Phodrang.

Day 14 The Fertility Stamp of the "Divine Mad One"

After breakfast, travel on to Punakha where you will visit the temple Druk Yangyal Lkakhang, which stands at the ridge of the Dochula Pass. You will have lunch together with the local residents in a farmhouse. Here you can also get insights into the culinary traditions of the country and the everyday life of its inhabitants while cooking together. Then visit the Temple of Fertility, Chimi Lhakhang. On foot, a small path will take you about 45 minutes through rice fields until you reach the 15th century sanctuary. It goes back to St. Drukpa Kinley, who received the nickname “Divine Mad One” for his unorthodox methods of propagate the Buddhist faith – through singing, humor and offensive behavior. He is said to have introduced in Bhutan, among other things, the practice of painting phallic symbols on the walls of the house to drive away evil spirits – this custom you will see many times on your way to the sanctuary. Pilgrims who visit the monastery are traditionally hit on the head with a 25 centimeter long wooden phallus. Overnight in Punakha.

Day 15 The Dzong of Punakha

The highlight of Punakha is its monastery fortress. Until 1955, the country was ruled from here. The unique Dzong is beautifully located at the confluence of the Po Chu and Mo Chu rivers and is popularly called the Palace of Merriment. After visiting the magnificent building, set off for a slightly longer drive to Paro, home to the country’s only international airport. On the way through small villages you can observe the rural bustle: many farmers offer their products on the roadside, so that you can taste fresh apples, peaches and pears at a stopover. Overnight in Paro.

16th day hike to the tiger nest

The small town of Paro is characterised by whitewashed houses, whose wooden windows are usually painted in an ornate manner. For a good overview of the history and culture of the Himalayan Kingdom, a visit to the National Museum Ta Dzong, which is housed in a former watchtower, is particularly suitable. A whole floor alone is dedicated to original stamps of the country, some of which are even made of silk. Afterwards, a highlight of your Bhutan journey is imminent: you take a hike through the impressive Berwelt to the “Tigernest Monastery” (Taktshang), which – 900 meters above the valley – nestles against the rugged rock faces of the Himalayas. The name comes from a legend according to which the second Buddha, Guru Padmasambhava, rode here on the back of a tigress to meditate. The place is a famous pilgrimage site for Buddhists and one of the most sacred places in the Himalayas. On your way back you will visit the 17th century Drukyel Dzong and the Kyichu Monastery. Overnight in Paro.

17th day return flight to Kolkata

Your time out in the Himalayas is coming to an end and you will fly back to Kolkata, from where you will go home tomorrow. Today there is still some time to explore the architectural highlights of the city on a walk with your guide. Then visit the Kalighat Temple, which is considered one of the most important pilgrimage sites in India. Once upon a time, even human sacrifices were offered here and still today goats are slaughtered here in honor of the goddess Kalighat. Overnight in Kolkata.

18th Day Home Travel from Kolkata

After two and a half weeks in the heart of the Himalayas, which will accompany you in your memories for a long time, you make your way home today. Your driver will take you to the airport for your return trip. Look forward to sharing your experiences and pictures with friends and relatives.
