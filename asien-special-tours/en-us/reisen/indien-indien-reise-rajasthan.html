India Journey - The Jewels of Rajasthan

India Travel - Experience the jewels of Rajasthan with India Expert Asia Special Tours. Here directly inquiries and individual advice.

Itinerary & services

English itinerary text is an offline machine translation of the German original for this team preview. Please confirm travel details with your advisor; the German source remains available through the language switch.

On no other trip you will experience the second largest state in India so up close and authentic. Start in the capital and explore Delhi before heading on to Mandawa in the Shekhawati region. Here you can admire the old Havelis with their gracefully beautiful frescoes. Other highlights are the scenic beauty of the Thar Desert, the golden desert oasis Jaisalmer, the Aravalligebirge, the Taj Mahal in Agra and much, much more. Enjoy an in-depth, varied and impressive journey that allows you to see not only magnificent facades, but also the life behind them. During your trip, stay in beautiful luxury camps, heritage, boutique and palace hotels, all of which exude their very special charm. You will almost feel yourself like a Maharaja or a Maharani from days gone by.

1st Day Arrival in India & Magical Water Show

Namaste and welcome to India! Your journey starts in Delhi, where you will be expected and driven to your accommodation upon arrival at the airport. Take a rest, have breakfast or relax by the pool of your beautiful 1903 Heritage Hotel, which boasts colonial charm. In the afternoon, meet your guide. You will visit Akshardham Temple, which is the world’s largest Hindu temple complex even in the Guinness Book of Records. After dark, a special highlight awaits you: a demonstration of the breathtaking Sahaj Anand water show – a fireworks display of light, water, music and drama. Enjoy this captivating presentation. Better your journey could hardly begin, right? Overnight in Delhi.

2nd day Old Delhi and New Delhi

After breakfast in the hotel, you will go on a journey through time in Old Delhi, where the Jama Masjid, one of the largest mosques in the world, testifies to Delhi’s past as the capital of Islamic India. Passing the Red Fort, the palace complex of the Mughals, your ride continues to the moonlight square – “Chandni Chowk” – one of the oldest and busiest market squares in the city. Experience the typical flair of an Oriental bazaar here: At Raj Ghat, where the father of the nation was cremated, you can pay homage to Mahatma Gandhi. In the afternoon, explore the imperial city of New Delhi with its vast avenues and imposing buildings. 1931 Designed by the British occupiers as a modern garden city, it now houses the Government District, the Presidential House and the National Monument India Gate, modeled on the Arc de Triomphe in Paris and commemorating the 1World War I was built. But New Delhi also offers fascinating historical sights: for example, with the 72 Metre-high Qutb minaret and the mausoleum of the great mogul Humayun, which is considered an architectural precursor to the Taj Mahal. Overnight in Delhi.

Day 3 Continue to the old trading town of Mandawa

From Delhi, head to Mandawa, an ancient trading town in the Shekhawati region. This was once on the Silk Road, where there was a lot of trade between China and West Asia. The resident merchants displayed their earned wealth with palatial country houses (Havelis). The frescoes and mosaics on the house walls tell of this heyday, which is why the region is often referred to as an “open-air art gallery”. A particularly beautiful example of the artfully designed Havelis Mandawas is also your hotel for the night, the magical Vivaana Culture Hotel. The Vivaana is a carefully restored 19th-century Shekhawati Haveli with stylishly furnished rooms and suites that reflect the charm of the old world. Both the exterior and interior impress with magnificent and rare artifacts and frescoes. Personal service and warm hospitality will make you feel at home. Overnight in Mandawa.

Day 4 Bikaner - former desert metropolis

Bikaner, the next stop of your trip, is also characterized by the richly decorated city residences of the merchants. The city still gives a very good impression of the medieval desert metropolis, which it represented as a station along the caravan road between Orient and West Asia. In the afternoon, take a trip to Devi Kund to the memorials of the Maharajas of Bikaner, which were created around an artificial lake. However, these memorial sites look more like serene summer pavilions because of their playful domed roofs of bricks, sandstone or marble and the richly ornamented freestanding columns that support the roofs. Extremely impressive is the white marble Chhattri by Maharaja Sardhul Singh. Later, you will learn about the daily business of India’s only camel farm. Overnight in Bikaner.

5th Day Curious Sanctuary: The Rat Temple Karni Mata

Today gives you the opportunity to explore the fascinating city further. The main attraction is the Junagarh Fort, built between 1571 and 1611, which was expanded over the years by 37 pavilions and temples. In the afternoon, take a trip to Deshnoke to the Rat Temple, Karni Mata. Its special attraction is thousands of rats that populate the temple. The animals enjoy complete protection within the sanctuary and are fed, venerated and worshipped by pilgrims and priests. The rodents are always full and therefore completely harmless – but since rats are not everyone’s thing, this excursion can of course also be canceled. Overnight in Bikaner.

Day 6 The “Golden City” Jaisalmer

Your journey will take you to the “Golden City” of Jaisalmer, located in the middle of the Thar Desert. It owes its name to the architecture of yellow-brown sandstone, which blends in color into the backdrop of the desert. Typical for Jaisalmer are especially the Havelis, former residential and commercial houses of wealthy merchants, as well as various temples of Jainism, a religion native to India that emphasizes the equality and dependence of all living beings on each other. After checking in at the hotel, you still have enough time for a first small walk through the city on your own. Overnight in Jaisalmer.

Day 7 Explore the most beautiful sides of Jaisalmer

Jaisalmer is not only known for its beautiful havelis, impressive temples and the mighty fort, but also for a very special treat: Ghotua Ladoo, small sweets shaped into balls from chickpea flour, ghee and water. Together with an English-speaking local, you will visit the Bhatia confectionery shop in the heart of the city. In the city’s oldest confectionery store, you’ll learn all about the process of making sweet balls, which spans six phases in total. If you want, you can also put on your own hand – and of course try the small treats. Together with your guide, you will then dedicate yourself to exploring the most beautiful sides of Jaisalmer. Your tour begins with the Salim Singh ki Haveli. Then make your way to the imposing Jaisalmer Fort, which is majestically perched on a hill above the city. Then explore the contemporary Jaisalmer with a stroll over the bazaars. Also a visit to the Patwon ki Haveli should not be missed. The complex consists of five magnificent merchant houses and was built between 1800 and 1860 by five Jain brothers. Overnight in Jaisalmer.

8th Day Continue to the luxurious Manvar Desert Camp

Your driver will take you to Manvar, where your luxurious camp awaits you in the middle of the Thar desert. Feel immersed in the fairy tales from 1.001 night and enjoy the magic of the place. Your exclusive and elegantly furnished tent offers you a lot of privacy, plenty of space and even your own dining tent where you can indulge in culinary pampering. Upon arrival, the rest of the day is freely available for you to relax, enjoy the tranquility or take a first excursion. For example, how about a walk through the nearby village? Overnight in Manvar.

Day 9 Experience the Magic of the Desert

Today you will learn that the desert is anything but barren. Your adventure begins with a Jeepsafari through the dunes of the Thar Desert, where you will stir up tons of sand, enjoy unique views over the surrounding landscape and get insights into life amidst this barren environment. Of course, you should not miss a camel ride. Because how better to experience the magic of the desert than from the back of one of these animals, with whom you inevitably connect the desert. Listen to the stories and stories of your English-speaking guide about this part of the Thar as your camel carries you through the desert. Overnight in Manvar.

Day 10 The “Blue City”

After the “Golden City” of Jaisalmer and the magical Thar Desert, your journey will now take you to Jodhpur, which is also called the “Blue City” because of the color of its houses. The place is dominated by the huge historical fortress Meherangarh, which lies on a 123-meter-high rock and offers a spectacular view over the bright blue tangle of houses at the foot of the hill. Considering the colossal dimensions, British writer Rudyard Kipling described this place as “the work of giants”. In the immediate vicinity is the marble mausoleum Jaswant Thada decorated with filigree ornaments. From here you have a beautiful view of the Rajputen Castle. After getting a glimpse of Jodhpur’s history in these buildings, experience the lively city life of today: from Meherangarh Fort, a tuk-tuk driver will take you to the center of Jodhpur. The blue labyrinth of the old town holds many surprises: Muslim dyers, puppet builders, traditional spice markets and much more. Overnight in Jodhpur.

11th day Visit of a Bishnoi village & traditional Bandhani art

Away from the hype of the city, you will catch glimpses of the everyday life of an Indian village on a Jeepsafari through the countryside – and one very special thing about it: The locals of the resident Bishnoi rural population have been active conservationists for centuries, are strict vegetarians and fight for the preservation of trees and the protection of animals – partly even at the risk of their own lives. The religious community, founded by Guru Jambheshwar in the 15th century after wars between Hindus and Muslim conquerors, drives all hunters and poachers out of their territories. In the afternoon, return to Jodhpur where you will be initiated into the art of Bandhani, a traditional type of batik. You will meet a family who have practiced this art for many generations and get an insight into the special binding and dyeing technique that is practiced especially by Muslims in Rajasthan and Gujarat. In Jodhpur alone, there are about 700 to 800 Muslim families who make a living from Bandhani art and have been supplying merchants and wholesalers with their products for centuries. Overnight in Jodhpur.

Day 12: Ranakpur to Udaipur

On the way to Udaipur, the next stop of your journey, you will make a stop at the Jain temples of Ranakpur, a temple complex marked by great attention to detail: a forest of columns supporting the dome decorated with intricate geometric patterns and figures of goddesses. The temple has 29 rooms and 1,444 columns, the architecture of which reflects the Jain myths and their symbolism. It is said that not a single pillar resembles another. Jainism is one of the original religions of India. His teaching is based on finding the way to liberation and happiness by living a nonviolent and renounced life. Afterwards you will continue the journey to Udaipur and will be taken to your hotel for the next two nights. Overnight in Udaipur.

Day 13 Udaipur – “Venice of the East”

Today is entirely dedicated to exploring Udaipur. Udaipur is dominated by its lakes – this is why the romantic city is also called “Venice of the East”. The royal past is reflected in the rich ornaments on the doors of houses in the Old Town, palaces, gardens and monuments. The highlight of Udaipur is its city palace: one part of the winding building complex is open to the public as a museum, another serves as a residence for the acting head of Mewar, another houses a luxury hotel. In the evening, you can see the city from a different angle on a boat trip over the artificially created Lake Pichola. During a stopover at Jag Mandir Palace, Hollywood feeling comes up: the building was the scene of such well-known films as "Tiger von Eschnapur" and James Bond's "Octopussy".An experience of a different kind awaits you at a course with an expert who brings you closer to classical Indian music. Today you will learn about the different types of this music and in particular its variety, because there is a large set of musical instruments, each more interesting than the previous one! Listen to the classical sounds and get a feel for Indian music. Overnight in Udaipur.

Day 14 Chittorgarh – Home of Rajasthan’s oldest fortress

In the morning, continue to Chittorgarh, where you will visit the oldest fortress in the state of Rajasthan. Even today, ruins tell of a time when fierce battles were fought here against the Muslim conquerors. You will also visit the stone Meerabai Temple and Padmini Palace. After visiting Chittorgarh, you will be taken to Pushkar, one of the most important pilgrimage sites in India. Spend the night at the Royal Jagat Palace. From the roof terrace of the luxury hotel, you can enjoy breathtaking sunsets with magnificent views of the spectacular snake mountain and the magnificent garden of the hotel. Let yourself be taken on a journey into the glamorous past and experience princely comfort. Overnight in Pushkar.

15th day Pilgrimage Place Pushkar and on to the “Pink City” Jaipur

The village of Pushkar is located on the sacred Pushkar lake, where many devout Hindus make pilgrimage every year to wash themselves clean and bless their family with health. These rituals are performed by resident Brahmins. The Brahman cult has a long tradition here: the dawn in Pushkar begins with the drums and bells of the Brahman Temple at four in the morning. Pushkar is home to one of India’s rare Brahma temples as well as around 400 smaller temples. Every year in October or November, the tranquil place of religious contemplation is also the scene of the largest known market in the world. After breakfast, your guide will show you the most beautiful sides of the enchanting place before continuing your journey. Your next destination is Jaipur, the fast-growing capital of Rajasthan state. In the late 19th century, the city was painted in shades of pink in preparation for the visit of the British king – in Rajasthan’s tradition, this is the color of hospitality. To this day, Jaipur is still called the “Pink City”. After arrival, the afternoon is at your disposal. Overnight in Jaipur.

Day 16: Mighty Amber Fort

Getting up early is worthwhile, as a true masterpiece of Mughal architecture awaits you: the imposing Fort Amber perched on a steep mountain slope north of Jaipur. Behind it you can expect the public audience hall, the magnificent Ganesha Gate and the Spiegelpalast. On the way back to the city, you will stop at the “Palace of the Winds”, behind whose ornate facade the ladies of the court observed the activity on the street. Jaipur himself also knows how to impress with magnificent architecture. You will visit the magnificent City Palace, which bears witness to an era of abundance and in part still serves today as the residence of the former Maharaja. However, the city has also preserved some craft traditions that have been passed down from generation to generation. You can observe this during a walk with your guide around Ghat Darwaza and Vishveshwar ji. During this special evening walk, you mix with the locals and try culinary delights such as Pakoras, Aloo Tiki, Samosas and sweets at the most popular street kitchen stalls in the city. Along the way, you’ll see silversmiths, jewelry makers and other local craft artists. Overnight in Jaipur.

17th day ghost town of Fatehpur Sikri

On your way to Agra you will visit the ruined city of Fatehpur Sikri, which is one of the most important testimonies of Mughal architecture. With red sandstone bricks, the Grand Mogul Akbar had his capital built here in the 16th century, but it had to be abandoned shortly after completion due to a lack of water. Nevertheless, it has been preserved almost ghostly to this day and combines two opposites: the figurative architecture of the Hindus and the geometrical architecture of Islam. Overnight in Agra.

Day 18 The Taj Mahal at Sunrise

Today it is finally time: you see the fairytale Taj Mahal with your own eyes. When early in the morning the fog hangs over the water basins of the garden and the sun rises slowly over the magnificent structure, the best time to visit the impressive monument is. In incomparable perfection and beauty, this “most expensive proof of love ever” built by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan for his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal still shines today in white marble. You will also visit the monumental Agra Fort, which is also a UNESCO World Heritage site alongside the world-famous Taj Mahal. Three generations of Mughal Emperors celebrated their power and glory there. But not only the building itself, also the view from here to the Taj Mahal is breathtaking. Afterwards, you will have the opportunity to admire some of the most beautiful Indo-Saracene buildings in northern India and get a glimpse of life and time during the reign of the British Raj. Overnight in Agra.   

Day 19 Back to Delhi

This morning you are on your way back to Delhi. On the way, stop at Sikandra where you will visit the tomb of the Mughal ruler Akbar. In the park, which surrounds the monumental red sandstone tomb, there are langurs, axish deer and deer goat antelopes. The Imperial Mausoleum was planned and started by Akbar himself and completed by his son Jehangir in 1605. Overnight in Delhi.

20th day Farewell to India

After three eventful and impressive weeks, it is now time to say goodbye to India, which has certainly delighted you with its beauty and richness of facets. With many exciting impressions of your journey in your luggage, you will be taken to the airport and take your return flight. Who knows, maybe one day you will return here.
