India Nature Tour - Mysterious Assam

India Nature Tour: Get to know India’s nature on an individual round trip with Asia Special Tours. Ask now without obligation!

Itinerary & services

English itinerary text is an offline machine translation of the German original for this team preview. Please confirm travel details with your advisor; the German source remains available through the language switch.

Assam is not only a type of tea, but an exciting region in the very northeast of India at the foot of the Himalayas. The cultivation of tea plays an important role here. However, the state has far more to offer than lush green plantations. You’ll find impressive ruins of the once-mighty Ahom Empire, the originality of Majuli – one of the world’s largest river islands – and insights into the country’s fascinating wildlife. The Kaziranga National Park offers a unique habitat for water buffaloes, elephants, tigers, leopards and especially Indian rhinos. Round off your journey through the peaceful Assam with a four-day river cruise on the Brahmaputra. On board the spacious and comfortable private houseboat MV Rudra Singha you can relax and get to places along the river that are hidden to most Indian travelers.

1st day Arrival in Jorhat & on to Kaziranga National Park

Your journey starts in Jorhat, where you are already expected when you arrive at the airport. From here you will immediately continue to the Kaziranga National Park. More than 75% of the population of the large Indian rhino lives in Kaziranga National Park, which also offers a unique habitat for many other species such as water buffaloes, elephants, tigers and leopards. Various species of monkeys and birds also frolic in the trees. Your accommodation for the next three nights is Diphlu River Lodge, located on the edge of the National Park, which blends beautifully into the countryside. Here you will enjoy an incomparable intimacy with nature. The twelve reet- and bamboo-covered cottages are inspired by the simple architecture of the Mising ancestral houses and furnished with handmade fabrics and furniture. Diphlu River Lodge is committed to responsible tourism, always putting nature, wilderness and local communities first. Overnight in Kaziranga.

Day 2 Varied Day in Kaziranga National Park

Nature is calling! Immerse yourself in the fascination of the wilderness and its inhabitants. You can explore the park with guided, private jeep safaris, private hiking safaris and an elephant ride (together with other guests) from very different perspectives. At dusk, you will finally return to your lodge. Overnight in Kaziranga.

Day 3 On more Jeep safaris in Kaziranga National Park

Today you go morning and afternoon on Jeep safaris in the national park. Experience the untouched nature up close and enjoy the stay in this very special place. It is best to always keep your camera ready, many of the shy animals show only briefly. Overnight in Kaziranga.

Day 4 Start of your river cruise on the Brahmaputra

After breakfast at the lodge, make your way to Silghat, where you board the charming houseboat MV Rudra Singha. Your exclusive river cruise on the Brahmaputra begins. The MV Rudra Singha is very spacious and tastefully furnished, taking you to places along the Brahmaputra that are hidden from most Indian travelers. The houseboat first sets course for the temple city of Vishnath, which is known for its beautiful Shiva temples from the Ahom period. After a tour of the city, return to your boat and continue upstream to Dhansiri Mukh. Enjoy a delicious lunch on board and relax on the sun deck as your houseboat glides past gloriously green landscapes. Dinner is also served on board. Overnight on board MV Rudra Singha.

Day 5 Insights into Rural Life

Spend another day on board your comfortable houseboat. If time permits, stop at a local riverside village for a glimpse of rural life in Assam. Overnight on board MV Rudra Singha.

6th day Tribal villages and Vishnu cult on the island of Majuli

Today you will reach the river island of Majuli. In the 16th century, the approximately 400 square kilometer island was the center of Assamese culture. Today, about 170,000 inhabitants live on Brahmaputra Island. Most of them in small villages. Many animal and plant species also find a safe habitat here, as the island is still relatively untouched by tourism. Accordingly, of course, are the landscapes that you expect on the island. Among the cultural features of the island are the monasteries – Satras – of Vishnu worshippers, who settled in Majuli as early as the 15th century. You will visit the Hindu Vaishnava Monastery of Auniati Satra as well as some villages of Mising (or Mishing), an ethnic group whose stilt house settlements line the shore of Brahmaputra. The Mising are followers of the Donyi Polo faith and worship the sun and moon as deities. Many of them also adopted the Vishnavite religion, and then travel to Neamati Ghat on the opposite bank of Brahmaputra. Overnight on board MV Rudra Singha.

7th day of pottery in Majuli

In the morning, return to the island of Majuli for another monastery. Here you can very nicely observe the way of life of the monks and their students. Also take the simple lunch in one of the monasteries. Well strengthened you will then visit a pottery village, where you can look over the shoulders of the potters at work. In the evening twilight, you will finally return to your boat with many new impressions. Overnight on board MV Rudra Singha.

Day 8 Testimonies of the Ahom Empire

After breakfast, it is time to say goodbye to your floating hotel. You drive on to Tinsukhia by car. Along the way, visit the Ahom monuments and temples in Sivasagar, which will take you on a journey back in time. Sivasagar, the former capital of the once so powerful Ahom empire, offers exciting archaeological sites. You will visit Rang Ghar, one of the oldest remaining amphitheaters in Asia, where the King and his officers watched indigenous games such as wrestling, bird and buffalo fights and the like. The beautiful park nearby invites you to stay. You will also visit the impressive Talaltal Ghat Palace. Of its seven floors, four are underground – an extraordinary architectural concept. Also the huge water reservoir in Sibsagar should not be missed. On its shore stand three temples, the most important of which – Sivadol – is crowned by a golden dome almost 2.5 meters high. It is one of the most important Shiva temples in the country. Towards evening you will reach your accommodation: the simple but historic Wathai Heritage Bungalow. The bungalow, furnished with beautiful old furniture and located in the middle of a green oasis, once served as the residence of the plantation owner and looks back on more than a hundred years of history. Overnight in Tinsukhia.

9th day Various bird life in Dibru-Saikhowa National Park

Today, your journey will take you to Dibru Saikhowa National Park, known for its scenic beauty and bird richness. Get to know the diverse birdlife on jungle walks and private boat trips on the Dibru River, while also looking for the rare Ganges dolphins. The Dibru Saikhowa National Park is home to over 300 bird species, which in twa corresponds to a quarter of all bird species found in India. This also includes the so-called “Big Four”: the Jerdon Schmätzer, the Schwarzkehl parrot ice cream, the Swampschmätzer and the Jerdon’s Schwätzer. For rare species such as Malay duck, marble beard and Bengal vultures, the National Park is one of the last refuges. With a bit of luck, you can also observe pig deer, Sambar deer, leopards, fog paddles, Eurasian otters, cap langure, rhesus monkeys, Assam macaques or a specimen of the Chinese scale animal. In addition, the National Park is home to one of the most important populations of the Asian elephant and is one of the few protected areas where wild water buffaloes live. You might even see some feral horses that probably escaped during World War II. The king of the jungle, the Bengal Tiger, on the other hand, you will probably only get to see with a lot of luck. It is estimated that only about 30 tigers live in Dibru-Saikhowa. Overnight in Tinsukhia.

10th Day Oil City Digboi & Buddhism in Namphake

Interesting in a completely different way is the city of Digboi that you visit today. Digboi, the first oil city in Asia, is still dominated by the petroleum industry and is said to owe its name to the command "Dig-boy-dig!" which the original founders gave their workers to dig for oil. The city has the second oldest oil refinery in the world, some of which is still in operation. You can learn more about the history of the city and oil extraction in the Oil Museum, the oldest oil museum in India. The exhibition of the small museum shows how oil production began in the jungles of East India. In addition, you will visit the soldier's cemetery, which is dedicated to the fallen in 2World War I commemorated against the Japanese who tried here to conquer Digboi from Burma. 200 The graves are permanently maintained by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission. In the beautiful village of Namphake on the banks of the river Buridihing you will get a glimpse into the life and traditions of tai pitches. The Tai-Phaken are Buddhists who have retained their traditions and customs to this day and still reside in traditional stilt houses. Also visit the monastery in Namphake before returning to Tinsukhia. Overnight in Tinsukhia.

Day 11 Farewell to India

Today it is already time to say goodbye to the mysterious East of India. Many more impressions that the Indian state has on the Brahmaputra, you will be taken to the airport in Dibrugarh and take your onward or return flight.
