Japan Travel - Gardens, Art and Architecture

Japan Cultural Tour: Experience the impressive culture of Japan on your individual round trip with Asia Special Tours. Ask now!

Japan is a true paradise for architecture and art lovers: from millennia-old, filigree shrines to imposing castles and aesthetic gardens to modern glass fashion temples, you will find everything your heart desires here. The contrasts between old and new are impressive and yet fit into a harmonious overall picture. Take a journey through the history of Japanese art and architecture. You’ll visit some of Tokyo and Kyoto’s best museums, stroll through Japan’s most beautiful gardens, explore the art island of Naoshima, and take a look from the world’s tallest city hall. Depending on your personal interest, you can also expect a traditional Kabuki show and an exciting photography tour. You will also spend the night in special hotels and ryokans, which will delight you with their individual design and state-of-the-art comfort. All around a dream for every architect!

Day 1 Welcome to Japan

Your journey begins in Tokyo, where you will be welcomed and warmly greeted by an English-speaking assistant. This accompanies you to the vehicle available for you, which brings you to your hotel. The English-speaking driver will be happy to tell you first anecdotes and interesting facts about the city during the ride. The rest of the day is at your disposal: time to make first explorations on your own in this breathtaking world metropolis. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 2 Tokyo – an architectural jewel

Start your exploration tour with a stroll through the Hamarikyu Garden. This impressive, once private garden of an Edo ruler fascinates not only for its scenic beauty, but also for the famous tea house, which is picturesquely located in the middle of the lake. In the hip Roppongi district, 21_21 Design Sight is an architectural masterpiece by renowned architect Tadao Ando and fashion designer Issey Miyake. Nearby is the National Art Center, which with its huge exhibition space is considered the largest of its kind in the country. This architectural jewel comes from the pen of visionary architect Kisho Kurokawa. From Roppongi you can walk to the noble shopping and residential districts of Omotesando and Aoyama. A real eye-catcher in Aoyama is the Prada flagship store, a glass building in diamond form. The next stop will take you to Yoyogi National Stadium, famous for its floating roof construction. This is where the 1964 Tokyo Olympics took place. Before returning to your hotel with many new impressions, visit the Meiji Shrine. Built in honor of Emperor Meiji and his wife, the shrine was built at the beginning of the 20th century and is surrounded by around 120,000 trees from 365 different species. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 3 Tokyo's Colorful World of Museums

Without guidance

Having already got an impression of the diversity of Tokyo yesterday, you can use today to discover exciting museums on your own. Start with the Nezu Museum, for example, and let yourself be fascinated by the variety of artwork ranging from Japanese calligraphy to Chinese ceramics, then enjoy a stroll through the enchanting Japanese garden. Nearby you will find the Mori Art Museum, located on the 53rd floor of the Mori Tower. Since its opening in 2003, the museum has become an absolute must for art lovers. After a stroll through the museum, you should definitely enjoy the breathtaking almost 360-degree views of Tokyo from the Mori Art Center Gallery. After all the impressive art, it’s time for a little break. Stroll through the bustling district of Azabu-Juban or sit down in one of the beautiful cafes. In the afternoon, more museums will attract you: At the Amuse Museum, you will discover unique art forms that can only be found in Japan, including ukiyo-e (woodcuts), textile coloring and painting. For lovers of Japanese woodcuts, a visit to the Sumida Hokusai Museum is recommended. Overnight in Tokyo. 

Day 4 Traditional Kabuki or rather a photography tour?

Use this day freely to spontaneously decide what you want. Do you long for even more modern art and fantastic architecture? A visit to the National Museum of Modern Art Tokyo should then definitely be program. Here, outstanding works of art from the 20th century are exhibited, accompanied by special exhibitions and film screenings. A highlight of the museum is its architecture, designed by renowned architect Yoshio Taniguchi, as well as the new design of the Museum of Modern Art in New York. Alternatively, the world’s tallest city hall in the Shinjuku district offers great views of the city and even Mt. Fuji with a clear view. Overnight in Tokyo.

Optional: Guided Kabuki Tour or Tokyo Night Photography Workshop
Experience a traditional theater performance at the famous Kabukiza Theater in Tokyo’s Ginza district of Kabuki, or let an English-speaking photographer take you to the hidden corners of the city that offer perfect photo opportunities and are rarely visited by tourists.

5th day Picasso in Hakone

Without guidance

With many colorful impressions of the art and architecture stronghold of Tokyo in your luggage, you can take the train to Hakone. The Hakone region, nestled in the mountains and volcanoes of Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park, lies on the shores of a quiet lake and is a popular destination for Tokyo residents who escape the hustle and bustle of the capital. Head straight to the Hakone Open Air Museum. Impressive sculptures combined with beautiful views of the surrounding valleys make the museum a real gem of the region. In addition, you have the opportunity to admire a considerable collection of Picasso’s works. Your overnight stay is very special today: you are a guest in a ryokan. The typical Japanese inns were already built in the Edo period (1603 – 1868). Staying here is especially worthwhile to experience Japanese hospitality. Among the highlights of a stay at Ryokan are meals. Tonight you will enjoy a multi-course Kaiseki dinner. At the end of the day, treat yourself to a relaxing bath in private onsen before bed for rest on your futons. Overnight in Hakone.

Day 6 The mountains and volcanoes of Hakone

Meet your guide at the property today and embark on an exploration tour of Hakone together. Your tour starts with a cruise over the Ashi-no-ko, a crater lake that offers you beautiful views of the surrounding peaks. At Hakone-machi you leave the ship and continue on foot to Hakone Shrine. This is one of the most photographed places in the region, as the red Torii Gate seems to float almost on the water. A small hike along the protected ancient Tokaido trail takes you from the shrine to the traditional Amazake Chaya. There you can fortify yourself with Japanese snacks and the sweet rice wine (Amazake) for the way back. Overnight in Hakone.

Day 7 In the Burgenstadt Kanazawa

Without guidance

This morning you are on your way to Kanazawa. The beautifully preserved castle city was one of the most prosperous regions in Japan in the 17th century and a center for art and culture. Once you have checked in at your hotel, the rest of the day is free. For those who are interested in architecture and philosophy, it is recommended to visit the D.T. Suzuki Museum dedicated to the life and work of the well-known Buddhist philosopher Daisetsu Suzuki (1870–1966). During his lifetime, he wrote numerous essays on Zen Buddhism and the spiritual tradition of Japan. The museum itself is an architectural tribute to Suzuki’s worldview. The building was designed by renowned architect Taniguchi Yoshio. Overnight in Kanazawa.  

Optional: Light Dinner with a Geisha
Enjoy local delicacies with geisha performance and interactive glimpses of dance and music at a historic Machiya house in Kanazawa.

Day 8 Stroll through one of the most beautiful gardens in Japan

Of course, you should not miss the Kenroku-en Garden, which is one of the three most beautiful gardens in Japan. Just a short walk away is the Kanazawa Castle, an absolute “must-see”. At noon, the famous Omicho market is known for its fresh seafood and delicious sushi. Strengthened, you will then head to the traditional candy shop Morihachi, which has been around for almost 400 years. Here you will learn how the traditional Japanese sweets are made, which are served during a tea ceremony. The culmination of the day is a visit to the former house of Samurai Nomura. The two-story house is known for its elegant tatami rooms, separated by artfully designed sliding walls. On the upper floor there is a small tea room. You will experience the highlight when you step outside over the porch into the garden. Here, in the middle of the city, you will discover a classic Japanese garden. A small creek splashes among exotic plants, the path to the waterfall is lined with stone lanterns, and graceful kois swim in a pond. Overnight in Kanazawa.

9th day Kyoto, city of 2,000 temples and shrines

With tour guide in Kyoto

Time for another ride with the super fast Shinkansen. Its destination is Kyoto, Japan’s cultural capital and home to some 2,000 impressive temples and shrines, including 17 UNESCO World Heritage sites. Upon arrival, meet your guide in the lobby of your hotel and start your exploration with a visit to the impressive Kiyomizu-dera Temple. The Kiyomizu Temple is one of the most famous attractions in the city and has been a World Heritage Site since 1994. What is especially noteworthy is that the main hall and the porch were built without a single nail. However, the view from the veranda over Kyoto may be even more remarkable. With these images in mind, stroll a bit through the Higashiyama district, known for its scenic views, historic temples and gently rising stone stairs lined by tea houses and craft shops. Overnight in Kyoto. 

10th day Gardens, temples and palaces – magical Kyoto

Together with your guide, you will first visit the Botanical Garden of Kyoto, Japan's oldest botanical garden, where you can admire more than 120,000 plants from 12,000 different species. The conservatory is just as remarkable. Continue your journey to Shimogamo Shrine, one of Kyoto’s oldest shrines and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The shrine is an excellent example of the Nagare Zukuri style, a traditional architectural style for Shinto shrines that impresses with its striking gable roof. Your next destination is the Imperial Palace of Kyoto, which served as a residence for the imperial family for centuries. The palace follows the Shinden-Zukuri style, which is characterized by a main building oriented towards the garden, as well as two symmetrical annexes. You can experience modern art at the Kyoto City KYOCERA Museum of Art, an imposing European brick building. The museum is one of the best in Kyoto and displays outstanding exhibitions throughout the year. The nearby National Museum of Modern Art and the Heian Shrine are also worth a visit. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 11 More masterpieces of Japanese architecture

Your guide will pick you up at the hotel this morning and show you first the Miho Museum, designed by the famous architect I. M. Pei. It houses the private collection of Asian and Western antiques by Mihoko Koyama, who was once one of the richest women in Japan. Not only does the collection of 2,000 exhibits impress; the architecture is also unique. Large parts of the plant are built directly into the mountain, the above-ground part has a unique steel-glass construction as a roof. Since you haven’t seen all of Kyoto’s most beautiful temples yet, the next step is to visit the Ryoan-ji Temple. Here you will get a deeper insight into the tradition of Japanese gardens. Nearby is the Kinkaku-ji Temple, also known as the Golden Pavilion. He is known for his relic hall, whose upper floors are completely covered with gold leaf. With radiant impressions you return to your hotel. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 12: Nara continues to Okayama

With tour guide in Nara

Full of impressions from traditional architecture and art, say goodbye to Kyoto today. In the morning, meet your guide at the property. After depositing your luggage together at Kyoto station, leave the city and take the train to Nara – Japan’s first permanent capital (710–784) before moving the seat of government to Kyoto. Even today, impressive temples, shrines and historical sites bear witness to Nara’s former significance. Most attractions are located around the sprawling Nara Park, where tame deer roam freely among visitors. A highlight is the visit to Todaiji Temple, one of the largest wooden structures in the world, inside which the “Daibutsu” is venerated – the largest bronze Buddha statue in the world. On the way to Kasuga Shrine, famous for its thousands of stone and bronze lanterns, you will experience a very special, almost mystical atmosphere. After the visit, return to Kyoto with your guide. In the afternoon, say goodbye to your guide and continue your journey on your own towards Okayama. Overnight in Okayama.

Day 13 The Art Island Naoshima is calling!

Without guidance

Okayama is best known for its impressive castle. Because of its black facade, it also bears the nickname “Krähenburg”. Another highlight of the city is the Korakuen Garden, one of the highlights of Okayama that you can not miss. It is one of the three most beautiful gardens in Japan and a place of unparalleled scenic beauty. Via the steel Tsukimi Bashi Bridge you can get from the “Krähenburg” directly into this enchanting garden. Now it's time for the island! By train you will first go to the port of Uno, from where you will transfer by ferry to the port of Miyanoura on the island of Naoshima. A shuttle bus will take you to your beautiful hotel, the Benesse House. Each of the 49 guest rooms has been individually designed and furnished by Japanese star architect Tadao Ando. Overnight in Naoshima.

Day 14 Naoshima and Benesse Art Site

Without guidance

Today is freely available: take the opportunity to discover the fascinating art installations on Naoshima. Admire works by artists such as Hockney, Pollock and Warhol, among others. Your hotel offers a free shuttle service that will comfortably take you to the main attractions of the island and stop at all attractions. Right next to Benesse House is the Chichu Art Museum, another masterpiece by Tadao Ando that is already a work of art in itself. Here you can admire works by Monet, Walter de Maria, James Turrell and many others. On the east side of the island lies the Ando Museum, whose interior design comes personally from the world-famous architect. The simple concrete structure, typical of Ando’s style, stands in fascinating contrast to the facade that cites traditional Japanese architecture. Another highlight is “I Love You”, a unique combination of art and public bathhouse that combines an impressive variety of wall art and design. Overnight in Naoshima.

15th day Last stop: Osaka

Without guidance

One last day, one final destination. Climb again in the Shinkansen and let yourself be brought to your destination in no time: to Osaka. The city is especially known for its lively and very special atmosphere. Along the way, you will meet Kani Doraku, a giant crab with moving legs, or the “Running Glico Man”, which are excellent photo opportunities. A beautiful motif for commemorative photos is also the castle of Osaka, the symbol of the city. Do not miss the trip to the Umeda Sky Building. From the observation deck, the third largest city in Japan with 2.6 million inhabitants spreads deep below you. Let the wind blow around your nose and enjoy the dreamlike view! Afterwards, you can revive the highlights of your trip together with a glass of wine. If you love it culinary, you can also try your hand at the highlights of Japanese cuisine together with an English-speaking food expert. Overnight in Osaka.

Day 16: Farewell to Japan

Today your journey through Japan ends. Over the past few days, you've gathered a lot of impressions of Japan's art and architecture that will stay with you for a long time to come with these fascinating images in your head and moving memories in your heart, and your driver will take you home on time to the airport. Goodbye and come home safely!
