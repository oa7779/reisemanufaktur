India, Nepal and Bhutan Journey - From the Golden Triangle to the Dragon Empire

Three fascinating countries await you: India, Nepal and Bhutan. At the beginning of your journey, you will explore the magnificent buildings of northern India – above all the fairytale-like Taj Mahal – and walk through metropolises where history dating back thousands of years meets modern urban bustle. A contrast to this is the tranquility of the region around Kathmandu with its venerable temples, monasteries and towns where time seems to have stopped, as well as the silence of the secluded Himalayan Kingdom of Bhutan. In the midst of beautiful mountain landscapes, visit charming places, impressive monasteries and get exclusive insights into everyday life in the Land of the Thunder Dragon.

1st day Arrival in India & first explorations in Old Delhi

Namaste and welcome to India! Your journey starts in Delhi, where you will be expected and driven to your accommodation upon arrival at the airport. Rest first or relax in your hotel. Your accommodation is the beautiful boutique hotel The Manor Hotel New Delhi, located in the exclusive area of Friends Colony. It has already received several international awards for excellent service, design, excellent cuisine and exquisite style and will certainly not leave any of your wishes open. Early in the afternoon, you will travel through time in Old Delhi, where the Jama Masjid, one of the largest mosques in the world, testifies to Delhi’s past as the capital of Islamic India. Passing the Red Fort, the palace complex of the Mughals, which owes its name to the outer walls of red sandstone, your ride will take you on to the Moonlight Square – Chandni Chowk – one of the oldest and busiest market squares in the city. Even deeper you can immerse yourself in the streets and alleys of Delhi during a rickshaw ride. Your driver will show you the beauty of Old Delhi from a different angle on various routes off the main trails, taking you to traditional shops and bazaars. Overnight in Delhi.

Day 2 Other highlights of the capital

After a leisurely breakfast, you will go on a visit to the Indian metropolis. In the imperial city of New Delhi, which with its vast avenues and imposing buildings is an exciting contrast to Old Delhi with its well over 1500 years of history, visit the National Monument India Gate, the Indian Parliament Building and Connaught Place – New Delhi’s bustling business center. Insights into the history of the country can be gained by visiting Gandhi’s former home. In the well-preserved rooms you can understand how the spiritual leader of the Indian independence movement lived. Afterwards, visiting Bangla Sahib Gurudwara, the city’s famous Sikh temple, you will get insight into the traditions of this religion that originated in India. Overnight in Delhi.

3rd day masterpiece of Mughal architecture: the monumental Agra Fort

Today you will continue your journey towards Agra Fort. Once a small village on the banks of the Yamuna, thanks to the two great Mughal monarchs Akbar and his grandson Shah Jahan, the city developed into the second capital of the Mughal Empire. The Mughals were not only rulers, but also great architects and their best architectural wonders in Agra and its surroundings can still be admired today. After your arrival, you can treat yourself to a short rest at the hotel. In the afternoon, together with your guide, you will visit the monumental Fort Agra, which is also a UNESCO World Heritage site alongside the world-famous Taj Mahal. Three generations of Mughal emperors celebrated their power and glory in this palace city on the banks of Yamuna. In the middle of the 17th century Emperor Akbar laid the foundation stone for the red sandstone fortress. Once upon a time, the palace city consisted of 500 buildings. Within the 2.4 kilometer long protective wall are the representative palace buildings, mosques and gardens. You can also admire the Itmad-ud-Daulah Mausoleum, nicknamed “Baby Taj”, which served as an architectural model for Agra’s tourist highlight. Overnight in Agra.

Day 4 The fairytale Taj Mahal at sunrise

If the fog still hangs over the water basins of the garden early in the morning and the sun slowly rises over the magnificent structure, the best time to visit the Taj Mahal is. In incomparable perfection and beauty, this “most expensive proof of love ever” still shines today in white marble – especially in the early hours of the morning before the onslaught of tourists. But not only from the outside, the building rightly impresses millions of tourists every year. The walls inside are decorated with precious gems and mosaics. After the visit, continue your journey to the last corner of the Golden Triangle. On the way to Jaipur, visit the ruined town of Fatehpur Sikri. With red sandstone bricks, the Grand Mogul Akbar had his capital built here in the 16th century, but it had to be abandoned shortly after completion due to a lack of water. Nevertheless, it has been preserved almost ghostly to this day with the great mosque and all palaces. In the afternoon you will reach Jaipur. In the late 19th century, the city was painted in shades of pink in preparation for the visit of the British king – in Rajasthan’s tradition, this is the color of hospitality. To this day, Jaipur is still called the “Pink City”. Overnight in Jaipur.

Day 5 Experience the most beautiful sides of the “Pink City”

After breakfast, another masterpiece of Mughal architecture awaits you: the imposing Fort Amber perched on a steep mountain slope. With the Jeep you drive up the winding pavement road to the main gate of the complex, behind which you expect the public audience hall, the magnificent Ganesha gate and the Spiegelpalast. From the galleries and terraces you have a wonderfully wide view over the valley and the mountain world of the Aravalli chain. On the way back you will see the “Palace of the Winds”, behind whose ornate facade the ladies of the court observed the activity on the road. Jaipur itself also impresses with magnificent architecture. You will visit the magnificent City Palace, which in part still serves as the residence of the former Maharaja, as well as the Jantar Mantar Observatory, where you can admire the largest sundial in the world. The city has preserved some craft traditions. You can observe this during a walk with an English-speaking expert around Ghat Darwaza and Vishveshwar ji. On this special evening walk, mix with the locals, tasting delicacies such as pakoras, aloo tiki, samosas and sweets. Along the way, you’ll see silversmiths, jewelry makers and other craft artists. Overnight in Jaipur.

Day 6 Back to Delhi & Alt-Delhi by night

With many new impressions of India’s rich cultural treasures, you make your way back to the capital this morning. After a break in your hotel, you will make your way back to Delhi’s old town together with an English-speaking expert. Even if you have already seen Old Delhi during the day, it is worth a second visit in the evening. Let yourself drift through the streets together with your guide. With dark, the atmosphere also changes. Your tour starts at Sisganj Gurudwara, a Sikh temple, followed by a visit to the neighboring Sunehri Mosque with its very interesting history. Don’t miss a visit to Kanwarji’s candy shop, an institution in Old Delhi. Your walk continues through Ballimaran with its many small bakeries and chai shops, towards Jama Masjid. The bazaars are open until early in the morning. Stop for dinner at Karim’s, another institution in Old Delhi or the restaurant of the atmospheric Dharampura Haveli (food and drinks are to be paid on site). Overnight in Delhi.

Day 7 - Continue to Kathmandu

Now it is time to leave the hustle and bustle of the Indian capital behind, as your next destination awaits you: Kathmandu, Nepal’s largest and most cosmopolitan city – a place where dozens of ethnic groups meet. Your driver will take you to the airport, from where you will continue to Kathmandu. The city is located at 1,400 m above sea level in a valley surrounded by green mountains with snow-capped, powerful peaks. In the old town are pagodas, narrow, cobbled alleys, anciently carved windows and the stone sanctuaries backdrop for loud and lively life. The Kathmandu Valley currently houses 7 UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Upon arrival, you will be received and taken to your hotel. The rest of the day is freely at your disposal to take a look around the fascinating city on your own. Overnight in Kathmandu.

8th Day Time Travel to the Past & Kathmandu's Greatest Sanctuaries

This morning you will take a trip to the nearby towns of Thimi and Bhaktapu. In Thimi, time seems to have stood still. There are numerous old houses in traditional architectural style. Watch potters at work and watch the grain being threshed by hand. In addition to the pottery, the colorful masks that are made here are also known beyond the place. When visiting a workshop, get an idea of the artistry of the craftsmen and create your very personal souvenir. Afterwards you will travel to the old royal city of Bhaktapur and stroll with your guide over the central square of the city, which is lined by a multitude of fascinating buildings from well over a thousand years of city history. Back in Kathmandu, visit Nepal’s most important Hindu shrine: the Pashupatinath temple complex dedicated to the god Shiva. You can only view the complex from the eastern bank of the Bagmati, as it is strictly forbidden to enter the temple of non-Hindus. Finally, visit the boudhanat stupa, which is considered the most important stupa outside Tibet for Tibetans. There is a meditative atmosphere here. Overnight in Kathmandu.

Day 9 - Paro to the capital of Bhutan: Thimphu

After breakfast, leave the Nepalese capital behind and make your way to the Kingdom of Bhutan, where you can expect peace and the fascinating expanse of the Himalayas. Just arriving is an experience: by plane you will cross some of the highest peaks in the world before your plane lands in Paro, at Bhutan’s only international airport. Once there, you will be welcomed and warmly greeted. But your daily destination today is Thimphu, the capital and largest city in the country. At an altitude of 2,350 meters, Thimphu lies in a steep valley surrounded by wooded mountains with ancient monasteries and temples. To this day it is the only capital in the world without traffic lights. Once there, the rest of the day is available for spontaneous activities. Take a stroll through the city, which looks like a gallery of traditional Bhutanese art, architecture, culture and tradition. Overnight in Thimphu.

Day 10 Explore Thimphu

Today is marked by one of the most unusual capitals in the world. City bustle or traffic chaos you will look for here in vain. For this you can find a lot of peace in the streets and alleys. Thimphu is the home of the royal family. At the beginning of your tour, you will attend a prayer ceremony and learn more about the life of the monks. Afterwards, visit the Museum of Traditional Textile Art and Changangkha Lhakhang, a temple perched on a mountain ridge above the city. In the afternoon, take a detour to the Institute of Traditional Arts and Crafts, which introduces its students to the 13 traditional arts and crafts of Bhutan in a six-year course of study. Here you participate together with the other students in a drawing lesson. You can also visit the Institute of Traditional Medicine or be impressed by the unique architecture of the fortress-like monastery Trashi Chhoe Dzong. In the evening, a guest speaker will explain more about the system of Gross National Happiness in a private lecture. You can ask all your questions and learn even more about Bhutan, the history, religion, culture and politics of the country. Overnight in Thimphu.

Day 11 - Via the Dochu La Pass to Punakha

After breakfast, continue your journey to the northeast. On your way to Punakha you will stop in two smaller villages, where you can get to know the rural life in more detail. Finally, via the Dochu La Pass, flanked by large chortens and colorful prayer flags, you will reach Punakha where you will visit the Temple of Fertility, Chimi Lhakhang. It goes back to Saint Drukpa Kinley, who for his unorthodox methods of propagating the Buddhist faith – through singing, humor and offensive behavior – received the nickname “Divine Mad One”. He is said to have introduced in Bhutan, among other things, the practice of painting phallic symbols on the walls of the house to drive away evil spirits – this custom you will see many times on your way to the sanctuary. Pilgrims visiting the monastery are traditionally hit on the head with a 25 centimeter long wooden phallus. You will also visit the Dzong of Punakha, a unique monastic fortress located at the confluence of the Po Chu and Mo Chu, popularly known as the “Palace of Happiness”. The monastery fortress has been completely restored in recent years and is one of the most beautiful of its kind in Bhutan. Overnight in Punakha.  

12th Day Evening Prayer at Nalanda Monastery School

After a short drive along the scenic Punakha Valley, take a small hike to Chörten Khamsung Yuely Namgyel, perched on a hill amidst rural idyll. Your path winds past rice, chilli and cabbage fields up to the sanctuary built by the queen, which stands for peace and stability in a fast-paced time. Once up there, you will have a great view of the magical Mo Chu River Valley deep below you. The Chörten himself is known for his beautiful and iconographically rich paintings. The deities represented are to drive out enemies and unfavorable influences and spread peace and harmony. Back in Punakha, visit the Nalanda Monastery School in the western part of Punakha, where the monks receive their Buddhist education. Here you will observe the monks during the evening prayer and have the opportunity for a cultural exchange. Take time to talk to the monks and learn more about their lives. After this unique experience, you will return to your hotel with many lasting impressions. Overnight at Punakha.
Your walk today: easy, 5 kilometers, about 1 hour

Day 13 Back to Paro

After breakfast, drive back to Paro. On the way through small villages you can observe the rural bustle: many farmers offer their products on the roadside, so that you can taste fresh apples, peaches and pears at a stopover. After check-in at the hotel, the rest of the day is freely available to you. Use the time to explore Paro a little on your own or just to let the soul dangle a little. Overnight in Paro.

Day 14 Hike to "Tigernest"

Your day starts with an energetic breakfast before heading out to one of the kingdom’s most important landmarks and cultural icons: Taktsang Goemba (monastery), also known as the “Tigernest”. The monastery nestles 900 meters above the valley on the rugged rock walls and is one of the most sacred places in the Himalayas. The walk leads through pine forests decorated with colorful prayer flags and requires a good condition. Alternatively, the climb can also be mastered with a pony or mule (for an additional cost). Arriving at the top, the magnificent outlook compensates for the effort. Is there anything better after a hike than a cool beer? Beer lovers will be happy, because craft beer has also arrived in Bhutan! Back in Paro, visit Namgay Artisanal Brewery and learn all about the production of the 6 varieties of beer that are obtained here. Something special is the creamy milk beer made of oats, chocolate malt and lactose. Or the dark beer with a trace of burnt barley. Even a sparkling-refreshing cider—the first cider in Bhutan—is brewed here. Overnight in Paro.
Your hike today: medium to strenuous, 9 kilometers, about 4 – 5 hours (return and return)

15th day Fortresses, temples and insights into traditional life

This morning you will visit Drukgyel Dzong, a 17th century fortress perched on a rocky outcrop. You will also visit the Shrine of Kyichu Lhakhang, a complex consisting of two identical temples. Around the sanctuary, countless colorful prayer flags blow in the wind. Its roots date back to the 7th century AD, when Buddhism was introduced in Bhutan. Around noon, you’ll be a guest at a local family’s farmhouse for a cooking demonstration of Bhutanese cuisine. If you wish, you can help your hosts prepare the food before you taste your delicious lunch. Well strengthened, you can then help the family with their daily tasks such as caring for livestock and grain and get an authentic insight into the traditional way of life. In the evening, experience the cheerful and colorful music and traditional dances of Bhutan at a show performed for you privately. The one-hour performance gives you a little insight into the various religious and secular dances and music. You will be amazed at the variety that ranges from mask dances to folk music to medieval dances. Overnight in Paro.

Day 16 Unique experience: hanging traditional prayer flags

Bhutan – many people associate the mysterious land with the colorful prayer flags that flutter in the wind and give the surroundings something magical. The cloths are lined up with prayers and mantras designed to bring happiness, wisdom, peace or health. The wind that blows through the flags carries the prayers out into the world. Therefore, the flags previously blessed by a monk in a sacred ceremony are preferentially hung in particularly exposed places such as bridges, mountain passes and peaks. You can also labor on houses to bless the inhabitants of the house. The colors of the flags represent the five elements: yellow (earth), green (wood), white (iron), blue (water) and red (fire). You too will hang some of the colorful flags today to ask for the blessings of the gods at the end of your journey or simply thank them for the many unique experiences during your journey. The best days to hang the prayer flags are the auspicious days Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday. Your guide will explain what you need to look for and will be happy to help. After this special and almost meditative experience, you can make the rest of the day as you wish. Overnight in Paro.

17th day home

After exploring the highlights of India, Nepal and Bhutan over the last two and a half weeks, your break from everyday life is coming to an end today, as your driver takes you to Paro airport from where you will take your flight home.
