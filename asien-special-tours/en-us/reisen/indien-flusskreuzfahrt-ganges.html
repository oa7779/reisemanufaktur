India River Cruise - Following Mother Ganga

Experience the Mother of All Rivers on a unique India river cruise with Asia Special Tours. Ask and enjoy them.

The fascination of Ganges: Mother Ganga – since ancient times the lifeline of the subcontinent, created by God Shiva and thus a place of deep religious worship – is still today a magical and powerful stream. No river in the world has a greater cultural, religious and economic significance than the sacred Ganges, which is sung in countless myths. He embodies the divine principle for Hindus in the form of Ganga, Vishnu and Shiva, who are closely connected with the mythical history of the river’s origin. On this trip you will explore places of deep religious significance, picturesque villages and important archaeological sites along the river course. On board the colonial-style river cruise ship RV Bengal Ganga, you will get to know the countryside and its people in a comfortable way and be able to visit sights that are difficult to reach by car. From the deck of the ship you will also have unique opportunities to observe the fascinating wildlife of the river. Interesting explorations in Kolkata, a melting pot of cultures, and in the spiritual Varanasi, which also looks back on an exciting colonial past, round off this varied journey.

1st day Arrival in Kolkata

Namaste and welcome to India! Your journey begins in Kolkata – more commonly known as Calcutta in German – where you are already expected and brought to your hotel. Kolkata is crowded, vibrant and rich in cultural heritage. The metropolis owes its origin to the trader Job Harnock. During British colonial rule, it was known as the jewel of the East and until 1911 the capital of the country. Even today it is the most important city in the east, the center of trade and industry. After checking in at the hotel, the rest of the day is free to take a look around the area on your own or relax in your beautiful hotel. Your accommodation for the next three nights is the elegant The Oberoi Grand. Built in Victorian style, Oberoi exudes a luxurious ambience and impresses with excellent service and unique architecture. Located in the heart of the city, near New Market, the grand bazaar, this luxury hotel is also the ideal starting point for exploring the city. Overnight in Kolkata.

2nd day Kolkata – a fascinating melting pot of cultures

On your walking tour today, you will find a colorful program that brings you closer to the interplay of different cultures. With an English-speaking expert, stroll from the Anglo-Indian-influenced Bow Baracks towards the lively Bara Bazaar, on whose east side the 47-meter-high minarets and the colorful dome of the Nakhoda Mosque – the largest Islamic house of worship in the city – rise out of the sea of houses. As you pass by impressive Anglo-Indian, Marwari, Bihari or Muslim style buildings, learn why Chinese breakfast in the Tiretta Bazaar is still the most popular, why the number of Parsis is decreasing day after day and how Armenians built the oldest Christian church in the city. After this tour, the rest of the day is freely available to you. For example, how about an insight into the diversity of Bengali cuisine? Join the chefs of The Oberoi Grand Kolkata on a fascinating culinary journey and immerse yourself in the world of herbs and spices that give Bengali cuisine its fine but sometimes fiery aromas. Or let the in-house sommelier introduce you to the peculiarities of local wines. Overnight in Kolkata.

3rd day Belur Math & in the footsteps of the great writers

The morning will take you to the Belur Math campus on the west bank of the Hugli River, the headquarters of the Ramakrishna movement. The mission unites elements from different world religions. The portal is inspired by early Buddhist sculpture, the windows have clear references to Islamic architecture and the floor plan is based on the Christian cross. In the afternoon, accompany an English-speaking expert on a very special tour of the city and immerse yourself in the rich and multi-layered literary history of Kolkata. Find out what connection literary greats like Twain, Kipling, Ghalib, Faiz or Grass have with the city, in which milieu Kolkata’s native literary giants such as Tagore, Vidyasagar, Nazrul or Derozio grew up and what makes the Bengals so enthusiastic about literature that the annual literature festival always attracts two million readers. Listen to fascinating stories about the many Nobel Prize winners that the city has produced. These and many other topics will be discussed with your literary expert in the traditional “Adda style”. Overnight in Kolkata.

Day 4 Start of your unique Ganges river cruise

After breakfast, board the RV Bengal Ganga, your floating accommodation for the next nine nights. Enjoy the comfort, exquisite colonial style and excellent service of the ship and let the panorama of the Ganges pass by. The RV Bengal Ganga takes you to fascinating places that are hidden from most travelers. In the afternoon, an orientation trip through the city (together with other guests and English-speaking tour guide) is on your plan. This will take you to Dalhousie Square in Kolkata’s former European Quarter, where you can see some colonial monuments and St. John’s Church, the oldest Christian church in Kolkata. Of course, you should not miss the imposing Victoria Memorial in white marble, with which the English wanted to document their claim to power visibly at the height of the Empire. The entrance is adorned by a statue of Queen Victoria, which is reminiscent of the monumental building. Towards the evening you go back on board your ship. Upstream, head towards Kalna, known for its beautiful 18th- and 19th-century Bengal temples. Overnight on board the RV Bengal Ganga.

5th day Bengali Temple of Kalna

Start the day with a delicious breakfast with a view of life on St. Ganges. During a rickshaw ride (together with other guests and English-speaking tour guide) you can visit the Hindu Lalji Temple and the terracotta temples of Kalna, among others. These belong to the Bengal Temples, a little-known special form of Hindu architecture that combines elements of mosques and Hindu temple architecture. Particularly noteworthy is the one consisting of two concentric rings with 34 FIG. 74 of the temple shrines existing building complex of 108 Shiva’-Temple, which is almost unique in this form throughout India. The other temples contain terracotta tablets depicting themes of Hindu epics and the mythical life of saints, gods and goddesses, as well as various aspects of daily life. Finally, return to your ship and sail aboard the Bengal Ganga past the sacred city of Mayapur, which is a center of Krishna faith at the mouth of Ganges and Jalangi, towards Matiari. Overnight on board the RV Bengal Ganga.

6th Day Traditional Craftsmanship in Matiari

Immerse yourself in India’s eventful history. Take a walk (together with other guests and English-speaking guide) to the village of Matiari, known for producing handmade brass goods. From recycled copper, beautiful pots, plates and much more are created here in the traditional way. Look over the shoulders of the craftsmen at their work and immerse yourself in their daily work. Back on board your ship you will then continue to Murshidabad. Along the way, you will pass Palashi, where in 1775 the loss-taking battle against the British East India Company ushered in a 190-year dependence of India on the British Crown. Finally, you will reach the small town of Murshidabad, which was the capital of independent Bengal before being subjugated by the British. Overnight on board the RV Bengal Ganga.  

7th day Impressive palaces and mosques in Murshidabad

On the traditional means of transport of the locals – so-called Tongas or German horse carts – you drive (together with other guests and English-speaking tour guide) to the famous Katra Mosque with the burial site of Murshid Quli Khan, governor of the Mughal ruler Aurangzeb and later first Nawab of Bengal. Continue to the great Hazarduari Palace, which is still one of the most striking buildings in Murshidabad. The palace, whose name literally means “Palace of a Thousand Doors”, today houses a museum that is considered the largest archaeological museum in India with almost 5,000 exhibits. Here you can admire, among other things, various antique weapons, oil paintings, old maps, rare books, but also the second largest chandelier in the world, which the Newab got from Queen Victoria. After the visit, return to the ship and continue on to Jangipur. Overnight on board the RV Bengal Ganga.

Day 8 Over the Farakka Lock into the main arm of the aisle

In the morning, aboard the RV Bengal Ganga, you will cross the historic Farakka Lock and enter the main arm of the corridor through the connecting channel. The Farakka Barrage, which regulates the flow of the Ganges between Bangladesh and India, can be seen at some distance. With a bit of luck, you can observe different native bird species while driving through the feeder channel. Finally, you reach Samtaghat. Overnight on board the RV Bengal Ganga. 

9th day Archaeological excavations at Bateshwarsthan

This morning the ship sets course for Bateshwarsthan. Upon arrival, travel with Jeeps to the archaeological site of Vikramshila University, which was established during the Pala Dynasty (ca.) 750 to 1161 AD was one of the most important Buddhist monastic universities in India. It was founded during the reign of the Bengali ruler Dharmapalades in the late 8th century and had a lasting impact on the spread of Tantric teachings to Tibet as well as East and Southeast Asia. Among other things, subjects such as theology, philosophy, grammar, metaphysics and logic are taught here. Overnight on board the RV Bengal Ganga. 

Day 10 The saga of the wise man Jahnu who swallowed the Ganges

A new day, a new goal. On board the RV Bengal Ganga, you will continue this morning to Sultanganj, an important pilgrimage site for Hindus. Your journey passes through a sanctuary established to preserve the endangered Ganges dolphins. You might see some of the rare animals in the water pulling their circles. In Sultanganj you will immediately notice the two rocky islands, which are connected to the mainland by a long dam. One of the islands is crowned by a mosque, while on the other a Shiva temple sits. According to legend, the river Ganges on its way to the ocean is said to have interrupted the sage Jahnu in his meditation, whereupon he swallowed the river in his anger in a train. On the orders of King Bhagiratha, the wise man released the river again by a cut into the thigh. Because of this history, the Ganges is also called Jahnavi. After visiting the two impressive buildings, stroll through a local market on your way back to your ship. Overnight on board the RV Bengal Ganga. 

Day 11: Insight into the centuries-old art of yoga

Their final destination on board the RV Bengal Ganga is Munger, one of the largest and most industrialized cities in Bihar state, where tobacco and dairy production plays a major role. The history of the city can be traced back to the mythological era of Ramayana and Mahabharata. In 1762, the city came to the forefront when the Nawab of Bengal moved its capital from Murshidabad to Munger and built an arsenal in the city for the production of firearms. The tradition of manufacturing weapons continues to this day. After lunch on board, visit the Swarmi Niranjanananda Bihar School of Yoga in Munger, a modern institute offering spiritual teaching for aspirants, yoga projects and medical research in collaboration with various companies, government agencies and public institutions. Here you will get an insight into this centuries-old Indian art. Overnight on board the RV Bengal Ganga. 

12th day Bodhgaya – one of the most important pilgrimage sites of Buddhism

Say goodbye to your floating hotel. After breakfast, disembark from the RV Bengal Ganga and meet your personal guide and chauffeur. Together you will travel to Nalanda, perhaps the oldest refuge of scholarship in India. Nalanda has a glorious past, a rich spiritual heritage and some extraordinary architectural ruins that were probably built in the 5th century BC. Continue to Bodhgaya, one of the most important and sacred Buddhist pilgrimage sites of the Buddhists. Here you will visit the Mahabodhi Temple, a UNESCO World Heritage Site that marks the place where Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, is said to have attained enlightenment under the "Bodhi Tree". Overnight in Bodhgaya.

Day 13 Spiritual Varanasi

Today, your journey will take you to the last stop of your journey: Varanasi, considered the most sacred site of Hinduism. For more than 2,500 years, believers have been making pilgrimages here. Varanasi is crowded, chaotic and full of spirituality – pure India. Pilgrims bathe in the Ganges, cows walk through the alleys, merchants offer their goods for sale – here the country awaits you in an intoxicating authenticity. After checking in at the hotel, you and your guide will embark on a first exploration tour of this fascinating city. From ancient times Varanasi was a city of learning and center of the great Sanskrit scholars. You will visit India’s oldest university, Benares Hindu University, which was founded in 1916 for the study of Indian art and culture and Sanskrit. The Durga Temple is one of the prescribed stations for pilgrims, as is the neighboring modern Tulsi Manas Temple. In addition, your guide will show you the Bharat Mata Temple with a large marble relief representing a map of India, as well as the Kashi Vishvanath Temple also known as the “Golden Temple” and the Gyanvapi Mosque. In the evening, you will experience the spirituality of the place up close at a traditional Aarti ceremony on the banks of the Ganges. In a solemn atmosphere, the faithful offer small ships with burning lights and flowers. Overnight in Varanasi.

14th Day Morning Boat Trip on the Ganges & Colonial Heritage

They greet the morning with a boat trip on the Ganges. Thousands of believers come daily to the shore in the morning to bathe and pray. Let the magical atmosphere work on you. After breakfast, drive to the place where Buddhism was founded over 2,500 years ago: Sarnath. After Buddha had found his enlightenment, he gave his first sermon here. At the Sarnath Museum, learn about the origins of Buddhism before returning to Varanasi and delving deeper into the colonial history of Varanasis during a very special walk through the city with an English-speaking expert. Together you will walk in the footsteps of the famous British artist, engineer, architect and visionary James Princep, who laid the foundation for modern Varanasi in just 10 years. With the bicycle rickshaw, you will ride through the colonial quarters of Varanasi, listening to stories of struggle, service and empire while watching British Indo-Saracen architecture such as churches, cemeteries, waterworks, police stations and the town hall. When you see the foundation stones of Lord Duffrin’s Bridge (Malviya Bridge) spanning the Ganges, you will feel like you have walked back in time. Overnight in Varanasi.

Day 15 Farewell to India

Today it is time to say goodbye to this fascinating country you have explored along the aisle. For your trip home, you will be escorted to the airport.
