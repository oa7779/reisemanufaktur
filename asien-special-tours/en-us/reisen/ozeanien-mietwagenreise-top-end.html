Australia Car Rental Trip - Fascinating Western Australia and Tropical Top End

Australia Rental Car Trip - Experience Australia's beaches, fertile land and desert on your trip with Asia Special Tours. Ask them in a moment.

Itinerary & services

Some of Australia’s best beaches, the busiest markets, wide parks and world-class restaurants offer Perth, the gateway to the southwest. Giant trees, whales as well as world-class wines, art galleries and boutiques can be found in this region. The Pinnacles are a three-hour drive north of Perth. Some of the rock needles are over four meters high. In the Kalbarri National Park you will encounter huge rock cliffs and gorges. Monkey Mia is famous for the manatees and dolphins that come here to the beach. In Exmouth you will find the fantastic Ningaloo Reef with its colorful corals and peaceful whale sharks, which is directly accessible from the beach. A highlight is the Karijini National Park with its paradise-like green gorges in the middle of red earth. As a self-driver, you travel individually, flexibly and independently. We book the most beautiful accommodation for you in advance. For easy orientation during the journey, a GPS-Navi provides. Darwin is the tropical capital of the Northern Territory in the north of the continent. An ideal base from which to explore the tropical top end with Kakadu National Park and Arnhem Land. The wildlife and plant life in this region with its numerous wetlands will delight you. This trip is best done during the months from April to October to avoid the rainy season in northern Australia and the summer heat in the desert regions of Western Australia.

1st day Arrival in Perth

You will be received at the airport in Perth and taken to the hotel. The rest of the day is at your disposal. Arrive and relax from the long flight journey. We recommend a walk through the bustling center with a visit to the vast, inner-city Kings Park. Overnight in Perth.

Day 2 Excursion to Rottnest Island by helicopter

Have you heard of the Quokkas? These cute short-tailed kangaroos the size of domestic cats are the undisputed stars of Rottnest Island. You can find countless, funny Quokka selfies on the Internet and today you have the opportunity to shoot a few on the island itself. The 11 x 4.5 km large and close to the coast attracts visitors with great beaches and paradisiacal nature. There are the southernmost corals in the world to see, as well as dolphins and sea lions. Motorized traffic is prohibited and you are on foot or by bicycle. You will be picked up from their Perth accommodation and visit the Perth Mint and Kings Park, from where you will have fantastic views over Perth, the Swan River and Darling Scarp. The lively and trendy harbor town of Fremantle near Perth offers you a wide selection of great restaurants and cafes directly on the sea, where you can enjoy your lunch. They fly by helicopter to Rottnest Island. On a guided bicycle tour you have some time to enjoy the beach and go swimming. Afterwards you will fly by helicopter back to Perth and will be taken to your hotel. Overnight in Perth.

3rd day Car rental: From Perth via Bunbury to Busselton and the Margaret River region - 285 km

Take over your rental car today. The size and category of the vehicle will be determined at your booking according to your individual wishes. Mandurah is a popular holiday destination south of Perth and a good place for refreshment. The estuary here was once a meeting place for Aborigines. In Bunbury, Boulters Lookout offers impressive panoramic views of the city. Here you will find some excellent hotels and cafes for your lunch break. When visiting Bunbury, make sure you don’t miss a visit to the Dolphin Discovery Centre, which is just a few kilometers from the city center. There you can experience free-living bottlenose dolphins (Great Bottlenose Dolphins) who regularly visit Koombana Bay. From here, head to Busselton, one of the oldest cities in Western Australia. Busselton is a seaside resort and at the same time the center of this large agricultural region. It is located 241 km south of Perth on the coast of Geographe Bay and on the Vasse River. The wineries around Margaret River are easily accessible from here. Overnight in Margaret River.

4th day Margaret River Region and Leeuwin Naturaliste National Park with visit to the caves

Today is at your disposal. An underwater observatory is located at the very end of the pier in Busselton and on a guided tour you have the opportunity to observe more than 300 marine animals. Drive to Dunsborough and 13 kilometers along Cape Naturaliste Road to reach the Cape Naturaliste Lighthouse. Built in 1903, the Cape Naturaliste lighthouse offers an untouched view of the rugged coastline and the Leeuwin Naturaliste National Park. From June to December, the whales make their annual trek along the coast to get to their summer feeding ground in Antarctica. The rough sea and moon-like landscape of the western tongues are a stark contrast to the gentle coastal scenery and tranquil lake of Bunker Bay to the east. Ngilgi Cave is named after its history with the Australian natives and offers an impressive collection of stalagmites and dripstones. Or spend the afternoon visiting fine wineries, tasting specialties and exploring the beaches in the area. Overnight in Margaret River.

Day 5 - Cape Leeuwin Lighthouse to Australia's southernmost point and visit the Giant Trees in Karri Forest - 169 km

Leave south on one of the most scenic itineraries in Western Australia, Caves Road. Don’t miss a stop for a walk through the impressive Boranup forest and visit one of the caves. The Cape Leeuwin Lighthouse, located south of Augusta, is the southernmost point where the Indian and Southern Oceans meet. In the lush rainforests around Pemberton you will find some of the largest hardwood trees in the world. Enjoy a forest walk or take the Pemberton Tram, which winds around some of the world’s largest trees. For a spectacular view, venture up Pemberton’s climbing trees. The large trees – “Karri” – are Pemberton’s biggest attraction and very impressive. Warren National Park has the Dave Evans Bicentennial Tree. From here you can see the lush Karri forest from a bird’s eye view. Pemberton has become a gourmet and wine region and you can visit some wineries and indulge in the local delicacies, including Marron and Trout. Overnight in Pemberton.

Day 6 From Pemberton to the Giant Trees in Walpole and to Albany - 236 km

Walpole is located 120 kilometers south of Pemberton. Drive deeper into the forest on Hilltop Road and look for the giant Giant Tingle Tree. On your way to Denmark, make sure you stop at the Giant Tree Top Walk in Walpole-Nornalup National Park! There is a floating bridge that runs 38 m above the forest floor. Surrounded by 20,000 hectares of towering trees, it is an exciting yet relaxed way to get to know the magical southern forest and admire the grandeur of these towering giants.
Before continuing to Albany, we recommend stopping at Elephant Rock, a massive rounded rock formation.The surrounding scenery of Albany and the beaches are very beautiful. Let yourself be transported back in time and explore the history of Albany’s penal prison, old taverns, whaling ships, settler huts and listed houses. You can end the day by tasting fresh marine specialties! Overnight in Albany.

7th day Albany with beaches, rugged coast and its whale history

Today is free to explore Albany and its surroundings. We have a number of suggestions for you to make the most of your day. Be sure to check out the Brig Amity here, a replica of the ship that brought Albany its first settlers and cargo. Drive to Torndirrup National Park, known for its natural attractions along the rugged coastline. Breathe salty ocean air and take fantastic photos of the Gap, where the ocean drops very dramatically 24 meters from the coast. Very close by is Natural Bridge, a fascinating granite formation in the shape of a bridge. Whales can be watched playing and frolicking in King Georg Sound during the season from July to October, and you can either watch them from the shore or take part in a booots trip. Middleton Beach has five kilometers of pristine white sand beach where you can take a three-kilometer walk along the wooden waterfront, from which you will have a fantastic view of King George Sound. Overnight in Albany.

Day 8 - Albany to Perth - 414 km

Leave in a northern direction to Perth. Right next to Mount Barker is a Banksia farm where you’ll find scenic wildflowers in a five-acre garden, alongside thousands of native Australian plants. You can participate in a visit here. Further north of Mount Barker is Stirling Range National Park – the only Alpine area in Western Australia and it’s famous for its colorful wildflowers. Five of the mountain peaks are over 1500 meters high and often shrouded in fog. Perth, founded by British settlers in 1829, has old buildings such as the Government House, Town Hall and His Majesty’s Theatre. The oldest park is Stirling Gardens, the oldest building is the Old Courthouse. You may also feel like going to the beach and driving to one of the 19 beaches that belong to Perth. Take the West Coast Highway along the coast and explore Scarborough, Hillary's Harbour and Sorrento in northern Perth or Cottesloe in the south. Popular and good restaurants can be found in Subiaco, Mt Lawley, Leederville and Northbridge. Overnight in Perth.

Day 9 From Perth to the Pinnacles - 202 km

Cervantes is located about 200 kilometers or about 2 hours drive north of Perth and about 53 kilometers west of the Brand Highway. Cervantes is the gateway to the world-famous Pinnacles, located in Nambung National Park about 17 kilometers south of the city. The Pinnacles are a collection of natural rock formations made of limestone, which mysteriously rise from the sand as pillars of very different size and height. You can see the Pinnacles as you drive along the 17-kilometer Pinnacles Drive through Nambung National Park. Follow the 3.5-kilometer Pinnacles circuit and admire these unique natural structures made of limestone, which was created from former shells. On the drive through Nambung National Park to the Pinnacles, you will have the fantastic opportunity to see kangaroos and emus in their natural habitat and even the magnificent diverse wildflowers depending on the season. Visit Hangover Bay and Kangaroo Point. Hangover Bay is an excellent place for snorkeling and is exquisitely equipped with protected picnic tables, benches and barbecue stations as well as public toilets. Overnight in Cervantes.

10th day Along the coast from Cervantes to Geraldton - 219 km

For your journey to the north, we recommend you take the coastal route. Dongara and Port Denison are great for a break. With its magnificent beaches, historic buildings and the wonderfully shaded main street lined with tall trees, the place invites you to take your legs here.
Then finally reach Geraldton. Geraldton is located on Champion Bay, a spectacular coastline also known as the Batavia Coast. Geraldton is the main port and administrative center of the central region to the west, offering its visitors plenty of culture, entertainment, shopping and sports opportunities. Geraldton has a sunny climate and mild winters throughout the year, making it one of Western Australia’s most popular holiday destinations. Geraldton and the surrounding areas are also known and noted for their beautiful wild spring flowers and magnificent scenery. Overnight in Geraldton.

11th Day Bushland and Animals in Kalbarri National Park - 160 km

Kalbarri is a magical coastal town, located at the mouth of the Murchison River into the Indian Ocean and the National Park offers a rich variety of unique bushlands and wildlife. For this afternoon, we recommend a trip to the Murchison River, with its spectacular gorges and viewpoints, including the famous “Nature’s Window”, where you can enjoy the view through a natural massive red rock frame and thus create the impression of an image. The rugged terrain and seclusion of the Murchison Gorge is fantastic. There are a number of short hiking trails that allow access to the viewpoints along the gorge. During the months of July to October, you can admire the unique variety of wildflowers that transform the area into an indescribable sea of color. A visit to the Rainbow Jungles, a center of parrot rearing with tropical gardens and waterfalls. In the evening you can watch the daily feeding of the pelicans on the beach. Wade through the shallow water and enjoy the sunset or relax with a dinner at a beachside cafe. Overnight in Kalbarri.

Day 12 From Kalbarri through the Desert to Monkey Mia - 401 km

Today’s destination is Monkey Mia, famous for its dolphins that regularly come to the shore. You will have the opportunity to watch these extraordinary animals play at close range and participate in the dolphin feeding that takes place daily. Rewardable and recommendable is a visit to the 110 kilometer long Shell Beach. This beach is made entirely of shells, up to 10 meters deep. In some places on Shark Bay, the shells are so densely and firmly pressed that they can be cut into blocks and used for construction work. Examples of this can be admired in Hamelin Pool and Denham. Monkey Mia has an extremely relaxed atmosphere and nature lovers will be amazed by the diverse wildlife and bird life that can be found in this area. Monkey Mia is located on a unique section of protected coastline with unspoiled rocky beaches, turquoise waters and pure white sand. The rest of the day is at your disposal. Explore the spectacular coastal region or just relax at your resort. Please note that the Monkey Mia Nature Park has to pay an entrance fee. Overnight in Monkey Mia.

Day 13: The Dolphins by Monkey Mia

Today is at your disposal. Rest, relax and enjoy the beautiful area! The highlight for any visitor to Monkey Mia is of course the dolphins. However, please always remember that the dolphins are wild animals and their appearance on the shore is therefore not guaranteed for every day. You can also join a boat tour from Monkey Mia to watch the dugongs or ‘mortar cows’. Already three generations of the so-called “bottlenose dolphins” regularly come to the sandy beaches of Monkey Mia ́s. Every day, some dolphins swim into the clear shallow water in the bay to connect with the people there, delighting visitors with their intelligence and grace. Since the dolphins are wild, their number and exact time of visit varies every day, but they usually visit the shore several times a day, most often in the morning.
Take advantage of the rest of the day to explore the spectacular coastal region or simply relax on the magnificent beaches. Overnight in Monkey Mia.

Day 14 - Monkey Mia to Carnarvon - 350 km

Leave Monkey Mia and continue along Highway No. 1 towards Carnarvon. Carnarvon is located on the Gascoyne River, which is an important supply stream for the flourishing horticulture and fishing industry. Due to the watering from the river, a number of delicious delicacies thrive on the plantations. Bananas, mangoes, papayas, melons and grapes as well as a wide range of vegetables are usually available at all times and fresh fish, shrimp and mussels are also available by the flourishing fishing industry depending on the season and extremely popular for the so typical Australian BBQs (barbecues or ‘barbies’).
The 2.5 km long hiking trail ‘Carnarvon Tramway’ takes you to a protected area where you can admire beautiful roads flanked by palm trees, tropical plantations and the ‘Fascine’ sea arm. Overnight in Carnavon.

15th day Carnavon to Exmouth, the gateway to Ningaloo Reef - 367 km

Today’s stage will take you from Carnarvon to Exmouth. Exmouth is a western Australian city with about 2,400 inhabitants, which swells to 6,000 in the months from April to October. It was founded in 1967 and goes back to the US Navy, which had its base there at that time. Those who visit Exmouth come for diving and snorkeling. The area is considered one of the best diving areas in the world.
Ningaloo Reef Marine Park is located just under 7 km from the coast and covers a more than 260 km long coral reef along the Coral Coast. Those who do not want to dive can see more than 220 coral species and a lot of fascinating fish while snorkeling in the park. Over water there are countless kangaroos and emus to observe. Overnight in Exmouth.

16th day Exmouth at Cape Range National Park with whale sharks from March to August

Today is free to explore this great coastal region and reef even further. Exmouth is the gateway to the fantastic Ningaloo Reef and Ningaloo Marine Park with its underwater observation platform. This colorful reef, with its biodiversity, rivals the Great Barrier Reef on the east coast for all divers, snorkelers and those who just want to enjoy the view. Maybe take a half-day snorkeling tour and get close to the majestic Manta rays and water turtles. Or take in the amazing marine world from the comfort of a glass bottom boat or a kayak, where you can get close to the whale sharks and snorkel with the gentle giants in the water. The Cape Range National Park offers wild gorges, rugged limestone, the 400-meter-high Cape Range and dreamlike sandy beaches along a 60-kilometer coastline. There are plenty of kangaroos, emus and a variety of birds. Finally, enjoy the evening in one of the great restaurants. Overnight in Exmouth.

Day 17: From Exmouth to Karijini National Park - 685 km

Today’s stage in the fantastic Karijini National Park covers over 600 kilometers. We recommend that you start early in the morning. Located in the dynamic Pilbara region of Western Australia, Karijini National Park is about 80km northeast of Tom Price. Karijini (originally Hamersley Range) National Park is the second largest national park in Western Australia and ranks among the five most popular visitor attractions in the state. Karijini National Park is an enchanting, unspoilt and natural destination that offers the visitor a unique and wonderful landscape as well as an unforgettable outback experience. Karijini is famous for its impressive rivers and streams, spectacular gorges, as well as the shimmering mountain crevices that are up to 100m deep. Ideally located in the middle of Karijini National Park is the unique Karijini Eco Retreat – the park’s first and only permanent luxury accommodation for the discerning traveler. Overnight in Karijini National Park.

18th day Karijini National Park

Today is free to explore Karijini National Park. Check out the winding and jagged gorges (Gorges) of the park. Visit Joffe Gorge and enjoy some leisurely hikes along various viewpoints or some more challenging hikes through the shimmering rock cliffs, past crystal clear waterholes and tumbling waterfalls. 
A visit that you should not miss is that of the Karijini Visitor Center, whose unique design is a landmark in itself. Built in the shape of a dragon, it represents the local Aboriginal culture and also serves to restrain any bushfires. Inside the center, you can learn a lot of interesting information about the local history, the wildlife and plant life of the park, as well as about the Aboriginal dreamtime stories. Overnight in Karijini National Park.

19th Day Through the Pilbara Iron Ore Region to Port Hedland - 380 km

Leave Karijini National Park and head north on the Great Northern Highway to Port Hedland. 
Port Hedland is the gateway to Australia’s extensive iron ore export to the surrounding Pilbara region. The town was named after Captain Peter Hedland, who founded the port in 1863. The cargo weight that Port Hedland implements today is the largest of all ports in Australia. Another important industry in the city is salt production. 
Worth a visit to the observation tower on Wedge Street at the Tourist Information Center. At Two Mile Ridge you can also find interesting Aboriginal drawings or engravings in the limestone vault there. Depending on the season, whale watching tours are sometimes offered and the birdlife of the area is also extremely attractive overnight in Port Hedland.

20th day Port Hedland to Broome along Eighty Mile Beach - 610 km

You have a long car journey of over 600km ahead of you today, so we recommend that you start quite early in the morning. Take the Great Northern Highway along the Indian Ocean and the famous ‘Eighty Mile Beaches’. The landscape is simply stunning with the glittering blue waters of the ocean and rugged coastline. The road also runs through the 'Great Sandy Desert' - the Great Sandy Desert, so please do not drive off the highway!
Broome is a vibrant outback town, located on the coast of the southern tip of the Kimberley region. Rich in natural attractions, Broome is famous for its pristine beaches and coves and the shimmering waters of the Indian Ocean. Broome’s world-famous beach – Cable Beach – is an example of this beauty with its 22 kilometers of white sand and clear tropical waters. Overnight in Broome.

Day 21 Broome – Camels on the Beach, Pearl Fishermen & Staircase to the Moon

In and around Broome there are many interesting historical sites. The Sun Pictures Theater on Carnarvon Street opened in 1916 and is considered the oldest open-air theater in the world. Furthermore, there is the cemetery where there are tombs of pearl divers that date back far into the 1880s. From March to October you can witness a breathtaking natural phenomenon, also known as ‘Stairs to the Moon’. This is caused by the reflection of the moonlight on the open Wadden Sea at extreme ebb. The phenomenon is best admired from the southern end of the Dampier Terrace. One attraction you should not miss is the gigantic dinosaur footprints, which are located about 5km southwest at Gantheaume Point. You can see the imprints at low tide from the cliffs from a distance of 30km. Also worthwhile is a trip to the Willie Creek Pearl Farm. Don’t miss the extraordinary experience of riding along the beautiful white sand beach on the back of one of the friendly camels, especially at sunset. Overnight in Broome.

22nd day Flight from Broome to Darwin

Today, the self-driving program ends. Drop off your rental car at the airport and check in for your domestic flight to Darwin. Pick up from the airport in Darwin and transfer to your hotel. We recommend a walk through the center or a visit to the markets. Overnight in Darwin.

Day 23 With the all-wheel drive car to the Kakadu National Park

Your guide will pick you up from your hotel at 8 a.m. in your luxurious, fully equipped, air-conditioned four-wheel drive car. The first stop is Fogg Dam Conservation Reserve, an extensive open wetland where many colorful birds live during the dry season. After a drive over the Marrakai Plains and Adelaide as well as Mary River, we take our morning tea or coffee break at Purple Mango Cafe. Then take the Arnhem Highway for a cruise on the famous Corroboree Billabong - part of the Mary River System. There are countless bird species to see here as well as the large saltwater crocodile.
Later, drive over the old Jim Jim 4WD Track into Kakadu National Park. You will have the opportunity to observe the diversity of birds and wildlife along the waterways and we will visit the Warradjan Aboriginal Cultural Centre. Overnight in Kakadu National Park.

24th day Australian jungle and Aboriginal rock paintings

This morning after breakfast we will head to Nanguluwur, where there is great rock art and a settlement of local Aboriginal people to discover. These sites are less well known, so you’ll be off the tourist trails. You will see some excellent examples of rock art, including the rare and ancient "dynamic figures", "X-ray" style art and the so-called art of the "contact period", which represents the first contact of Aboriginal people with the whites. Your guide will be happy to explain these unique testimonials to you. After lunch, we drive south on a 4WD track to the Maguk Gorge (Barramundi Gorge) and spend the afternoon in this fantastic area. At the end of a rainforest walk, you can swim on request and refresh yourself in the natural diving pool under the cascade of a waterfall. Overnight in Kakadu National Park.

25th Day Billabongs in the Magela Wetlands, Arnhem Land and Aboriginal Art

Today, after breakfast, continue north along the Magela Wetlands and follow a steep step where you will take a guided tour of the Aboriginal people on the eastern Alligator River. You will enjoy a fantastic view of the Arnhem Escarpment and get to know many aspects of the local Aboriginal culture. After a short boat trip, with special permission from the traditional owners, we will head to Arnhem Land, where we will visit a small Aboriginal community called Gunbalanya. Here you can meet the local artists and weavers and watch them at work. From the settlement, there is a magnificent view of the Arnhem Land wetlands. After lunch, we start our journey back to Darwin and see more wetlands and billabongs along the way. We take a break at Bark Hut Inn and come back to Darwin in the late afternoon.
NOTE: We reserve the right to change this program due to weather conditions, seasonal road conditions or Aboriginal cultural reasons. Overnight in Darwin.

26th day return flight from Darwin

Today the program ends. We will pick you up from the hotel and take you to the airport. Farewell and return or flight.
