Monastery trip through Mongolia - Following the footsteps of the monks

If you are interested in foreign cultures and religions, you will be well served with this trip. After the big city life in Ulaanbaatar, go into the breathtaking expanses of the country. Your path will take you past steppes, forests and mountain regions, fascinating animals and traditional nomadic yurts. During your trip you will visit some of the most beautiful monasteries in the country and immerse yourself in the special, spiritual life of the monks.

1st day Arrival in Ulaanbaatar

Welcome to Mongolia. You will land in Ulaanbaatar and will be warmly welcomed by your tour guide. After a break in the hotel, you will embark on an exploration tour of the capital metropolis. With the visit to Gandan Monastery, the most important of Mongolia, the first spiritual highlight awaits you. After the communist wave of destruction in the 1930s, it gained a very special significance, as from 1944 it was the only monastery in all of Mongolia to resume operation on a small scale and thus became THE center of Buddhism in Mongolia. The beautiful architecture and precious collection of religious artifacts make the visit a must for every Mongolian traveler. Do not miss the Choijin Lamyn Museum. Nowhere else do you get such an authentic and comprehensive impression of the Lamanese art and culture. If time permits, your guide will then lead you to the somewhat hidden Dashchoilin khiid monastery. The monastery is the successor to one of the formerly largest monasteries of Ulaanbaatar which was destroyed in 1937. After this first day full of fascinating impressions, you will be brought back to your hotel. Overnight in Ulaanbaatar.

Day 2 The Stone Forest of Baga Gazriin Chuluu

Early in the morning you will leave Ulaanbaatar and go south to the province of Dundgovi following Mongolian tradition. There, the stone forest Baga Gazriin chuluu awaits your visit. Let yourself be captivated by its unique landscape of rounded rock formations and dramatic granite gorges. The remains of an old temple are also worth a visit. During the period of Buddhist persecution in the 19th century, two highly revered monks lived there, whose rock carvings can be found in the area. Even today, locals make pilgrimage here to commemorate the two monks. The environment is also rich in minerals and crystals and is excellent for hiking. You will spend the night for the first time in a yurt camp and can admire the infinite starry sky above you. Overnight in Baga Gazriin Chuluu.

Day 3 Visiting Ongi Monastery

Your next destination is the ruins of the hidden monastery “Ongi”. On the way, visit the Sangiin Dalai khiid Monastery, which 1810 Founded and then 300 Lamas were inhabited. After your tour of the former monastery complex, of which only the main temple has been preserved today, continue your journey to Ongi Monastery. The monastery once housed over 500 Buddhist monks and is located on the river of the same name. During religious persecutions in the 30In the years before, it was almost completely destroyed and many of the monks living there were sent to labor camps. Today there is a small, new monastery. With a little luck you will meet the monks who take care of the monastery and have the opportunity for a personal conversation. Overnight near the monastery of Ongi.

Day 4 Fascinating Khogno Khan Mountains

Today you travel across the vast Mongolian steppe to the Khogno Khan uul mountain region. Rising to 2,000 meters, the mountains are covered in woodland and mountain steppe and offer excellent hiking. On the way, visit Erdene Khamb Monastery to learn about its history and the fate of the monks of the Old Monastery. Founded by the Red Hats in the 14th century and destroyed in the 17th century, the old monastery was never rebuilt. A 45-minute climb brings you to its ruins, giving you an impression of the former complex and rewarding you with wonderful views. The present New Monastery was founded in the late 17th century by followers of the reformed Buddhist school, the Yellow Hats, and destroyed in the 30s. Reconstruction began in the 90s, and ten monks now live here again. After your visit, continue to your yurt camp. Overnight in Khogno Khan.

5th day of free time in the Khogno Khan Mountains

You can make this day according to your own ideas. Spend time in your yurt camp, enjoy the peace and quiet, take walks through the beautiful surroundings or get interesting insights into the life of the nomads as you look over their shoulders in their activities. Overnight in Khogno Khan.

Day 6 In the footsteps of Genghis Khan

As the sun rises over the infinite expanses of Central Mongolia, head towards Karakorum. Along the way, visit Shankh Khiid Monastery, one of the oldest and most important monasteries in Mongolia, founded by the first Buddhist leader, Zanabazar. In 1921, the monastery still housed 1,500 monks and consisted of a good 20 buildings, but was dissolved at the end of the 1930s. Then continue your journey to Karakorum, which became the capital of the Mongolian Empire under Genghis Khan and his successors. Scientific excavations repeatedly reveal new impressive relics of this multicultural trading metropolis founded in 1220. After visiting the excavation site and museum, you will visit the Erdene Zuu monastery built on the ruins of Karakorum in the 16th century, one of the largest in Mongolia. Of the formerly more than 60 buildings, only three have survived after the Cultural Revolution, but with a total of 108 stupas and the white border wall, the monastery still offers an impressive picture. Extensive renovation work also helps to restore the shine of the building complex. Overnight in Karakorum.

Day 7 Hot Springs of Zenkher

Your first destination for today is the Tuvkhun Temple, which is spectacularly located on a mountain top. This temple also dates back to the first religious leader of the Mongols, Saint Zanabazar, and was founded in 1653. Zanabazar himself lived there for more than 30 years and wrote his most important works during this time. While the complex at that time consisted of 14 temples inhabited by over 1,000 monks, today only the ruins of four temples and a stupa remain, bearing witness to Mongolian history and tradition. The approximately eight kilometer long hiking route up to the temple leads you through the dense forest on the southern flank of the mountain and runs steeply uphill and downhill. At the top, you will be rewarded with the impressive view. With many exciting impressions of this enchanting place, you will continue to the hot springs of Zenkher, where you can enjoy a mineral bath under the starry sky in beautiful weather and relax. Overnight in Zenkher.

8th Day About Zezerleg to Ogii Nuur Lake

After breakfast, you will continue to the town of Zezerleg, where you will first visit Zayain Khuree Monastery, founded in the 17th century. In the 19th century, Russian and Chinese traders settled around the monastery, which also gained importance as a commercial center. The role as a commercial center saved large parts of the monastery, as the premises were now used as storage rooms. Since 1960, the Provincial Museum has been housed here – one of the best in Mongolia. Behind the museum, a small path leads up the steep slope to a small temple ruin. From there, you have an excellent view of Zezerleg and the surrounding area. Even higher, under the summit of the “Three Bulgan”, three large Buddha figures are engraved in the rock that you should not miss. Then continue to the lake Ogii Nuur. Overnight at Ogii Nuur Lake.

Day 9 - Continue to Erdenet

Today your journey will take you to Erdenet, the second largest city in Mongolia. On the way, visit the provincial capital Bulgan with the Dashchoinkhorlon monastery located just outside. The monastery fell into the 30years of repression, but was 1992 rebuilt. Now living again 30 monks there. Arriving in Erdenet, you will be taken to your hotel. Overnight in Erdenet.

Day 10 The Monastery of Amarbayasgalant

Today you will visit the Monastery Amarbayasgalant, one of the three largest monastic complexes in Mongolia. Built in the early 18th century, more than 40 temples were once a center of Buddhism and many monks made pilgrimage here to marvel at the complex. The partially destroyed monastery still offers many monks a home today and was named a World Heritage Site in 1996. Due to its secluded location, it is also called the Monastery of Quiet Beatitude. Overnight in Amarbayasgalant.

Day 11 The Kharaagiin Monastery: a log cabin-style gem

On your way to your last yurt camp, stop at the town of Darkhan. The city is on a level, in 700 Metre height and is surrounded by gentle hills. Darkhan has been 1961 As a new industrial center, it has been shattered with ca. 74.000 Residents today the third largest city of Mongolia. The city seems to be a boring Russian prefabricated monster at first. However, the Kharaagiin Monastery located in the old part of the city is absolutely worth a visit. It appears as a gem in the middle of gray houses and surprises with its architecture, which is different from the rest of the monasteries. It is built in Siberian log cabin style and painted in deep dark red and sunny yellow. Once known for its Tsam dances, this monastery was also 30The years were partially destroyed. After that, it served as a kindergarten until finally 1996 Renovated and re-established as a monastery. After your tour of the monastery, continue your journey to the yurt camp. Overnight in Urikhan.

Day 12 Return to Ulaanbaatar

Today we return to Ulaanbaatar. On the way, visit the temple Aglag Buteeliin Khiid. In a beautiful area with wooded slopes, the complex towers on a small mountain that is relatively easy to climb. The monk who resides here is a well-known artist in Mongolia. In particular, his religious statues and figures are famous. In addition to the statues and murals customary in Buddhist monasteries, the granite rocks decorated with mythical creatures or fantasy creatures on the site stand out. After visiting, you will continue your journey and finally reach the capital where you will check into your hotel. The rest of the day is at your disposal. Use the time for a stroll through the city, for example, to buy some souvenirs for your loved ones at home. Overnight in Ulaanbaatar.

13th day Departure from Mongolia

Your time out from everyday life is coming to an end. Even if your holiday is soon over, the precious memories, beautiful photos and exciting stories of your trip will accompany you forever. Look forward to letting your friends and family share your experiences in the distance at home. Your driver will take you to Ulaanbaatar airport for your return flight, from where you will board your return flight.
