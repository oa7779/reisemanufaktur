Bhutan for the senses - Luxury trip with overnight stay at Six Senses Bhutan Lodges

Bhutan Luxury Tour: Enjoy the luxury of Six Senses Resorts on an individual luxury tour of Bhutan! Contact Asia Special Tours!

This unique journey takes you back to the past. Let yourself be enchanted by Bhutan’s breathtaking landscape and its rich cultural heritage. Druk Yul, Bhutan’s official name, is known as the land of dragon people whose unique customs and cultural treasures remain hidden to most people. Embrace the kingdom’s natural beauty, mingle with the locals and understand why this country is called the “happiest place in the world.” During your trip, you will stay in three of the Six Senses Hotel Group’s dreamlike lodges, nestled picturesquely in the breathtaking landscapes of the Thimphu, Punakha and Paro Valleys. In addition to fantastic views, you’ll enjoy the unique service that Six Senses Resorts is known for, an extensive spa offering and culinary delights that delight your senses. Your personal guide accompanies you from arrival to departure and guarantees you an eventful and impressive journey that you will never forget.

Day 1: Arrival in Paro and Departure to Thimphu

Your journey begins with a drive to Thimphu, the capital and largest city in the country. At an altitude of 2,350 meters, Thimphu lies in a steep valley surrounded by wooded mountains with ancient monasteries and temples, and to this day it is the only capital in the world without traffic lights. After checking in at Six Senses Thimphu, enjoy a light lunch while you get used to the height difference. Thanks to the magnificent design and large areas of water reflecting the sky, Thimphu Lodge also bears the nickname “Palace in the Sky”. In the 25 suites and villas with wonderful distance vision, you can enjoy the coziness of an open fireplace on cool days. For today, we recommend smaller activities to avoid over-exerting your body. Spend the afternoon by the pool and enjoy the fascinating views of the Thimphu Valley and a view of the huge Buddha Dordenma, which rises 52 meters high. Relax your muscles at a yoga class or take a short walk through the scenic lodge’s apple garden. Overnight in Thimphu.

Day 2: The Giant Buddha Dordenma

Wake up in the gentle light of Bhutanese morning and enjoy a hearty breakfast overlooking Buddha Dordenma.After breakfast, visit Buddha Point to see the gigantic statue up close. Inside the statue are thousands of smaller bronze buddhas, which are between 20 and 30 centimeters high. During the subsequent visit to the National Textile Museum, you will get insights into Bhutan’s traditional weaving art. After lunch at a local restaurant, you’ll have some time to browse the shops for pretty souvenirs for your loved ones at home before diving into Bhutanese life at the Folk Heritage Museum with its authentic replicas of traditional households. Together with your Six Senses guide, you will then visit Pangri Zampa Lhakhang, a 16th-century monastic school of astrology and hear some anecdotes about Zhabdrung, the Union of Bhutan. The fortress of Thimphu is also worth a visit. It is the traditional seat of Druk Desi, the head of the Bhutanese civil government and currently houses the throne room and offices of Druk Gyalpo (the Dragon King of Bhutan). Overnight in Thimphu.

Day 3: Continue to Punakha via the Dochu La Pass

Getting up early is worthwhile today. Watch the enchanting sunrise over the Thimphu Valley as you taste your breakfast. The road from Thimphu to Punakha winds through pine forests and leads past streams, waterfalls, chorten (stupas) and wind-flopping prayer flags to the Dochu La Pass with its magnificent views. Finally, you will reach the picturesque Punakha Valley, one of Bhutan’s deepest valleys. In the former capital of the kingdom is the Punakha Dzong, a magnificent 17th century monastery castle that is one of the most historically important Dzongs of Bhutan and a true masterpiece of Bhutanese architecture. From here it is only a fifteen minute drive along Mo Chhu to the beautiful Punakha Lodge, which is several hundred meters above the bottom of the hillside. The 19 luxurious suites and villas are open and decorated with lots of light wood, offering magnificent views of the fascinating landscape of the valley. With a delicious dinner in the excellent restaurant, you will finally end the day with great taste. Overnight in Punakha.

Day 4: Explore around Punakha

Take advantage of today to explore the beautiful Punakha Valley extensively. After breakfast, take a pleasant hike to Ningpo Chörten, an old Chörten dedicated to Bhutan’s favorite patron, the “Divine Mad One” Lam Drukpa Kuenley. Continue to the village of Khasaba, where you will visit a small Buddhist educational institution that was founded in 2005. Here orphans find a home and are taught in Buddhist scriptures, but also Western subjects. After a picnic in the midst of dreamlike nature, you will drive to Punakha Dzong, also known as the “Palace of Great Happiness”. Its majestic structure rises six floors high and was constructed as an embodiment of Buddhist values. Finally, return to your lodge where you can arrange the rest of the day as you wish. Relax by the pool, meditate or enjoy a refreshing foot bath at the spa. The day ends with an excellent dinner. Let your taste buds indulge and enjoy the magnificent view from the terrace. Overnight in Punakha.

Day 5: Bicycle through the picturesque river valley

Start the day with a relaxing bike ride along the river valley and discover the scenic beauty of Punakha. For lunch, enjoy a delicious picnic arranged for you on the river bank. Well strengthened, we then continue to the villages of Talo and Nobgang, which are located on the slopes of the Punakha valley at an altitude of about 2,800 meters. Here you will visit the nunnery Dorji Lhuendrup and the Nobgang Monastery. Talo is also known as the birthplace of the Kingdom’s four Queen Mothers. Your next destination is the Chimi Lhakhang, a fertility stamp from the 15th century. The temple is especially welcomed by couples who want to start a family and ask for the blessing of the saint. In the afternoon, return to your lodge. Spend the afternoon by the pool, relaxing at the spa or doing yoga before ending the day with an exquisite dinner. Overnight in Punakha.

Day 6: Back to Paro

After breakfast, we return to Paro. Once again, cross the Dochu La mountain pass towards Simtokha Dzong, the first Dzong of the kingdom to house an ancient clay statue of Buddha. Continue your journey to Chuzom, the confluence of the Paro and Thimphu rivers, where three chantens were erected reflecting the three designs characteristic of the country: Nepalese, Tibetan and Bhutanese, with the Bhutanese-style chanten being the last to emerge. Arriving in Paro, fortify yourself at lunch at a local restaurant for the afternoon program. This first leads you to the Rinpung Dzong, whose name translates to “fortress with many jewels”. The Paro Tshechu Festival also takes place here every year. In the Paro National Museum you can admire, among other things, a number of festival masks. Finally, you will reach the dreamlike Six Senses Paro Lodge, also known as “Stone Ruins”. Situated at 2,675 meters, the 5-star lodge in the noble, modern-rustic chic captures the essence of the Paro Valley and is a tribute to the culture and history of the country. Imposing stone walls and spacious fireplaces skilfully take up the sensational backdrop architecturally. Overnight in Paro.

Day 7: Relaxed walk through dreamlike nature

After a relaxing night, start your day with a relaxing walk around the lodge surrounded by apple plantations and pine forests. You can continue your trek and hike to Jele Dzong, a small fortress strategically built on a mountain ridge in the early 16th century to cater for travelers traveling between Paro and Thimphu. On a clear day, the view of the northern mountains is breathtaking. This route is also taken at various times by nomads and yak herders. After a light picnic, return to the lodge for a spa treatment. The rest of the day you can do as you wish. In the evening, a delicious Indian dinner under the starry sky awaits you for a change. Overnight in Paro.

Day 8: Hike to the famous “Tigernest”

Your day starts with an energetic breakfast before heading out to one of the kingdom’s most important landmarks and cultural icons: Taktsang Goemba (monastery), also known as the “Tigernest”. The monastery nestles about 900 meters above the valley on the rugged rock walls and is one of the most sacred places in the Himalayas. The hike up leads through pine forests decorated with colorful prayer flags and requires a good condition. Alternatively, the climb can also be overcome with a pony or mule (for an additional cost). Arriving at the top, the magnificent outlook compensates for the effort. You can enjoy your lunch either in the form of a nice picnic or at a local restaurant. After your hike, return to the lodge, where you will enjoy a private dinner in the evening by the stone ruins of a 12th century dzong. Your royal feast in this enchanting atmosphere is undermined by live music and folk dances. Overnight in Paro.

Day 9: Farewell to Bhutan

Even the most beautiful journey comes to an end. Even if it is sad that you already have to say goodbye to the “happiest place on earth”, you will be taken to the airport after an early breakfast for your trip home. Perhaps one day you will return here, because the fascinating kingdom still holds many secrets that need to be discovered. If you would like to relax a bit on the beach afterwards, we are happy to organize a bathing extension for you. We recommend the dreamlike Six Senses Yao Noi on the Thai island of Koh Yao Noi.
