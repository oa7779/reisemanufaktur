Winter travel through Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan

Breathtaking winter landscapes, culture and eventful history – all this is packed into this eventful journey in Central Asia. We promise you an authentic view of Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan far from the tourist paths.

1st day Arrival in Bishkek & excursion to the gorge of Kaschka Suu

Early in the morning you will land in Bishkek; your personal guide will be waiting for you and taking you to the hotel. Make yourself fresh and relax a little from the long flight – then your guide will pick you up for a short city tour. Then take a trip to the Kaschka Suu gorge. There you can expect a fantastic view of the mountain panorama of the four-thousanders. Are you in the mood for deep snow descents, a sleigh ride or an easy hiking tour? Everything is possible here, only the decision is difficult. Overnight in Bishkek.

Day 2 Bishkek – Red Front – Tschon-Kemin

Today you will go to Tschon-Kemin. On the way, visit the village of “Red Front”, which was originally called “Bergtal” and was occupied by Russian-German Baptists in 1927. Not far from Red Front are the ruins of the former capital of the Kara Kitai Empire. Here, the Burana Tower is a good example of 11th century architecture. Your guide will introduce you to a few mysterious legends surrounding the structure. Also visit a small museum where archaeological finds from the surroundings of Balasagun can be seen. In the afternoon, you will ride a horse-drawn sleigh through the wonderfully snowy landscape of the National Park to a viewing spot from which you will have a fantastic overview of the magnificent Tschon-Kemin valley. Overnight stay in Tschon-Kemin.

Day 3: Tschon-Kemin – Dolon – Kochkorka

The day begins with a trip to the Dolon Pass. From the Passanhöhe you can enjoy a fabulous view of the Kochkorka mountain valley and the glacier world of the five-thousanders. The journey continues to Kochkorka, where you will visit a felt manufacturing facility. In the small museum next door you will see interesting products of Kyrgyz craftsmen from the 19th to 20th centuries: carpets, mats, curtains and tableware – everyday things of use made of leather, wood, velvet, wool or reeds. Overnight with a Kyrgyz host family in Kochkorka.

Day 4 Cooking Corka – Tamga

The day’s destination is Tamga on Lake Issyk-Kul, in the middle of the Tienshan mountain range. The journey leads along the Orto-Tokoi reservoir and on the southern shore of Issyk-Kul and is dominated by magnificent panoramic views of the legendary lake and the opposite snow giants. Lake Issyk-Kul, at an altitude of about 1,600 meters and with an area of 6,200 square kilometers, is the second largest inland lake in the world. In the afternoon, wander through the Tamga Gorge to the “split stone”. This is shaped like a yurt and provided with an Old Tibetan inscription. From here you have a fantastic view of Lake Issyk-Kul – which, by the way, never freezes over. This is why many species of birds come here to hibernate. Overnight in Tamga.

Day 5 Tamga – Jety-Oguz – Karakol

Continue on to Karakol. On the way, you make a stopover in the Jety Oguz Gorge. This is known for its bizarre red rocks, the “Broken Heart” and the “Seven Ox”. You walk through the forested gorge, past frozen waterfalls, streams and rivers. From all sides, magnificent views of the snow-capped mountains open. Afterwards you can look forward to a big highlight: a real falcon hunt. A hunter will show you how to train golden eagles and falcons to catch rabbits and foxes. Overnight in Karakol.

Day 6 Karakol

Breakfast in peace – then your guide will take you through Karakol, which was founded as a border town in the Tsar period. The old town still carries the charm of the founding period. You will visit the wooden Orthodox Trinity Cathedral and a pagoda-style dungeon mosque. Continue in Karakol’s National Park, where you can simply hike through the impressive snow landscape, or ski in the deep snow – away from the busy slopes. Overnight in Karakol.

Day 7 Karakol – Bulan-Sogotuu

The destination of today is the village of Bulan-Sogotuu on the northern shore of Lake Issyk-Kuls. On the way, they make a stop to experience the fabulous Tschon Aksuu Gorge. The gorge fascinates with its mountain landscape with its hanging glaciers, fir and guard forests. Frozen streams with bizarre ice forms, the huts of the local nomads and for us exotic animals offer an unforgettable natural spectacle. The clear air, the freshness of Lake Issyk-Kul, the scent of the coniferous forests and the frosty cold of the mountains is a blessing for body and mind. Overnight in Bulan-Sogotuu.

Day 8 Bulan-Sogotuu – Bishkek

Today your journey will take you back to Bishkek and its magnificent surroundings. Take a break from historical murals from the 7th – 3rd century BC showing hunting scenes. Your guide will be happy to tell you more about it. Overnight in Bishkek.

Day 9 Bishkek – Tashkent (Uzbekistan)

They do not stay long in Bishkek, but fly on to Tashkent in the morning. The city is still shaped by Soviet architecture – and a place where Europe and the Orient merge. Your guide will take you to your hotel and then wait for a first small tour of the city: visit the Barak Khan Medrese, composed of several buildings of different ages, and the Kaffal Shaschi Mausoleum, which consists of a portal and several domes. In addition, a visit to a bazaar and craft facilities is on your program, so you will get a good insight into the bustle of the city. In the evening, a cooking class will give you valuable insights into the secrets of Uzbek cuisine. A chef will show you how to prepare the traditional rice dish “Plov” – the absolute favorite of the Uzbeks. Overnight in Tashkent.

Day 10 Tashkent – Samarkand

Continue on to Samarkand! The city of Samarkand is considered the heart of the legendary Silk Road and looks back on a 2000-year history marked by a rapid change of powers. Once there, visit the famous Registanplatz with its impressive medres Ulugbek, Tilla Kari and Scher Dor. They are among the most magnificent buildings in the Islamic world. You can also visit the Mausoleum Gur Emir. Overnight in Samarkand.

Day 11 Samarkand

Samarkand has so much to offer that you will also spend today getting to know the city better. Start your tour with the Bibi Khanim Mosque. The conqueror Tamerlan had ordered their construction in 1399 after his victorious return from India. You can also enjoy the colorful activity on the old bazaar. Also interesting: the archaeological museum Afrossiab and the Shakhi-Zinda mausoleums, a street of tombs where family members Ulugbeks and Timurs are buried. Their resting places are decorated with the finest Majolika works. The afternoon is at your disposal. Overnight in Samarkand.

Day 12 Samarkand – Chess Risabs – Bukhara

After breakfast, your guide will take you to the house of an Uzbek family. There you will get insights into the art of carpet weaving and livestock breeding. Take the opportunity to talk to the family over a cup of tea and learn more about their everyday life. Your guide will be happy to help you with your understanding. Then it goes on: the name Chessrisabs means “green city”. It is not only a UNESCO World Heritage Site, but also historically significant as the birthplace of Tamerlane. Once there, visit the ruins of Timur Ak Serai’s residence, Kok Gumbas mosque, Gumbasi Seidon mausoleums, Kulol Shamsitdin, Dshachangir and Timur’s tomb. In the afternoon, drive to Bukhara. Overnight in Bukhara.

13th day of Bukhara

Bukhara was once one of the most splendid commercial centers on the Silk Road. People from all over the world met in the bazaars, and the city developed into an important cultural and spiritual center. Even today, the atmosphere of bygone times is noticeable when you stroll through the narrow alleys of the old town, stroll over the gold market and marvel at the magnificent architecture of the beautifully decorated Oriental architecture. The city shows you the most untouched image of a medieval Central Asian city – and it is full of life. After breakfast, your guide will take you to the most beautiful places: He will take you to the Fortress of Ark, the Bolo Mosque, the Mausoleum Tchaschma Ajub and the Samanid Mausoleum. After the lunch break, we will continue with a visit to the architectural complex Poi Kaljan, the mosque and the minaret Kaljan, the Miri Arab medre and the architectural complex Kosch with the medres Ulugbek and Abdul Asis Khan. You will also see the trading dome of goldsmith Toki Sargaron, cap maker Toki Telpak Furuschon and silk fabric bazaar Tim Abdulla Khan. Overnight in Bukhara.

Day 14 Bukhara

An interesting program is also offered for the second day in Bukhara: You make a trip to Gijduvan. Along the way, stop at the town of Vabkent to visit the 12th century minaret. In Gijuvan, best known for its excellent ceramic craftsmanship, you will visit a ceramic workshop where a master will show you how pottery is made. The ceramic styles there are particularly beautiful to look at due to the elaborate ornaments and combinations of geometric figures. In a museum you will learn more about various schools of Uzbek ceramics. Another Uzbek specialty is magnificent textiles. The weavers still work on the traditional hand looms today. Their products are great souvenirs for your family and friends at home! You will return to Bukhara and visit the Chur Minor monument, the city center of the 17th to 18th centuries, the Ljabi Chaus with the Kukeldasch madrassas, the Nadir and Hanako caravanserai, the Toki Sarrafon trading dome and the Magoki Attori mosque. Overnight in Bukhara.

Day 15 Bukhara – Khiva

Through the desert and along the Silk Road we continue to Khiva. Along the way, pause in a tea house on the banks of Amudarja and of course make as many photo stops as you like. During the trip, your guide will be happy to tell you interesting facts and background about the country and its people. Arriving in Chiwa, you will be taken directly to your hotel. Overnight in Chiwa.

Day 16 Khiva

Breakfast vigorously, because a full-day city tour is planned for today. In the small oasis town in the middle of the desert, time seems to stand still. The winding old town of Itchan Kala resembles an open-air museum and the fairy tales from 1001 nights. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1990. Behind every corner and behind every gate, new backdrops open up, be it the courtyards of the mosques, sparkling minarets in the sun, or carved wooden doors or a view into small workshops where the inhabitants still pursue traditional crafts. Chiwa has preserved the fairytale atmosphere of an ancient, oriental city to this day. Part of the tour are the Kunja Ark Fortress, the Muchammad Amin Khan Medret, the Kok Minor Minaret, the Muchammad Rachim Khan Medret, the Mosque and the Dshuma Minaret. Afternoon sightseeing destinations are the Mausoleum Pachlavan Machmud, the Minaret and Medrese Islam Khodsha, the Ak Mosque, the Medresen Kutlymurad Inak and Allakuli Khan, the Caravanserai, the Palvan Darvosa City Gate and the Château of Khan Tash Hauli. Overnight in Chiwa.

Day 17 Khiva – Urgench – Tashkent

The morning is at your disposal. Explore the city on your own. In the afternoon you will be taken to Urgentsch Airport. From there you will fly back to Tashkent, be picked up and driven to your hotel. Overnight in Tashkent.

Day 18 Tashkent – Travel home

Your trip to Central Asia ends today. With many new experiences and 1,001 fairytale memories in your luggage, your chauffeur will take you to the airport from where you will fly home.
