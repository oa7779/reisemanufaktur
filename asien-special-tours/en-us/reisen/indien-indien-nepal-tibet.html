Best of Tibet, India and Nepal

India Combination Tour - Discover the most beautiful of Tibet, Nepal and India on this unique combination tour of Asia Special Tours. This is a question.

Itinerary & services

Tibet, Nepal and North India – three as fascinating as different countries await you. Explore the sacred city of Lhasa on the “Roof of the World” at an altitude of more than 5,000 meters and – far from mass tourism – the lonely cities and passes amidst the breathtaking backdrop of the Himalayas. Immerse yourself in the world of Tibetan Buddhism and enjoy moments of silence amidst impressive mountain scenery. In the Kathmandu Valley, discover the diversity of Nepalese culture and its mysticism. In Delhi, Agra and Fatehpur Sikri you will experience impressive locations of Indian history and today’s life on the subcontinent. A journey that could hardly be more diverse!

1st day Arrival in Chengdu, Old Town Stroll & Taste Fire Pot

Welcome to China! Your journey begins in Chengdu, where you will be expected and driven to your accommodation upon arrival at the airport. The capital of Sichuan province is a modern city, but its origins go back far into the past. The great past of the city is clearly noticeable in the numerous, well-preserved temples to this day. Especially pretty gardens and tea houses shape the face of the city, making Chengdu one of China’s most popular cities. After a short break at the hotel, you and your guide will take a stroll through the old town district of Jinli, where beautiful hotels and small shops in traditional style line up. If you get hungry, all the better: The district, which is very popular with Chinese and tourists, offers a large selection of restaurants. The residents of Chengdu like to eat spicy food. The fire pot is particularly popular. Whether you prefer it less or really spicy, tonight you will get the opportunity to get to know the traditional cuisine of Sichuan. Overnight in Chengdu.

Day 2 On the “Roof of the World” & first explorations in Lhasa

After breakfast, you will be taken to the airport. They fly to the “roof of the world”, to Tibet. At Gonggar Airport you will be picked up and driven to your hotel in Lhasa. If you visit the Jokhang Temple in central Lhasa after a short break at the hotel, you will immediately get to know the most sacred temple of Tibetans. Entering the temple, one receives the distinctive smell of incense and butter lamps. From the roof of the temple, you have a good view of the forecourt and the Barkhor pilgrimage road, where worshippers circle the temple and repeatedly throw themselves reverently to the ground. The most famous street in Lhasa is located in the middle of the beautiful old town. The path is lined with well-preserved old buildings, on which countless pilgrims crowd themselves who visit the monastery. In the small shops on both sides of Barkhor Street, Tibetan artisan products are offered. After visiting the temple, you will also mingle with the people and stroll along the street a little before returning to your hotel. Overnight in Lhasa.

Day 3 Visiting the headquarters of the Tibetan God-Kings

Today, an absolute highlight of Lhasa awaits your visit: the Potala Palace, the imposing seat of the Tibetan god-kings. On Mount Mar-po-ri (Red Mountain) sits the awe-inspiring complex 130 meters high above the capital of Tibet. Today the Potala Palace has the whole 13 Floors with total 999 rooms. At the top are the private rooms of the acting Dalai Lama, including prayer and meditation rooms, and at the bottom are the tombs of previous Dalai Lamas. The tomb of 5Dalai Lamas stand out from the others: It's about 15 meters high and with 3.700 Kilogram of gold decorated. Later you will visit the Sera Monastery, which is just outside Lhasa. The former state monastery was 1419 founded by students of the Gelugpa Order, also called Yellow Hats, and formed a monastic city populated by several thousand monks. Even today, it is known throughout Tibet for its excellent academy. Every day against 16 At the clock, the monks gather to discuss theological questions with ritualized gestures. It's a very fascinating sight. Overnight in Lhasa.

Day 4 Excursion to Ganden Monastery

Ganden is the furthest away from Lhasa, but probably also the most interesting monastery, which owes it above all to its location high up on the Gokpori ridge. Picturesquely, the Ganden Monastery nestles on the slopes of the Kyichu Valley and offers an enchanting sight from far away. The founding monastery of the Gelug School, which was largely destroyed in the Cultural Revolution, is located approximately 45 km east of Lhasa at an altitude of 4.300 meters. Since the 80In the years since, it has largely been restored according to historical plans and has also been a political issue between China and Tibet again and again in recent history. In the main building Serdung Lhakhang stands a large stupa made of gold and silver with the mortal remains of the founder. More beautiful than the buildings of the monastery is the pilgrimage route up there. On the approximately one-hour hike you can enjoy views of the spectacular landscape. On the mountains around Gandan Monastery hang prayer flags so that the wind may carry the prayers of the faithful. After an impressive day, you will finally return to Lhasa. Overnight in Lhasa.

Day 5 On the Friendship Highway to Gyantse

On the Friendship Highway towards Kathmandu, your journey continues to Gyantse. The journey takes you through one of the most spectacular and impressive landscapes in the world. Along the way, a detour will take you to a viewpoint near the sacred Yamdrok Lake where you can picnic and get a little legs. The turquoise blue lake, set in many color shades, is one of the four sacred lakes of Tibet and offers a truly spectacular sight. Via the Karola High Pass, which is over 5,000 meters high, you can finally reach the small town of Gyantse. More than in other cities, ancient Tibet has been preserved in Gyantse with its white houses and colored window cladding. Here you will visit the monastery complex Palkhor Chöde, an 18 religious and residential building in the old town. From the simple gods on the lower levels, one comes up to the Most High, the origin of all being. Whoever celebrates the stupa clockwise and shows his honor to the deities in each chapel of the four floors, can increasingly purify his consciousness during the ascent – at least faith. Overnight in Gyantse.

Day 6: The seat of the Buddha of the Endless Enlightenment

On the next leg on the Friendship Highway, travel to Shigatse and visit the Tashilunpo Monastery, which is also the seat of the Panchen Lama. He is considered the “born-again Buddha of the endless Enlightenment”. The monastery with the golden roofs was built in 1447 AD under the first Dalai Lama and represents the upscale Tibetan architecture as well as its architecture. Thousands of Buddhists come here every year. With around 600 monks, including 100 novices, the monastery is today one of the most important religious centers in Tibet. Later, continue your journey towards Mount Everest. The Friendship Highway winds from now on in serpentines over the passes Yalung La (4,520 meters) and Gyathso La (5,220 meters) to Xegar, your today's destination. Overnight in Xegar.

Day 7 Mount Everest – the roof of the world

Today is great: In the early morning you drive to the foot of Mount Everest, the highest mountain in the world at 8,848 m above sea level. Keep the camera ready and enjoy the truly majestic view in good weather. Afterwards you will visit the Rongbuk Monastery at a spectacular 5,250 m above sea level. It is the highest monastery in the world and only 40 km from Mount Everest. The king of all mountains and the other 8000s seem even more impressive here on the north side than from the south, as they rise directly from the highlands of Tibet. Overnight in Xegar.

Day 8: Road to the Border

Your journey on the Friendship Highway continues. Today’s stage also has some highlights to offer: a magnificent view from the Bhotekosi River deep into the valley, spectacular waterfalls and the ever visible Himalayan panorama. In the evening you will reach Kyirong, the last Tibetan place before the border with Nepal. Overnight in Kyirong.

Day 9 - Over the Friendship Bridge to Nepal

After breakfast, cross the border. There you have to do a few entry formalities, then it goes on to Kathmandu. Life is raging here! The Himalayan state is a paradise for mountaineers and trekking tourists. Their path sometimes leads them through crowds and traffic noise. In addition to eternally honking cars, free-running cows, stray dogs and leisurely driving oxcarts, rickety buses and cross-riding moped drivers sometimes provide an adrenaline rush. Kathmandu is also full of cultural treasures that you will discover during the coming days. But not anymore today. After arriving in Nepal’s capital, you will be taken to your hotel and relax from the long journey. Overnight in Kathmandu.

10th Day Time Travel to the Past & Kathmandu’s Greatest Sanctuaries

This morning, together with your guide, take a trip to the two nearby towns of Thimi and Bhaktapu. There are still numerous old houses in the traditional architectural style and life is mostly played out in the streets. In addition to the pottery, which is made in Thimi according to ancient methods, the colorful masks that are made here are known beyond the place. When visiting a workshop, get an idea of the artistry of the craftsmen and create your very personal souvenir. Then continue on to the old royal city of Bhaktapur. Like Kathmandu, Bhaktapur was once on an ancient trade route to Tibet that brought great wealth to the place. Numerous impressive buildings bear witness to this glorious past – the architectural legacy of the city is a UNESCO World Heritage site. With your guide you stroll over the central square of the city. Back in Kathmandu, visit Nepal’s most important Hindu shrine: the Pashupatinath temple complex dedicated to the god Shiva. Finally, visit the Boudhanat-Stupa. Overnight in Kathmandu.

Day 11 Excursion to the countryside: The Newar villages of Bungamati and Khokana

Start the day by strolling through Patan Durbar’s central square, where you’ll find a collection of finely crafted pagoda temples and stone statues. The old palace of the Malla kings and the ancient stone baths are each associated with legends and episodes of history. It will then be meditative at a sound bowl ceremony. Listen to the sounds of the Himalayan Bowls – sound bowls of different sizes that produce different tall notes. In the afternoon, visit Bungamati and Khokana, two small Newar villages just south of Patan. Both were founded in the 16th century and here too, the visit will feel like a journey back in time to the past. The place consists mainly of the brick houses typical for the Newari, decorative elements are the doors and especially the ornately carved windows. As you walk down the alleys from a viewpoint to Bungamati, you will encounter some locals. Not far from Bungamati is Khokana. In the evening, return to Kathmandu overnight in Kathmandu.

12th day India awaits you

Today it is time to say goodbye to the mountain peaks of the Himalayas, but your journey is not over. Your driver will take you to the airport, from where you will travel on to India. There you will already be expected and warmly welcomed. Delhi, the “city of two faces”, still forms the gateway to northern India today. The metropolis, which with its 17 million inhabitants is one of the largest cities in the world, is not only the most important economic and commercial center of the country, but also cultural center due to its long history. There is a lot to see here. After check-in at the hotel, the rest of the day is freely available to you. Use the time for first explorations on your own or to relax. Overnight in Delhi.

13th day Delhi's most beautiful sites & on to Agra

After breakfast, meet your guide to explore the Imperial City of New Delhi together. But New Delhi also offers fascinating historical sights: for example, with the 72 Metre-high Qutb minaret and the mausoleum of the great mogul Humayun, which is considered an architectural precursor to the Taj Mahal. Afterwards you will drive a bit through New Delhi and can, among other things, the National Monument India Gate, which is modeled on the Arc de Triomphe in Paris and commemorates the 1World War I was built, marvel. Afterwards, you will travel back in time in Old Delhi, where the Jama Masjid, one of the largest mosques in the world, testifies to Delhi’s past as the capital of Islamic India. Passing the Red Fort, the palace complex of the Mughals, your ride will take you to the Moonlight Square – Chandni Chowk – one of the oldest and busiest market squares in the city. Experience here the typical flair of an oriental bazaar on a bicycle rickshaw ride. At Raj Ghat, where the father of the nation was cremated, you can pay homage to Mahatma Gandhi. Then continue your journey and continue to Agra. Overnight in Agra.

Day 14 The fairytale Taj Mahal at sunrise

Once a small village on the banks of the Yamuna, thanks to the two great Mughal monarchs Akbar and his grandson Shah Jahan, the city developed into the second capital of the Mughal Empire. The Mughals were not only rulers, but also great builders and their best architectural wonders in Agra and its surroundings can still be admired today. You see the fabulous Taj Mahal with your own eyes. When early in the morning the fog hangs over the water basins of the garden and the sun rises slowly over the magnificent structure, the best time to visit the impressive monument is. You will also visit the monumental Fort Agra, which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site alongside the world-famous Taj Mahal, as well as the Itmad-ud-Daulah Mausoleum, nicknamed “Baby Taj”. On the way to the next stop of your trip, visit the ruined town of Fatehpur Sikri. It has been preserved almost ghostly to this day and combines two opposites: the figurative architecture of the Hindus and the geometrical architecture of Islam. In the afternoon you will reach Jaipur. Overnight in Jaipur.

Day 15: Mighty Amber Fort

This morning, another masterpiece of Mughal architecture awaits you: the imposing Fort Amber perched on a steep mountain slope north of Jaipur. With the Jeep you drive up to the main gate of the facility. Behind it you can expect the public audience hall, the magnificent Ganesha Gate and the Spiegelpalast. From the galleries and terraces you also have a wonderfully wide view over the valley and the mountains of the Aravalli chain. On the way back to the city you will see the “Palace of the Winds”, behind whose ornate facade the ladies of the court watched the bustle on the street. In Jaipur, visit the magnificent City Palace, which witnesses an era of abundance and in part still serves today as the residence of the former Maharaja, as well as the Jantar Mantar Observatory, only a few steps away, where you can admire the largest sundial in the world. However, the city has also preserved some craft traditions that have been passed down from generation to generation. You can observe this during a walk with an English-speaking specialist around Ghat Darwaza and Vishveshwar ji. Overnight in Jaipur.

16th day Farewell to India

Even the most beautiful journey has to come to an end. After breakfast, make your way back to Delhi, where you will be taken to the airport. Enriched by many exciting experiences from three fascinating countries make your way back home from there. We wish you a pleasant flight!
