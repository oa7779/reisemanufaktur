Guided Private Tour - The Natural Wonders of New Zealand

New Zealand Private Travel: New Zealand’s natural wonders will take you to this travel idea of Asia Special Tours. Ask here directly and experience your own dream journey.

In hardly any place in the world awaits such a scenic diversity as in New Zealand. On your round trip from north to south you will explore secluded coves, huge glaciers, dense rainforests, mighty volcanoes and idyllic farmland. Let yourself be carried away into the adventure of New Zealand. This tour is carried out with a private guide. You travel in a comfortable vehicle and you don’t have to worry about anything other than enjoying the journey to the fullest with all its natural wonders. Ideal for travelers who do not want to drive themselves and appreciate a German-speaking tour guide. Overnight at the Bed & Breakfast in New Zealand means a personal and exclusive experience. You will spend the night mostly in lovingly and high-quality restored, historic buildings with wonderful hosts.

Day 1 Welcome to Auckland

Auckland with its suburbs has about 1 million. Residents. It is the largest city in the country and spreads generously over a narrow landing strait between the Pacific Ocean and Tasman Sea. Your guide will welcome you at the airport. On a city tour you will get to know the highlights of Auckland, the “City of Sails”. Thousands of boats roam the Hauraki Gulf, the recreational paradise on Auckland’s east coast, on weekends. The Victorian-restored Parnell district with its beautifully restored colonial houses will please you as much as the 'War Memorial Museum' with its impressive collection of Maori art and cultural objects. Mount Eden, with its 200 meters tallest of the 63 extinct volcanoes on which Auckland is located, offers a magnificent view of New Zealand’s largest city. Overnight in Auckland.

2nd Day Hot Water Beach in Hahei

In the morning you will leave the city of Auckland. With its wildly rugged mountains, its rough and steep rock cliffs as well as the enchanting, south sea sandy beaches, the Coromandel Peninsula impresses everyone who visits it. Along the east coast, head to Tairua and start from there for a short drive to Hahei, near which is the hot beach of Hot Water Beach. Overnight in Tairua.

3rd day Natural paradise Coromandel peninsula by kayak

In the morning, a spectacular kayak tour awaits you along the famous Cathedral Cove. The “Te Whanganui-A-Hei Marine Reserve” offers a dreamlike natural paradise with endless beaches, islands and rock gardens. Paddle through ocean caves and, with a bit of luck, see dolphins, seals and other sea creatures. Go ashore on the beach of the unique Cathedral Cove and strengthen yourself with cappuccinos and hot chocolate. If you do not want to climb a kayak, your guide will certainly find an alternative. Overnight in Tairua.

Day 4 Tauranga and Rotorua

Today you will leave the Coromandel Peninsula and cross the Bay of Plenty, known for fruit cultivation, especially the kiwi fruit. In the port city of Tauranga, the largest shipping point for New Zealand fruit export, a detour to Mount Maunganui is recommended. You can hike or climb this cone-shaped mountain depending on your desire and enjoy the views, relax in New Zealand’s only saltwater thermal pool or admire the surfers. After that, the journey to Rotorua continues. At the end of the day, we have planned an evening with the local Maoris for you. You will be picked up from your accommodation and drive to a marae (Maori gathering place), experience the dances and songs of the Maori and take part in a hangi meal from the “Erdofen”. Overnight in Rotorua.

5th day Rotorua – Sky-high geysers and hot springs

Rotorua is known for its bubbling, geothermal activities. Millions of years ago, volcanic eruptions created a fantastic landscape of mountains, lakes and steaming earth. Experience dramatic geothermal energy here, recognizable by the typical sulfur smell. Today is at your disposal (Legal free day of the tour guide). Overnight in Rotorua.

6.-7. Day Famous Fireflies Cave in Waitomo and Waikato

Today it goes through the “King’s Country”, an important center of agriculture, industry and education. In Waitomo, visit the Footwhistle fireflies caves. With a guide, explore the cave and see some fireflies up close. After the tour, they will end the excursion with a Kawakawa tea in a tyic Ponga hut. The journey continues on a scenic side route to Egmont National Park. The area is extremely fertile and the main area for dairy farming. Overnight in Waikato.

8th day of free time at Mount Taranaki

Today is at your disposal in New Plymouth. Many old buildings have been preserved and the attractive location between the sea and picturesque cones of Mount Taranaki makes the city popular not only with hikers and wave riders. With its green areas, black beaches, museums and galleries, it invites you to explore. Overnight in Taranaki.

Day 9 - New Zealand's oldest city to Wellington

Via Stratford, travel along the coast to Wanganui, one of the oldest cities in the country, located on the largest navigable river and equipped with numerous parks. Through the fertile farming areas of Levin, the journey continues south along the wild Kapiti coast to Wellington, the capital of New Zealand. Overnight in Wellington.

Day 10 Explore Fascinating Wellington

Today is at your disposal. We recommend that you visit the superb, modern "Te Papa" National Museum, which mainly impresses with various interactive programs about the natural history and culture of New Zealand. You can walk in the footsteps of European pioneers, gain deeper insights into native flora and fauna, learn about Maori history and culture and admire their ornate carvings (admission free). Also worth seeing is the surroundings of the Parliament with the former government buildings, of which the oldest – from 1876 – is considered the second largest timber construction in the world. Opposite is the current government. Architecturally striking is the modern cultivation, known as the “bee basket”. In addition, you should not miss a trip in the capital by cable car – the historic tram that goes up to the Botanical Garden. From here there is a beautiful view of the city and its port. Overnight in Wellington.

Day 11 - Ferry to the South Island to Nelson

You will leave Port Nicholson to take the Interislander ferry southwest to cross Cook Road. 23 km wide is the narrowest point of this waterway, which connects the Tasman Sea with the Pacific Ocean. The long entrance to the Queen Charlotte Sound makes this crossing a particularly impressive experience. From the pretty village Picton, located at Queen Charlotte Sund, you will first drive to Blenheim in the Marlborough plain, an area of viticulture. The viticulture in this region began in the 70s, when the winemakers of “Montana Wines” from Auckland were looking for new cultivation areas. The composition of the earth, the high sunshine duration, a long autumn and a cool winter are excellent conditions for a first-class wine, especially Sauvignon Blanc. Continue on to Nelson, also known as the sunniest city in New Zealand. Overnight in Nelson.

Day 12 The Bays of Abel Tasman National Park

Today, free time is called for. Optionally, you can take a trip to Abel Tasman National Park. The special charm of the national park lies in its varied landscape: cliffs, sandy beaches, karstized limestone hills, regenerated jungle, waterfalls and wide river estuaries. Overnight in Nelson.

Day 13 Wild West Coast of the South Island

From Murchison, follow the Buller Gorge and experience an impressive change of vegetation until you reach the subtropical rainforest of the wild west coast. Striking on the way is the lush green of the "Mamaku" tree fern. If you have enough time, you can watch a large colony of seals ("NZ Fur Seal") on a short hike at Cape Foulwind near Westport. Between Westport and Greymouth, a wild romantic landscape awaits you: Parallel to the coast runs the up to 1500m high folded mountain belt of the Paparoas. Canyon-like gorges provide the only access to the rugged mountainous lands of this national park. Famous are the limestone rocks of the "Pancake Rocks", near the small village of Punakaiki. We continue to the small coastal town of Hokitika. Overnight in Hokitika.

Day 14: The mountains of the Southern Alps

Along the west coast you drive through evergreen rainforest to the glacier region of the southern Alps. As a natural weather barrier, the very close high mountain ridge is known for extensive precipitation. Above the snow line, large masses of ice collect, forming fast-flowing valley glaciers. The approximately 12 km long Fox and Franz Josef glaciers - unique in temperate latitudes - slide down to an altitude of 300 m above sea level. Overnight at Fox Glacier.

15th day Helicopter over the glaciers

In the morning (according to the weather conditions) you take a scenic flight by helicopter over the glacier world of the Southern Alps, which you will certainly not soon forget. From the air you have an impressive view of the glacier tongue, which reaches far down into the valley. After landing on the glacier, you can take hikes and turn to the numerous photo opportunities. Depending on how time permits, you can take a walk to Lake Matheson, whose calm waters reflect the wonderful mountain scenery. The onward journey takes you past Westland National Park near Haast to Wanaka. Overnight in Wanaka.

Day 16 The lakes and fjords around Queenstown

Today you will first go to Queenstown. Due to its beautiful mountain location on the shores of Lake Wakatipu, this small resort has developed into a popular tourist center on the South Island. The long sea stretches between steeply rising mountain ranges. Then continue to Fjordland National Park, New Zealand’s largest and at the same time undeveloped national park. Hardly any landscape deserves the term wilderness more than fjordland. To the west, 14 fjords penetrate deep into the primordial forests of a rugged Alpine world. Snow-covered mountain peaks rise over alpine grass mats up to 2700 m. Overnight in Te Anau.

17th Day By boat to the unique Flora & Fauna of Doubtful Sound

Today, drive to Lake Manapouri in Fjordland National Park. Since the rainfall here is one of the world's top, a very special vegetation has developed. Declared a World Heritage Site, this rainforest area with its numerous fjords is the attraction of many nature lovers. The Doubtful Sound is less frequented, as it is not directly accessible by car. First, you will cross Lake Manapouri. Via the Wilmot Pass you can reach by bus Deep Cove and thus the Doubtful Sound. In this remoteness, you will embark on a boat expedition to the animal kingdom of New Zealand. Surrounded by steep mountains, lush green vegetation and in the midst of a spectacular play of colors, you will get to know the flora and fauna of the fjordland. One often encounters bottlenose dolphins, and seals are numerous on isolated rock formations. Muttonbird, Bellbird, Tui, Shearwater and native pigeons can be easily spotted on a landing. Overnight in Te Anau.

Day 18: The Contrasts of McKenzie Country

Continue inland through McKenzie Country. Sparse rainfall makes this highland appear as a barren steppe landscape. Wide “Tussock” hallways that span the hills like a carpet transform the landscape into a peculiar inner alpine basin landscape. In this land full of contrasts, Lake Tekapo is the end of a busy day. Overnight at Lake Tekapo.

19th Day Paradise Lake Tekapo

Today is at your disposal in Lake Tekapo (Legal Free Day of the Guide). On Lake Tekapo stand the picturesque 'Church of the Good Shepherd' and the bronze statue of a shepherd dog. These monuments are very impressive for their remoteness and history. We recommend a visit to the thermal baths (optional) or a hike up to Mt John. In good weather, the snow-capped 3000s of the Southern Alps are reflected in the turquoise-colored, mirror-smooth lake. Overnight at Lake Tekapo.

20th day of Christchurch

Today you will experience an impressive change of vegetation. They first reach highland farms. The journey then continues through Canterbury, the fertile heart of the South Island, where vast farmlands stretch like a patchwork. The final destination is Christchurch, the metropolis of the South Island. For a hundred years, Christchurch was characterized by beautiful gardens and parks as well as impressive architecture – much of it reminds of England. Unfortunately, an earthquake in 2011 fell victim to many of the city’s treasures. A lot of work is still being done on reconstruction. Overnight in Christchurch.

21st day End of travel and return or onward flight

Today your New Zealand trip ends. You will be taken to the airport to board your onward or home flight from there.
