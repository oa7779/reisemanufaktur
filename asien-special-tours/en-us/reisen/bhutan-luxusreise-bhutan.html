Day 1: Arrival in Thimphu

Even arriving by flight to the Buddhist kingdom gives an idea of the magnificent landscapes you will be traveling in the coming days. In good weather, when you land, you will have a breathtaking view over the majestic mountain ranges of the Himalayas. Upon arrival, you will be expected by your personal guide and will be taken to the capital to your hotel. Thimphu is located at an altitude of about 2,320 meters in a fertile valley on the banks of the Chu Thimphu River. It is one of the smallest capitals in the world and – next to the seat of government of the island state of Palau – the only capital without traffic lights.
After you have fortified yourself at lunch, you can already start your first explorations. You have a choice: visit Bhutan’s National Monument, a memorial to peace and prosperity dedicated to the penultimate king of the country, or visit the Museum of Traditional Textile Art, the Folklore Museum or the National Library for a first glimpse into the country’s culture. Overnight in Thimphu.

Day 2: Explore around Thimphu

Today you will explore the surroundings of Bhutan’s capital. In the morning you will first travel to Pangri Zampa, a special monastic school where Buddhist monks are introduced to the art of astrology. Around 100 students study here the traditional teachings about stars and planetary constellations that play an important role in Bhutanese religious and everyday life. Another interesting monastery in the area is Chagri Dorjeden, which you can reach after a short walk through the valley. It is located on a slope and offers a beautiful view of the area. If you are interested, you can still get an insight into the local craftsmanship in the afternoon. Let us show you, for example, how traditional paper or hand-rolled incense sticks are made. Overnight in Thimphu.

 

Day 3: Hidden Phobjikha Valley

After breakfast, leave Thimphu and head towards Gangtey. The route takes you over the 3,050-meter-high, breathtaking Dochula Pass. The ridge is lined with countless colorful prayer flags that flicker in the high wind. With a clear view, you will once again have an outstanding view of the surrounding peaks of the Himalayan Mountains. Their goal is the Phobjikha glacier valley, which is under special protection by the Royal Society for the Protection of Bhutanese Nature, in particular due to its biodiversity – where 14 endangered species live worldwide. With a bit of luck, you’ll even see one of the shy black-necked cranes between the end of October and the end of March. However, the valley is not only home to many natural treasures but also impressive monasteries – such as Gangtey Goemba. This is one of the oldest monasteries in the country and overlooks the valley from a height of 3,000 meters. Overnight in Gangtey.

Day 4: Travel to Bumthang

Today, a long but very entertaining drive through some of Bhutan’s most beautiful landscapes awaits you past river valleys, forests and small farming villages. Along the way, stop at Chendebji Chörten, where legend has it that the spirit of a demon killed at this point is included. Another stopover is in Trongsa. Here you will visit the monastery fortress Trongsa Dzong, which lies in a rugged, rocky landscape against the backdrop of the Black Mountains. Finally, via the impressive Yotongla Pass, you can reach the picturesque Bumthang region. Upon arrival, an unreally beautiful landscape awaits you with emerald green fields, waterfalls and rivers. The Bumthang region in northern Bhutan comprises four mountain valleys, Choekhor and Chumey, Tang and Ura. On the fertile soil of Bumthang, farmers, buckwheat, rice and potatoes cultivate. The valley is also famous for its Yatra weavings. If you wish, you can visit a workshop and learn more about the production of this traditional craft. Overnight in Bumthang.

Day 5: Exploring the Bumthang Valley

Today you will explore the picturesque Bumthang region. First, visit the Wangdi Choeling Palace, the former summer residence of Bhutan’s first and second kings, and the associated prayer wheels. Continue on to Kurjey Lhakhang, a monastery where the remains of Bhutan’s first three kings rest. According to legend, Guru Rinpoche, the founder of Buddhism in Tibet, meditated here leaving an imprint on a rock. It was here that the first of the three temples of the sanctuary was built in the 17th century. After so much spirituality, at the end of today’s excursion you turn to secular realms again: You visit the Red Panda White Beer Brewery – and with a little luck also meet the people who brought the beer to Bhutan: Fritz Maurer came to the country in the mid-sixties – due to a quirky job advertisement: The King of Bhutan was looking for a cheesemaker for a year. In addition to Swiss cheese and beer, Maurer now also makes apple juice and honey, which he sells in a small Aunt Emma shop. Overnight in Bumthang.

Day 6: The Fertility Stamp of the “Divine Mad One”

Early in the morning you leave for Punakha. The ride takes you over steep mountain passes and past beautiful landscapes. Along the way, visit the Temple of Fertility, Chimi Lhakhang. On foot, a small path will take you about 45 minutes through rice fields until you reach the 15th century sanctuary. The history of the temple dates back to St. Drukpa Kinley, who received the nickname “Divine Mad One” for his unorthodox methods of propagate the Buddhist faith through singing, humor and offensive behavior. He is said to have introduced in Bhutan, among other things, the practice of painting phallic symbols on the house walls to drive away evil spirits – this custom you will see on your way to the temple itself many times. Pilgrims who visit the monastery are traditionally hit on the head with a 25 centimeter long wooden phallus. Overnight in Punakha.

Day 7: Exploring Punakha

After breakfast, take a small hike along rice, chili and cabbage fields that will take you to Khamsum Yulley Namgyal Chörten, a temple built by the mother of the reigning king, representing peace and stability in a fast-paced time. Back in the valley, the highlight of Punakha awaits you: its unique monastery fortress, which lies at the confluence of the Po Chu and Mo Chu and is popularly called the “Palace of Happiness”. At the end of the day, your path will take you to the Sangchen Dorji Lhundrup Chholing nunnery, which sits above the valley. Here you have the opportunity to talk with some nuns and experience a moment of inner peace. The main temple is dedicated to the bodhisattva of compassion, Avalokiteshvara. Next to the temple, there is an ornate stupa and finely crafted slate carvings with religious motifs – a place of silence and spiritual retreat with a wonderful view of the Punakha Valley. Overnight in Punakha.

Day 8: Return to Paro

You will return to the starting point of your journey, Paro. The city is characterized by whitewashed houses, whose wooden windows are usually painted in an ornate manner. Take the rest of the day slowly: take a walk through the charming small town or indulge in the spa area of your hotel. For interesting insights into the history and culture of the Himalayan Kingdom, a visit to the National Museum Ta Dzong, which is housed in a former watchtower, is particularly suitable. A whole floor alone is dedicated to original stamps of the country, some of which are even made of silk. Directly behind the Amankora Lodge also begins a beautiful hiking trail through dense forests. Overnight in Paro.

Day 9: Hike to the “Tigernest”

Last but not least, a highlight of every Bhutan trip awaits you today - a hike (about 4-5 hours in total) to one of the most famous sanctuaries in the country. The Tigernest Monastery (Taktshang) nests about 900 meters above the valley on the rugged rock faces of the Himalayas. The name comes from a legend according to which the second Buddha, Guru Padmasambhava, rode here on the back of a tigress to meditate. The place is a famous pilgrimage site for Buddhists and one of the most sacred places in the Himalayas. After the hike, enjoy the breathtaking view over the Paro Valley. Overnight in Paro.

Day 10: Paro - Departure

After ten days in Bhutan, your round trip through the fascinating state in the Himalayas ends today. From the experiences here, far from everyday life, you will live for a long time. Your driver will take you to Paro airport for your trip home.
