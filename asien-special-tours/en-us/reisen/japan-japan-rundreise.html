Japan Tour - In the Land of Smiles

Japan Tour: Welcome to the Land of Smiles! Your individual Japan round trip with Asia Special Tours, your Asia expert. Ask now!

Itinerary & services

On this trip, in addition to the most important sights of the mega-metropolis of Tokyo and the former imperial city of Kyoto, authentic insights into the culture of the Japanese await you. Experience the countryside and its people up close: watch the Sumoringers exclusively during their training and stay in a traditional guest house in the Japanese Alps. Explore Kyoto by bicycle and spend a night in the monastery at St. Koyasan. Here you get your own picture of Shingon Buddhism and can observe the monks at their morning prayers - a truly spiritual experience. You will also explore the historic port city of Hiroshima, which has much more to offer than a tragic past. On a food tour in Osaka, you will end your trip with great pleasure. An all-round successful journey through the “Land of Smiles”, on which especially the friendly people will inspire you. This trip offers you plenty of space for exploration on your own, but can also be carried out in the company of a tour guide – according to your ideas and wishes.

1st day Arrival in Japan

Irasshaimase and welcome to Japan! Your journey begins in Tokyo, where you will be welcomed and warmly greeted by an English-speaking assistant. This accompanies you to the vehicle available for you, which brings you directly to your hotel. The rest of the day is at your disposal: time to make first explorations on your own in this breathtaking world metropolis. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 2 - Multifaceted Tokyo

Today you can look forward to a day full of varied impressions in the exciting metropolis. Your guide will wait for you in the foyer of your hotel and will be the first to take you to Tsukiji Market. Even though the world-famous fish market has been relocated to the island of Toyosu, the Tsukiji outdoor market with traditional shops and world-class sushi restaurants remains a lively hub for visitors and locals to enjoy delicious food and buy local specialties. Stop at one of the restaurants for a super-fresh sushi breakfast. Then leave the market behind and make your way to Hamarikyu Park, a true oasis in the middle of a sea of skyscrapers. The perfect place to dangle your soul over a cup of tea while the guide tells you something interesting about the Japanese tea tradition. In Asakusa, visit the Sensoji Temple, Tokyo’s oldest Buddhist temple. It is also home to the oldest geisha district in the city. The visit to Meiji Shrine will round off your day impressively. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 3 - A sumo training live

Early in the morning, your guide will pick you up and take you to the center of Japan’s sumo world. All wrestlers (rikishi) belong to a so-called unit (heya) and live, train and eat there. Today you have the opportunity to visit one of these Heya and experience their sumo everyday life. A Yokozuna, a great champion, can earn an income of up to 17,000 US$ per month. After this immersive afternoon experience, visit Yanaka Ginza, one of the last shitamachi (old quarters) in Tokyo, where food stalls, specialty stores, tea houses and small workshops line up. In Ueno, one of the busiest tourist hotspots in the northwestern part of Tokyo, you can relax as you stroll through the beautiful park. However, the district is mainly known for its large number of art galleries, history and science museums. Continue your adventure to Ameyoko, a street market with hundreds of merchants and market cries promoting traditional sweets, among others. Try a Japanese cream delicious here. After this snack, you have the opportunity to make the rest of the day spontaneous and flexible according to your wishes. Overnight in Tokyo.

Day 4 The Temples of Kamakuras

Today, leave the metropolis of millions behind and travel with your guide to Kamakura, which is located southwest of Tokyo in Sagami Bay. The Kotoku-in Temple houses the “Great Buddha”, one of the most important depictions of Buddha Amitabha. Another famous sanctuary is the Hase-dera with its over nine meters high Kannon statue made of solid wood. It is one of the 33 temples on the Bando Sanjusankasho pilgrimage route dedicated to Kannon, the goddess of mercy. In the afternoon, your trip will take you to the Hakone region, located in the mountains and volcanoes of Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park. This evening, a special highlight awaits you: your overnight stay in modern Ryokan. This exclusive Japanese inn combines traditional design with luxurious comfort and offers you a unique experience. A stay at Gora Kadan is the perfect opportunity to experience Japanese hospitality in a modern interpretation. Of particular note is the multi-course Kaiseki dinner, which you can enjoy in a stylish ambience. Afterwards you will have the opportunity to relax in a private or public onsen. Overnight in Hakone.

Day 5 Hakone and the Japanese Alps

Without guidance

Spend the morning exploring the surrounding area with your included Hakone Pass, which offers you a variety of ways to get around. A ride on the Hakone Tozan Switchback train will take you to the mountains, from where breathtaking views of the surrounding valleys open up. Then the cable car recorded in the Guinness Book of Records will take you up to Mount Owakudani, past steaming sulfur fields, hot springs and volcanic rivers. Arriving at the top, Hakone’s culinary speciality awaits you: Kuro-Tamago – an egg boiled in hot sulphur springs and said to provide seven more years of life. In the afternoon, continue your trip to Takayama in the Japanese Alps. First, the world-famous Shinkansen will take you up to 270 km/h to Nagoya, where you will change to the Hida Wide View Express. This ride is one of the most beautiful train routes in Japan and takes you through picturesque valleys and along crystal clear rivers. Arriving in Takayama, let the day end relaxed. Overnight in Takayama.

Day 6 Discover Shirakawago

Without guidance

After a typical Japanese breakfast in your ryokan, make your way to the picturesque village of Shirakawago on your own. Nestled in the idyllic landscape of dense forests stand here the traditional Gassho-zukuri houses with their striking, up to 60 degrees steep, reet-covered roofs that defy even the heavy snow masses of winter. Stroll through the charming alleys and immerse yourself in the village’s farming past. A particularly impressive view of the village is offered by the Shiroyama observation platform. In the afternoon, return to Takayama. We recommend a leisurely stroll through the historic Shitamachi, where centuries-old merchant houses and traditional sake breweries shape the streetscape. Visit one of the breweries to taste the local sake, or try the famous Hida beef sushi at one of the specialty stalls. Overnight in Takayama.

Day 7 Kyoto – Discover the Cultural Capital of Japan

Without guidance

Today you will travel by train to Kyoto, Japan’s cultural capital. For over a thousand years the imperial court lived here, and even today magnificent palaces, impressive shrines and ornate gardens characterize the cityscape. Unlike many other cities, Kyoto was largely spared war damage, allowing it to preserve its unique cultural heritage. To this day, the city is considered the epitome of Japanese tradition and aesthetics. Upon arrival, you will be left with the rest of the day to discover the city in peace on your own. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 8: The most beautiful sides of Kyoto

In the morning, meet your guide in the foyer of your hotel and set off together to explore some of Kyoto’s most impressive attractions. The prelude is the famous Fushimi Inari Shinto Shrine, known for its bright orange torii gates that stretch up the mountainside in long tunnels. Then take the train to the Sanjusangendo Temple. Inside the world’s longest wooden building, you’ll find 1001 artfully crafted golden Kannon statues. Continue by taxi to the Nishiki market. In the covered market hall, shops and stalls line up, from fresh fish and tofu to traditional handicrafts. In the afternoon, visit the impressive Nijo Castle, built in the early 17th century as a residence for the shogun. At the end of the day, take the metro to the eastern foothills of Kyoto, where you will visit the Nanzenji Zen Temple. Since 1291, this peaceful retreat serves as a meditation site for monks. After an eventful day, say goodbye to the guide and you can either return to the hotel or continue exploring Kyoto on your own. Overnight in Kyoto.

Experience 9th day tea ceremony and explore Kyoto by bike

Today a very special experience awaits you: dedicate yourself to Japanese traditions and participate in a tea ceremony. You will go on your own to the private residence of a master of tea ceremonies, where today you will be introduced to the high art of tea ceremony under expert guidance. Try the traditional preparation of the tea and enjoy it, of course. Then explore the city on the favorite vehicle of the local population. Meet your guide at the station and pick up your bike together – the tour can begin! First, drive through the Gion district, which was once designed to accommodate visitors to the shrine. Over the centuries, however, it developed into the most famous geisha district in all of Japan. Continue along the Shirakawa Canal to the park of the Imperial Palace of Kyoto, which for centuries was the residence of the imperial family (1331 - 1868). From there, drive along the Kamo River back towards the train station. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 10 Kyoto or Nara – according to your wishes

Without guidance

This day is at your disposal to explore the former imperial city further completely your own ideas. There is still much to discover! Alternatively, take a day trip to Nara. Leave the city and take the express train to Japan's former capital (710-784) in about 45 minutes. From the former splendor testifies to today large temples, shrines and ruins. On foot, explore the sights and you can watch tame red deer during a walk through Nara Park. Some of the highlights belong to the UNESCO World Heritage Site. Particularly impressive is the “Daibutsu”, the largest bronze Buddha statue in the world, venerated in the Todaiji Temple, one of the largest wooden buildings in the world. Along the way to Kasuga Shrine, thousands of stone and bronze lanterns create a very special atmosphere. Enchanted by the impressions, you will return to Kyoto in the afternoon. Overnight in Kyoto.

Day 11 Monastery Accommodation on Koyasan

Without guidance

Exciting information about Japanese Buddhism can be found at Koyasan, the next destination of your journey. Start the day early for the scenic ride to the sacred mountain. This train ride is one of the most beautiful in Japan. On the plateau of the Koyasan mountain group there are numerous temples, where many monks live. Mount Koya is the center of Shingon Buddhism, a major Buddhist current introduced in Japan by Kobo Daishi in 805. Since then, more than 100 temples have been built in the streets of Koyasan. The most important of these are the Kongobuji, the main temple of Shingon Buddhism with Japan’s largest rock garden and Okunoin, the largest cemetery in Japan with the mausoleum of Kobo Daishi. Beginning at the Ichinohashi Bridge, the cemetery stretches two kilometers with over 200,000 tombs of feudal rulers, well-known monks and even Japanese entrepreneurs. Tonight you will spend in a Shukubo, a Japanese temple accommodation. For dinner, enjoy Shojin Ryori, a traditional Buddhist vegetarian dish. Overnight in Koyasan.

Day 12 Morning Prayer and Hiroshima

Without guidance

Morning hours have gold in their mouths: If you want to experience the monks at their morning prayers, you should get up this morning, as they usually start around 06:00. You will also have the opportunity to take a look inside the prayer room (please refrain from seeing photos in the prayer room). After this spiritual experience, strengthen yourself with a Shojin Ryori breakfast. Well strengthened you continue your journey by train to Hiroshima. The port city, whose roots date back to the 16th century, is known worldwide mainly because of the atomic bombing on 6 August 1945. But even beyond this tragic chapter, you can feel how the city, like a “phoenix from the ashes”, has blossomed again to new life. Today, Hiroshima is considered one of the most relaxed cities in Japan. At the same time, life pulsates here on the extended strolling miles, in restaurants and art museums. After arriving, you still have some time to convince yourself. Overnight in Hiroshima.

Day 13 - The island of Miyajima

Without guidance

During your stay in Hiroshima, you should definitely plan a trip to the island of Miyajima. These can be reached after a short ferry ride from the mainland. The island, which has long been considered sacred, is home to trustworthy macaques and other wild animals living in the original forests. However, Miyajima is particularly famous for the Itsukushima Shrine, whose buildings are built on pillars, so that they seem to float in the water at high tide. The red Torii Gate is the striking landmark of the complex. After exploring the island, return to Hiroshima and be sure to visit the Peace Museum. Learn more about the history and impact of the nuclear attack on Hiroshima here. The museum collects memorabilia and reports from survivors and actively promotes international peace. Shinkansen continues on to Osaka, the culinary capital of Japan. Overnight in Osaka.

Day 14 Culinary Highlights for Connoisseurs

Explore Osaka on your own today. In the late afternoon, an evening summer tour of the city awaits you. Your guide will take you on a “food tour” where your taste buds will get their money’s worth. No city offers a better opportunity to experience the extravagant and colorful world of Japanese cuisine than Osaka. The city’s motto is “kuidaore,” which means “eat until you drop.” Your first stop will take you to a hidden restaurant that offers a modern twist on the classic tempura preparation. In addition to the traditional prawn tempura, you can also expect creative variations such as asparagus tempura with cheese. Continue to a restaurant specializing in high-quality Wagyu beef. After a walk through the lively alleys, you will reach your final destination: a cozy Teppanyaki restaurant. Here you will experience how Gyoza and Negiyaki are prepared right before your eyes on the hot metal plate – a culinary highlight at the end of the evening. Well strengthened you stroll a little further through the district with its countless small bars and immerse yourself in the lively nightlife of Osaka. Overnight in Osaka.

Day 15 Goodbye to Japan!

The time until your planned airport transfer is free. Enjoy a relaxing breakfast, take a last walk through the area or get some more souvenirs for your loved ones at home. Your break from everyday life is coming to an end. Even as the holiday comes to an end, the precious memories, beautiful photos and exciting stories of this trip will always stay with you. Look forward to sharing your experiences with friends and family. A shuttle bus will take you to the airport in time for your return flight. This ends your unforgettable trip to Japan with Asia Special Tours. We wish you a pleasant journey home!
