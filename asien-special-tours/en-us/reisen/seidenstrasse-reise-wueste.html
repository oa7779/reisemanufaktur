Iran Travel - Between Desert Sands and Mountains

Iran journey between desert sands and mountains. Embark on an individual Silk Road round trip through Iran with Asia Special Tours. Now ask me.

Itinerary & services

Join us on new paths through grandiose landscapes with imposing mountains and deserts. An abundance of treasures of Islamic architecture, great treasures of the past and a very warm population await your visit. Memories of the fairy tales from 1001 Night will awaken as you walk through Isfahan or visit the historically unparalleled, magnificent Persepolis! While the flair of Shiraz also captivates you thanks to the verses of its great poets, Yazd looks like from another world with the fire temple Zarathustras. They are housed in sometimes simple, country-typical accommodation and experience Iran in all its facets, away from the classic tourist trails. With your adventure spirit, you look behind the scenes on this round trip and let yourself be infected by the fascination of the ancient Persian Empire!

1st day Arrival in Tehran

Welcome to Iran. They are already expected at Tehran International Airport and taken to the national airport. From here you will fly on to Mashhad, where you will be received by your guide. Together you drive to your hotel. Depending on the arrival time, you can already start with first sightseeings or rest in the hotel. Mashhad is the second largest city in Iran. Overnight in Mashhad.

Day 2 Visits to the Holy City of Mashhad

After breakfast, you will first visit the city of Tus, an ancient city in northeastern Iran. The most famous citizen of the city is Ferdowsi, one of the most famous Persian poets. He wrote the epic Shahnameh, the Book of Kings for over 30 years. Then return to Mashhad. Mashhad is considered one of the seven holy sites of Shia Islam because it is home to the holy Goharshad Mosque. The city is a political and religious center; annually they visit numerous Shiite pilgrims. The special religious significance of Mashhad for Iran stems from the fact that the (eighth) Imam Reza buried here is the only one of the twelve Shia imams whose tomb is located on Iranian soil. Together with your guide you will visit the sacred district and feel the very special atmosphere of this place (only outdoor visit possible). Overnight in Mashhad.

3rd day drive to Golestan National Park

With the first imposing impressions you leave Mashhad today and drive to Golestan National Park, the oldest national park in Iran. The area covers about 90 000 hectares and the park contains a large variety of plants and animals. The diverse landscapes from Caspian forests to steppes are also notable. In the late afternoon you will reach the Turkmen Ecolodge in the northwest of the National Park. Overnight in Golestan National Park.

4th day walk in Golestan National Park

Convince yourself of the beauty of the national park on a hike. The extremely diverse landscape zones include grass steppes, bush areas and rock regions. To the west of the park, visit the Mirza Bayloue Wildlife Museum, where the diversity of wildlife and native plants is displayed. To the east of the park, you can, with a bit of luck, observe deer, steppe sheep and wild goats in the Alme Valley. Overnight at Golestan National Park

Day 5: Continue to Tehran

After breakfast, leave for Tehran. A long day of travel lies ahead of you, but the varied overland trip will give you deep insights into rural life in Iran. Keep the camera ready and enjoy the scenic beauty of the country. Of course, you can always stop and take a break if you want. In the evening you will reach Tehran, the bustling metropolis at the foot of the Elburs Mountains. What a contrast to the last few days! An endless sea of houses - as far as the eye can see. Overnight in Tehran.

Day 6 Magic of 1001 Nights in Tehran

The capital of Iran spreads its treasures in front of you: good starting points for a sightseeing tour are the Golestan Palace and the Archaeological Museum, where you can admire the golden foundation tablets from Persepolis. At the Golestan complex, the former seat of government of the Qajarian kings, the neo-baroque style of the palaces immediately catches the eye. Inside the complex you can walk through gardens with roses, plane trees, fountains and watercourses. You can also visit the Museum of Jewels – one of the most important museums of its kind. The outstanding collection includes gems, jewelry, ornate furniture, weapons and other invaluable devices. Afterwards you will be taken to the airport and fly on to Kerman. Known for its carpet production for centuries, Kerman looks back on an eventful history. Already in the writings of ancient Greece, the place is mentioned under the name Karamani. World traveler Marco Polo was very fascinated by the city. In his travel descriptions he describes the quality and beauty of the carpets from Kerman. The city then became the center of carpet production. Overnight in Kerman.

Day 7: The Wind Bumps of Shahdad

Today, your journey will take you to the village of Shahdad on the edge of the Lut Desert. Nearby is the hill Gamdom Beryan, which is considered the hottest place in the world with a temperature of up to 67 degrees Celsius in the shade. About half an hour's drive from Shahdad, you will visit some of the fascinating Jardangs, Kalouts or wind bumps - a geomorphological, table-shaped form of erosion in loose sediments created by wind and water erosions over time. From a distance, they resemble buildings. The area also invites for small hikes. Also, do not miss the cypress of Sirch, which with an estimated age of 4,000 to 4,500 years is considered one of the oldest living things on earth. According to a legend of the Zoroastrians, the tree of Zarathustra was planted. Therefore, it is considered a pilgrimage site for Zoroastrians. Overnight in Shahdad.

Day 8: Mahan and Rayen back to Kerman

After breakfast, head to Mahan, where you will visit the Shahnematollah-Vali Mausoleum, the burial place of the great Sufi guide and the garden Baghe Shazdeh, considered the most beautiful garden in the country. With a size of 5.5 hectares, it is a true oasis in the middle of the desert. The palace pavilion in the center of the garden served as the prince’s summer residence. The rectangular garden is surrounded by a wall and consists of several terraces with water basins and pavilions. A central watercourse with fountains leads to the castle, using the natural slope of the soil for its operation. Afterwards you will go to Rayen, where you will explore the citadel. The citadel is well preserved and gives a good impression of the former structures of Bam. In the evening, return to Kerman. Overnight in Kerman.

9th Day Gigantic Bakhtegan Salt Lake

A long day lies ahead of you – but even today the scenic beauty will make you forget the long journey time. Of course, do some sightseeing en route, such as the huge Bakhtegan Salt Lake, Iran’s second largest lake. Although it has dried up for years, persistent rainfall always creates a small area of water, in which the opposite mountains are reflected. Due to the high content of minerals, the water colors in a beautiful violet clay. You will also visit the ruins of Sarvestan Palace, built by the Sassanid ruler Bahram in the 5th century. Arriving in Shiraz, you will be taken to your hotel. How about ending the day with a little refreshment? Try Faludeh. This particular ice cream is made from glass noodles, rose water and lime juice. Also very popular are safraneis and vanilla ice cream with carrot juice. Overnight in Shiraz.

Day 10 Shiraz – City of Roses and Nightingales

Many people associate Shiraz with two things: it is the city of poets and the city of gardens and parks. Start your visits today with a walk to the impressive Karim Khan fortress in the center of the city. Parallel to the fortress walls are the Vakil Karim Khan Zands residence buildings. The Vakil complex with the Vakil Mosque and the Vakil Bazaar is one of the most famous urban complexes of Islamic architecture in Iran. The domed Vakil bazaar is one of the most beautiful in the country and still offers a rich range of spices, textiles and crafts. The Shah-e-Cheraq Shrine is the third most important Shia sanctuary in Iran. The dome is built in bud form, typical of Shiraz, and the interiors are decorated with magnificent, sparkling mirror mosaics. In the Nasr-ol-Molk mosque, especially the colorful stained glass windows are worth seeing and in the Nasr-ol-Molk house the precious interior decoration from the Qajaren period. Of course, you will also visit some of the city’s numerous gardens such as the Narenjestan Garden (“Garden of Organ Gene Trees”) with the Zinat-ol-Milk House and the Eram Botanical Garden of Shiraz University. Overnight in Shiraz.

Day 11 Excavations of Persepolis and Pasargadae

Getting up early is worth it – because there is another highlight of every trip to Iran waiting for you. You will leave Shiraz and visit the Old Persian Persepolis (UNESCO World Heritage Site), the ancient Achaemenid capital of the Persian Empire, first built by Darius I in 520 BC. Even today, the ruins of the former palace city bear witness to its former splendor. After a detailed visit, continue to Pasargadae, the capital of the residence of the founder of the Achaemenid Empire, Cyrus the Great (599 – 539 BC). There you will visit the extensive facility. Your journey continues through the desert, past impressive mountain landscapes to Yazd, one of Iran’s oldest cities and built in traditional clay construction. Overnight in Yazd.

Day 12 In the footsteps of the Zorastriers

After breakfast, you will first visit the important fire temple of the Zoroastrians, whose open flame has been kept burning continuously for more than 100 years. Afterwards, your sightseeing tour will take you to the Jameh Mosque (Friday Mosque), the Amir Chakhmaq complex and the “Towers of Silence”, also called Dakhmeh. From the minaret of this complex you have a beautiful view of this fascinating mud brick town. Then a very special adventure awaits you. You leave civilization and reach the desert Mesr. Your accommodation for this night is easy, but the location makes all the more compensation. The deep black night with the glittering starry sky is enchanting! Take this special atmosphere deep within you. Overnight in the Mesr Desert.

Day 13: Desert Adventure in the Mesr Desert

Enjoy the clear morning hours and the silence in the desert. After breakfast, it goes on the back of a camel comfortably and stylishly through the desert. If you wish, you could also take a Jeepsafari. Then continue over the Central Iranian mountain range and the foothills of the Zagros chain to the cultural metropolis of Iran – to Isfahan. Welcome to 1001 nights! Overnight in Isfahan.

14th day Cultural metropolis of Isfahan

To call Isfahan a wonder of the world is no exaggeration. The city is full of splendor and culture. During the reign of the Safavids, Isfahan was chosen as a city of gardens and palaces. As one of the most important trading centers, Isfahan was even called “half of the world” at that time. Your visit starts best at Imam Khomeini Square. It is the second largest place in the world – after Tiananmen Square in Beijing. Around him you can see the imposing Imam Mosque, the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque (also called Women's Mosque, since ladies were also allowed) as well as the palaces Chehel Setoon and Alighapoo. The name of the latter means "High Gate", because the palace forms the entrance to the palace district, which lies west of the square. Afterwards you will stroll comfortably over the legendary bazaar, where you can try your luck in trading and haggling. Let the day end relaxed! How about a visit to an enchanting tea house or one of the modern cafes where youth like to meet? Overnight in Isfahan.

15th day Further visits to Isfahan

After an extensive breakfast, we continue with Isfahan, because you can not get enough of this city. Together with your driver guide, you will visit the historic Friday Mosque, a 1,000-year-old association of pre- and Islamic architecture and art, the Christian Armenian Vank Cathedral in the district of Djolfa built by Shah Abbas the Great for Armenian settlers. Then visit the Music Museum of Isfahan. Music plays a major role in the history of Iran. The museum exhibits over 300 classical and traditional musical instruments, giving you insight into the country’s musical traditions. Overnight in Isfahan.

Day 16: The Houses of Kashan

We will head to the final destination of your trip to Iran. You will drive to Kashan, a city on the desert edge of the large salt lake. Along the way, stop at Abyaneh, where the village’s red-brown mud brick houses nestle against a slope. During a walk you can hear the villagers speak an ancient Persian dialect. A small mosque with a blue dome also invites you to visit. Then you reach Kashan. In no other Iranian city is there such a high density of citizen houses. Because Kashan was a city of rich merchants thanks to the carpet trade, rose water and ceramics production. Its spacious construction, which includes water basins and its own hammams, and the rich furnishings of the rooms are absolutely worth seeing and give an insight into the life of the Iranian bourgeoisie. Especially magnificent is the house Boroujerdi with a typical Iranian garden. In addition, you will see the Sialk settlement mound with traces of settlement from the 7th millennium BC and the fin garden. It is said to be the oldest existing garden in Iran (from the 6th century BC). Overnight in Kashan.

17th day Departure from Kashan

On your journey back to Tehran, you have some time to stop in Qom. In the metropolis of Shia Islam, you will have the opportunity to feel the special atmosphere in the sanctuary Fatima Masumeh. She belonged to the prophet family and the tomb, which was erected in her honor from 819 onwards, has been further expanded over the years, as well as lined with tiles and mirror mosaics. Back in Tehran, unfortunately, it is time to say goodbye to this fascinating country. They are taken to the airport and fly back home with 1001 new impressions in their luggage. The memories and photos of your exciting time in Iran will bring you a lot of joy in everyday life at home for a long time.
Note: Non-Muslims in Qom are not allowed to enter the interior of the shrine, only an outside visit is possible.
