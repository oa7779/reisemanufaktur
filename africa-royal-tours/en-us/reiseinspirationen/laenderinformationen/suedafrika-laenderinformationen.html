South Africa - Country information from A to Z

Current information: Use water consciously

The filling levels of the water reservoirs are currently stable, and Cape Town's government continues to focus on the responsible use of water. We would like to ask you to take part in the local water saving measures. Small changes can make a big difference: take shorter showers, avoid unnecessary water consumption while brushing your teeth and remember to use towels and bed linen for several days during your stay.

Their contribution helps to conserve the valuable resource of water and to support Cape Town's beautiful, sustainable initiatives. Thank you for sharing a conscious and respectful approach to nature with us!

For further information, see www.capetown.gov.za/thinkwater

Best time to travel


Kruger National Park: Depending on the interest of the traveler, the most suitable travel time is also different. If one wants to observe the many newly born young animals, the best time to travel is during the rainy summer months (from mid-December to mid-March). However, the risk of malaria increases during this visit period. If you want to see the most diverse animal species in the smallest area, the winter months (from mid-June to mid-September) are ideal. Due to the absence of rain, the animals collect concentrated at a few remaining water holes and can be observed particularly well.  


Cape Town: For a city trip to Cape Town you should choose a travel time during the summer months (October to April). However, bathing in the Atlantic is not suitable (average 17°C). The winter months are also suitable for a visit, as the temperatures in winter climb up to 25°C during the day and it does not get too cold at night.

Entry requirements

EU citizens do not need a visa for a vacation stay of up to 90 days. The passport must be valid for at least thirty days after return and must be machine-readable. In addition, the travel documents must have at least 2 free pages for visas even on departure. The same applies to children's passports, which are only valid with a photo. When traveling from South Africa to other countries with a subsequent return to South Africa, travelers should therefore be careful to have free pages in the passport for all entry and exit stamps. Children's entries in a parent's passport are no longer valid since 26.06.2012. Each child needs his/her own ID document. Non-EU citizens must apply to the relevant embassy or consulate for a visa. The passports may not have been extended in principle.

Instructions for the entry of minors:

On 26.05.2014 new regulations for traveling minors came into force. Persons under the age of 18 must have an international birth certificate on entry and exit from 01.06.2015. Unless the country of birth issues international birth certificates, a national birth certificate and a certified translation into the English language must be presented (http://www.dha.gov.za/files/Brochures/Immigrationleaflet.pdf). If a minor does not travel in the company of both caring parents, it must also be demonstrated that the parent who is not present either agrees with the trip (affidavit), passport copy and contact details of the parent who isn't present) or that the consent declaration is not required (judicial decision on sole custody or death certificate).

Persons traveling with minors who are not their own children must provide a copy of the child's full birth certificate, as well as affidavit, passport copies and contact details of both parent and legal representative of the child.

In addition, minors traveling alone must present a letter of confirmation and the contact details and a passport copy of the person to whom the minor is to travel in South Africa.

It is strongly recommended to provide English-language explanations, documents or translations. In this context, only the Department of Home Affairs (www.dha.gov.za) and the South African missions abroad. Please note that the documents on entry with minors in practice are already available from many airlines (e.g. E. g. SAA) can be checked at check-in and a check-in is refused in case of missing incomplete documents.

Food & Drink

The cuisine in South Africa is influenced by specialties from India, Malaysia and Europe depending on the region. There is a wide selection of fruits, vegetables and seafood that are always freshly prepared.

Enjoy also the many good typical meat dishes of the South Africans. Here you must not miss to participate in one of the typical "Braais" (grill evenings) to taste grilled meat and sausages. "Biltong" (air-dried meat) as well as the local specialty "Mieliepap" / "Pap" (maize porridge with meat) are not missing on any trip.

Meanwhile, South African wines are among the best in the world. In any case, try the wines from the respective region or visit one of the renowned wineries for a wine tasting. You can also enjoy the beer and fruit juices from South Africa.

In good restaurants – but especially in high season – the reservation of a table in advance – is always recommended for highly sought-after restaurants several months before arrival. A special feature in many places is that you can bring your own bottle of wine if you pay a fee for opening the bottle. This "corkage fee" is between ZAR 25.00 and ZAR 80.00. Note that on Sundays no alcohol is usually sold.

The tap water in South Africa has a very good quality, for your own safety we still recommend you to drink packed water from the supermarket.

Fauna

The wildlife alone is worth a trip to South Africa and once experienced, it will captivate you. In South Africa's national parks, some of which extend beyond national borders to neighbouring countries such as Mozambique and Zimbabwe, you can experience the Big Five in your natural environment. The "Big Five" wildlife includes lions, leopards, elephants, rhinoceros and buffalo. In many national parks you can also marvel at shark and whale species – they combine the "Big Seven".


Flora

With its numerous national parks, South Africa is an absolute paradise for plant lovers. Along the garden route with its lush vegetation and the many botanical gardens, over 24,000 different plant species can be discovered. The wildflower is particularly impressive after the rainy season in the months of August and September.

Photographing/Filming

Think of enough storage space and spare batteries. Camera accessories are available in many shops, but are mostly more expensive than in Europe. For perfect shots, we recommend a photosensitive camera. A good binoculars is indispensable for safaris and animal watching. Photos of locals are usually not a problem. Keep in mind, however, that you travel South Africa as a guest and that every resident always meets you with respect. In return, one should treat the locals equally respectfully and ask for permission before pressing the trigger.


Money/currency

The currency is the South African rand. 

Bank opening hours are usually open daily from 9.00 a.m. to 3.00 p.m. in all cities and larger places and on Saturdays from 9.00 p.m to 11.00 p.m . Note that banks in rural areas often only have short opening hours. The easiest way to get cash are ATMs, in remote areas ATMs or travel cheques are less common here is recommended cash.EC cards that carry the V-Pay sign are not accepted in South Africa. For larger purchases, make sure that you receive an invoice with declared VAT (VAT), which you can get refunded at the airport if it is goods that you carry out. You will also find all relevant banks at all airports.
You can withdraw money from many ATMs using EC and credit cards. Make sure that you are always standing alone in front of the switches and do not accept any offered help!

The import of foreign currency is unlimited and subject to declaration from an equivalent value of USD 10,000. The import of national currency is allowed up to an amount of ZAR 25,000 and is subject to declaration for higher amounts. The export of Rand is limited to 5,000 ZAR, from foreign currency to the amount declared on entry.

Geography

South Africa is three times the size of Germany (about 1.22 million km2) and has a variety of geographical and climatic zones - from the Karoo semi desert to the KwaZulu Natal coast and from the Cape of Good Hope to the Mpumalanga bushland in the east.


Check Baggage

At the departure airport you will usually receive all boarding passes for your booked flights. After landing in Johannesburg, Durban or Cape Town, after completing the entry formalities, follow the signs to the baggage belt and pick up your luggage. After that, you pass the customs check and return your luggage to the "Re-check-in/Bag Drop" counters. It is only necessary to transfer the luggage, no new check-in. Make sure not to remove the baggage tag. Then go to the terminal for domestic flights and go through the security check before proceeding to the departure gate. To facilitate this process, it is possible to book an assistance service.

On the return flight you do not have to pick up your luggage in Johannesburg in most cases, since the customs control takes place only in Germany.

The procedure described above does not apply to international forward flights. In this case, your baggage will be checked directly to your international destination, provided both flights are booked on the same ticket. The customs check for your baggage is only carried out at your international destination.

Health/vaccination

When entering Europe directly (direct flight!) no compulsory vaccinations are required. When entering or transiting (even by changing aircraft) through a country declared by the WHO as a yellow fever region (e.g. A valid yellow fever vaccination is required for Ethiopia, Kenya or Zambia. This also applies to a transit-related interim stay of more than 12 hours. ==References====External links== Vaccination is also recommended in case of shorter transit, since in individual cases the period of stay in transit cannot be traced or delays can occur. The border authorities were encouraged to implement these regulations consistently and travelers with a corresponding travel profile were either sent back directly without proof of vaccination or to separate them at their own expense for 6 days (quarantine). Especially in the region of the Kruger National Park (especially in the north) there is an increased risk after heavy rains (especially during the rainy South African summer months and shortly afterwards).

In addition, all standard vaccinations should be reviewed or refreshed in accordance with the vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute. We recommend a consultation with your family doctor.
There is a pharmacy emergency service in larger cities. Pharmacies usually also carry drugstore articles and are called "Apteek" or "Chemist" in South Africa.

In case of emergency or for medical care you should visit the so-called "Medi Clinics". These private clinics have a very good standard and are comparable to those in Germany.


Clothing

In the summer months, airy and comfortable clothes are recommended, along with an umbrella or a rain jacket. To protect against mosquitoes, long, body-covering clothing is recommended in bright colours. For the cooler evenings and especially for the cold nights in the winter months a jacket or sweater should not be missing. You should also have a slightly finer evening wardrobe especially for hotel or restaurant visits.

Especially in the summer months you should carry sunscreen with you due to the high UV radiation with high protection factor as well as a headgear. Think also of an appropriate mosquito protection.

Climate

The seasons are exactly opposite to those in Europe due to the position in the southern hemisphere. Overall, South Africa has a temperate, pleasant climate and is very good to travel all year round.  
The South African Spring (mid September to October/November) offers mild temperatures and spring blooms along the west coast and the Garden Route.  
In summer (November/December to mid-March) is main travel time. The climate is warm; it can get very hot during these months, especially in the Kruger National Park and on the east coast. During this time, there may also be light to heavy rains.   
Autumn (mid-March to mid-June) is temperate and characterised by cooler temperatures.  
In the South African winter (mid June to mid September) there is a warm and dry climate comparable to the German spring. Especially in mountain regions, it can become cool to very cold in the morning and at night.  

The weather varies from province to province. In the Mpumalanga and Kwazulu Natal in the east it can be very hot in the summer months; the humidity can reach up to 90%. In addition, summer rain often occurs, but the Cape Province and the Garden Route are excluded.  

Average sunshine per day: approx. 8 hours.

National parks

Western Cape

  • Agulha's National Park: Coastal and marine reserve with rich flora and fauna, fascinating archaeological sites and historic shipwrecks.
  • Bontebok National Park: Home to endangered species such as Buntbock antelopes and Cape mountain zebras, embedded in the gentle hills of the Langeberg Mountains.
  • Table Mountain National Park: A symbol of South Africa with diverse flora and fauna, the famous Table Mountain and the Cape of Good Hope.
  • Karoo National Park: Wild reserve in the semi-desert of the Great Karo with mountain zebras, skirmishes and pointed rhinos, surrounded by mountainous and barren desert landscape.
  • Knysna National Lake Area: Coastal and marine reserve for endangered species such as seahorses, with impressive rock formations and the picturesque Knysna Lagoon.
  • Tsitsikamma National Park: Coastal and forest landscape with gorges, rocks and tidal pools. Highlights are the Storms River Mouth and numerous hiking, snorkeling and diving opportunities.
  • West Coast National Park: Coastal and marine protected area with species-rich birds, ideal for water sports and bird watching.
  • Wilderness National Park: Landscape of rivers, lakes, lagoons and forests, surrounded by Fynbosvegetation. Perfect for hiking and canoeing.

Free State
Golden Gate Highlands National Park: Dramatic sandstee information in the Maluti mountains, habitat for antelopes, bearded vultures and other animal species. A paradise for hikers.

Northwest Province
Pilanesberg National Park: An artificially created game reserve that houses the Big Five as well as numerous bird and plant species.

Province of Mpumalanga/Limpopo
Kruger National Park: South Africa's most famous wildlife reserve with incredible diversity: over 147 mammal, 507 bird and 114 reptile species, including the Big Five.
Mapungubwe National Park: Combination of wildlife reserve and archaeological site. Significant for medieval gold finds and as a habitat for many mammals.
Marakele National Park: Stunning mountain landscapes with the Big Five and a variety of other species.

Eastern Cape and Northern Cape

  • Addo Elephant National Park: Founded to protect the elephant population. Today, there are more than 420 elephants living here, as well as rhinoceros, antelopes and the fascinating pill turner beetles.
  • Camdeboo National Park: Semi-desert landscape with the impressive Valley of Desolation and its 100 meters high stone columns.
  • Mountain Zebra National Park: Protected area for the rare mountain Zebras, embedded in a breathtaking mountain landscape.
  • Augrabies Falls National Park: Home to the impressive Orange River gorge, a variety of birds, antelopes and reptiles, and a unique semi-desert landscape in the border area between the Kalahari and the Great Karoo.
  • Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park: Rusty dune landscape with Kalahari lions, leopards, antelopes and over 200 bird species.
  • Namaqua National Park: Coastal and Marine Reserve, known for over 3,500 plant species that transform the landscape into a colorful flower sea.
  • Ai-Ais Richtersveld Transfrontier Park: Desert and mountain reserve with bizarre rock formations and diverse wildlife along the Orange River.

KwaZulu Natal

  • iSimangaliso Wetland Park: A UNESCO World Heritage Site with crocodiles, hippos and an impressive bird world around Lake St. Lucia.
  • Hluhluwe-Imfolozi Park: One of the oldest reserves in Africa, forest hiking trails and observation possibilities for birds, rhinos and other animals – embedded in the mountainous landscape
  • Ithala Game Reserve: Varied landscape with a rich bird world as well as wide and pointed rhinos.
  • Mkhuze Game Reserve: Forest hiking trails and observation possibilities for birds, rhinos and other animals.
  • Ukhahlamba-Drakensberg Park: South Africa's highest mountain range with breathtaking landscapes, ideal for hiking and nature lovers.

Politics/Society

South Africa is also called "Rainbow Nation" due to its ethnic diversity. About 51 million. People of different nations and cultures shape the entire country. The population can be divided into four groups: Black Africans (79%), Whites (9%), Coloreds (9%) and Asians (3%). The white population comes from French, Dutch, British and German settlers.

South Africa is one of the wealthiest countries in Africa and has a versatile and modern economy. After the 2010 World Cup, a very good infrastructure is one of the special features in the country.

The time of the outlawed apartheid system began in 1948 under the National Party. It was not until 1990 that the black population turned to politics after years of struggle. After the state elections in April 1994, the transition to democracy was relatively peaceful; according to the Constitution, South Africa has been a presidential democracy with federal elements since 1997.

Today there are three main parties in South Africa: the ANC (African National Congress), the DA (Democratic Alliance) and the EFF (Economic Freedom Fighters). In addition, there are some smaller marginal parties. The South African government was last re-formed in May 2014 and ANC chairman Jacob Zuma was elected for a term of office of 5 years.

The key figure in South Africa is undoubtedly Nobel laureate Nelson Mandela: the country's first black African president. On 05.12.2013 he died in Johannesburg.


Safety instructions/road conditions

South Africa is a safe travel destination in large parts and also pleasant to travel. In some parts of the country and parts of the city (especially in townships and after dark) the crime rate is increased. In principle, one should always observe the current safety instructions of the Federal Foreign Office. We will be happy to advise you on any further questions.

As in any other travel country, valuables should never be carried openly. Valuables as well as passports, travelers' cheques and airline tickets should be delivered to the reception or stored in your room safe.

Tip: For security, we recommend that you keep a copy of your travel documents and passports separately from the originals.

Make sure that the car is always well locked, do not leave any valuables in the car.    

On South Africa's beaches you should note the references to sharks (especially on the coast of Kwazulu Natal). Well visited beaches are usually equipped with steel nets. Watch out for the partly very strong ocean currents while swimming.

Driving in South Africa: left-hand traffic in South Africa. An international driving licence is required in combination with your national driving licence. There is compulsory seat belting and alcohol ban during the journey (allowable alcohol limit 0.5%).

The directional speed lies in Innerorts at 60 km/h, on the road 80-100 km/h and on motorways 120 km/h. Compliance with traffic rules and speed limits is regularly monitored by the police.

An approaching traffic light is shown by the road reference "ROBOT" . At intersections, the car has an entrance, which is the first to reach the intersection.

The right lane is used for overtaking, but on multi-lane roads, especially in the Western Cape, the left lane is also overtaken. Slow vehicles drive on the stand lane so that faster cars can pass by. When passing by, one usually thanks with the short turn on of the warning flashing system.

The road network is well developed and sights can be easily found by a good signage. In rural areas, roads are not busy and a pleasant driving experience is guaranteed. Note that some roads charge tolls.

The petrol stations are easily visible and accessible in cities and along the motorways. They usually open 24 hours. Cash payment is recommended. IMPORTANT: Do not underestimate the driving distances in South Africa! Refuel wherever possible, especially before traveling longer distances. Make sure you always have enough drinks.

In the city: Make sure that your car is locked correctly when you park for safety reasons. When exploring the township regions, we recommend taking part in a guided tour. In larger cities so-called "Park Marshals" coordinate the parking spaces and usually charge a parking fee of approx. ZAR 5.00 per 30 minutes. The local park guards, who guard the cars parked in the evening, should give a tip (between ZAR 2.00 and ZAR 3.00).

In the national parks: The maximum speed limit is 40-50 km/h. Leaving the car is not allowed.

Behaviour of police controls: When driving in a rental car, traffic controls and speed measurements by the South African Police (SAPS) or the local traffic police (Metro Police) are always to be expected nationwide.    

If you are stopped and asked to pay a penalty, please note the following information:

  • The handing over of cash to traffic police or other officials on the spot of a traffic control is strictly prohibited.
  • Legitimate penalties should only be paid at the nearest police station or the nearest local court ("magistrate" court) for issuing a receipt. 
  • Tolls are charged exclusively at the official toll points. Especially on the roads towards Kruger National Park, unfortunately, cases are repeatedly registered in which police insist on cash payment of a local penalty.

In part, traffic signs have a different meaning than in Germany. For example, a green-enlighted bend arrow at the traffic light - in contrast to the rules in Germany - does not mean that neither pedestrians nor oncoming traffic must be taken into account. Only when the green bend arrow flashes can pedestrians and oncoming traffic no longer be expected.

Please always note the current travel and safety instructions of the Federal Foreign Office.

Power supply

The current voltage is 220/240 volt. The sockets are three-pin and plugs have a different format to the German plugs. Here an adapter is necessary, which is available in electrical shops or supermarkets. Many accommodation units provide their guests with an adapter free of charge or have already equipped the rooms with additional European sockets. 

In recent times, South Africa has repeatedly experienced the so-called load-shedding phenomenon: if electricity grid congestion threatens, certain areas are taken precautionaryly from the electricity grid. As a rule, this is placed in marginal times (e.g. E. g. 4-6 in the morning or 22-24 in the evening) and announced in advance. However, deviations may always occur. ==References====External links== For example, the V & A Waterfront in Cape Town are excluded.

Larger hotels and restaurants now have an emergency supply of generators. In the other accommodations you can help yourself during this time with candles and flashlights, which are now part of the basic equipment in almost all hotel rooms.


Telephone/Internet

The code for Germany is 0049.

To make cheap calls from South Africa, send messages or go online, we recommend buying a local SIM card (e.g. the providers Vodacom, MTN, Cell C and Virgin Mobile). A card costs about 0.5 euros. The "Air Time" credit can be charged in supermarkets or gas stations.

Conversations abroad can be conducted by hotels, public telephone booths (with coins or telephone cards) or by post. Telephone cards can be purchased at Telkom, post offices or supermarkets.

Many accommodations offer fast Internet connections or Wi-Fi. In larger cities you can go online in tourist information or internet cafes.
