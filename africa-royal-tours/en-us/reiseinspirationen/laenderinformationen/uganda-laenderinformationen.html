Uganda - Country information from A to Z

Best time to travel

In principle, Uganda can be visited all year round, the great rainy season lasts from April to May, the small rainy season from October to November/December. However, nature has its own rhythm and does not follow a strict schedule, so seasonal deviations are always possible. Due to the tropical climate and the almost daily, short rains, the land is very green and the fauna unique. Rainfalls are more frequent in the south of the country, which is why a visit to the Bwindi Impenetrable National Park in the rainy season should be well considered.
Depending on the interest of the traveler, however, the most suitable travel time is different.

Entry requirements

German nationals need an entry visa. The visa application is online on the website visas.immigration.go.ug ==References== In this case, scanned passport copies, passport pictures, yellow fever vaccination evidence and other evidence, if any, shall be uploaded.

The visa fee – currently $50 (US-$) for a simple tourist visa – depends on the type of visa and is displayed in the application process, while online payments usually incur 3% handling fee. For yellow fever vaccination, see also "Medical advice". The authorised entry period may differ from the application.

Non-EU citizens must apply to the relevant embassy or consulate for a visa.

Food and drink

The cuisine in Uganda mostly consists of the basis Matoke (cooked banana), to which there are usually fish, meat, vegetables and various sauces as a side dish. Also, you often get potatoes, sweet potatoes, rice and corn porridge.

In any case, as a snack you should also try a cooked maniocnoll or the popular Rollex (omelette made from egg in flat bread with tomatoes). Also recommended is the fish directly from Lake Victoria, especially in Jinja, Entebbe and the region around the Murchison Falls National Park.

Drinks include all soft drinks, as well as mineral water and fruit juices. Or try one of the beers of the Ugandan breweries or the traditional soft drink Chibuku (drink prepared from manioc, millet or corn).

Drinking water: for your own safety, we recommend you to have packed drinking water from the supermarket.

Typical fruit varieties include pineapple, mango and bananas, the Jackfruit and Papaya. You should also try a fresh avocado.

Fauna

Uganda's diversity of animals is very rich and especially rare animals such as the shoebill stork and the mountain gorillas can be seen in Uganda. In the wild you can find elephants, lions, buffaloes, hyenas, occasionally geopards and leopards, various antelope species, warthogs and many birds in all national parks. In Murchison Falls NP and in the Kidepo Valley NP you will also find the rare Rothschild giraffe.

In addition to these animals in the parks, there is a rhinoceros project, which should resettle the formerly resident broad-nosed rhinoceroses, because they were threatened with extinction. The stars of the animal world are the many different primates. Mountain gorillas, chimpanzees, sea cats and other species of monkeys can still be experienced in Uganda up close and in the wild. Bird lovers also get their money's worth because Uganda offers a unique variety of birds.


Flora

The main vegetation in Uganda consists largely of small tropical rainforests and wetlands in the south and west of the country. In addition, there are in the higher areas around Mt. Elgon, the Ruwenzori Mountains and the Virunga volcanoes, the mountain forest characteristic of Uganda. At the western border of the country runs the Central African Rift, which strongly emphasizes the panorama in this region. The north and northwest are characterized by semi-deserts and Dornbuschsavanne. Uganda is home to over 20,000 plant species. Particularly worth seeing is the fauna in the Ruwenzori Mountains, which was named UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994.

Photographing/Filming

Think of enough storage space and spare batteries. Camera accessories are available in some shops in Kampala and Entebbe, but are mostly more expensive than in Europe.

A good binoculars is indispensable for safaris and animal watching.

Taking photos of locals is usually no problem, as the people there are very friendly and accommodating. Remember, however, that as a guest you travel to Uganda and that every resident always meets you with respect. In this respect, one should treat the locals equally respectfully and thank them with words.

Attention: Photography and filming of military installations, soldiers, police officers, security buildings is strictly forbidden. This can be designed widely, so you should also be careful not to make landscape images with farms or similar motifs. This also applies to pictures taken with the mobile phone. In the event of an infringement, they may lead to an arrest.

Money / currency

Official means of payment is Uganda-Shilling.

In most upscale accommodations, VISA and Mastercard are generally accepted. You can also use € or USD for tips. At airports, banks and exchange offices (Forex offices) money can be exchanged in local currency. The larger hotels also offer currency exchange.

Larger banknotes will only be changed upon presentation of a passport. Travel checks are almost nowhere accepted anymore. In larger cities you can withdraw money from the banks by credit card, and in better hotels credit cards (mostly Visa and Mastercard) are accepted as means of payment.

In Uganda there is no obligation to report or restriction on the collection (import and export) of foreign currency or Ugandan cash. Where appropriate, restrictions in the country of departure or destination should be observed (e.g. Compulsory to report from € 10,000 for trips from or to the European Union). The exchange of euro into national currency is possible at Entebbe International Airport, Kampala and in larger cities. US dollars ($) are only accepted or changed nationally if they are new notes (from 2006). Travel cheques achieve an unfavourable exchange rate and can only be redeemed in larger cities.

Cash can be withdrawn from ATMs of different banks with the Visa card, at ATMs of Stanbic Bank and some other banks also with the EC card (Maestro). This possibility is not always available. The maximum amount that can be deducted with credit or EC card varies between 250,000,- and 700,000,- UGX per day. The most practical thing is to bring cash in USD.


Geography

Uganda is about 241,550 km2 in size, which corresponds to almost two thirds of the area of Germany. Uganda is bordered to the north by South Sudan, to the east by Kenya, to the south by Tanzania and Rwanda, and to the west by the Democratic Republic of Congo. From north to south, the land is crossed by the Central African trench. The landscape changes from tropical mountain forests in the west of the country to tea plantations and savannahs in the east of the country, because here the Central African rainforest meets the East African savannah.


Health/vaccination

The proof of yellow fever vaccination for all travelers from the age of one year onwards is mandatory upon entry and also medically useful due to the repeated outbreaks. Without the appropriate vaccination certificate, you are denied entry. Yellow fever vaccine is not always available in Uganda. Uganda is still demanding a booster dose every ten years. The Federal Foreign Office also recommends that the standard vaccinations be reviewed and completed in accordance with the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute for Children and Adults.
Rabiestimmunglobulin is not safely available in the country.
Due to the second Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, all passengers arriving in Uganda via international airports or via a national border to the DRC are currently required to fill in a health questionnaire and, if necessary, will be subject to a further survey/investigation. In case of confirmed suspicion of Ebola, extended measures, e.g. Quarantine to go out.

Malaria prophylaxis is recommended for certain regions. In addition, all standard vaccinations should be reviewed or refreshed in accordance with the vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute. We recommend a consultation with your family doctor or a tropical physician.

Since medical care is not comparable to German standards, a well-equipped travel pharmacy should be carried along.

Especially in the summer months you should carry sunscreen with you due to the high UV radiation with high protection factor as well as a headgear. Think also of an appropriate mosquito protection.

Bilharziosis: Avoid bathing in the wild. Not only do crocodiles and hippos live in most lakes and rivers, but there is a high risk of contagion with bilharziosis, especially in standing waters.

  • Please wash your hands before trekking.
  • Please don't leave any garbage behind - "leave nothing but footprints"
  • At the beginning of your visit, the Ranger will lead you to the spot where the gorillas were sighted the day before. From this place you will follow the traces of the gorillas.
  • If you approach the gorillas, your ranger will let you know. You will then leave your day backpacks behind and cover the last piece with a drawn camera.
  • Please try to be quiet while you are in the forest. This gives you greater opportunities to observe the world of birds.

If you're with the gorillas:
  • Keep at least 7 meters away from the gorillas. This is used to protect gorillas from infection with human pathogens.
  • Do not leave your group if you are near the gorillas.
  • Please try to be as quiet as possible if you are near the gorillas.
  • Nevertheless, of course, it is fine to ask questions to your ranger with a muted voice.

Gorilla Trekking

The total population of mountain gorillas is estimated to be around 880 worldwide and about half live in the Bwindi Impenetrable National Park in Uganda, declared a World Heritage Site by UNESCO. The rest of the population lives near the Virunga volcanoes in Uganda, Rwanda and the DR Congo. You should be physically fit for gorilla tracking, because depending on the whereabouts of the gorilla, you can also travel for up to eight hours. The area is rather impervious and mountainous (as the name "impenetrable" says). The minimum age for Gorilla tracking is 15 years.

On the way to the gorillas:

If you feel sick or have an infectious disease and are already in the National Park, please volunteer. If your travel plan allows, an alternative visit will be arranged for you or you will get your money back according to the UWA (Ugada Wildlife Authority) guidelines.

The maximum number of visitors per gorilla family must not be exceeded in order not to disturb the gorillas too much and to minimize the risk that the gorellas will become infected with a human disease.

  • Please do not eat or drink while you are near the gorillas.
  • If you feel the urge to cough or sneeze, please turn your head away from the gorillas to prevent infection.
  • Sometimes the gorillas get very close. In this case, follow the instructions of the gamekeeper, bend your head "humid" and don't look the gorilla in the eyes. Wait until the gorillas get away again and don't run away.
  • Photographing with flash is not allowed. When you take photos move slowly and carefully.
  • Don't touch the gorillas, they're wild animals.
  • Visitors can spend a maximum of one hour with the gorillas. If the gorillas get nervous, their ranger will end the visit earlier.
  • Please try to be as quiet as possible after your visit until you are at least 200 meters away from the gorillas.

Clothing

It is recommended to wear airy and comfortable clothes that are easily washable, along with an umbrella or rain jacket. Long clothing with bright colours is recommended to protect against mosquitoes. Natural fibers and durable materials are best suited. For the cooler ones
Evenings and especially for the winter months should not be missing a jacket or sweater and if necessary a cap/headgear.
Especially for the gorilla safaris you should wear clothes in green, brown or khaki tones. Solid footwear is to be taken to gorilla trekking please. Gloves (to push branches, if necessary with thorns aside) and gaiters (against wet feet in rain) are recommended. Ideal are long-sleeved tops and trousers with long trouser legs. Please also pack a daily backpack, for a low fee (about 10 USD) you can hire a carrier that will take it from you. Please be sure to ensure sufficient sun protection. Waterproof clothing is advisable.
For chimpanzee trekking and sea cat trekking comfortable, sporty and above all completely closed shoes are sufficient.


Climate

The tropical climate is characteristic of Uganda. The average temperatures can range from 15 °C in the highs and 27 ° in lowers. The climate of Uganda can be divided into two different periods.
The dry seasons are in the months of January and February/March as well as June-September, in which light rains can occur. The main rainy seasons are in April-May and October-December. Please note that temperatures in the tropics drop significantly at night, so you can pack a sweater or something like that.

Rwanzori Mountains National Park: The third highest mountain in Africa was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994 and offers many opportunities for active holidaymakers. The area characterized by glaciers, rivers, lakes and moors is the most vegetative area on earth and worth a visit.      

Lake Mburo National Park: The animal-rich habitat of open grassland, acacia forests, swamps and several small lakes is ideal for hunting and walking safaris. On Lake Mburo, the lake's name-giving lake, there are boats with a very nice view.  

Murchison Falls National Park: The main attraction of the park are the waterfalls. The Nile falls here over a 7 m wide rock slab into the depth and causes loud and impressive noise. The park includes Red Shield giraffes, elephants, lions, antelopes, Ugandan kobs, hyenas, river dwellers and some species of monkeys.  

Kidepo Valley National Park: The National Park, which is dominated by impressive mountain massifs, is part of the Sudanese Trockensavanne. The rather remote but very original and wild park makes your stay in Uganda a very special experience. You can only travel by plane.

Mount Elgon National Park: Located directly on the Kenyan border, the National Park is something for experienced climbers, as Mount Elgon, which is over 4320m high, offers a number of challenges when walking and climbing through the various vegetation zones.

National parks in Uganda

Bwindi Impenetrable Forest National Park: The highlights of the park are the mountain gorillas, which are native to the rainforest. Best time to travel: June to September and December to February.

Mgahinga Gorilla National Park: The national park bordering Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of Congo hosts the part of the Virunga volcanoes in Uganda. In addition to the unique, isolated and wooded volcanoes, the mountain gorillas are an absolute highlight.

Kibale Forest National Park: This park has the highest primate density worldwide. In a remote location you have the chance to meet sea cats, bear monkeys as well as chimpanzees and other monkey species on foot. In addition, rare forest elephants live there, which you discover with a little luck. Over 140 different butterfly species and over 300 bird species make the visit very exciting.  

Queen Elizabeth National Park: A varied national park with wetlands, rivers and lakescapes, which mainly houses Nile Warans and hippos. In addition, there are elephants, buffaloes, hyenas, leopards and lions in the savannah areas and bush landscapes. The lions living in the Ishasha sector are known for their climbing skills because they live on the trees.  

Policy/economy/society

The approximately 35 million inhabitants of Uganda consist of more than 40 different peoples with different customs, cultures and languages. The peoples and tribes are very proud of their traditions and the different kingdoms. More than 60% of the population belong to various Bantu people.

For decades there were violent conflicts and reigns of terror in Uganda, which cost more than 300,000 lives between 1971 and 1986. People are now very grateful for the peace and security in the country.  

Since 1986, President Yoweri Museveni has been President of the Presidential Republic and has since passed many comprehensive reforms leading to promising growth in the country. The Ugandan government was last re-formed in February 2016 and NRM-O Chairman Yoweri Museveni was elected for a term of office of 5 years.


Safety instructions

Uganda is a safe travel destination in most parts and also pleasant to travel. In some parts of the country and parts of the city, the crime rate is increased. In principle, one should always observe the current safety instructions of the Federal Foreign Office. We will be happy to advise you on any further questions.
As in any other travel country, valuables should never be worn openly, especially in places with larger collections of people. Valuables as well as passports, travelers' cheques and airline tickets should be delivered to the reception or stored in your room safe. After dark, with the exception of the larger streets in the center of Kampala, which are considered relatively safe, walks should be avoided.
Tip: For security, we recommend that you keep a copy of your travel documents and passports separately from the originals.

Ethnic conflicts in the Eastern Congo are currently causing a strong influx of refugees from the Eastern Congo to Uganda. This is linked to a strong military presence in the border region.

Since it cannot be ruled out that unrest can also occur in the immediate vicinity of the border with Uganda, an approach to the Ugandan-Congolese border line, in the southwest and west of Uganda, should be avoided. We are in close contact with our local partners and, of course, also observe the security instructions of the Federal Foreign Office. In no case will we put you at risk and, of course, we will take action immediately if necessary.
The unrest in the east of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and in South Sudan occasionally affects adjacent areas in Uganda, in particular by the influx of refugees. Demonstrations for political and social reasons can spontaneously develop and escalate unpredictablely. Travelers are advised to keep themselves informed about local media and avoid crowds.
Please always keep in mind the current information provided by the Federal Foreign Office.

Language/Society

The majority of the population belongs to Christianity, only about 10% are Muslims. In addition to Kisuaheli, the official language is English. In addition, various Bantu languages such as Lusunga, Luganda and other East Nilotic languages are spoken. Kisuaheli is only an official language in Uganda, but is hardly spoken by the native population. English and Luganda are the most widely used languages in the country.

Power supply

The current voltage is 230/240 volt. Sockets are two- or three-pin and have a different format to the German plugs, an adapter is necessary. The appropriate adapter is usually available in supermarkets. Many accommodations provide their guests with an adapter free of charge.


Telephone/Internet

The code for Germany is 0049.

Mobile reception in Uganda is quite good in the cities, but there are many network-free zones in more remote areas and national parks. Ask your mobile phone provider about the current roaming contracts.

Many accommodations offer internet connections. In larger cities you can go online in Internet cafes. Conversations abroad can be conducted by hotels. We recommend buying a SIM card from a national mobile phone provider. Telephone cards can be purchased in most gas stations and shops. Please note that during international talks from Uganda you choose from 3 zeros instead of 2 zeros as usual. For example, call Düsseldorf in Germany, dial 00049 211 (call number).
