Zimbabwe - Country information A-Z

Best time to travel

In principle, Zimbabwe can travel all year round; depending on the interest of the traveler, the most suitable travel time is also different.
Those who want to observe animals in particular should set off on their journey in the Zimbabwean winter: during the months of May to October, the daily temperatures are more moderate and reach an average of 20°C. Due to the lack of rain, the animals collect concentrated at a few remaining water holes and you can observe the animals particularly well. In any case, pack warm clothes for the evenings and nights – here the thermometer often sinks under the freezing point.
Viktoria Falls: Floods occur between February and July and peak between March and April. During this time, the rise of the Sambesi by one meter leads to an increase of the water masses by five meters below the falls, which are forced by a narrow gorge. The swirled water forms a dense fog and provides spectacular rainbows, which is an impressive spectacle especially from the air.
The low water period ranges from August to January and the river reaches its lowest level between November and early December. During this time there is very little fog and visitors can marvel at the geological formation and the full extent of the waterfalls. The water level in the gorge below the falls sinks and the Sambesi meets its reputation as one of the best rafting destinations in the world.
While low water is best visited the Viktoria Falls on the Zimbabwe side. At certain times of the year activities such as wild water rafting or visiting Livingstone Island cannot be carried out due to the water level of the Sambesi.

Entry requirements

German, Austrian and Swiss people need a visa to enter Zimbabwe. This can be granted upon entry at both national borders and airports. Detailed information is available from the Department of Immigration Zimbabwe or from the Zimbabwean Embassy in Berlin. Entry visas valid for 30 days will be issued at both Harare and Victoria Falls international airports and at national borders for a fee of USD 30. Please note that these fees are payable in cash. We recommend keeping the exact amount available, as there is often no change available. The fees may change at any time. It is therefore advisable to check the current visa requirements and fees at the Embassy of Zimbabwe or on official government websites prior to departure.

 The visa can also be applied online at least 8 weeks before entry. After issuing the visa, you must arrive within 90 days.  

The Zimbabwean Immigration Authority has also introduced a new e-Arrival form to facilitate entry to border posts, including Victoria Falls Airport. This is the electronic replacement of the previous paper form, which had to be completed at the passport check. Fill in the electronic form at least 48 hours before your departure and submit it online.

Recommendation: Create a screenshot of the completed form to show it to the immigration officer on arrival in case of connection problems.

Please note: The digital form does not replace the visa.

 The passport must be valid for at least 6 months after returning from Zimbabwe. In addition, the passport must have at least two completely free sides for visual inspection. The same applies to children's passports, which are valid only with a photo. Each child needs a passport to enter Zimbabwe. Children's entries in a parent's passport are no longer valid. Upon entry, a return flight ticket must be available.

Non-EU citizens are asked to contact the relevant representative abroad. 

Food & Drink

The cuisine in Zimbabwe is very similar to the South African cuisine. The typical local dish in Zimbabwe is the Sadza (maize meal) which resembles the Italian polenta. Sadza is usually served with vegetables and sauce. Grilled meat, vegetables and potatoes as well as rice are part of many dishes. The Biltong (air-dried meat) serves as a snack, in any case you can also taste regional specialties such as zebra, crocodile or game meat. For breakfast, the British Porridge is often served, as well as fruit, cereals with coffee and tea.
Drinks consist of local beers or try the self-brewed chibuku (brain-maize beer). Wine varieties are imported from nearby South Africa. Drinking water: for your own safety, we recommend you to have packed drinking water from the supermarket.
Please note that special meals such as: It is difficult to obtain gluten-free or vegan products in Zimbabwe.

Fauna

Much of the country has been declared a protected area in Zimbabwe. In many national parks the "Big 5" are found. In part, one encounters in part the endangered rhinoceros. More than 700 bird species include the well-known Angolapitta, the Swynnertonia, the migrating falcon and the osprey.


Flora

In the east of the country, with its rainy areas, there are often evergreen forests. In the south, on the other hand, there is a zone with semi-desert vegetation. Here and in the southwest of the country there are also many cactus plants, aloes, jacaranda and wild flowers. Most of the country is covered by savannas with their characteristic acacia, monkeybread and mohobohobo trees as well as grassy avanna. Among the most common plant species are the breech nut tree, the river lily and the crown of fame, which is also Zimbabwe's national flower.

Money/currency

The former currency, the Zimbabwe dollar, has disappeared completely from the market since 2009. Since then, the US Dollar or the South African Rand have been common means of payment. Amounts are usually rounded up or rounded up as there are hardly any coins in both currencies. It is recommended to take printed notes in small denominations as of 2007 and to change US dollars already in the home country, as there is usually the better exchange rate.
The provision of cash by withdrawing international bank cards at ATMs is almost impossible. Credit cards are only very rarely accepted, if at all, then mostly Visa credit cards, Mastercard on the other hand hardly anymore. Travelers should therefore start your journey with sufficient cash. The increased risk of theft is explicitly mentioned. It is advisable to deposit larger amounts of money in hotels etc. after arrival in the safe to minimize the risk of theft.
In accommodations, restaurants and shops frequented by tourists, VISA, rarely Master Card, were accepted. Lately, credit cards are rarely accepted. Please provide yourself with sufficient cash before departure (best in small denominations).
In the case of lack of money or loss of travel funds, it is possible to transfer money from Germany to smaller quantities via "Western Union". A branch is located in Harare in No. 12 Samora Machel Avenue. In Germany, "Western Union" cooperates with the "Travel Bank", which can be found at railway stations in all major cities.
According to the competent authorities, the exemption for duty-free imports of goods and goods on entry was reduced to US$2000 at the beginning of 2016. It is recommended to inform the Zimbabwean authorities about the current situation before traveling.
The supply of cash by withdrawing international bank cards at ATMs has become almost impossible.
On 28 November 2016, the Central Bank introduced so-called ‘bond notes'. The Bond Notes are intended to supplement, not replace the US dollar, and are only valid in Zimbabwe. The same applies to the Bond Coins, which have been in circulation for a long time. Whether the exchange
"Bond Notes" in US dollars in a ratio of 1:1 can actually be guaranteed, remains to be seen.
The import of cash is still possible in unlimited amounts. However, it is essential that the imported quantity is declared at the Zimbabwean customs office at the time of entry so that the balance can be returned on departure. According to the Zimbabwean Customs, the ceiling is $2,000.

Health/vaccination

When entering Europe directly (direct flight!) no compulsory vaccinations are required. When entering a country declared by the WHO to be a yellow fever epidemic (e.g. A valid yellow fever vaccination is required for Ethiopia, Kenya or Zambia. It is recommended to carry the vaccination certificate.

Malaria prophylaxis is recommended all year round for certain regions, especially after the rains in the first quarter of 2017. In addition, all standard vaccinations should be checked or refreshed according to the vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute. Hepatitis A and typhus are recommended as travel vaccinations, and hepatitis B and rabies are recommended for long-term or special exposure. We recommend a consultation with your family doctor. Since medical care is not comparable to German standards, a well-equipped travel pharmacy should be carried along. Because of the often missing medical

Prerequisites for proper medical treatment at the place are recommended to take out a valid travel health insurance before the start of the trip, which includes an ambulance rescue flight. Medical evacuations are available. E. g. Medical Air Rescue Services International - MARS and Emergency Medical Rescue Services – EMRES. In a medical emergency, the complete patient transfer, including notification of all specialist departments, will be organized to South Africa.

Especially in the summer months you should carry sunscreen with you due to the high UV radiation with high protection factor as well as a headgear. Think also of an appropriate mosquito protection.

Due to the appropriate food and drinking water hygiene most diarrhea diseases and especially cholera can be avoided. Some basic rules: Drink only water of safe origin (bottle water). For food: cooking, peeling or disinfecting. Keep flies away from your food and wash your hands with soap as often as possible.

Bilharziosis: Avoid bathing in the wild. Not only do crocodiles and hippos live in most lakes and rivers, but there is a high risk of contagion with bilharziosis, especially in standing waters.

Please always keep in mind the current information provided by the Federal Foreign Office.

Geography

Zimbabwe is about the same size as Germany and Switzerland together (about 390 757 km2). As an inland land without access to the sea, the climate zones in the north range from the largest river in Africa – the Sambesi – to the east the mountain range of the Eastern Highlands, to the south the Limpopo River to the west, the foothills of the Kalahari Desert. In the center of the country there is predominantly hilly highlands, in which the numerous rivers spring, which flow to the north of the country and to Botswana.

Clothing

In the summer months, airy and comfortable clothes are recommended, along with an umbrella or a rain jacket. We recommend the onion principle – so wearing several layers of clothing on top of each other, so that you can take on or off a layer if necessary. A hat or cap protects against excessive sunlight. To protect against mosquitoes, long, body-covering clothing with bright colors is recommended (please do not use bright colors or white during safari). For the cooler evenings and especially for the African winter months (June to August/September) a (fleece) jacket or sweater and scarf/cap should not be missing. Gloves should also be in the luggage. Be sure to pack firm, comfortable footwear. You should also have a slightly finer evening wardrobe, especially for hotel or restaurant visits.

Climate

Zimbabwe has a subtropical to tropical climate. The seasons can be divided into two periods. The seasons are opposed to those in Europe due to the situation in the southern hemisphere.
In the Zimbabwean winter (May to October) warm, almost Mediterranean temperatures prevail in Zimbabwe during the day; at night it can become sensitively cold. This season is considered a dry season. In the months of June and July, cold to frosty temperatures can occur at night (highs). Temperatures can range from 7°C to 21°C.
The summer of Zimbabwe (November to April) is the rainy season in which, in low altitudes, precipitation is too strong and very strong in higher altitudes. Temperatures range from 16 °C to 32 °C. In addition, high humidity.

National parks

Hwange National Park: Zimbabwe's largest national park in the north-west of the country. The population of the wild elephants in the park is one of the largest in the world. Versatile landscape and wildlife, excellent wildlife viewing opportunities.

Mana Pools National Park: Belongs to the UNSECO World Heritage Site. The Sambesi and its many islands are home to many animals such as buffalo, hippo, crocodiles, but also leopards, cheetahs, lions, elephants, zebras and many kinds of bucks and game. The landscape offers some individual and adventurous safaris.

Matobo National Park: Part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site, beautiful mountain landscape with rock and rock formations made of granite. The diversity of the landscape offers home to black and white rhinos, leopards and cheetahs.

Matusadona National Park/Lake Kariba: Located on the edge of the Kariba reservoir, the park offers wild and water-rich areas. Here are lions, hippos, crocodiles, elephants, buffaloes and wild species.

Nyanga National Park: Offers the most promising landscape, as it lies at an altitude of 1800 m. There are fantastic views, numerous waterfalls and a variety of wild species such as Gnus, Kudus, Zebras, Impalas and Bockarten can be seen in the park.

Gonarezhou National Park: Located in the southeast, part of the Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park, Zimbabwe's largest park. Varied landscape with lowveld vegetation, rivers and a variety of animal species. Here you can see: lions, leopards, cheetahs, giraffes, zebras, buffaloes, numerous antelope and bird species

Policy/economy/society

The majority of Zimbabwe's population consists of Black Africans. More than 80% of this represents the Shona group, followed by the Ndebele (15%). The rest is composed of inhabitants from Europe, Asia and mixed ethnic groups (2%). Zimbabwe became independent from Britain in 1980. Every year it is remembered with celebrations throughout the country.
The introduction of the dollar as a universal means of payment in 2009 has led to a noticeable improvement in living conditions in the otherwise authoritarian state.
In November 2017, ZANU-PF Chairman Robert Mugabe resigned after a military coup, the new head of government has been Emmerson Mnangagwa ever since.

Safety instructions

Zimbabwe is a largely safe country. Due to the difficult political and economic situation, the crime rate in some parts of the country and parts of the city has increased. In the downtown area of Harare and Bulawayo, the number of pickpockets has increased. Foreigners should leave the immediate surroundings of demonstrations immediately and not photograph them. Furthermore, foreigners are not allowed to report on the current developments in the country without state accreditation. This also includes any collection of information, conversations with the population or snapshots with the mobile phone camera, as this can be interpreted as an illegal journalistic activity.
In principle, it is advisable to observe the current security instructions of the Federal Foreign Office.
In the case of exports of a larger quantity of handicrafts and Shona sculptures intended for sale, an authorisation shall be submitted to the customs authority. The rules may constantly change and it is therefore appropriate to ask directly at the customs office in Harare.

Language

The official language in Zimbabwe is English. In addition to the languages Shona, Ndebele and Fanalago, the Bantu languages Cishona and Isindebele are also common languages.


Power supply

The current voltage is 220 volts. Sockets are flat or round and three-pin; they have a different format to the German plugs and an adapter is necessary. Many accommodations provide their guests with an adapter free of charge. Make sure that there are frequent power outages in Zimbabwe and that there is no 24h power supply in the lodges/camps.


Telephone/Internet:

The code for Germany is 0049.
The mobile phone reception is only guaranteed in larger cities and at the Viktoria Falls. Ask your mobile phone provider about the current roaming contracts.
Conversations abroad can be conducted by hotels or by public telephone booths. (blue = coin phone, green = card phone). We recommend buying a SIM card from a national mobile phone provider. Telephone cards can be purchased in gas stations and shops.
It is also possible to buy a SIM card from local providers such as Net One Cellular, Telcel Zimbabwe or Econet to make affordable calls.
Many accommodations offer guests Internet access.

Time Shift

There is no time difference during Central European summer time. Zimbabwe is one hour ahead during the winter season (CET + 1 hour).
