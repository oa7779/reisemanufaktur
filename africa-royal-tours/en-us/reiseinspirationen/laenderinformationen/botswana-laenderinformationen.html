Botswana - Country information A-Z

Best time to travel

In principle, Botswana can travel all year round; depending on the interest of the traveler, the most suitable travel time is also different.

In the months of September and October: Daily temperatures are more moderate and reach an average of 30°C. Due to the lack of rain, the animals collect concentrated at a few remaining water holes and you can observe the animals particularly well.

If you want to observe the many newly born young animals, the best time to travel is during the rainy summer months (from November to December).

In the Okavango Delta some accommodation is closed during the rainy season (November to February). Fly-In Safaris are however possible in all other parts of Botswana without any problems. During this time, however, the conditions for animal observation are difficult. The most popular travel time for the Okavango Delta is June-August, where the water level in the Delta is highest and attracts numerous wildlife.

At the same time, winter reigns, and June to August are the coldest months of the year. The maximum daily temperatures are at pleasant 20 to 28 degrees, while it cools down to 0 to 8 degrees at night and in the early morning hours.

Entry requirements

German, Austrian and Swiss do not require a visa in advance for entry to Botswana. An entry permit for tourist purposes is granted free of charge at the border station upon entry (stay max. 90 days per calendar year). The passport must be valid for at least 6 months after return. In addition, the passport must have at least two completely free sides for visual inspection. Children's entries in a parent's passport are no longer valid since 26.06.2012. Each child needs his/her own ID document. On entry, a return flight ticket must be available.According to information provided by the Botswana Immigration Authority, by way of derogation, entry with a temporary passport requires a valid visa. One cannot rely on a visa being issued at the Botswana border. A visa should therefore be applied for prior to entry with a temporary passport. The Embassy of the Republic of Botswana in Berlin (contact details see below) provides you with more detailed information on the application of visas for entry to Botswana.

Non-EU citizens must apply to the relevant embassy or consulate for a visa.

Note for traveling with minors: The South African "Immigration Regulations 2014", which entered into force on 01.06.2015, contain, among other things, new regulations for traveling by and with minors, which also concern travelers from and to Botswana (namely transit via Johannesburg airport). Further information can be found in the travel and safety instructions for South Africa. Also for Botswana, regardless of South Africa, the birth certificate is valid for children under the age of 16 who travel with their parents. In the event of entry with adults other than parents, a certified authorisation of the parents or guardians shall also be provided. For further questions in this context, only the Department of Home Affairs (www.dha.gov.za) or the responsible South African foreign representative can answer bindingly.

In 2017, the government of Botswana decided to introduce a new tax of USD 30 per person. This should actually enter into force from 01.06.17, but this was postponed for an indefinite period shortly after notification. She's not coming up right now.
The plan was to exclude children up to nine years of age. The tax must be paid directly on arrival. Payment is possible in cash (USD) or by credit card. Afterwards you will receive a receipt, which must be removed and shown to the immigration authorities. Passport and receipt will be stamped and handed over to you again. The receipt remains valid for 30 days and can be used for multi-ride entry during this period.

Food and drink

The food in Botswana is characterized by many different influences from all over the world. There are many curry dishes, typical grilled specialities from neighboring South Africa and English-influenced desserts such as "Scones" (a kind of small cake) and "Pie" (overbaked tart).
The specialities from Botswana include the "pape" corn porridge, which is served as a side dish of beef, chicken or vegetables. But you can also try the country-typical, somewhat more unusual foods such as the protein-containing Mopani worms. On festive occasions, the "Seswaai", a kind of meat stew, is prepared. Fish lovers should definitely try fresh fish such as bream or catfish in the Okavango Delta.
A popular soft drink that is non-alcoholic is the Rock Shandy, which consists of mineral water, lime juice and a few drops of angostura. Otherwise, in Botswana you drink mainly beer from the local brand Namibia Breweries, imported beers from neighbouring countries or South African wines. Drinking water: for your own safety, we recommend that you have packed drinking water from the supermarket.    
Please inform us in time for special nutritional requests. In Botswana, special ingredients such as gluten-free or vegan products are difficult to obtain.

Fauna

The many different habitats in Botswana offer home for various animal species. In addition to the "Big Five" (elephant, buffalo, rhinoceros, lion and leopard), there are also cheetahs, giraffes, zebras, hippos and gnus found in the various protected nature reserves (about 40% of the country area). In the Okavango Delta there are also a variety of bird species to marvel at.


Flora

Botswana's plant wealth is gigantic, thousands of species are native here as e.g. The Baobab tree (also called a monkey bread tree), which can become over 100 years old, as well as a variety of other impressive tree species such as the liver sausage tree, the umbrella acacia and various palm species. In the desert regions there are mostly different types of desert grass. In the Okavango Delta especially the colorful water lilies species and the Riedgrass meadows are impressive.

Photographing/Filming

Think of enough storage space and spare batteries. A good binoculars is indispensable for safaris and animal observations, as is a tripod for animal photos at dusk.

Protect your equipment from dust and heat.

Taking photos of locals is usually no problem. Remember, however, that as a guest you travel Botswana and every resident always meets you with respect. In return, one should treat the locals equally respectfully and ask for permission before pressing the trigger. As a thank you for the photo, a small fee is often expected. Please do not photograph military or government facilities, border crossings and airports.

An important note: Due to the limited storage space in the small charter aircraft your free luggage is here on 20 kg incl. Hand luggage limited. Should you therefore arrive with a complete photo equipment, it may be necessary to book an extra seat. Please inform us about this in advance.

Money/currency

The official means of payment is Pula (in the native language Pula means rain, by the way).
In larger cities you will find ATMs. With credit cards and with the EC card (only Maestro system) plus PIN you can withdraw money there. VISA and Master Card are generally accepted in the accommodation. The entrance fees for national parks as well as at the gas station are paid with Pula. In addition to Pula, the US Dollar is a widely used means of payment; for tipping, the US dollar is recommended. Often only banknotes printed after 2007 are accepted. Credit cards (especially VISA, but hardly American Express) are widely accepted. Most lodges and camps also accept US dollars in cash (only bills).

Geography

With an area of about 581 730 km2, Botswana is about the size of France and borders Namibia and Zambia to the north and west, Zimbabwe to the northeast and South Africa to the south and southeast. The largest climatic zone is the Kalahari Desert - the largest contiguous sand area on earth - and extends to neighbouring Namibia. To the north is the Inner Delta of the Okavango River and the Ngami Lake, to which the Makgadikgadi salt pans border in the southeast.


Luggage

The fastest and most convenient way to overcome the large distances within Botswana is by small aircraft. The domestic flights from National Park to National Park or from Lodge to Lodge are therefore used in small charter aircraft (e.g. E. g. Cessna 206 or Cessno 208). Only limited storage space is available here. Therefore, please comply with the requirements regarding the baggage, otherwise an additional seat will be charged or the baggage will only be transported with the next flight. In general: max. 20 kg per person incl. Hand luggage, not hard case or bulky trolleys, but snug bags (maximum size 25 cm width x 30 cm height x 62 cm length). Please give us your weight in advance of your trip, as the airlines need this for logistical reasons.

Health/vaccination

When entering Europe directly (direct flight!) no compulsory vaccinations are required. When entering a country declared by the WHO to be a yellow fever epidemic (e.g. A valid yellow fever vaccination is required for Ethiopia, Angola, Kenya or Zambia. Malaria prophylaxis is recommended for certain regions. There is a high risk in the northern Okavango Delta, Chobe National Park, Ghanzi, Moremi Wild Reserve, North East Province and Francistown, especially in the rainy season (November to May). In the southern delta with Maun, Ngamisee region is expected to be at medium risk of malaria, in the Central Kalahari National Park there is still a (low) risk of malaria. From November to May there is also a risk of malaria in the further east and southeast parts of the border areas with Zimbabwe (regions Bobirwa and Selebi-Phikwe) including Francistown and Palapye. The southern parts of the country, including Gaborone and Tshane, are malaria-free. In addition, all standard vaccinations should be reviewed or refreshed in accordance with the vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute. We recommend a consultation with your family doctor. Medical care in Botswana is largely not comparable to Western European conditions – especially in the country, it is not sufficient everywhere. A sufficient health insurance cover valid there and a reliable travel return insurance/emergency evacuation are strongly recommended. The camps are usually far away from any medical facilities and after dark no evacuations can be made (except for Okavango Air Rescue by helicopter). Remember to carry important drugs in your hand luggage and in sufficient quantities (if necessary with appropriate import certificates).

Especially in the summer months you should carry sunscreen with you due to the high UV radiation with high protection factor as well as a headgear. Think also of an appropriate mosquito protection.

Since medical care is not comparable to German standards, a well-equipped travel pharmacy should be carried along. Bilharziosis: Avoid bathing in the wild. Not only do crocodiles and hippos live in most lakes and rivers, but there is a high risk of contagion with bilharziosis, especially in standing waters. Please also note the current information provided by the Federal Foreign Office.


Clothing

In the summer months, airy and comfortable clothes are recommended, along with an umbrella or a rain jacket. We recommend the onion principle – so wearing several layers of clothing on top of each other, so that you can take on or off a layer if necessary. A hat or cap protects against excessive sunlight.

To protect against mosquitoes, long, body-covering clothing with bright colors is recommended (please do not use bright colors or white during safari). For the cooler evenings and especially for the winter months a (fleece) jacket or sweater should not be missing. Be sure to pack firm, comfortable footwear.

Climate

Botswana has a subtropical, hot and dry climate. The seasons are opposed to those in Europe due to the situation in the southern hemisphere.

Spring (September to October) in Botswana offers high temperatures that can climb up to 40°C during the day. The animals gather at the water points and the conditions for animal observation are ideal.

Rainfalls occur in summer (October to March) and temperatures reach an average of 35°C during the day, at night about 15°C. This season is also called the "Green Season": short and heavy rains that alternate with long dry periods. Often the rain falls are accompanied by strong thunderstorms with storms. The humidity can rise to 70%.

The autumn (March to May) is the most pleasant season in Botswana, because there is a warm, clear weather and at night the temperatures are pleasantly cool.

In winter (May-September) the vegetation decreases due to the cold temperatures; the average temperature during the day is about 28 °C, at night the thermometer can drop to about 0 °C.

National parks

Botswana National Parks are mostly not fenced, so that the animals can move freely. Wild animals on roads are therefore not uncommon.

North

Okavango Delta with the Moremi Wildlife Reserve: Wetland reserve with always wet meadows, islands and watercourses as well as flooded grassland and forests. There are over 122 species of mammals, including the "Big Five" and some rhinoceros species, 71 species of fish, 444 species of birds, 64 species of reptiles. Ideal: Boat trip in the traditional "Mokoro", a built-in canoe.

Chobe National Park: Animal protection area with swamp and flood areas. Due to the different vegetation forms, the park has an enormous animal variety, famous for the largest elephant population in Africa and huge buffalo herds. In the Chobe National Park you will find the "Big Five", antelope and goat species, giraffes, zebras, monkeys, hyenas and jackals as well as over 460 different bird species.

Nxai Pan National Park consisting of the Nxai-Pan, the Kgama-Kgama Pan and the Kudiakam Pan focus: former salt lakes, which today are characterized by grass-covered pans with individual trees. The Baines monkey bread trees are special. Protected area with giraffes, lions, cheetahs, wild dog species, zebras, jumping goats, gnus, impalas, antelope species, partly elephant and buffalo occurrences.

Macgadikgadi salt pan: World's largest salt pan area with an area of 12,000 km2, with seemingly unending vegetationless plains surrounded by grass-savannahs, where gnus and zebras, as well as in the Sowa and Nata protected area with large flamingo, pelican, duck and geese occurrences, can be found in the rainy season giraffes, elephants, goat species, antelope species and kudus.

Central

Central Kalahari Game Reserve: The second largest wildlife reserve in the world with wide, hip-high grasslands, crossed with tree and bush populations with giraffes, antelope species, gnus, predators and many species of birds.

Khutse Game Reserve: Protected area with hilly grass, bare and overgrown pans, dunes and river beds. There are a large number of jumping goats as well as hyenas, jackals, kudus and antelope species to discover.

South-West

Kalahari Transfrontier Park: Largest national park with river valleys, grassy landscape and coloured sand dunes. Habitat for leopards, cheetahs, lynxes, , Kalahari lions, wildcat species, spoon dogs, silver foxes, antelope species, hyenas and jackals.

Policy/economy/society

Botswana has about 2 million people. Inhabitants, mostly consisting of Bantu tribes (76%). The rest consists of different bush peoples such as the Shona, the San and the Khoikhoi (about 2%). Botswana has been an independent democratic country since 1966. Since 2003, the existing constitution of the country has been amended to make all peoples and tribes equal and thus strengthen the unity of the country. Head of State is His Excellency, Ian Khama.

At the time of independence still one of the poorest countries in the world, Botswana is now one of the wealthiest countries in Africa. The most important industry is the export of diamonds, which accounts for around 70% of the total export.In the meantime, Botswana has become the center of the global diamond trade. Diamond mining contributed to a significant improvement in the standard of living of the people of Botswana. Large diamond companies spend large amounts of money annually in infrastructure and energy supply building as well as in the formation of the inhabitants of individual regions.

Other economically important branches in Botswana are the export of copper nickel, mineral water and beef to tourism. Here above all sustainable tourism.

Safaris

The safari activities (usually two activities per day are included) last about 3-4 hours and depend on when the animals are active. As a rule, safari days are as follows:

Early in the morning (at 05:00/06:00) the first activity begins. They strengthen themselves with tea/coffee and biscuits. In the late morning you can enjoy a late breakfast or an extensive brunch in your lodge. The midday hours are hot and the animals are little active here, so that you can also use this time for yourself to rest. In the afternoon, after an afternoon tea, take the second activity, which lasts until sunset (sometimes a bit beyond that). Back at the camp, it's time for dinner and a drink at the campfire.

No special prior knowledge or special physical fitness is required to participate in the hunting trips. However, it should be noted that the safari vehicles are open and you are traveling a lot in rough areas on bumpy slopes. Please never underestimate the power of solar radiation and be careful to provide enough fluid.

Safari and children: Many lodges and camps require a minimum age for the reception of children (usually 12 years, in individual cases also younger). Whether the children can participate in the activities is decided on site by the Camp/Lodge Manager or Ranger. Often special conditions apply, also in relation to the accommodation (e.g. - if you are pregnant (e. g. with an adult). These are different from lodge to lodge.

Safety instructions/road conditions

Street crime in Gaborone and other cities is essentially limited to pocket theft and credit card robbery. Intrusion and robbery of pedestrians and motorists are rare. No items, especially bags, should be left in the car.

Also driving cars should be locked, e.g. To prevent pickpocketing on red lights. In principle, it is advisable to observe the current security instructions of the Federal Foreign Office.

As in any other travel country, valuables should never be worn openly. Valuables as well as passports, travelers' cheques and air tickets should be delivered to the reception or stored in your room safe. Tip: For security, we recommend that you keep a copy of your travel documents and passports separately from the originals.

The road network in Botswana is only partially developed, obstacles such as parked or abandoned vehicles and above all free-running animals are not uncommon, accordingly long journeys of only a few kilometers can take.

Language/Society

The official language is English and Setswana. Other Bantu languages are spoken in large parts of the population.

Botswana has an excellent education system that, together with a free health system, contributes to the stability of the country. The supreme value enshrined in the law is a peaceful coexistence. All these factors help to keep you warm and warm by the locals.

Power supply

The current voltage is 230 volts. Sockets are two-pin and/or three-pin and have a different format to the German plugs, an adapter is necessary. The appropriate adapter is usually available in supermarkets. Many accommodations provide their guests with an adapter free of charge. Many camps and lodges are powered by generators, a few offer 24 hours of stom supply. Some camps offer a charging station in the main area, as the guest accommodation is not equipped with their own sockets.

Telephone/Internet

The code for Germany is 0049.

Mobile phone reception is only available near larger cities. Ask your mobile phone provider about the current roaming contracts.

Conversations abroad can be conducted by hotels or by public telephone booths (blue = coin phone, green = card phone). We recommend buying a SIM card from a national mobile phone provider. Telephone cards can be purchased in gas stations and shops.

Many accommodations offer Internet connections, in remote areas (e.g. E. g. Okavango Delta) is not connected to the Internet. In larger cities you can go online in Internet cafes.

We would like to point out that taking a Samsung Galaxy Note 7 mobile phone on board various domestic airlines is not permitted for security reasons until further notice.

Tips

Although tips are not mandatory, they are accepted worldwide as a thank you and appreciated by all employees. The amount of tip should reflect the quality/service of your safari. In Botswana, tips are best given in local currency (Pula), but common foreign currencies (USD, Euro, British Pounds, South African Rand) are also accepted. In Zimbabwe and Zambia, it is better to give tips in foreign currency (euro and USD).

To give you an idea of who and how much you can give, we have drawn up the following directive:

  • Camp guides – USD 10.00 – USD 15.00 per day, per guest
  • Mokoro driver – USD 10.00 per driver
  • Other employees (boaters, bar staff, housekeeper) – USD 5.00 – USD 10.00 per day
