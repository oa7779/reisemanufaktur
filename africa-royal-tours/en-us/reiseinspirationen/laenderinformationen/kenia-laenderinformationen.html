Kenya - Country information A-Z

Best time to travel

Basically, Kenya can be visited all year round, the big rainy season lasts from March to May/June, the small rainy season from October/November to December. However, nature has its own rhythm and does not follow a strict schedule, so seasonal deviations are always possible. This also applies to the so-called "Great Migration" – the annual Gnu Migration between Tanzania and Kenya. Depending on the time and amount of rainfall, the gnus also depends on the local conditions. The route remains largely the same – but the exact schedule varies from year to year.
Depending on the interest of the traveler, however, the most suitable travel time is different.
If you want to observe the migration in the Masai Mara, you should travel from August to October/November.

Food and drink

In the big cities of Kenya and in many hotels there are international specialities such as China, India, South Africa, Brazil and Europe. For breakfast there is "Mandazi" in Kenya (baked dough piece) and tea with milk and ginger, which has already been cooked with sugar.

Local dishes are "Ugali" (unsalted corn porridge) or "Chapati" (wheat flaks), which are served with plenty of cooked white cabbage and partly with meat inlays such as goat or chicken. In the coastal regions, fresh fish, shrimps and crabs are also on the menu. If you get an opportunity, you will definitely take part in a Kenyan barbecue, the Nyama Choma. Don't miss the fresh fruits of Kenya! The warm climate gives you delicious pineapples, mangoes and baby bananas and other exotic fruits. Try the delicious Kenyan Tusker beer or the Kilimanjaro or Safari Camps from the neighboring Tanzania. The ginger lemonade "Stoney Tangawizi" is also popular, which often has a very intense ginger taste for Europeans.

For your own safety, we recommend drinking packed water from the supermarket. Make sure the water bottles are sealed correctly.

Flora and fauna

Kenya has a large biological diversity due to its diverse landscapes. With its extensive savannahs and pasturelands, rainforests along the coast and volcanic areas, Kenya is a paradise for plant lovers. There you can especially admire many wildflower species, orchids, man-high lobelias and fields full of coffee and tea plants. Of course, the Baobab trees and umbrella acacias, which are significant for Africa, can also be discovered in the vast expanses of Kenya. Papyrus and giant bamboo also shape the landscape.

With an estimated 80 different mammalian species Kenya offers a unique wildlife experience. These include the "Big Five" (elephant, buffalo, rhinoceros, lion and leopard) as well as various antelope species such as the Dik Dik and over 1000 different bird species. In many Kenyan national parks there are also indigenous species that are protected from extinction. Every year there is an impressive migration of herds of animals from the Masai Mara to the Tanzanian Serengeti.

Photographing/Filming

Think of enough storage space and spare batteries. Camera accessories are available in many shops, but are mostly more expensive than in Europe. For perfect shots we recommend a light sensitive camera and a tripod.

A good binoculars is indispensable for safaris and animal watching.

Shooting photos of locals is usually no problem. Remember, however, that you travel as a guest and that every resident always meets you with respect. In return, one should treat the locals equally respectfully and ask for permission before pressing the trigger. As a thank you for the photo is often expected a small thank you.

Money/currency

Means of payment is Kenyan shilling. Before traveling in exchange for US dollars, make sure that the bills were printed after 2006, better even after 2008 (due to counterfeits). It is recommended to take notes in all denominations – change is rarely available. The US Dollar is a widely used means of payment and is also gladly accepted as a tip

The easiest way to get cash is ATMs. In remote areas, ATMs or travelers' cheques are less common; here cash in the form of US dollar bills is recommended. 

All relevant banks are located at the airports. Many ATMs can be used to withdraw money with VISA Cirrus or Maestro. Make sure that you are always standing alone in front of the switches and do not accept any offered help!


Health/vaccination

When entering Europe directly (direct flight!) no compulsory vaccinations are required. When entering a country declared by the WHO to be a yellow fever epidemic (e.g. (e.g. Ethiopia or Zambia) is required to demonstrate a valid yellow fever vaccination. Malaria prophylaxis is recommended for certain regions. In addition, all standard vaccinations should be reviewed or refreshed in accordance with the vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute. We recommend a consultation with your family doctor. In case of emergencies or medical care, hospitals are available in the larger cities. In remote areas there is the organization of the Flying Doctors. Since medical care is not comparable to German standards, a well-equipped travel pharmacy should be carried along. Especially in the summer months you should carry sunscreen with you due to the high UV radiation with high protection factor as well as a headgear. Think also of an appropriate mosquito protection.
Please observe the current information provided by the Federal Foreign Office.

Geography

Kenya has an area of 586 600 km2 and borders Sudan, Ethiopia and Somalia to the north and east, and Tanzania and Uganda to the south and west. The landscape can be distinguished in two ways: in the west of the country is the deepest area around Lake Victoria, which passes east into the Rift Valley and the plateaus with its hilly land. The western half of the country falls to the Indian Ocean coast. The highest point is Mount Kenya with a height of 5199 m. There are eight provinces: Western , Nyanza, Rift Valley, Eastern, North-Eastern, Central, Nairobi and Coast.


Clothing

In the summer months, airy and comfortable clothes are recommended, along with an umbrella or a rain jacket. Long clothing in bright colours is recommended to protect against mosquitoes. For the cooler evenings and especially for the winter months a jacket or sweater should not be missing. A finer evening wardrobe is recommended for high-quality hotel or restaurant visits.

Climate

Kenya's climate can be divided into two zones, which result on the one hand from the altitude as well as the dry and rainy season.
On the coast there are high temperatures as well as high humidity. The most precipitation months are from April to June. The highest tenperatures are reached in January/February.
In Kenya's highlands, there is a temperate climate that provides temperatures around 25°C during the day and pleasantly cool temperatures at night.
In the cities the temperatures are very pleasant (at 19 °C) due to the high locations.
In the months of April to June, the long-lasting rain falls. In the months of October to December you have to expect short but regular rain showers. The Long-Term Rainfalls
Average sunshine per day: approx. 8 hours.

National parks

Aberdares National Park: Hilly mountain country crossed by jungle forest, waterfalls guarantee a varied wildlife observation. Elephants, buffaloes, hyenas and antelope species are native here.

Amboseli National Park: Well known for its large elephant deposits. Located at the foot of Kilimanjaro, the park offers a variety of animal species for safari enthusiasts. Wild animals include: goats, antelopes, wild cats, hyenas, spoondogs, zebras and over 400 bird species.

Lake Nakuru National Park: Hilly grasslands and lakes are among the predominant landscapes. Let yourself be impressed by the variety of flamingos and over 400 bird species as well as by black and white rhinos, lions and leopards.

Masai Mara: Known for the typical African savannah and grass landscape, the Masai Mara is the site of the annual animal migration of zebras and gnus to the Serengeti in neighboring Tanzania. The incredible wealth of animals will impress you. Wild animals include the Big Five (lion, leopard, elephant, rhino and buffalo), as well as cheetahs, antelopes, hyenas and hippos.

Mount Kenya: Stunning glacier and high mountain landscape unite at the top of Kenya's highest mountain. Fascinating mountain lakes and meadows make an expedition to the peaks unforgettable.

Samburu National Park & Shaba National Reserve: Located in the heart of Kenya, the park enchants with rugged and unspoilt wilderness. Semi-desert-like landscape and Dornbuschsavanne alternate here with vukan-like hills. It is only the river Uaso Nyiro that brings to life the area around which the many wildlife species gather. Thus, the adapted desert inhabitants unite with grass-loving animal species: antelope and goat species can be found as well as zebras, giraffes, elephants, lions, leopards and over 400 bird species.

Tsavo East & Tsavo West National Park: Grass plains with isolated old volcanic cones. The eastern park is different from the western larger and untourist. Here you can watch the Big Five, cheetahs and giraffes as well as the many hippos and crocodiles in the wild.

Policy/economy/society

The name Kenya comes from the highest mountain in the country, Mount Kenya (5.199m). About 34 million. People of different nations and cultures shape the entire country. The population consists of about 40 different ethnic groups: the largest groups are Bantu (22%), Luhya (14%), Luo (13%), Kalenjin (12%), Kamba (11%), Kisii (6%), Meru (6%), Massai (approximately 2%), other African ethnic groups (13%), non-African (1%). The prevailing religious communities are Protestants (38%), Roman Catholic (28%), traditional faiths (26%), Muslims (7%), and other faiths (1%). Kenya has a presidential republic and a democratically elected parliament. The Kenyan government was last re-formed in April 2013 and KANU Chairman (Kenya African National Union) Uhuru Kenyatta was elected for a term of office of 6 years. He is also President and Chief of the Armed Forces. Kenya's economy has grown steadily in recent years, important economic factors are still agriculture, livestock farming and, above all, the export of coffee, tea, flowers (roses) and, of course, the growing tourism industry.  


Safety instructions/road conditions

Kenya is a safe travel destination in most parts and also a pleasant place to travel. In some parts of the country and parts of the city (especially in townships and after dark) the crime rate is increased. In principle, one should always observe the current safety instructions of the Federal Foreign Office. We will be happy to advise you on any further questions.

As in any other travel country, valuables should never be worn openly, especially in places with larger collections of people. Valuables as well as passports, travelers' cheques and airline tickets should be delivered to the reception or stored in your room safe.

Tip: For security, we recommend that you keep a copy of your travel documents and passports separately from the originals.

Caution should be exercised with the matatus, the small minibuses or collective taxis. They represent a high safety risk, as speed limits are often not met or the vehicle is not technically safe. On the other hand, train connections are very safe and pleasant.

Please always keep in mind the current information provided by the Federal Foreign Office.

Power supply

The current voltage is 230/240 volt. The sockets are three-pin and plugs have a different format to the German plugs. An adapter is necessary, which is available in supermarkets.


Telephone/Internet

The code for Germany is 0049.

Mobile phone reception is only guaranteed near larger cities. Ask your mobile phone provider about the current roaming contracts.

Conversations abroad can be conducted by hotels, public telephone booths (with coins or telephone cards) or by post. Telephone cards can be purchased at the International Call Offices. The international discussions can be chosen themselves (pre-election 000) or mediated (pre-dial 0195/6). We recommend buying a SIM card from a national mobile phone provider. Telephone cards can be purchased in gas stations and shops.

Many accommodations offer fast Internet connections or Wi-Fi. In larger cities you can go online in tourist information or internet cafes.

Time Shift

During Central European summer time, Kenya is one hour ahead of Europe (CET + 1 hour). In winter, Kenya is two hours ahead of Europe (CET + 2 hours).
