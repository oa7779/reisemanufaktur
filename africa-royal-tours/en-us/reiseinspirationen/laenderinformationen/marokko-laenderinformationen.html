Morocco - Country information A-Z

Best time to travel

Coastal regions: The best time to travel to the coasts of Morocco are the months between April and November. The temperature is pleasantly windy due to the influence of the sea and it does not get too hot.
Interior: The best time to travel is between October and May for the central regions of Morocco. The temperatures are then not too hot and exploration tours are pleasant to undertake. Snow and floods due to heavy rainfall are expected in the area of the High Atlas during the winter months.  

Note that travel during the fasting month of Ramadan is of course possible, but a few things need to be considered. ==References== Many of the sights in the past, including banks and offices, have shortened opening hours. Some restaurants and restaurants outside the hotel facilities remain closed during the day, but you will always be able to take care of food and drinks during the day as a traveler. During the day, public life is somewhat sluggish and subdued, but after sunset, daily fasting begins and the streets fill with life – a very special atmosphere and an intense insight into the authentic and traditional life that can only be experienced during Lent. Tourists are exempt from the fasting regime, but should nevertheless, for the sake of consideration, solidarity and respect, continue to refrain from smoking, eating and drinking in public as well. For this you should withdraw to an inconspicuous corner, to the hotel room or to the vehicle. In many hotels and restaurants no alcohol is given during this time. In the larger tourist centres, Ramadan has far less noticeable effects than in more rural areas.


The Sacrifice Festival (Id al-Adha) is the highest Islamic festival. It lasts about four days and is celebrated to the climax of Hajj (walking to Mecca) about 70 days after the fasting break in memory of the prophet Abraham. One can afford to kill an animal (e.g. E. g. Sheep or goat) and pass one third of the meat on to needy people. Also during this time special opening hours apply for many shops, museums and sights.

Entry requirements

German citizens do not need a visa for a vacation stay for tourist purposes up to a maximum of 90 days. A passport is required for entry, which is valid for at least six months after return and has at least two free sides. The same applies to children's passports or children's travel passports, which are only valid with a photo. Children's entries in a parent's passport are no longer valid since 26.06.2012. Each child needs his/her own ID document.

Travel documents must be in perfect condition, they must not be damaged (for example, torn, cracked, even ‘repaired' or accidentally washed) or illegible due to pollution and/or severe readability. Non-EU citizens must apply to the relevant embassy or consulate for a visa. Entry with the identity card is not possible. A longer stay constitutes a violation of the right of residence and is punishable by criminal law. In principle, an extension of the stay is not possible, unless the alien police complete an application with proof of financial means, if necessary. Employment contract in Morocco and justification for the need for longer stay. In all other cases – even if unpaid activities such as internships, study visits, voluntary services, etc. - a visa is required which must be applied for at an early stage prior to entry.
The entry requirements for Morocco may change in the short term. Legally binding information and/or information about the entry regulations that goes beyond this information can only be obtained directly from the embassy or one of Morocco's Consulates General.

Food and drink

A trip to Morocco is also a journey for all senses. Various spices, scents and colours make Moroccan cuisine a culinary experience. To a traditional Moroccan food is always served also a jug full of water, which is meant to wash the hands. During the meal you should always give yourself enough on the plate, try a lot and reject little, as this is considered rude. Usually it is eaten with the hands, but only with the right hand – the left hand is considered unclean.

The most famous national dishes are couscous (depending on season and region in different variations) and tajine, a meat, fish or poultry ragout, which is prepared in a traditional clay vessel with conical lid with vegetables or a fruit supplement. Fresh round flat bread is a popular side dish. You should try the pastilla (with dove's breast, parsley, egg, almonds and honey filled puff paste) or Harira (tasting soup of tomatoes and chickpeas or lentils). Sweet seductions: Moroccan pastries such as: E. g. Crepes with honey and sesame grains or almond cakes form the crowning conclusion of a Moroccan menu. A mint tea is served. For your own safety, we recommend you to have packed drinking water from the supermarket.


Fauna

Depending on the landscape, you can observe many species of birds, gazelles and bucks as well as desert foxes, hyenas and jackals. Wild boars, rabbits, otters and wild goats are also represented. In the Atlas Mountains there is also the threatened Berbaraffe. Horses, donkeys, mules and camels are a common means of transport. Large cats such as the Serval and the Karakal (desert lynx) are threatened with extinction. In addition, in Morocco there are geckos, lizards and in the dry areas live chameleons. Vultures, buzzards, dwarf eagles and storks are among the predominant bird species. In the water-rich areas live herons and flamingos. In the desert areas and dunes, special care is taken against scorpions. In the national park of El Hoceima are three different dolphin species located, the world's last monk seal colony lives in the bay of Dakhla in the south of the country.


Flora

Morocco's flora is divided into two broad zones by the Atlas Mountains: Mediterranean vegetation prevails in the northwest of the mountains. To the southeast of it extends the desert steppe. In the rain-rich mountain areas you can find closed forest stands with stone and cork oaks, Atlas cedars and aleppokiefern. Beyond the Atlas Mountains, bushes and thorn bushes dominate the landscape. The oases are shaped by date palms.

Photographing/Filming

Taking photos of locals is usually no problem. Keep in mind, however, that you travel to Morocco as a guest and that every resident always meets you with respect. In return, one should treat the locals equally respectfully and ask for permission before pressing the trigger. For example, many people cannot be photographed for religious reasons. A small fee is often expected as a thank you for a photo. Please think of sufficient replacement batteries and memory cards and do not take photos of airports, military facilities, police stations etc.


Money/currency

In Morocco, strict foreign exchange regulations apply: Dirham amounts may not be imported or exported. The Moroccan Customs website (www.douane.gov.ma) should be consulted in advance.
1 MAD=0.09111 € (as at 09/18).
Bank opening hours in all cities and larger locations are usually from 8.30 a.m. to 11.30 a. m. on weekdays and from 2.30 p.m. – 5 p. m. During the Ramadan, the opening hours are between 09.30 and 2 p.m. Saturdays 09.00 – 11.00. Note that banks in rural areas often only have short opening hours.
We recommend that you change money already at the airport. There are various exchange offices. Currency exchange is also possible in hotels and in almost all banks (the passport is usually required).
You can withdraw money from many ATM ATMs with your credit card (Euro/Mastercard, Visa). Make sure that you are always standing alone in front of the switches and do not accept any offered help!
It is recommended to always have enough cash in small notes with you for tips or small purchases.

Geography

Morocco is located in North Africa and has a diverse landscape on comparatively small national territory. Surrounded by the Mediterranean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean, the various geographical zones range from the High Atlas Mountains to the dry desert zones and foothills of the Sahara, to the fertile lowlands and dream beaches of the Atlantic coast.


Health/vaccination

Vaccine protection

Compulsory vaccinations are not required to enter Morocco.

The Federal Foreign Office recommends to check and complete the standard vaccinations according to the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute for Children and Adults on a trip (www.rki.de). This includes vaccinations against tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis for adults, if necessary. Poliomyelitis (infection), measles-mumps rubella (MMR) and influenza.

Hepatitis A, hepatitis B, typhus and rabies are recommended as travel vaccinations, long-term stay or special exposure. 

Diarrhea

Most diarrhea diseases can be avoided by appropriate food and drinking water hygiene. If you do not want to endanger your well-being during your stay, please refer to the relevant leaflets with the relevant behavioral and hygiene instructions.

Rabies

Rabies are transmitted to humans by bite and saliva of infected animals, mostly dogs. The only possible protection is pre-exposure vaccination (= before traveling), avoiding animal contacts or - after a bite - post-exposure vaccine with a modern rabies vaccine (and immunoglobulins – sometimes difficult to obtain in the country).

Malaria

The occurrence (exclusively malaria tertiana by P. vivax) is limited to individual rural areas of the province of Chefchaouen from May to October. There is no need for drug-related malaria prophylaxis.

In order to prevent mosquito-bound infection risks, all travelers are recommended to:

  • to wear body covering clothes (long trousers, long shirts),
  • in the evenings and at night, apply insecticides to free places;
  • sleep under a mosquito net if necessary.

Medical care

Medical care in the country is not entirely comparable with Europe. In Rabat and Casablanca, however, there are excellent private clinics of high standard. In rural areas, however, medical care can be problematic in terms of equipment or hygiene. Some European-trained, English or French-speaking doctors are missing here. It is recommended to take out a travel health insurance with return transport (medically useful).

Clothing

Morocco is recommended to wear inconspicuous and casual clothes that cover shoulders and knees in public. You should always be aware that you are traveling in an Islamic country. For the cooler evenings and especially for the winter months a jacket or sweater should not be missing. Please also think of firm and comfortable footwear. For visiting a mosque, women should think of a corresponding head covering (wide cloth scarf or similar).


Climate

The temperatures in Morocco are pleasant all year round and it is therefore easy to travel all year round. On the coasts of the country there is a maritime climate, which in summer influences the temperatures stable and mild. In the Atlas regions, there is a contrasting climate: at altitudes between 1,800 m and 4,000 m, the temperatures in the summer months are pleasantly cool, similar to the climate in the Alps. The areas south of the Atlas Mountains are strongly influenced by the Shirokko, the hot desert wind of the Sahara. In the interior of the country there is a consistently continental climate, which brings very hot temperatures (up to 40°C) in summer and relatively cool temperatures in winter.

Please note that the hotels and especially the riads do not usually have heating, as we are used to according to Western standard. In the cooler months, please pack warm clothes accordingly.

The Royalty of Morocco

Marrakech

Let yourself be enchanted by the sight of the earth-colored house labyrinth, from which colorful flowers and green palm giants lurk and in the background the bright blue sky and the bright white mountain massif of the High Atlas. Hardly any city can shine with such a backdrop. Take a journey of discovery and experience the confusion of sounds, colours and smells of Morocco's Capital of Culture. Unforgettable will be a stay in a typical but luxurious riad from which you can start for unforgettable city explorations.

Fes

The city in northern Morocco is considered the most royal of all cities with its size of museums and palaces. It has the largest medina in the world and is famous for its beautiful old town. The Royal Palace, the Jewish district of Mellah and the artfully crafted monuments invite visitors to visit the city. Fes is known as the center of Moroccan culture with its famous pottery and embroidery. The history of the Arab royal house, the fineness of the Andalusians, the cleverness of Judaism and the determination of the Berbers unite in the city.

 

Meknes

Not far from the Ir-Rif mountain range, Meknes is also known as the "city of the hundred minarets". Meknes impresses with its large number of beautiful mosques. In addition, many of the tasty specialities come from the region: On the markets of the city you can admire the many prized olives and olive products and try a lot.

Rabat

It is located at the mouth of the Bouregreg River, which flows into the Atlantic Ocean. Completely facing the sea, Rabat attracts with its versatility. Whether festivals, art exhibitions, shopping or relaxation – the coastal city offers something for everyone. Discover the historic center with its authentic monuments and elegant buildings.

Politics/Society

Morocco owns approximately 33 million. Population divided into Arabs (60%) and Berbers (40%). The dominant religion is Islam with almost 99%, the rest professing Christianity or Judaism. In addition, Morocco has the ethnic minority of the Gnaoua, which are the descendants of the West African slaves. They preserved their own culture and enriched them with local influences such as Judaism, Arab and Berber culture.
The country has a constitutional monarchy. ==References====External links== Head of State of Morocco and is worldly and spiritual head and commander of the
Moroccan armed forces. The political day-to-day business is led by the Prime Minister. Since 2011, this has been the PJD Chairman (Party of Justice and Development) Abdelilah Benkirane, who was elected for a four-year term. After the 2016 parliamentary election in Morocco, which also won the PJD, Benkirane failed to form a new government. On March 15, 2017, he was officially dismissed by the king as Prime Minister, and his party colleague Saadeddine Othmani was entrusted with the formation of government.


Safety instructions/road conditions

Morocco is one of the most politically stable countries in North Africa, making it a relatively safe destination. In principle, it is recommended to observe the current security instructions of the Federal Foreign Office:

The security risks for travelers vary from country to country and are regularly assessed differently within a country. The Federal Foreign Office therefore strongly advises to always observe the country-specific security instructions.

Crime

In general, but especially in the metropolises, property offences have increased. Near tourist attractions and historic city centers there is an increased risk of robbery and theft.

Traveling over land

It is strongly advised not to travel to and from the territory of Western Sahara, which is controversial under international law. Consular assistance by the Embassy of Rabat is not possible there for legal reasons.

Women traveling alone should be cautious and show healthy mistrust. Although many Moroccan women work, the understanding of roles, especially in rural areas, remains determined by traditional patterns.

Infrastructure / Road transport

Morocco has a good transport infrastructure in the larger cities and on cross-border connections.

Care should be taken in road transport. Motorists such as pedestrians cannot rely on mutual consideration and respect for traffic rules. Night trips should be avoided. On motorways, pedestrians and animals can also be expected. Enforcement of legitimate claims for damages is hardly possible. The land border to Algeria has been closed since 1994.

Global Safety Advice

It is also requested to observe the worldwide safety advice: www.auswaertiges-amt.de

Special criminal law provisions

The possession of narcotics is punishable in Morocco by imprisonment of up to 10 years as well as by high fines and customs. Even the possession of smallest quantities is punishable. Out of wedlock and same-sex sex relations (also consensual) are criminal offences in Morocco.

It is strongly advised to take out an international travel health insurance including return transport before the start of the trip to Morocco. The exclusions from benefits due to certain pre-diseases must be taken into account and, where appropriate, otherwise insured.

Language/Society

The official language is Arabic, and there are many different Berber dialects. French, English and Spanish are also spoken as a foreign language in many parts of Morocco.
In Morocco, as Islamic custom demands, women and men should always keep a certain distance from each other. As a welcome, the handshake is common, women and older people should usually only be politely nodded.

Power supply

The current voltage is 220/240 volt. The sockets are suitable for F plugs (as in France) and C plugs (like in Germany). An adapter is partly necessary, this is available in larger cities to buy. Many accommodations provide their guests with an adapter free of charge.
