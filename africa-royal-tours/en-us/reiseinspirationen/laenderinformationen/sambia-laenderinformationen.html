Zambia Country Information

Here you will find all important country information from A to Z to Zambia. Africa Royal Tours likes to create your private Zambia round trip.

Zambia - Country information A-Z

Best time to travel

In principle, Zambia can be visited all year round; depending on the interest of the traveler, the most suitable travel time is also different.
In the months of September and October: the daily temperatures are high and reach an average of 30°C, also up to 40 degrees. Due to the lack of rain, the animals collect concentrated at a few remaining water holes and you can observe the animals particularly well.
If one wants to observe above all the many newly born young animals, the best time to travel is in the rainy, hot summer months (from November/December to March/April).
Many camps in Zambia are closed or mined during the rainy season (November to April). In Kafue all camps in the Busanga area are closed, while camps remain open outside the park. In Lower Zambezi all camps are closed in the National Park and in the Chiawa concession. In South Luangwa it is generally true that all bush camps close in the park and only a few larger camps remain open in the park as well as most camps outside the park. During this time, it is only possible to have a limited number of hunting trips, as the roads have become unpassable due to the rain.
The most popular travel times are the months of June-August, as the water level in the delta is highest and attracts numerous wildlife. At the same time, winter reigns, and June to August are the coldest months of the year. The maximum daily temperatures are at pleasant 20 to 28 degrees, while it cools down to 0 to 8 degrees at night and in the early morning hours.
If you want to visit the Viktoria Falls in full and spectacular beauty, the rainy months from December to April are ideal. Floods reign between February and July and peak between March and April. The swirled water forms a dense fog and provides spectacular rainbows, which is an impressive spectacle, especially from the air. Low water: August to January, lowest water level: between November and early December. During this time there is little fog and the geological formations and the entire extent of the cases can be admired. The water level in the gorge below the falls sinks and the Sambesi meets its reputation as one of the best rafting destinations in the world. During the low-water period, the best way to visit the Victoria Falls is from the Zimbabwean side. At certain times of the year, activities such as: E. g. Rafting or visiting Livingstone Island due to the water level of the Sambesi will not be carried out.


Entry requirements

Germans, Austrians and Swiss people need a visa to enter Zambia. The fees for issuing a single entry visa are USD 50 for German nationals.For double entry, visa fees of USD 80 are charged. The passport must be valid for at least 6 months after return. In addition, the passport must have at least one completely free side for visual inspection. The same applies to children's passports, which are only valid with a photo.

Non-EU citizens must apply to the relevant embassy or consulate for a visa.


Food & Drink

The cuisine of Zambia is similar to that of South Africa. Grilled meat and grilled sausages are among the popular specialties, but also good quality vegetables, potatoes and rice. In the lake areas there are also regional and fresh fish dishes. You can also enjoy the typical Zambian variations of corn porridge with all kinds of sauces and vegetables. You should also try regional game specialities such as Impala or zebra, buffalo or crocodile meat.

A popular soft soft drink is the Rock Shandy, which consists of mineral water, lime juice and a few drops of angostura. Otherwise, the local beer brands Mosi, Castle and Rhino are mainly used in Zambia. The Amarula liqueur is particularly popular after eating. Drinking water: for your own safety, we recommend that you have packed drinking water from the supermarket.

Fauna

Through multifaceted landscapes Zambia is home to a diverse animal world. The abundance of species is almost immeasurable: elephants, buffaloes, rhinos, giraffes, zebras, primates and predatory cats as well as over 700 different bird species belong to the native animals.


Flora

Zambia offers its visitors a rich and diverse flora thanks to its various vegetation zones. The majority of the country is crossed by savannah area; in the bush and tree-savannas many different tree species can be found in fascinating shape. In the forest areas of Zambia you can also discover the typical Miombo forests or take trips to the many swamp and sponge areas as well as the foothills of the Kalahari desert.

Photographing/Filming

Think of enough storage space and spare batteries. Camera accessories are available in some shops, but are mostly more expensive than in Europe. A good binoculars is indispensable for safaris and animal watching. Taking photos of locals is usually no problem. However, remember that as a guest you travel to Zambia and that every resident always meets you with respect. In return, one should treat the locals equally respectfully and ask for permission before pressing the trigger. As a thank you for the photo, a small fee is often expected.


Money/currency

The currency is the Zambian Kwacha. 

In addition, the US dollar is a common method of payment in tourist facilities and hotels.

In larger cities, bank opening hours are usually daily from 8.15am – 3.30pm and on Saturdays from 8.15pm – 11.30am. At many ATMs you can withdraw money with the VISA Card, but NOT with the Mastercard. In most accommodation and tourist facilities, VISA and Mastercard are generally accepted. You will find all the relevant banks for your currency exchange at airports and shopping streets.

 

Luggage

The fastest and most convenient way to overcome the great distances within Zambia is by small aircraft. The domestic flights from National Park to National Park or from Lodge to Lodge are therefore used in small charter aircraft (e.g. E. g. Cessna 206 or Cessno 208).
Only limited storage space is available here. Therefore, please comply with the requirements regarding the baggage, otherwise an additional seat will be charged or the baggage will only be transported with the next flight.
In general: max. 20 kg per person incl. Hand luggage, not hard case or bulky trolleys, but snug pockets (maximum size 25 cm width x 30 cm height x 62 cm length). Please give us your weight in advance of your trip, as the airlines need this for logistical reasons.
Some airlines also allow 23 kg per person incl. Hand luggage, which we mention in your travel creation – if you have any ambiguities, please contact us.
Please take important medically prescribed medications in your hand luggage!

Geography

Zambia is twice as large as Germany (approx.752,614 km2) and is characterized above all by the highland plateau and the surrounding expansive lakes and marshes in the highland valleys. The Sambesi River, which also gave the country its name, forms the southern border. The bordering countries are the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the north and northwest, Tanzania in the northeast and Malawi in the east, Mozambique in the southeast and Zimbabwe in the south, Namibia in the southwest and Angola in the west.


Health/vaccination

When entering Europe directly (direct flight!) no compulsory vaccinations are required. Malaria prophylaxis is recommended for certain regions. In addition, all standard vaccinations should be reviewed or refreshed in accordance with the vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute. We recommend a consultation with your family doctor.
Especially in the summer months you should carry sunscreen with you due to the high UV radiation with high protection factor as well as a headgear. Think also of an appropriate mosquito protection.
Since medical care is not comparable to German standards, a well-equipped travel pharmacy should be carried along.
Bilharziosis: Avoid bathing in the wild. Not only do crocodiles and hippos live in most lakes and rivers, but there is a high risk of contagion with bilharziosis, especially in standing waters.


Clothing

In the summer months, airy and comfortable clothes are recommended, together with a rain jacket. We recommend the onion principle – so wearing several layers of clothing on top of each other, so that you can take on or off a layer if necessary. A hat or cap protects against excessive sunlight.
To protect against mosquitoes, long, body-covering clothing with bright colors is recommended (please do not use bright/intense colours or black or white during the safari). For the cooler evenings and especially for the winter months a (fleece) jacket or sweater should not be missing also gloves should be in the luggage. Be sure to pack firm, comfortable footwear.
Since most safari activities are carried out in open four - wheeled vehicles or boats, wearing suitable clothing is very important.
Many of the camps and lodges offer free laundry services. Hotel and guest houses charge for this service.
A warm jacket / fleece offers wind protection in winter and protection from rain in summer.
It is recommended to pack a cap and gloves against the cold in the winter months (May – Sept).
Please think of swimwear for a dip in the pool.
Please note that camouflage clothing is acceptable on safari, but should not be worn in cities and especially at border crossings.
Please also think of comfortable trekking boots (for normal guided bush hikes no heavy hiking boots are needed) and sandals / flip flops.
Other personal items:
- Quick closure bag to store personal items and keep memory cards dry and dust free
- In most camps flashlights are provided, but it is recommended to carry your own head lamp, as they are often needed, especially on mobile tent safaris.
- Camera equipment, binoculars, additional batteries and additional memory cards. The possibilities of emptying memory cards are very limited.
-Reading material – some camps have a small library, but it is still possible to bring books for lunchtime.

Climate

Zambia has a mild, tropical climate and is therefore pleasant for visitors to travel. The seasons can be divided into three weather periods: The dry season falls in spring (September to November) and hot temperatures are between 23 and 33 °C. The rainy season falls into the African summer (November to March) and is characterized by a tropical hot and humid climate. Temperatures range from 27 to 38 °C. The weather is predominantly variable and there are isolated storms. The Zambian winter (May to August) is characterized by cooler, moderate temperatures between 15 and 27 °C, but in June and July temperatures may fall at night and in the mornings to one-digit degrees.

Policy/economy/society

Zambia has about 10 million inhabitants and consists mainly of the Bantu population.

The economy consists mainly of copper exports and mining other precious metals; agriculture is very low, as only a few percent of the country's land is used. Tourism has not yet played a major role, but the well-known Viktoria cases are bringing more and more visitors to the country and could develop into a driving economic force.

Up to 1964, Zambia had the name Rhodesia and was ruled under British rule. It became an autonomous state in 1964, in which the first free elections took place in 1994.
The Zambian government was recently re-formed in 2011 and PF chairman Michael Sata was elected for a term of office of 5 years.

National parks

Kafue National Park: One of the best nature reserves in Africa and largest in the world. The Kafue and Lufupa rivers flow through the park, which provides for the large occurrences of hippos. Home to many wildlife species such as all predatory cat species, elephants, buffalo, gnus and numerous antelope species.

Liuwa Plains National Park: Especially suitable for observing the Gnu migration from Angola to Zambia, which is accompanied by the hyenas of the Liuwa Plains. Also ideal for bird watching.

Loher Zambezi National Park: Located along the Sambesi River below the Karibastausee. Elephants, hippos, water antelopes and even predators can be observed on sand islands.

Mosi-oa-Tunya National Park: Here are the famous and imposing Viktoria Falls located. Some animals such as giraffes, zebras and antelopes are native to the river banks of the Zambesi River. The rare white rhinos have also been spotted in this area.

North Luangwa National Park: Known for its buffalo herds and its natural beauty.

South Luangwa National Park: Transition from North Luangwa National Park. In the subsequent change from dry season to flood, a breathtaking variety of animals can be discovered. These include elephants, giraffes, rhinoceros, hippos, zebras, lions, leopards, and numerous bird species.

Safety instructions/customs regulations/criminal regulations

Zambia is a politically stable country. Due to the situation in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, refugees on the Zambian side are often admitted, which may affect security and supply in these border areas. In principle, it is recommended to observe the current security instructions of the Federal Foreign Office.

As in any other travel country, valuables should never be worn openly. These and important documents such as passports, travelers' cheques and airline tickets should be delivered to the reception desk for storage or stored in your room safe.

Tip: For security, we recommend that you keep a copy of your travel documents and passports separately from the originals.

It is noted that the export of parts of game trophies from Zambia is prohibited. The export ban includes not only tusks or horns, but also objects from the national parks that are often regarded as tourist souvenirs. E. g. Skeletal or dental parts of dead wild animals. Those who are found at the airport or at the border points with appropriate objects must expect arrest. Each export shall be subject to approval by the Zambian Game Protection Authority (ZAWA).

Further customs information on the import of goods can be obtained from the embassy of your destination country. Only there can a legally binding information be given to you. You can find the customs regulations for Germany on the website of the German Customs and by app "Customs and Travel" or ask there by telephone.

Infringements against entry and residence regulations are relentlessly punished by the Zambian immigration authorities. Foreigners without a valid visa or residence permit have to expect high financial and/or prison sentences and/or deportation (see also entry regulations).

Photographing of military and other security facilities, even if they are not always recognizable as such at first glance, as well as military and police personnel, is expressly prohibited and may lead to immediate detention. Staying in the immediate vicinity of the Presidential Palace (State House) is prohibited. Basically, it is recommended to ask for permission before photographing buildings and persons.

Sambish conceptions of sexual morality are sometimes distinctly different from those prevailing in Europe. ==References====External links== "Unnatural sex practices") can be punished with prison sentences ranging from 15 years to life imprisonment and forced labour. Prostitution and possession of pornographic material are punishable.

The import, possession and consumption of narcotics, including so-called "soft drugs", are threatened with high penalties, often long - term deprivation of liberty. Please also note the special note on the import of medications for personal use in the Medical Notes section.

Smoking in public places is prohibited by law. Infringement may be punishable by fines and/or prison sentences of up to two years.

Sambish prisons are overcrowded and usually have simple sanitary facilities as well as inadequate medical and food supplies.


Language/Society

The official language is English, but the dialects and languages of the Bantus tribes are spoken in large parts of the population.

About three-quarters of the population of Zambia are Christians, about a quarter is Muslim.

Despite often simple circumstances, the warmth of the locals will delight you and make your journey a unique experience.


Power supply

The current voltage is 230 volts. Sockets are two-pin and/or three-pin and have a different format to the German plugs, an adapter is necessary. The appropriate adapter is usually available in supermarkets. Many accommodations provide their guests with an adapter free of charge.

During the rainy season there may be more power outages.


Telephone/Internet

The code for Germany is 0049.

Mobile phone reception is only guaranteed near larger cities. Ask your mobile phone provider about the current roaming contracts.

Conversations abroad can be conducted by hotels or by telephone cards. We recommend buying a SIM card from a national mobile phone provider.

Many accommodations offer internet connections.

Tips

Although tips are not mandatory, they are accepted worldwide as a thank you and appreciated by all employees. The amount of tip should reflect the quality/service of your safari. In Zambia, it is good to give tips in foreign currency (euro and USD). To give you an idea of who and how much you can give, we have drawn up the following directive:

- Guides in the camps – USD10.00 – USD15.00 per day, per guest
- Mokoro driver – USD10.00 per driver
- Other employees (boaters, bar staff, housekeeper) – USD5.00 – USD10.00 per day

Time Shift

There is no time difference during Central European summer time. During the winter season, Zambia Europe is one hour ahead (CET + 1 hour).
