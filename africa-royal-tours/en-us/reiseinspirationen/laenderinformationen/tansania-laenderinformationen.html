Tanzania - Country information from A to Z

Best time to travel

Basically Tanzania can be visited all year round, the great rainy season lasts from March to May, the small rainy season from October/November to December. However, nature has its own rhythm and does not follow a strict schedule, so seasonal deviations are always possible. This also applies to the so-called "Great Migration" – the annual Gnu Migration between Tanzania and Kenya. Depending on the time and amount of rainfall, the gnus also depends on the local conditions. The route remains largely the same – but the exact schedule varies from year to year.

Depending on the interest of the traveler, however, the most suitable travel time is different.

If one wants to observe the migration in the Serengeti, one should travel from December to March or June to August/September. Depending on the month, the Great Migration is then located in different regions of the Serengeti.

For local migration In the Tarangire National Park (not to be confused with the Great Migration), a visit in the months from June to October is recommended.

Food and drink

Tanzania's cuisine is characterized by local specialities such as Mtori (cooked meat with bananas), Mchicha (vegetable stew), Ugali (maize flour polenta eaten with meat and stews) and Chips Mayai (egg cake with potatoes). The lodges and camps mainly serve international cuisine, often with South African spices or vegetables. In the interior, the kitchen is rather meaty, fish and crustaceans are served on the coast. Since Zanzibar is characterized by Islam, instead of pork there is chicken and beef.

You can also enjoy the local specialty Chai Maziwa, a tea with milk that is served with a lot of sugar. You should also try one of the local beers such as Kilimajaro, Serengeti, Safari or Banana beer. Konyagi, a ginger-like drink is served after dinner.

Typical fruit varieties include pineapple, mango and bananas, the Jackfruit and Papaya. For us Central Europeans, what is the potato is the manioc root for the East African.

Fauna

Tanzania's wildlife is one of the richest species in Africa. The Serengeti offers habitat for many wildlife and is ideal for observations and safaris. In addition, there are the many other national parks and protected areas, especially the Ngorongoro crater, which is distinguished as a world natural heritage. Here a concentration of wild animals is given due to the relatively "small" area. Lovers of the underwater world will find their money on the islands of Pemba, Zanzibar and Mafia, where clear water and colourful coral reefs and marine animals await you.


Flora

The vegetation of Tanzania ranges from the dry desert over rainy areas to the eternal ice of the Kilimanjaro glaciers. In most parts of the country there are predominantly dry and wet-savannah areas, in which a variety of tree species such as the well-known umbrella acacia or the Baobab tree can be found. The rest of the country is covered by coastal and swamp areas as well as semi-deserts.

Photographing/Filming

Think of enough storage space and spare batteries. Camera accessories are available in many shops, but are mostly more expensive than in Europe. For perfect shots we recommend a light sensitive camera and a tripod.

A good binoculars is indispensable for safaris and animal watching.

Taking photos of locals is usually no problem. However, remember that as a guest you travel to Tanzania and that every resident always meets you with respect. In return, one should treat the locals equally respectfully and ask for permission before pressing the trigger. As a thank you for the photo, a small fee is often expected.

Money/currency

The currency is Tanzanian shilling.

Before the trip, exchange money into US dollars and make sure that the notes were printed after 2006, better even after 2008 (due to counterfeits). It is recommended to take notes in all denominations – change is rarely available. The US Dollar is a widely used currency and is also widely accepted as a tip.
At airports, banks and exchange offices (Forex offices) money can be exchanged in local currency. The larger hotels also offer currency exchange. Larger banknotes only on presentation of a passport. Travel checks are almost nowhere accepted anymore. In exchange offices you will always get a better course than in banks. In larger cities you can withdraw money from the banks by credit card, and in better hotels credit cards (mostly Visa and Mastercard) are accepted as means of payment.


Geography

Located in East Africa, Tanzania borders Uganda and Kenya in the north, Mozambique, Malawi and Zambia in the south, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi and Rwanda in the west.

With an area (about 945.087 km2) Tanzania is almost three times the size of Germany.

To the north begins Masai Savanne, which is adjacent to Kenya, which passes to the east into the coastal plain. This is followed by the high plateau that reaches far to the south of the country. Special highlight is the highest mountain in Africa, the Kilimanjaro with 5,895 m in the north of the country. Through tectonic plate shifts runs through the entire country of the East African trench. In addition, many craters and volcanoes such as the Ngorongoro crater or the Rungwe have been created, which offer a unique vegetation.


Health/vaccination

When entering Europe directly (direct flight!) no compulsory vaccinations are required. When entering a country declared by the WHO to be a yellow fever epidemic (e.g. A valid yellow fever vaccination is required for Ethiopia, Kenya or Zambia. See also notes under the item "Entry regulations" .

Malaria prophylaxis is recommended for the entire mainland, including national parks. In the last major rainy season in 2013 and 2014 and since mid-May 2017, there has been a significant increase in malaria cases in Dar es Salaam. Rainfalls and floods in the Arusha region and in the coastal region can lead to an increase in the mosquito population.

In addition, all standard vaccinations should be reviewed or refreshed in accordance with the vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute. We recommend a consultation with your family doctor.

Following the emergence of cholera in refugee facilities in the Kigoma region, in early 2015, there is now widespread spread across large parts of the country. Also affected are Dar es Salaam (especially slums), Arusha and Zanzibar. It is affected exclusively the native population. Outbreak control measures are under way, but there is currently no prospect of an end to the outbreak and further development. In compliance with the recommended hygiene measures (see below), there is still no or very little risk for tourists.
Cholera is transmitted via insufficiently processed drinking water or raw food. Only a small part of the people infected with cholera suffer and of these in turn the majority with a comparatively mild clinical course. (See also leaflet of the Federal Foreign Office) www.diplo.de/travel medicine).
Cholera vaccination is generally available. It requires a double dose with a minimum of two weeks' advance. The indication for cholera vaccination is usually only at special exposures (e.g. Work in hospital with cholera patients). In case of doubt, an individual consultation by a tropical or travel physician is recommended.
Especially in the summer months you should carry sunscreen with you due to the high UV radiation with high protection factor as well as a headgear. Think also of an appropriate mosquito protection. Please observe the current information provided by the Federal Foreign Office.

Clothing

In the summer months airy and comfortable clothes are recommended. Short trousers or skirts as well as shoulder-free outerwear should be avoided. On Zanzibar, especially in Stonetown, one should pay attention to covered knees and shoulders in the public out of respect for the population and religion. Long clothing in bright colours is recommended to protect against mosquitoes. For the cooler evenings and especially for the winter months and the higher layers a jacket or sweater should not be missing.

Climate

Temperatures are around 25-30°C all year round. In the highlands you can expect similar temperatures during the day, and at night it cools down noticeably. On the coast tropical climate, with high humidity, can be reached by day up to 36°C, at night it cools only slightly. In the Tanzanian spring (October to December) there may be short rains again and again, the main rainy season is in autumn (March to May), here there are heavy rains.

National parks

Arusha National Park: Forested national park with mountain and lake landscapes, with Mount Meru (4.556 m). Hyenas, leopards, giraffes, zebras, dik dik and numerous bird species are home here.

Gombe Stream National Park: Smallest national park in Tanzania, located on the shores of Lake Tangayika and only accessible by boat. Many mammals and monkeys live in the mountain ranges of the Gombe Mountains. The rainforests growing on the river banks contain a variety of bird species, chimpanzees and other wildlife.

Katavi National Park: Located in the west of the country, near Katavi Lake, Katuma River and Chada Lake with its huge plains. The water sources are the survival basis of buffaloes, elephants, hippos, lions, impalas and giraffes.

Lake Manyara National Park: At the Great Rift Valley, home to the climbing lions, hippos and buffalo. On the banks of Lake Manyara there are many species of birds, such as birds. Pelicans and flamingos.

Mount Kilimanjaro Nationalpark: One of the Seven Summits, with a height of 5,895 m, the highest mountain in Africa and a challenging mountain, which is climbed in stage destinations with a team of mountain guides and chefs. There are six different routes, ranging from simple natural paths to heavy, high alpine climbs.

Saadani National Park: The only lake in Tanzania that borders the coast. Unique coastal, lake and bush landscape. Due to the different landscape forms, sea and savannah animals are found here, such as Turtles, hippos, crocodiles, elephants, monkeys, zebras, antelopes, kudus, antlope species and wart pigs.

Serengeti National Park: The largest treasure in Tanzania, with a huge number of animal species. In addition to grassy plains and savannas with their distinctive acacia trees to wooded hilly landscapes, crossed by repeatedly appearing lakes, swamps and rivers. Here you can experience the annual migration of millions of gnus, zebras and gazelles between December and March (South Serengeti) and between August and October (North Serengeti).

Tarangire National Park: Impressive savannah landscape with monkey bread trees. From June to October migration of elephants, giraffes and antelopes. Also domestic are leopards and rhinos.

Udzungwa Mountain National Park: Mountain range in eastern Tanzania, mainly home to various species of monkeys and a variety of insects, amphibians and over 400 bird species. Extremely rare animals such as the grey-faced ruffle dog, the dwarf galago and some rare monkey species are at home here.

The Tanzanian government is considering a regular adjustment of parking charges. This adjustment can also take place in a very short time.

Kitulo Plateau National Park: In the mountain regions of Kipengere, Poroto and Livingstone at about 2,600 m altitude. Known for its flowers as well as wild mountain animals such as goats, bird species and insects.

Mahale Mountain National Park: This park is home to a few chimpanzees who live wildly in freedom. The dreamlike Tanganyika lake with beautiful shoreland forms a dreamlike backdrop to the Mahale Mountains, pompously rising in the background. In the lowlands of the Miombo bush area, there are buffaloes, antelopes, zebras, wild dogs and even lions.

Mikumi National Park: Forms the northern tip of the Selous National Park. Here you will find the Big Five of the animal world and many other different wildlife species, which feel very comfortable there especially because of the Mkata flood plains.

Mkomazi National Park: Between Kilimanjaro and the border of Kenya, the park is home to rare black rhinoceros, oryx antelopes, spiral and gerenuk gazelles, as well as wild dogs, giraffes, zebras, buffalos, elephants, elephant and cow antelope. Predominant landscape is the hilly dry savannah.

Ruaha National Park: Known for its semi-dry vegetation with baobab trees and the high concentration of elephants. The endangered wild dogs as well as leopards, spoondogs, jackals, chimpanzees, giraffes, zebras, Elenantilopes, Kudus and Impalas also live here. Crocodiles, amphibians and reptiles are at home along the waterways of the Great Ruaha and the Mzombe River.

Rubondo Island National Park: Important breeding ground for many bird species in the southwest of Lake Victoria.

Policy/economy/society

Formerly called Tanganyika, independence was declared after German and English colonial occupation in 1961 and the state of Tanzania, as it is known today, was founded. In 1964, the mainland area of Tanganyika and Zanzibar (Islands of Pemba and Unguja) merged with what is now Tanzania.

The population is made up of the Bantu people, which are divided into many small tribes. The largest groups are the Sukuma (12%), the Nyamwezi (9%), the Hehet and Bena (8%), the Haya (7%), the Swahili (6%), the Changga (6%) and the Masai (3%). There are also Europeans, Arabs, Pakistanis and Indians living in Tanzania, as well as refugees from Burundi and the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Tanzania is a presidential republic, with extensive autonomy of Zanzibar.

In mid-2015, the government introduced a so-called "infrastructure tax" for hotel overnight stays on Zanzibar. This is USD 1 per person per day and will be charged directly from the hotel. Unfortunately, it is not possible for us to include this new tax in advance, as it has to be paid locally.


Safety instructions/road conditions

Tanzania is a safe travel destination in most parts and also pleasant to travel. In some parts of the country and parts of the city, the crime rate is increased. In principle, one should always observe the current safety instructions of the Federal Foreign Office. We will be happy to advise you on any further questions. As in any other travel country, valuables should never be worn openly, especially in places with larger collections of people. Valuables as well as passports, travelers' cheques and airline tickets should be delivered to the reception or stored in your room safe.

Tip: For security, we recommend that you keep a copy of your travel documents and passports separately from the originals.

Since October 2012, there have been repeated demonstrations and clashes with the police in parts of Dar es Salaam and other parts of the country. At the beginning of May 2013, an attack on a church in Arusha was committed. On April 13, 2014, the explosion of an explosive device that injured 17 people occurred in a lively bar in Arusha. On the evening of June 13, 2014, an explosives attack occurred in Stonetown, Zanzibar, killing one person and hurt nine more. An attack with a hand grenade was carried out at an Indian restaurant in Arusha on 7 July 2014. Several guests were injured.

Travelers are advised to avoid demonstrations and crowds. Increased caution and vigilance in public places as well as visiting tourist attractions and religious sites are advised. This is especially true on national and Islamic holidays as well as during and after Friday prayers.

Relations between religious groups on the island of Zanzibar, which is located on the mainland of Tanzania, are tense. Since May 2012, there have been demonstrations and clashes with the police in Stone Town / Zanzibar City. Clergy dignitaries were repeatedly attacked; in February 2013, there were violent attacks and arson attacks on Christian institutions. This is also the reason why increased vigilance is advised, especially when visiting religious sites.

There is still a very high risk of pirate attacks and capers off the coasts of Somalia and its neighbouring countries, as well as in adjacent waters. Ships deep in the Indian Ocean (around the Seychelles and Madagascar) as well as Kenya, Tanzania, Mozambique, Yemen and Oman are still at risk of being attacked and captured. Shipmasters in the above areas are strongly advised to exercise the utmost caution. Despite international efforts to curb piracy, the number of pirate attacks remains high and effective protection cannot be guaranteed. Shipmasters in the endangered waters will be registered with the Maritime Security Center under External Link, opens in new windowwww.mschoa.org highly recommended.

At present, there are numerous cases where tourists are approached by alleged taxi drivers and offered driving opportunities to rob them and/or force them to withdraw money from the vending machine with their bank card under threat of violence. Several times tourists were forced to obtain money transfers via Western Union through family or acquaintance calls. In and around Dar es Salaam, this type of crime is increasing significantly. Preferential places are the station, ferry stop and bus stations. It is therefore strongly recommended to use exclusively registered taxis. These can be seen by a green, yellow or blue longitudinal stripe on the white body. In case of doubt, the driver should be asked to show his registration. It is expressly advised not to accept offers of rides or help in withdrawing money or bank transfers from strangers.

Unguarded beaches and secluded areas should also be avoided during the day. This applies in particular to the beaches in the coastal towns. With nightfall, walks and cross-country trips were to be avoided. In the case of overnight stays, in particular at campsites and in remote areas, adequate safeguards should be taken into account.

Please always keep in mind the current information provided by the Federal Foreign Office.

Power supply

The current voltage is 230/240 volt. Sockets are two- or three-pin and have a different format to the German plugs, an adapter is necessary. The appropriate adapter is usually available in supermarkets. Many accommodations provide their guests with an adapter free of charge


Telephone/Internet

The code for Germany is 0049.

Mobile communications reception in Tanzania is quite good, but there are network-free zones in more remote areas. Ask your mobile phone provider about the current roaming contracts.

Conversations abroad can be conducted by hotels. We recommend buying a SIM card from a national mobile phone provider. Telephone cards can be purchased in gas stations and shops.

We would like to point out that taking a Samsung Galaxy Note 7 mobile phone on board various domestic airlines is not permitted for security reasons.

Free Wi-Fi is available in many hotels and lodges, mostly in public areas (restaurant, reception). The connection is sometimes somewhat unstable, simple messages and emails can often be sent well.
