Namibia - Country information A-Z

Best time to travel

Basically, Namibia can be visited all year round. The rainy season in the north of the country lasts from November to March, in these months one sees above all many young animals. From March to October is the dry season, with pleasant temperatures, most animals are seen at the end of the dry season.


Entry requirements

EU citizens do not need a visa for a vacation stay of up to 90 days and receive a free entry stamp (Visitor ́s Entry Permit) upon entry. The passport must be valid for at least six months after return and have at least two free sides. The same applies to children's passports or children's travel passports, which are only valid with a photo. Each child needs his/her own ID document. Travel documents must be in perfect condition, they must not be damaged (for example, broken, cracked, even "repaired" or also accidentally washed) or unreadable or severely legible due to pollution. Non-EU citizens must apply to the relevant embassy or consulate for a visa.

The entry stamp "Visitors Entry Permit" can be issued for a period of up to 90 days. However, this does not necessarily have to be the case. As a rule, the period of validity is limited to the period of actual intended stay resulting from the return flight date. However, it is strongly recommended to check immediately upon entry whether at least as many days of residence have been authorised as is actually needed. An overdraft of the residence permit, even inadvertently, can lead to the imposition of drastic (liability) penalties. If there is a need for renewal during the stay – e.g. In case of illness, for example, an extension of the visa should be requested in time at the Ministry of Home Affairs, Independence Avenue/Ecke Casino Str., Windhuk. Here, however, a fee of approx. 780,- N$ is charged.

In all other cases – even if unpaid activities such as internships, study visits, voluntary services, etc. - a visa is required which must be applied for at an early stage before entering the country.

When entering Namibia via South Africa, it should be noted that new entry requirements will apply there from 1 July 2014. Important changes include: Any transfer of a visa leads to an explanation of the unwanted person and an entry ban. For entry and transit with minor children, an international birth certificate, from which the parents are visible, as well as a declaration of consent of the non-accompanying parent or proof of the sole custody right, are required since 1 June 2015. Detailed and binding information can be obtained from the South African representative responsible for your place of residence.

Food and drink

The cuisine in Namibia is characterized by seasonal and regional products depending on the region. So you can find many fish and seafood dishes, especially in places close to the coast.

You can also enjoy Namibia's many good meat specialties such as beef, game meat and ostrich meat. Here you must not miss to participate in one of the typical Braais (grill evenings) to taste grilled meat and sausages. The meat has a very good quality, as the animals in Namibia are kept on natural pastures. You should also try the well-known "Biltong" (dried meat) and "Mieliepap/Pap" (meat porridge with meat).

The many wines from southern Africa or the Namibian beer awarded with prizes (Tafel Lager, Windhuk Lager) are recommended for eating. Especially in the larger cities such as Windhuk and Swakopmund, the range of fresh products is very large. In rural areas, especially in the north, only a few fresh foods are often offered.

The tap water in Namibia has a good quality, for your own safety we recommend you still packed drinking water from the supermarket.

 


Fauna

The conditions are rough, yet Namibia has a diverse flora and fauna. The animals have adapted to the conditions and enchant with their abundance and richness of species. The nature conservation conditions of the country allow the preservation of the versatile flora and fauna. Namibia's wildlife reserve now accounts for almost 15% of the country's area. The typical African savannahs such as zebras, giraffes, the Big Five and numerous antelope and predator species are mainly found in the Etosha National Park. In the deserted areas of southern Namibia, the Oryx antelopes and also the ostriches, ostrichs, kudus, mountain zebras and leopards have settled justly in the dry climate. There are over 600 bird species and also a large number of different amphibian, reptile and poisonous snake species.

Flora

In the dry season Namibia seems colourless at first glance. But there is a variety of vegetation due to the different climatic zones. When the first rain is used, bare areas turn into a dense sea of flowers. Most of the country's land is savannah. Here there are thorn bushes, wide grass areas, acacia trees, but also camel thorn bushes. In the northern Mopanesavannen there are also some tree species, in the Sambesi Stripe (ehem. Caprivi strips) increase the tree-savannas with monkey-bread trees and palm trees. In the dry desert areas of the south there are mainly water-storing plants such as the quiver tree.


Photographing/Filming

Think of enough storage space and spare batteries. Camera accessories are available in many shops, but are mostly more expensive than in Europe. Protect your equipment from dust and heat.

A good binoculars is indispensable for safaris and animal observations, as is a tripod for animal photos at dusk.

Photos of locals are usually not a problem. Remember, however, that as a guest you travel to Namibia and that every resident always meets you with respect. In return, one should treat the locals equally respectfully and ask for permission before pressing the trigger. As a thank you for the photo, a small fee is often expected.

Some local tribes, such as the San or Himba tribes, are given a small gift, such as: Tobacco, corn flour or sugar. Financial gifts are not recommended here. If you have any questions, your tour guide is of course at your disposal.


Money/currency

Means of payment is the Namibian dollar, which is connected to the South African rand and is valued equally.

Bank opening hours in all cities and larger places are usually from 09.00 a.m. to 3 p.m. on weekdays and from 09.00 p.m to 11.00 p.m on Saturdays. Note that banks in rural areas often only have short opening hours.

We recommend you to change money at Windhuk Airport. There are various exchange counters as well as an ATM – but this only accepts VISA cards.

You can withdraw money from many ATM ATMs with EC and Maestro cards. Make sure that you are always standing alone in front of the switches and do not accept any offered help!

Service stations are not allowed to accept credit cards, so please plan enough cash for round trips with the rental car.

Currency restrictions: The import and export of the local currency is limited to NAD 2,000. The import of foreign currency by visitors is unlimited, but must be declared upon entry. The export of foreign currency is unlimited up to the amount that has been introduced and declared as long as the departure is within 12 months. There are no limits to travel between Botswana, Lesotho, Namibia, South Africa and Swaziland, as these countries are members of the same common area.

Geography

Namibia is about 824.290 km2 and is bordered to the north by Angola, to the east and south by the two countries Botswana and South Africa and to the west by the Atlantic. ==References== Caprivi Stripe) in the north-east of the country also borders the neighbouring countries Angola, Zambia and Zimbabwe.

The present climate zones can be divided essentially into: the deserts Namib (located in the west) and the Kalahari (in the east), which are separated from the inland highlands. Adjacent begins the dry Kalahari highlands.

The country is divided into the headquarters, the Northern, the Eastern, the Southern and the Western regions.

Health/vaccination

When entering Europe directly (direct flight!) no compulsory vaccinations are required. When entering a country declared by the WHO to be a yellow fever epidemic (e.g. A valid yellow fever vaccination is required for Ethiopia, Kenya or Zambia.

The Federal Foreign Office recommends that standard vaccinations be checked and completed on a trip according to the current vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute for Children and Adults ( www.rki.de ). For adults, these include vaccinations against tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis (cheuch cough), possibly also against polio, mumps, measles, rubella (MMR), pneumococci and influenza. Hepatitis A and hepatitis B, typhus, rabies and meningococcal disease (ACWY) are recommended as travel vaccinations and in case of long-term or special exposure.

Since July 2018, 14 regions 4 - Khomas, Omusati, Erongo and Oshana - have been affected by an outbreak of hepatitis E with over 2400 patients and 10 deaths in pregnant women. The epicentre of the outbreak is the capital Windhuk. Pregnant women in the last third of pregnancy are particularly at risk from genotype 1 prevalent in the region.

There is a high risk of malaria all year round in the north of the country, along the Kawango and Kunene rivers as well as in the Caprivi Strip. There is a medium risk (higher in the rainy season, lower in the dry season) in the provinces of Omusati, Oshana (eastern Ovamboland) Ohangwena, Oshikoto (Etosha National Park) and in the northeastern parts of Otjozondjupa and Omaheke. There is a low risk in the subsequent parts of the country going south and in Windhuk. In addition, all standard vaccinations should be reviewed or refreshed in accordance with the vaccination calendar of the Robert Koch Institute. We recommend a consultation with your family doctor.

Due to appropriate food and drinking water hygiene, most diarrhea diseases and especially cholera can be avoided. In recent years, there has been a repeated increase in cholera cases, especially in the north.

Some basic rules: Drink only water of safe origin, e.g. Bottled water, never tap water. Use filtered, disinfected or boiled water in an emergency. On the way, also use drinking water for washing dishes and brushing teeth. For food: cooking, peeling or disinfecting. Keep flies away from your food. Wash your hands as often as possible with soap, but always after bowel movements and always before preparing food and before eating. Hand disinfection, where appropriate, perform single-use towels.

Especially in the summer months you should carry sunscreen with you due to the high UV radiation with high protection factor as well as a headgear. Think also of an appropriate mosquito protection.


Clothing

In the summer months, airy and comfortable clothes are recommended, along with an umbrella or a rain jacket. In winter the daily temperatures are pleasant, in the morning and at night it can become sensitively cold, often up to frost temperatures. The temperatures can vary greatly, so we recommend the onion principle. On the one hand, you should take summer clothes, on the other hand, warm transitional clothing on your journey. To protect against mosquitoes, long, body-covering clothing is recommended in bright colours.

Climate

Namibia has a dry, pleasant climate and is therefore very good to travel all year round. The seasons are opposed to those in Europe due to the situation in the southern hemisphere.

The cold Benguela stream keeps the coast cool, humid and rain-free almost all year round. In the country, most precipitation falls during the summer (November to April). January and February are hot, with daily temperatures exceeding 40 degrees Celsius. The nights are usually cool. During the winter nights it can get quite cold, but it is usually warm and pleasant during the day.

In summer (December to March) heavy thunderstorms are expected and the climate is tropical-humid. After the summer rain the land begins to green. In this hot season, sea fog often forms on the coast, but it loosens up at noon.

Autumn (April to June) is characterized by pleasant temperatures during the day (approx. 25°C) and at night (approx. 10°C). In the dry Namibian winter (July-September) the thermometer climbs to a pleasant 20°C during the day, while night frosts in the desert and inland areas are possible.

Average sunshine duration per year: about 300 days.

Literature

For a detailed travel preparation we recommend e.g. "IWANOWSKI Namibia", "DuMont travel manual Namibia", or the guide "Marco Polo NAMIBIA". You should only buy required maps after arrival on site.

National parks in Namibia

Central Region

Daan Viljoen Game Reserve: Highland plain with hilly landscape with freshwater dam. It attracts more than 200 bird and other species. One finds among other things a variety of buck species, Kudus, Oryx and also baboons.

Northern Region

Etosha National Park: Protected area with extensive, flat sand and bush-savannah, grassland plain in the hilly forest area with elephants, giraffes, lions and leopards.

Skeleton Coast Park: Mountainy desert and dune landscape with rugged volcanic gorges by the sea. Versatile mineral world, lions, hyenas, jackals, desert elephants and seals.

Khaudum National Park: Flood plain of the Kavango river and Kalahari forests with elephants, lions, leopards, buffaloes, wild dogs and antelope species.

Waterberg Plateau Park: Green vegetation with sandste information with rhinoceros, antelope species and gnus.

Onguma National Park: Flat sand and bush canopy, wetlands with over 30 different mammal species and over 300 bird species.

Caprivi region

Bwabwata National Park: River delta areas of the rivers Kwando and Kavango with wetlands and floodplains with great biodiversity such as elephants, buffaloes, hippos, giraffes, lions, leopards and over 400 different bird species.

Mamili National Park: Wetland and moorland with reeds and many islands. Especially during the rainy season gathering point of elephants, buffaloes, moor antelopes, Riedböcken, but also crocodiles. Eldorado for bird lovers.

Mudumu National Park: River Kwando River with forested areas and plains with papyrus reeds. There are many water loving species such as elephants and hippos, crocodiles and otters, as well as lions, leopards and hyenas. In addition, bird species such as cranes, storks and ibiss.

Southern Region

Namib Naukluft Park: Dry and mountainous protected area with dried up river beds and the Sesriem gorge. Beautiful and contrasting desert landscape.

Sossusvlei: Part of the Namib desert with high sand dunes, at whose feet there are pans of clay. Sossusvlei has one of the most impressive landscapes in Namibia, along with the Deadvlei: white clay pan with centuries-old camel thorn trees, surrounded by red desert sand and contrasting blue sky.

Policy/economy/society

About 2.1 million. People of different ethnic groups and cultures characterize the entire country. The population can be divided into 11 different population groups with their own culture, language and history. The most famous are the Damara, the Herero, the Himba and the San.
Since the 1990 Declaration of Independence, in which all social groups in Namibia were treated as equals, any resident can live where he wishes. However, it can still be seen that certain tribes have settled in certain "homelands" (home countries).
Most people live in the African and rural north, followed by Central Namibia with its capital Windhuk. On the other hand, the south of the country is the least populated.

Safety instructions/road conditions

Namibia is a safe travel destination in large parts and also pleasant to travel. In some parts of the country (especially in urban areas and after dark) the crime rate is increased. In principle, one should always observe the current safety instructions of the Federal Foreign Office. We will be happy to advise you on any further questions.

As in any other travel country, valuables should never be carried openly. Valuables as well as passports, travelers' cheques and airline tickets should be delivered to the reception or stored in your room safe.

Tip: For security, we recommend that you keep a copy of your travel documents and passports separately from the originals. Make sure that the car is always well locked, do not leave any valuables in the car.

Driving in Namibia: There is left-hand traffic in Namibia. An international driver's license is required in combination with your national driver's licence (the driver has been required since min. 2 years in possession of the national driving licence). There's a seat belt duty. The directional speed lies at 60 km/h, on gravel roads 80-100 km/h and on asphalt roads 120 km/h. Compliance with traffic rules and speed limits is regularly monitored by the police. Please be careful to keep track of the speed and not to lose control of your car on damaged roads. For drivers under 21 or 23 years, the car rental companies usually charge a surcharge.

At intersections, the car has an entrance, which is the first to reach the intersection. The road network is good and attractions can usually be reached with a normal rental car. Never underestimate the vastness of the country. Please note that paved roads in Namibia are rather rare. The majority of the road network consists of gravel roads and farm roads. For damage caused by improper use, driving away from the designated roads/paths etc. is not held liable. It is recommended to carry a GPS device as well as a satellite phone. In the Kaokoveld, in the Kaudom and for the last kilometers, an all-wheel drive is required.

The petrol station network is good in larger towns and urban areas. There are few petrol stations in rural areas. IMPORTANT: Do not underestimate the driving distances in Namibia! Refuel wherever possible, especially before traveling longer distances. Make sure you always have enough drinks. At the gas stations, it is often only possible to pay with cash. Usually the tanker gets a tip of about 2-3 NAD.

In the national parks: There is a speed limit of 60 km/h.


Language/Society

Namibia has 7 official languages. In addition to the official English language, various languages such as Afrikaans, Oshivambo, Othjiherero, Nama, Damara and German are spoken.

The whole of Namibia is characterized by friendly people, who are especially pleased when visitors show an honest interest in you, your cultures and customs.


Power supply

The current voltage is 220/240 volt. The sockets are three-pin and plugs have a different format than the German plugs. An adapter that is available in supermarkets is necessary.


Telephone/Internet

The code for Germany is 0049.

In order to make cheap calls from Namibia, send messages or go online, we recommend buying a local SIM card. With some German network providers, roaming options for Namibia can be booked at cheap rates.

The mobile phone network is usually well developed in the area of larger places on the main routes.

Many accommodations offer fast Internet connections or Wi-Fi. In larger cities you can go online in tourist information or internet cafes.

Time Shift

During the Central European summer time Namibia is one hour behind Europe (CET – 1 hour). During the European winter time Namibia Europe is one hour ahead (CET + 1 hour). The time change in Namibia takes place on the first Saturday night in April and on the first saturday night in September.
