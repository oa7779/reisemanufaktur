Etosha National Park - "Great white place"

Experience the Etosha National Park on your individual Namibia round trip with Africa expert Africa Royal Tours. Enquire now and get advice.

09.05.2019

The Etosha National Park in the north of Namibia is part of the huge Etosha salt pan and offers optimal conditions for animal observations.

The Etosha salt pan

The uniqueness of the Etosha National Park is the large salt pan, which occupies about a quarter of the total area of the National Park. The salt pan is 130 km long and 50 km wide so large that it is visible from space itself. This makes it unique in Africa. Etosha means "Great white place" and describes the salt and lime pan very aptly. During the dry season from November to April, white dust and clay is deposited, resulting in a dust-dry level in the Etosha pan. Geographically, the Etosha level was formed as a reduction about 100 million years ago. About 16,000 years ago a river flowed through the Etosha Pan and formed a lake at times. The course of the river changed slowly due to tectonic movements of the earth's plates and the lake dried out. In the end, a huge salt and clay surface, the current Etosha pan, remained behind.

The Etosha National Park

The Etosha National Park is the largest national park in Namibia and can be reached by four entrance gates from the south, east, southwest and north. The park is open daily from sunrise to sunset. The National Park is very accessible to visitors. Well-accessible paths, numerous accommodation options, restaurants, gas stations and shops offer optimal conditions for visitors. From campsites to luxury camps, there is a wealth of accommodation. The three main camps are Okaukuejo, Halali, and Namutoni. For travelers with high expectations, the exclusive Dolomite and Onkoshi camps are also located inside the park. In general, it is recommended to book accommodations early, as the Etosha National Park is very popular.

Stunning wildlife

Despite the heat and large dry area, there is a large wildlife population in the Etosha National Park. Some of the most common and some of the rarest species in Africa can be found. The elephants in the Etosha National Park are among the largest in Africa, as they find special vitamins and nutrients in the bottom of the salt pan.  

The protection of the endangered rhinos in the park is particularly successful. Since 1990 these have been actively protected, so that today a healthy population of about 300 animals can be found in the park.

Four of the Big Five can be discovered in Etosha: elephant, rhinoceros, lion and leopard. Only the African water buffalo has its habitat in other parts of Africa. In particular, the brine springs are very important for many animals, as they serve as salt licks. Especially in the rainy season from November to April small ponds are formed in the salt plain. In rainy years, the entire Etosha pan may be filled with up to 10 cm of water. This natural event attracts thousands of flamingos and other bird species and is a unique spectacle. The dusty Etosha National Park then turns into a green area

Tips for observing wild animals

In the Etosha National Park there are artificial water holes as well as natural springs. Generally, around the water holes there are the best chances to observe large and small wildlife. Especially during the dry season from May to October, animal sightings are very likely, as the animals come from their hiding places to drink at the water holes. In addition, the grass is very low in the winter months, which makes it easier to see the animals. Each water hole has its own characteristics and biodiversity changes depending on the season. There are also many viewpoints in the Etosha National Park. Equipped with a good binoculars, plenty to drink, a little patience and a portion of luck, you can also see animals far away from above.

Employees of the national park, such as guides or the staff of the camps, also like to give tips when which animals are where. There are also guest books in many camps, in which recent visitors write down their experiences and references. Especially for photographers, the Etosha National Park is a highlight, because the silhouettes of the animals can be photographed particularly well in front of the expanses of the salt pan.
