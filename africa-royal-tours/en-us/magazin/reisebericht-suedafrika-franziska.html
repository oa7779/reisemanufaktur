Our Africa expert Franziska in South Africa

Our Africa expert Franziska explored the garden route: from Cape Town to Sanbona Game Reserve to Franschhoek – tips, lodges and unforgettable first-hand experiences.

December 16, 2025

Our Africa expert Franziska takes you on her personal journey along the Gardenroute. From safari adventures to dreamy beaches to luxurious lodges – an authentic insight into South Africa.

Adventure Gardenroute with the rental car

Two girls discover the Gardenroute: A good friend and I are going to South Africa. Our journey along the Gardenroute began in Munich. From there we flew with Qatar Airways via Doha to South Africa and reached Cape Town after about 16 hours of travel. In addition to unforgettable nature experiences and warm encounters with the local people, we also had the opportunity to visit numerous accommodations in person. We also met our local colleagues and partners, gained valuable insights into their work, expertise and regional specificities. These experiences helped me to make our journeys even more authentic and accurate in the future.

Cape Town – vibrant metropolis with a special flair

Cape Town fascinated us from the very first moment. The combination of Table Mountain, Ocean, modern skyline and relaxed atmosphere immediately captivated us. We dived completely into the South African vibe. The city was excellently explored on its own: from the company's garden with a leisurely coffee break through Greenmarket Square to the colourful Malay Quarter. There was so much to discover.

To the Cape of Good Hope we let ourselves be chauffeured – an absolute recommendation to enjoy the spectacular views along the Chapman - Peak Drive.

We stayed at Victoria & Alfred Hotel directly on the Waterfront. The location was ideal for strolling through the souvenir shops in the evening or enjoying a sundowner at Quay. After three intense days in Cape Town, our road trip finally started. The rental car was delivered directly to the hotel, which we appreciated very much, as we saved ourselves the waiting at the airport. Left-wing traffic was no problem for us either. Our first stage took us about two and a half hours inland to the Little Karoo – to the safari in the Sanbona Wildlife Reserve.

Sanbona Game Reserve – Safari in the semi-desert

The Little Karoo formed an impressive contrast to the green garden route. As a transition region between coast and highlands, semi-deserts, rugged rock formations and wide river valleys shaped the landscape. This is the setting that made the safari so special. The sunsets were spectacular, and we watched swimming elephants as well as majestic lions. With a little luck, you even have the opportunity to discover all the Big Five.

The Sanbona Game Reserve offered three very different lodges. The Gondwana Family Lodge was ideal for families and guests who liked to exchange ideas with others. In the Tilney Manor we felt moved back to another time and enjoyed the tranquility of a historic estate. For peace seekers, the Dwyka Lodge with its hidden Tented Lodges offered a great deal of privacy and privacy. So the suitable accommodation was for every taste.

Knysna – charming coastal village on the lagoon

From Sanbona we drove about four and a half hours further to Knysna. The charming coastal village was perfect for a relaxing stopover. At Knysna Waterfront we tried local cuisine – a paradise for lovers of fresh fish, oysters and seafood. Those who wanted to be more active could visit the Knysna Heads or take a detour to nearby parks such as the "Birds of Eden Park".

We stayed in the Falcons Manor, which with a fantastic view over the entire lagoon convinced and historical charm combined with modern comfort.

Plettenberg Bay – Relaxation on endless beaches

After two nights we went on to Plettenberg Bay, known for its extensive beaches. Directly by the sea we stayed in the small Guesthouse Periwinkle. The atmosphere was familiar, almost like in your own living room. Especially we enjoyed the excellent breakfast on the terrace, accompanied by the noise of the waves – pure vacation feeling.

The Robberg Nature Reserve was just a few minutes' drive away and was ideal for relaxing walks along the coast. The various circular paths offered options for each fitness level. Only odor-sensitive visitors should be warned: The nearby seal colony was not only clearly visible, but also clearly perceptible.

Grootbos Nature Reserve – nature and luxury in harmony

Another highlight of our trip was the Grootbos Nature Reserve. Here, luxury and nature merged in an impressive way. We enjoyed excellent gourmet cuisine and selected wines, recommended by the in-house sommelier – in the middle of a dense Fynbos forest. When walking through the vegetation you don't have to be afraid to encounter larger animals except insects and birds. Instead of a classic safari, we experienced a botanical safari. The experienced guides explained the unique, endemic plant world and drove through the reserve with open jeeps – an absolute highlight, especially for hobby botanists. For active vacationers there were also numerous other companies to choose from. Again, there were different accommodations with Garden and Forest Lodge as well as the private villas to be aimed at peace seekers as well as families. 

Franschhoek – culinary conclusion of the journey

The end of our journey was Franschhoek, nestled in the picturesque vineyards. Already the trip over the Franschhoek Pass was spectacular and opened a valley that reminded us of the Lake Garda region. We stayed directly at the winery Le Clé, a real recommendation. The spacious garden rooms led directly to the pool overlooking the vines, surrounded by an impressive mountain panorama.

The breakfast at the large communal table gave us a feeling of home. Le Clé was the perfect starting point for visiting numerous wineries and wine tastings as desired. In addition, the center of Franschhoek with its restaurants and the famous Weintram were just a stone's throw away. Even though parts of the route were occasionally traveled by bus, the well-connected system made it possible to visit many wineries.

Conclusion - The perfect start to discover South Africa

Time went by like it was flying. A vibrant metropolis, impressive safari experiences, picturesque vineyards, spectacular coastal landscapes and dense forest areas – all this combined this journey in a unique way. It was an incredibly exciting tour. Thanks to the rental car, we traveled at our own pace and discovered South Africa individually. The Gardenroute really offered something for everyone and was the perfect start for us to get to know this fascinating country.
