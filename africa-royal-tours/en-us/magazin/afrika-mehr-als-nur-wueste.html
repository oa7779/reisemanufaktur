Africa - More than just desert, elephants and safari

Africa is the second largest continent in the world after Asia. With an area of approximately 30 million square kilometers, Africa covers about one fifth of the total land area of the world and is home to about 13% of the world's population. All 53 countries of Africa together count more than 120 UNESCO World Heritage Sites and rightly so, because the cultural and natural diversity of the black continent is worth being protected.

In order to describe Africa as a whole, one has to look at many facets. The natural wonders of Africa alone range from dry deserts to vast steppes, to ravenous waterfalls and huge rainforests.

When missionary David Livingstone saw the thundering Victoria Falls for the first time in 1855, he described them as the most beautiful thing he ever had in Africa. At the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe, the water masses plunge down to 108 meters and produce a spray mist that rises up to 300 meters.

But not all regions in Africa have so much water. In the deserts, as in the Sahara, the Namib desert or the Kalahari, there is no rain for months. These barren landscapes still offer spectacular experiences. For example, a sunrise in the Namib desert is an incomparable experience. The sand and the dunes shine in all imaginable shades of red and orange and give the viewer the feeling that one is in a different world.

The green and lush wine regions of South Africa are equally impressive. A wine tasting on a traditional winery in the middle of the regions Stellenbosch, Paarl or Franschoek is not only a unique experience for wine connoisseurs. A typical Braai – delicious, grilled meat – and the evening is perfect!

Africa has few islands compared to other continents. Paradise sandy beaches and palm trees can be found in Zanzibar, Madagascar, Mauritius and the Seychelles. A true paradise for sun worshipers, snorkelers and divers.

After the Amazon, the world's second largest, interconnected rainforest is located in the Congo Basin. The forest areas of Central Africa are home to numerous primates, including the gorillas. The mighty silver ridges do not occur on any other continent and are among the endangered animal species, as their habitat is destroyed especially by the clearing of the rainforests. In the Bwindi Impenetrable National Park in Uganda, adventurers can follow the trail of the mountain gorillas and, with a little luck, watch the silver ridges at close range.

But impressive animals do not only live in the rainforests of Africa. The famous "Big Five" – lion, elephant, leopard, buffalo and rhinoceros – can be found mainly in savannas. The "Big Five" are, however, not the five largest animals in Africa, as many believe, but the animals that were formerly the most difficult and dangerous to hunt by large - scale game hunters.

Most of the large animals live in African protected areas, such as the Serengeti in Tanzania, the Chobe National Park in Botswana or the Kruger National Park in South Africa. The richness of species and the size of the herds of wildlife make Africa unique. Another spectacle is the great animal migrations in the Serengeti. Every year at the same time from August to October, around 1.5 million Gnu and Zebra herds migrate from the Serengeti to Kenya in the wetter and greener Masai Mara. An impressive and unforgettable natural spectacle!

As a contrast to the Big Five, you can also search for the Small Five. The five smallest species in Africa are the rhinoceros beetle, the elephant pointed mouse, the leopard turtle, the buffalo weaver and the ant lion. In order to find these fellow species, however, one has to look closely, because they are easy to overlook.

Another facet of the black continent is the moving history of Africa, which is reflected in numerous places. Slave trade and colonization by European states have had a lasting impact on the geographical division of states as well as on various religions and peoples. For example, there are places in Namibia, such as Lüderitz and Schuckmannsburg, or you can find street names such as Kaiserstraße and Bismarckstraße.

The indigenous peoples are equally firmly rooted in African history. One of the most famous peoples is the Massai, which live in the south of Kenya and in northern Tanzania, and especially through the autobiographical novel by Corinne Hofmann "The White Massai".

Africa also has some curiosities to offer. For example, in the Kingdom of Swaziland, the existence of witches has been believed to this day, and by law, they cannot fly higher than 150 meters on their brooms. In the event of a breach, prison sentences or a fine will be imposed by the Authority. Or have you ever heard of the liver sausage tree? The sausage-shaped fruits of the Kigelia Africana are used for the production of medicines and the Africans carve small canoes out of the trunk.
