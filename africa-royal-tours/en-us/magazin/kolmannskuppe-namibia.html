Kolmannskuppe: blown away by the wind

At the beginning of the twentieth century, the German settlement Kolmannskuppe was the richest city in Africa in the desert of modern Namibia. After the end of the diamond boom, Kolmannskuppe now has only one ghost town left – buried under tons of desert sand.

It is a sunken city – not in the water, but in a sea of sand. Plaster leaves from walls, sand floods through shattered window panes, smears the paint from wooden railings, lifts bathtubs from the floor. This is left in Kolmanskop from the once richest city of Africa.

A Sisypho work in the desert

It all starts about a hundred years ago with twenty kilometers of railway line in the desert sand of today's Namibia. In 1883, the German Empire acquired the region as a colony. About twenty years later, the asthmatic railway official Stauch will be transferred here – the climate will do his breathing troubles good, according to the reason.
Day after day, Scuba now fights against the hot desert wind, which repeatedly burys this piece of rail under millions of grains. It is a Sisyphos work that the German railway officer and his local assistant do every day, here in the desert of German South West Africa.

In the Diamond Rush

April 10th, 1908, changes everything: Stauch's African help finds a shimmering stone in the desert sand, August Stauch immediately suspects that it could be a diamond.
After his suspicions have been confirmed, he quits his job at the railway and secures himself at the Imperial Mining Office. Instead of shoveling the sand from rails, he now searches for the expensive gemstones – a task that is much more satisfying, because the pure diamonds hide in large numbers between the grains of sand.
It takes barely three months for the diamond boom to begin and attract adventurers from far away. Now a multitude of lucky knights are digging through the sand next to Stauch – until soon after that the government of the German Empire puts an end to the whole. An area of more than 300 kilometers by 100 kilometers is declared as a restricted area, the Deutsche Diamanten-Gesellschaft alone has the right to dig for the gemstones here.

The rise of Kolmannskuppe

However, dive does not give up the search for diamonds: He founded the settlement Kolmannskuppe on the edge of the restricted zone, a city that is to become the center for the treasure hunters. In the middle of the merciless desert with burning hot days and bitterly cold nights, a German small town with beautiful gabled roofs, Art Nouveau villas and flower borders is created on the walls.
The inhabitants of Kolmannskuppe do not have to do without electricity, water – or even ice. You go to the swimming pool and drink lemonade from the city's own production, for the theater the women put on their silk tights and Sunday hats, on the bowling alley the bowling club "Good wood" is amusing.
The inhabitants of Kolmannskuppe also have access to the first hospital with an X-ray device in southern Africa. It is particularly used to convict workers who have swallowed diamonds to keep the profits to themselves.

Leave and decay

For a few years the inhabitants enjoy the sweet life in the middle of the desert. But already in the thirties, the region is almost completely exploited. Without diamonds there is no reason for the population of Kolmannskuppe to stay. Gradually, more and more people are leaving their homes behind, building up their lives elsewhere. In 1956, the last family moved away from the already spooky abandoned place. There's a ghost town left behind.
Those who visit the surroundings of Lüderitz on their trip to Namibia should plan a detour to Kolmannskuppe – more commonly known as Kolmanskop – in order to see for themselves how the power of the desert takes back a very special piece of German history.
