Franziska in the Seychelles – jungle green and ocean blue

Dreamy island world in the Indian Ocean: Discover Mahé, Praslin & La Digue. A travel report about gigantic granite rocks, jungle & nature

23.07.2026

The Seychelles are considered to be the classic paradise for honeymoon trips and they deserve that reputation. When I went there with a friend, I quickly realized that the islands are far more than just a backdrop for honeymoon. They are an interplay of tropical nature, calm rhythm of life and beaches that seem like painted.

Deceleration from the first minute on Mahé

The journey took Turkish Airlines from Munich via Istanbul to Victoria on the island of Mahé. After about eleven hours of flight, you land in one of the smallest capitals in the world – and immediately everything feels decelerated. 

Left-hand traffic, tropical warmth and a calm atmosphere characterize the first impression. Mahé is green, hilly and characterized by an impressive natural setting. Already the first kilometers with the car show: Here you don't drive fast – but enjoy. Curved roads lead over small mountain passes, always accompanied by views of jungle, coast and turquoise sea. 

Geological peculiarity: The unique granite rocks

What makes the Seychelles unique are its so-called granite islands. Unlike many other islands in the Indian Ocean, they are not of volcanic or coral origin, but consist of ancient granite rocks. 

This geological peculiarity is almost unique worldwide. The huge, round-cut granite rocks, which rise directly from the turquoise sea, shape the image of the islands and give them their distinctive silhouette. Especially on Mahé, Praslin and La Digue this creates landscapes that at the same time seem rough, soft and surreal. 

The combination of white sand, tropical green and grey rocks in the warm light of the Indian Ocean ensures this "watercolor feeling" that makes the Seychelles so special. 
 

Culture, excursions & beaches on Mahé

Mahé is the largest island in the Seychelles and at the same time an ideal entrance to the island hopping. Victoria herself is quickly explored: 

• Victoria Clock Tower, small markets and some colonial buildings are enough for a short walk. 
• Botanical garden: The first giant turtle and the famous Coco de Mer-Palme are found here. 
• Spice Garden & Rum Distillery: The Spice Garden with local spices as well as the Takamaka Rum Distillery offer exciting insights into the local production. 

If you love nature, there are numerous hiking trails on Mahé – such as the Morne Blanc Trail with panoramic views over the island. And the beaches are also remembered: Beau Vallon as a lively classic and Port Launay as a quiet, almost lagoon-like bay. 
 

Onward journey to Praslin

By ferry we continue to the island of Praslin. The crossing takes about an hour – and already here the feeling of the journey changes. Praslin is smaller, quieter and looks even more original. French is more present here in everyday life, life is even slower. Praslin is especially known for its nature. In the Vallée de Mai you walk through an ancient palm forest, home to the famous Coco de Mer. If you like it more sporty, choose the Fond Ferdinand Trail – less known, but with fantastic viewpoints. A special experience is the trip to Curieuse Island. Free-running giant turtles live there, surrounded by mangroves and crystal clear water – an almost surreal place. 

The beaches of Praslin are among the most beautiful in the world. Especially known is the Anse Lazio, which with its white sand, turquoise water and distinctive granite rocks is often considered one of the most famous beaches in the world. The Anse Georgette is somewhat more hidden and quiet, which impresses with its seclusion and exclusivity and offers an almost private bathing experience. The Côte d'Or, on the other hand, is a long, palm-lined beach with a relaxed, lively atmosphere.  
 

La Digue – the decelerated island

Finally we went to the island of La Digue. Time seems to be running even slower here. Cars are hardly available, instead bicycles and footpaths determine everyday life. The absolute highlight is Anse Source d'Argent – a beach like another world: granite rocks, shallow turquoise water and a light that seems almost unreal. La Digue invites you to just go, stop, jump into the sea and let yourself drift. Before the return flight, it is worth a last night on Mahé to calmly end the vacation.

Conclusion: An unforgettable island trip

 In the end, the Seychelles leaves a feeling that is hard to describe: a mixture of calm, colours and lightness. Each island has its own character – Mahé alive and green, Praslin rich in nature and varied, La Digue quiet and picturesque. And back there is a collection of images of infinite shades of blue.
