Travel for Impact – Responsible tourism in Botswana

The charitable organization Travel for Impact is active in the north of Botswana and combines responsible travel with the promotion of local projects.

02.04.2020

Travel for Impact - Goals & Vision

Travel for Impact, or TFI for short, is a social enterprise from Botswana, which aims to connect the travel industry and projects of the local community in the north of Botswana. The four main themes are: Children & Youth, Women, Nature Conservation and Cultural Heritage.

In the spirit of responsible tourism, Travel for Impact uses selected projects to establish contact between the local population and tourism companies. In this way, tourism providers and ultimately travelers can contribute their part to sustainable tourism development. The creation of responsibility and ownership is the key to success.

Actions

In concrete terms, this means that TFIs promote responsible tourism in Botswana through various measures and partnerships. Travel for Impact has maintained valuable partnerships with safari companies in northern Botswana since 2014. These companies generously donate some of their income through the so-called bed banks or the USD 1 fund. The donations collected in this way go directly to the projects and partners of Travel for Impact. Some of the measures are:

1 USD per night: With a Botswana booking via Africa Royal Tours, you automatically donate 1 USD per person per night to Travel for Impact.

Bed banks: As already mentioned, the participating accommodations donate beds or nights to TFI, and forward the full proceeds. With this money, charitable projects are financially supported.

Responsible tourism: Tourism is the second largest industry in Botswana and is growing steadily. TFI is committed to shaping this growth responsibly towards nature, wildlife and the population. All partners have committed themselves to supporting local projects. Among other things, the social, cultural and economic well-being of the community is improved and nature is protected. In this way, Botswana travelers can enjoy the unspoilt landscapes and wildlife while supporting local initiatives.

Partnership: Travel for Impact provides core funding for organisations engaged within the four core issues mentioned above. In addition to financial support, TFI is a permanent contact person and actively supports the projects.

Selected projects

Rehabilitation center Bana Ba Letsatsi

Bana Ba Letsatsi is a rehabilitation center for vulnerable children founded in 2004 in Maun. For reasons such as lack of parental care, child labour, drug abuse or early school leaving, the number of vulnerable children is increasing. Bana Ba Letsatsi offers support to children and adolescents in the form of advice, psychosocial care, training, daily meals, clothing, medical care and sports activities. The aim is to rehabilitate the children and support them to become functioning members of society.

AGLOW – Polokong

The organization AGLOW – Polokong supports people between the ages of 70 and 85 in their home with daily visits, clothing, food shopping, hot meals, cleaning service or escorts to doctor meetings. The aim is to improve the quality of life of older people and to protect them from loneliness. This applies in particular to seniors who do not have family members. Through human care, older people feel that they belong to society and are accepted.

Crafthood

The Crafthood initiative was launched in 2015 and is a central marketing and sales platform for Weber in northwest Botswana. Weaving baskets is a complicated process that requires skill, patience and sensitivity. The craftsmanship of weaving has a centuries-old tradition in Botswana and is therefore an important part of cultural identity. Crafthood supports the Weber with quality controls and product developments and thus creates an independent basis of life for the people. The majority of weavers in the north of Botswana are women, who usually have a significantly lower income than men. With the Crafthood project, Travel for Impact is helping to improve equality between women at the same time.

Discover exciting projects in Maun

Maun is the largest city in the north of the country and the gateway to the impressive Okavango Delta. As a rule, visitors land in Maun and fly directly to the next station of their journey. However, we recommend you stay in Maun for at least one day and take advantage of the local culture and handicrafts. Look behind the scenes and get an insight into people's lives.

You can choose from a tour of a traditional village, a course where you can learn the high art of basket weaving or a demonstration of traditional dances. We are also very happy to organize a meeting with the Travel for Impact partners, where you can discuss the projects in detail.
