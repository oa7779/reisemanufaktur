Zanzibar: Tropical island with its own life

The tropical island of Zanzibar is very different from the Maldives, which consists mainly of tourist resorts, or the Seychelles: Zansibar is an island with its own life.

The name Zanzibar means not only a single island, but the entire island archipelago, consisting of the main islands of Unguja (mostly called "Zanzibar Island"), and Pemba, as well as a number of smaller islands nearby. The capital is Zanzibar-Town, but is only known as "Stonetown" and is located on Unguja, where most tourists are drawn.
The Zanzibaris are 95 percent Muslims, the small rest is composed of Christians, Hindus, Sikhs and various mixed religions. Despite this dominant relationship, the inhabitants are very tolerant when it comes to other religions. In Stonetown alone, there are 60 mosques, four Hindu temples, three Christian churches, a Zoroastrian fire temple and a Buddhist temple.
Zanzibar is a semi-autonomous island state that belongs to the Republic of Tanzania and is divided into five government districts: North-Unguja, South-Unguya, Urban/West, North-Pemba and South-Pemba. Of the approximately 1.1 million inhabitants, about 400,000 live on Pemba Island with the highest population concentration in and around Stonetown. There are about 1700 inhabitants per square kilometer here (for comparison: in Berlin there are about 4,500 inhabitants per square kilometer).

No dairy farming in Zanzibar

Since the 1980s tourism has increased sharply and is still the main economic factor ahead of agriculture. The main agricultural products are carnations and coconuts and the main trading partners of the United Arab Emirates (VAE), India, Singapore, Denmark and Great Britain. Interesting: There is no dairy industry on all Zanzibar. So you can imagine what logistical effort international hotels are facing, for example, offering a continental breakfast.

Average low temperature: 22 degrees Celsius

There are two rainy seasons in the area of Zanzibar: the small rain from November to mid-December and the large rain from the end of March to the beginning of June. The coolest months are June to August with pleasant average temperatures from 22 to 24 degrees Celsius. The warmest months are November to February with average maximum temperatures of 30 to 32 degrees Celsius. For Central Europeans, the dry summer months from July to October are the most pleasant. April and May are less recommended, as the rains are violent and permanent. Most of the hotels and lodges on Zanzibar close at this time anyway.
