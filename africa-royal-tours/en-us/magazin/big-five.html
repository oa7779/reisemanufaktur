Following the Little Five and Big Five in Africa

The Big Five in Africa – elephant, lion, leopard, rhinoceros and buffalo – are the stars in the national parks between Tanzania and Namibia. A nice contrast: the Little Five.

28.03.2022

Africa's wildlife is fascinatingly diverse. Especially in the national parks of South and East Africa, travelers can go on hunting trips on a discovery tour – the sight of lion, elephant & co. in the wild is guaranteed goose bumps. The focus is mostly on the "Big Five", which everyone wants to ban on the camera chip. A fascinating contrast program: the "Little Five" among the animals of Africa. 


 

Experience the Big Five in Africa on safari trips

The term "Big Five" was coined by large game hunters in the 19th and early 20th centuries. They did not have the height in their eyes, but put the five animals on their hit list, which were particularly dangerous and particularly difficult to hunt. Their trophies, from elephant tusks to buffalo heads to leopard fur, were accordingly sought after and unfortunately led to mass shooting in the savannahs of Africa. Hundreds of thousands of wild animals fell victim to trophy addiction. The Big Five are:

  • – Elephant 
  • – rhinoceros 
  • – African buffalo
  • – Lion
  • – Leopard

The times of the big game hunters are fortunately over. Today, visitors to the national parks among others in Botswana, Tanzania, Namibia and South Africa the Big Five, of course, only with binoculars and camera. Over the years, the topics of conservation and responsible handling of nature and animals have become the focus of many parks. In many countries, new protection reserves have been established. As a local traveler, too, one contributes a small portion by only going on safari with responsible partners and being respectful of the animals. And who knows – with a little luck even all five of these fascinating creatures run in front of the camera lens in return.

The giant under the Big Five

The African Elephant is the largest – at least by height and weight. The largest land mammal can measure up to four meters, weighing up to 7.5 tons. Accordingly, he has a tremendous appetite. Elephants eat up to 200 kilograms a day and consume record 250,000 calories: leaves, grass, roots and bark are on the menu.

Females who support each other in the birth and rearing of the offspring, and juveniles live together in herds – led by a lead cow. Elephant bulls, however, leave the herd with puberty and become loners. Elephants also act good-natured, fun is not with them. When an angry elephant bull approaches the safari jeep, you should press the accelerator pedal. The animals are much faster than you might think, and real power packs. 

Other heavyweights: buffalo and rhinoceros

African buffalo (Caffer buffalo) are the largest cattle of the continent and often appear in large herds of 50–500 animals, which, like the elephants, consist of females and their young and walk through their territory on constant tours. Young bulls gather to form bachelor flocks. 

In Africa, we find broad-mouthed and Spitmouth rhinocerosOnly the latter belong strictly to the Big Five. In order to spot them, you have to be lucky – hardly 5,000 specimens still live in the savannahs of Africa today, they belong to the endangered species. The demand for the horn of animals as a potency agent in Asia is great, which guarantees poachers attractive profits. Spitzmouth rhinos are also loners who prefer to hide in the bush. At first glance, they also seem cumbersome: with a top speed of 55 kilometers per hour, the massive animals are real sprinters.

The Big Five Cats

In contrast to the vegetarian elephants, for example, the predatory cats have a desire for meat. Going in the morning and at dusk Lions hunting for their favorite dishes: Zebra, Buffalo, Gnu, Antilope and Gazelle. After the tiger they are the second largest predatory cats in the world with a shoulder height of about 120 centimeters and a weight of about 190 kilograms among the males. 

Some 30,000 lions still live in the wild in South and East Africa, and Serengeti in Tanzania is home to the largest population of around 3,000. The trademark of the males is the flowing mane, which on the one hand protects from nightly cold, but also attracts the women's world, which prefers partners with long, dark manes. 

Leopards were once hunted because of their attractively spotted fur and are today sought after photo motifs. However, they are also loners and know how to hide well. Experienced safari guides, however, know their favorite places, which often lie on trees. In the shade of acacia branches, the predatory cats find peace and quiet to eat and sleep and hide their prey – preferably impalas, gnus and gazelles – from other animals of the savannah.

"Little Five" as a contrast program

The "Big Five" are a fixed size. But who has heard of the Little Five? This is not the smallest animal species, but small animals of the savannah whose name derives from the Big Five. It is worth taking a closer look at them: 

  • – Jumping pointed mouse (elephant shrew)

The nose of the spring mouse is similar to the trunk of the elephant – hence the English name "elephant shrew". The Mini-Rüssel is perfect for sniffing for insects, which they then also push themselves into the mouth with the help of the trunk. The shy animals, who hate to pose as models, are gifted long jumpers – with their long hind legs they hop up to three meters far.  

  • – rhino beetle

With a length of 2.5 centimetres, the rhinoceros beetle is a tiny one and is one of the largest beetles in Africa. In relation to body weight, it is also one of the strongest animals in the world, because it can lift 850 times its weight. The name-giving rhinoceros can't keep up. Like this, the rhinoceros beetle on the top of the head has a characteristic horn to which it owes its name.

  • – buffalo weaver

How the buffalo weavers came to their name will probably remain a mystery. At first and at second glance, they have little in common with buffaloes. Both the black and white buffalo weavers belong to the weaver birds and especially love savannah areas with acacias and monkey bread trees in southern Africa. There they build their artistic nests.

  • – Leopard turtle

The shell of the leopard turtle resembles the leopter's fur in its drawing. In the brooding heat of the savannah she feels particularly comfortable and becomes up to 100 years old. 

  • – ant lion 

A great hunter is the ant lion – the tiny one among the "Little Five". The "little one" – actually the larva of the antsmaid – has it, however, fist-dick behind his ears and brings his victims treacherously to the ground by digging funnels into the sand to catch ants and other insects. With poison, he stuns the prey and sucks it out.
