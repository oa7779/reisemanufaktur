The highlights of the Cape Peninsula

South Africa – a dream country and popular destination. Often the metropolis Cape Town is the starting point of a South Africa Round Trip. In Cape Town you will find the famous Table Mountain, the V & A Waterfront and Robben Island as well as an interesting surrounding area. The Cape Peninsula with the famous Cape of Good Hope offers great landscapes and experiences. Our tip – take at least two days and explore the diverse Cape Peninsula. It's worth it!

The Cape Peninsula

The Cape of Good Hope is the most famous hotspot and first point of contact for many South Africa Travelers. But the Cape Peninsula has much more to offer. South of Cape Town it extends over 35 kilometers. Geographically, it begins in the north at Table Mountain and ends at the Cape of Good Hope and Cape Point in the south. To the west, the impressive panoramic road Chapman's Peak Drive offers impressive views of the Atlantic Ocean and the mountain range of the Twelve Apostles. In the east of the Cape Peninsula, the eyeglass penguins live on Boulders Beach. Like Table Mountain, the entire headland of the Cape Peninsula belongs to Table Mountain National Park and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

It is best to explore the Cape Peninsula with your own rental car and start in the morning to have enough time. Depending on your mood, you can ride the 120 km from Cape Town and back as a round trip or take the same route on the way back and forth. Follow our recommendation and take at least two days - so you will have enough space for short hikes or walks on the Cape Peninsula.

Chapman's Peak Drive

With Chapman's Peak Drive, an imposing coastal road awaits you – on one side the blue waters of the Atlantic Ocean, on the other side steep rock walls. Nine kilometers and 114 curves provide thrills and amazement and connect the two villages of Hout Bay and Noordhoek. Make a stopover at one of the viewpoints and enjoy the unique scenery. Not without reason is the Chapman's Peak Drive worldwide a popular scene for advertising shots of the latest car rental models.

The construction of the coastal road seemed hopeless at first, but after a construction period of seven years and under extreme working conditions, the Chapman's Peak Drive was opened in 1922.

At present, driving the Panoramastraße costs 50 Rand tolls to cover maintenance costs. We strongly recommend that you keep to the straightening speed of 40 km/h, because despite its beauty, the track is winding and requires the full concentration of the driver.

Noordhoek Beach

At the end of Chapman's Peak Drive is the small town of Noordhoek with its beautiful sandy beach. Noordhoek Beach stretches over eight kilometers, is very wide and with its white sand and turquoise sea a great photo motif. Compared to the beaches around Cape Town, Noordhoek Beach is also less visited. Here you will surely find a quiet place to enjoy the sunset.

Due to the strong current it is too dangerous to swim and swim at Noordhoek Beach. However, the beach is ideal for long walks or horseback riding trips. Be prepared, however, that it can become very windy. The wind and the high waves also attract numerous surfers.

Cape Point Vineyards

The Cape Point Vineyards Winery provides culinary variety. Idyllically, it is located on the narrow strip of land between Noordhoek and False Bay to the west of the Cape Peninsula. A unique maritime climate reigns here, which provides ideal conditions for the production of noble and award-winning white wines. The winery is also the only one on the Cape Peninsula.

From Cape Point Vineyards you can enjoy an enchanting view over the vineyards, the vineyard's lake and Noordhoek Beach. The lovingly landscaped winery has an excellent restaurant and of course offers guided tours and tastings. In addition, there are numerous picnic spots in the countryside.

Our recommendation: Every week on Thursday evening the locals of the Cape Peninsula meet here for the so-called Community Market. A large selection of various delicacies is offered at numerous small snack stalls. From typical South African dishes to Italian antipasti to a wide variety of wines, every gourmet will certainly get his money's worth. An absolute highlight!

Lighthouse at Cape Point

Cape Point and the Cape of Good Hope are often confused. Cape Point is a steep cliff at the southern end of the Cape Peninsula. The two lighthouses are a marker for the Cape Point. The oldest of the two lighthouses is 238 meters above sea level and can be reached via several stairs. Alternatively, a funicular railway called Flying Dutchman leads up to the lighthouse and viewpoint. The next highlight awaits you at the top: a unique view of the ocean, the Cape Peninsula, False Bay and Diaz Beach.

It is said that at the Cape Point the warm Indian Ocean and the cold Atlantic meet. It is certain that the water of False Bay in the east of the peninsula is getting warmer than the Atlantic Ocean in the west.

Diaz Beach

Between Cape Point and the Cape of Good Hope there is a bay called Diaz Beach. A walk along the wooden planks path to Diaz Beach and on to the Cape of Good Hope is highly recommended. The Diaz Beach is located between the imposing cliffs and can only be reached on foot. In particular, the ascent is very sweaty and leads over numerous steps. But the effort is worthwhile: fine sand, the turquoise sea and the huge rocks give an impressive picture.

The Diaz Beach is not suitable for swimming due to strong currents and high waves. Nevertheless, take the time to sit in the sand for a while and feel the forces of nature up close.

Cape of Good Hope

Two kilometers west of Cape Point lies the famous Cape of Good Hope with its wooden sign as the ultimate photo spot. To this day, the mistake is that the Cape of Good Hope is the southernmost point of Africa. The reason for this is that the coast on the Cape Peninsula changes its direction significantly and the sailors earlier assumed to leave the Indian Ocean at this point. However, the Cape Agulhas is the southernmost point of Africa.

The Cape of Good Hope bears its name because of the dangers that used to lurk for seafarers in this passage. There are numerous pointed rocks very close to the water surface, so that the risk of collision is very high. In addition, strong winds and high waves at the Cape of Good Hope ensured that at least 23 shipwrecks were already lying on the bottom of the sea.

You can reach the Cape of Good Hope either on foot from Cape Point or via the paved road. Bring some time, because the obligatory photo behind the wooden sign with the coordinates can lead to small queues.

Boulders Beach

If you drive along the east coast, there are some cute animals waiting for you at Boulders Beach in Simon's Town. The penguins from Boulders Beach are an absolute highlight, because where else can you see penguins in South Africa?
The Boulders Beach colony consists of about 1,800 eyeglass penguins and has attracted numerous visitors since 1985. The penguins live in a sheltered bay between round granite rocks. You can reach a platform via wooden bridges, from where you can observe the animals. To protect the penguins, it is forbidden to feed them, touch them, photograph them with lightning or get too close to the animals.

Although Simon's Town is primarily known for its penguins, it is also worth a visit to the center of the town. Here you will find lovingly restored Victorian houses and very good fish restaurants.

Muizenberg

Surely you have already seen photos of the colorful, small wooden houses on the beach and maybe wondered where to find them. The answer: On the beach of Muizenberg on the Cape Peninsula in South Africa. Muizenberg lies on False Bay and is a suburb of Cape Town. The relaxed surfer town is known for its colorful houses and white sharks.

The bright Edwardian Beach Houses are an eye-catcher and now serve as changing rooms. But you should also pay attention to the flags that blow alongside the wooden houses and along the 32 km long sandy beach. In False Bay, white sharks are often spotted so that the flags show the current danger of sharks. Muizenberg has its own observation station, where so-called Shark Spotter watch the sea and warn swimmers and surfers on the beach of possible sharks.
