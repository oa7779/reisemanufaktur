Africa Expert Antonia Süß in Namibia

Our Africa expert Antonia Süß traveled the wonderful Namibia in May 2018 and experienced unforgettable sunsets and wonderful landscapes.

13.09.2019

Africa Expert Antonia Süß in Namibia

Our Africa expert Antonia Süß traveled the wonderful Namibia and experienced unforgettable sunsets, wonderful landscapes and impressive animals. Go with Antonia on a journey through the land of endless horizons. 

Of clocks and time

I have always liked airports: a modern gateway to the world that takes me within a few hours to my destination and to my next adventure. When I stand in Windhuk on the immigration snake, a discussion arises in front of me about who's next. In the eyes of the airport staff, I can formally read their thoughts: impatient Europeans. We have the clocks, but Africans have the time, I think. How much I missed Africa. 

To the Kalahari

When I pass the Bahnhofsstraße together with my tour guide, I recall that Namibia was once a German colony and many testimonies of the former German South-West Africa can still be found in Windhuk. We end the evening comfortably in Joe's Beerhouse, a restaurant popular with locals and international guests. The next morning we leave for Mariental in the middle of the Kalahari. The streets are becoming more and more deserted and the earth is taking on a deep red. Once there, we climb a hill that offers a fantastic view of the desert and the endless horizon. I let this spectacle affect me, and I get a sense of freedom that has always fascinated me about Africa.  

The Elegant Farmstead

Our next destination is northeast of Windhuk in the city of Okahandja. We leave the main road and drive along a lonely road until we arrive in a small oasis called The Elegant Farmstead. There we are received very friendly and I am enthusiastic about the ambience with great attention to detail. The farm exudes a cozy flair and is surrounded by 2,500 hectares of land. 
According to the motto Don‘t judge a book by it‘s cover, this rather inconspicuous place surprises me with a moment that I definitely count among the highlights of my trip. The manager of the lodge awaits us in the late afternoon with a private Sundowner ride across the grounds. We cross grassy landscapes and suddenly see several giraffes eating cozy leaves from the trees. After a while we continue our journey and reach our destination. High up on a hill, with the view of hundreds of acacia trees and the African savannah, we consider our Sundowner. It extends once again the endless horizon in front of us and in the light of the setting sun the landscape is dipped in golden evening light. Slowly I understand why Namibia is known for the beauty of its sunsets. 

Okonjima Game Reserve

A new day in Namibia. We're leaving and heading for the Okonjima Game Reserve, a paradise for animal lovers. As soon as I arrived at the Okonjima Plains Camp, I am already in the open off-road car and we drive through the African bushland. 
One of the highlights in Okonjima is leopard tracking. All leopards are equipped with radio collars. Our guide puts the receiver on the signal of the leopard we want to find today. The closer we get to the leopard, the clearer the beeping of the receiver becomes. The signal leads us into an empty riverbed, where there is no leopard to be seen far and wide. But so quickly our guides don't give up and lead us off the road until we discover in the scrub a hungry leopard who is enjoying her prey for dinner. We are completely focused on the leopard as our guides draw our attention to a rustling in the grass right in front of us. A young leopard can also taste a piece of prey that his mother hunted for him. 
The crowning conclusion was also here an unforgettable sunset over the lake. In front of our eyes, the sun approaches the horizon and begins with a spectacular vision of the most beautiful colors. After an excellent dinner, the day ends as it began: On an off-road vehicle in the midst of the African wilderness. My first hunting trip at night shows a completely different insight into the animal world and leaves me tired but happy to return to the lodge. 

Rain in the Namib Desert

Today the attraction of the South awaits me: The Namib Desert! It follows a long drive from east to west until we arrive. The Namib desert is considered the oldest desert in the world and is a hostile place where you can only hope for rain showers every few years. Not the day I got there. I sit in the car and look at the windscreen wiper, which opens up and knocks off in front of me. It's raining. The locals tell us that it hasn't rained in seven years.
The next morning we leave early and pass the gate of the Namib Naukluft National Park. To the famous dune landscape Sossusvlei we follow a lonely road and then arrive at the place where shuttles for visitors who are not traveling with a four-wheel drive park their cars and take a shuttle to Dead Vlei.
Due to the rain, the way to the dunes is now partly under water. And then it happens. We start towards Dead Vlei when the car suddenly shoots forward and then sinks. A short moment of shock later, we steer the car a little off and see that the root of a tree has stabbed our tires. And so what every Namibian traveler hopes to avoid is: a tyre change in the middle of the desert. However, we are not far from the parking lot and so another guide comes to our help. Meanwhile, we observe how the root of two more cars becomes a disaster. The conclusion of this day: Rain in the desert is not only a blessing.
With new tires we start towards Dead Vlei and Sossusvlei. And finally, the dune landscape that we've all been waiting for is spreading out in front of us. On all sides dunes rise up around us and form an impressive backdrop. After about 30 minutes, the Dead Vlei, a clay pan surrounded by dunes, spreads out in front of us. Since the Tsauchab river changed its direction many years ago, the trees no longer get water, dry up and thus form the characteristic image of Dead Vlei. A little later we say goodbye to the desert and head towards Swakopmund. Goodbye desert and hello sea!

From the coast to the mountains

Swakopmund is an idyllic town on the west coast of Namibia and probably the country's most German city. After a short breakfast we leave to board a catamaran in Walvis Bay. The weather is wonderful and the crew is in a good mood. The fact that I was standing in the middle of the desert a day ago and was sitting on a boat in the Atlantic just 24 hours later confirms the diversity of this wonderful country. 

Erongo Wilderness Lodge

We continue our journey and reach the beautiful Damaraland. Our accommodation for this night is a real highlight: the Erongo Wilderness Lodge. One of the most beautiful accommodations on my trip and a perfect example of the harmony between architecture and landscape. After a hike on the nearby mountain, I enjoy another Sundowner here. In front of me lies the impressive landscape of the Damaraland and behind us the lodge, embedded in the mountain landscape of the Erongo Mountains. A wonderful place for all those who want to live for a few days in peace and seclusion in complete harmony with nature. 
The next morning I wake up with the baboons screaming and decide to take part in a Morning Walk. The guide explains all the details of the flora and fauna in this area. After a strengthening breakfast we set off for the Etosha National Park. 

A rhinoceros close up

We leave Damaraland and a few hours later we are in front of our nearest accommodation, the Etosha Safari Lodge. After a small visit we are already on a off-road car again and start our first hunting trip in the Etosha National Park. We see large zebra herds, antelopes and a rhinoceros at a water site. The rhinoceros always trots leisurely parallel to our path and we can watch it for minutes. 

In the evening, my highlight of the day awaits me: a herd of giraffes that play and drink at sunset, surrounded by acacia trees at a watershed. After we can get away from this sight, we return to the lodge and enjoy a great dinner. 

Crossing the Etosha National Park

Being on safari means getting up early and so my day starts at 6 a.m. We leave from the south of the Etosha National Park and head for the east side. Our companions today are giraffes, antelopes and zebras. We stop at the Etosha Pan and marvel at the infinite expanse that lies ahead. A small leopard was especially good for me, lying right next to the road in the grass. He makes his first hunting attempts and observes an antelope. 
Then we stop at a water point. Although there is nothing to discover far and wide, our guide promises that waiting will be worthwhile. And in fact, after about 20 minutes we see an elephant slowly making his way to us. After he only drinks at first, this soon is not enough for him and he starts a cheerful bath, which entertains all the spectators for the next hour. Several giraffes, zebras and antelopes join him and offer us a beautiful farewell image of the Etosha National Park.
 

Once you've been there, you'll come back

My last day in Namibia starts and it goes back to Windhuk. Now it's time to say goodbye to Africa. But it won't be the last time this time either. "Once you've been there, you'll come back," I was told when I first visited. And it's come true.
