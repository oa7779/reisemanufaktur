Infinite expanses in Namibia

Rusty dunes, a diverse wildlife and a stretch of coast where the sea and desert kiss. We introduce you to the destination Namibia!

21.01.2020


Infinite expanses in Namibia 

The land of contrasts

Rusty dunes reaching to the horizon, a huge salt pan that attracts many animals and a stretch of coast where the sea and the desert kiss. The different regions of Namibia will delight you with great safari experiences, endless expanses and impressive landscapes. Namibia is hard to beat in diversity and an optimal destination for a varied trip to Africa!

A wealth of national parks

Although Namibia is almost twice the size of Germany, only about 2.6 million people live there. Overcrowded roads and densely built cities will not be found here. On the contrary, the seemingly endless streets extend to the horizon. Namibia is the first country in the world that has enshrined the issues of nature conservation and ecotourism in its constitution. Almost 43% of the country's territory is occupied by 20 national parks and numerous private reserves. The variety of national parks ranges from the dry Etosha pan to the Neblige Skeleton Coast and the hot Namib to the green Caprivi Strip.

Caprivi Stripes - Namibia's Tropical Paradise

The Caprivi stripe in northeastern Namibia is so far little noticed, but extremely worth seeing. Here you will experience a tropical and very water-rich Namibia with a wealth of wildlife. The strip extends over a length of 500 kilometers and is a maximum of 100 kilometers wide. It is bordered by the Okavango, Kwando and Sambesi rivers, which offer numerous animals a home and food. The region owes its name to the German Chancellor Georg Leo Count of Caprivi. Under the Helgoland-Zanzibar Treaty with the United Kingdom of Great Britain in 1890, it was agreed that the British will receive Zanzibar and Germany will receive the strategically important Caprivi strip with access to the Sambesi.

Along the highway there are numerous national parks and highlights, such as Popa Falls or Bwabwata National Park. Since here regularly large elephant herds cross the road, signs point to the animals at the edges of the road. Good chances to see more animals can be found on a boat trip on the Kwando. While enjoying the ride, you can watch hippos, crocodiles, elephants and water buffalo on the banks.

Etosha National Park - "Great White Place"

The most famous and largest national park is the Etosha National Park in the north of the country. And rightly so: it offers optimal conditions for animal observation. Unique is the large salt pan of the Etosha National Park, which occupies about a quarter of the total area of the National Park. The salt pan is 130 kilometers long and 50 kilometers wide so large that it is visible from space itself. Etosha means "Great white place" and describes the salt and lime pan very aptly. In the dry season from November to April, white dust and clay are stored here, creating the dust-dry plain in the heart of the National Park.

The Etosha National Park is accessible by four entrance gates from the south, east, southwest and north. It is open daily from sunrise to sunset and is very easily accessible through numerous easily accessible paths, overnight accommodations, restaurants and gas stations. Despite the heat and large dry area, there is a large wildlife population in the Etosha National Park. The elephants of Etosha are among the largest in Africa, as they find special vitamins and nutrients in the bottom of the salt pan. The protection of the endangered rhinos in the park is particularly successful. Since 1990 these have been actively protected, so that today a healthy population of about 300 animals can be found in the park. Maybe you'll catch a glimpse of the rare animals. Four of the Big Five can be discovered in Etosha: elephant, rhinoceros, lion and leopard. Only the African water buffalo has its habitat in other parts of Namibia, such as the Caprivi Strip. The best chances to see animals, you have at the numerous water holes.

In the footsteps of desert elephants

Waterholes are far more difficult to find and thus also wildlife in the region of the Damaraland. Characteristic of this dry and hot area are rugged rock formations as well as numerous dry rivers. Here you will find geological and archaeological features, such as the imposing Fingerklippe or the San-Paintings at Twyfelfontein. The name given to the Damaraland is the Damara tribe living here. A very special experience in this region of Namibia is the search for rare desert elephants. These have adapted perfectly to the living conditions in the desert and usually stay in dry river beds. Since the pachyderms often have to hike for miles in search of water, they have wider soles and longer legs than their counterparts. In addition, they are slightly smaller, can withstand temperatures up to 75°C and can survive up to four days without water.

You have the best chances to discover the thick skins on a guided tour in the off-road car. One of the providers of these hunting trips is the unique Damaraland Camp in the Huab Valley. The luxurious safari tent camp consists of ten comfortable tents, built on wooden platforms and offering a wonderful view of the Brandberg Massif, some 100 kilometers away. The hotel's own rock pool provides cooling off. Also in the Damaraland Camp ecotourism is important: a large part of the staff are from the region and the tents without solid walls are environmentally friendly and close to nature.

On hot soles in Sossusvlei

An absolute highlight of every trip to Namibia is a visit to Sossusvlei. In the heart of the Namib Naukluft Park, this salt-tone pan with the highest dunes in the world is surrounded by sand dunes. The probably photogenic dune is the dune 45, which is located exactly 45 kilometers from the park entrance. The so-called Big Daddy Dune is recommended for adventurous people. At 380 meters, it is considered one of the highest dunes in the world. If you have the goal to climb it, you will leave early to avoid the midday heat. You should also have plenty of water, a headgear and a good sun protection in your luggage. But the effort will be worthwhile, because from the summit there is a wonderful view of the Dead Vlei and the surrounding dune landscape. And one thing is for sure: the path down is much more fun than the ascent.

Once in a Lifetime - Ballooning over the Namib

The much more convenient way to marvel at the oldest desert in the world is a balloon ride over the Namib. As the sun slowly rises on the horizon and wraps the environment in a golden light, the balloon safari starts into the morning sky. The silence is accompanied by the beauty of the Namib, a sea of sand and mountains, an endless view of shade and light. It is amazing what shapes and colors nature has to offer. Let the view wander over the endless expanses of Namibia, while under them Oryx antelopes and ostriches leave their footprints in the desert sand. After about an hour in the air, this unique experience is rounded off with a hearty champagne breakfast.

Wolwedans Dunes Lodge

The perfect place to get to know the Namib is the Wolwedans Dunes Lodge in the NamibRand Nature Reserve. The lodge is located on a plateau and is thus your personal lodge with a fantastic view of the landscape. Each of the nine spacious chalets has a private terrace and large panoramic fronts that reveal the view of nature. Wolwedans is the secret tip for nature lovers looking for unique desert experiences off well-known paths. The center of all activities is the picturesque nature. Whether hiking, hunting, picnics in the wilderness, sundowners in the dunes or a candle light dinner: the excellent guides and experiences will make your stay on Wolwedans unforgettable.

Where the dunes kiss the sea

There are only a few places in the world where desert and ocean meet directly. One of these so-called coastal deserts is the Namib on the west coast of Namibia. The ice-cold currents of the Atlantic give rise to cold air masses. From the land warm winds blow to the ocean, which is why the two air masses are superimposed. The resulting inversion layer prevents warm and cold air from mixing and leaves clouds and rain out. Since the dry periods in the Namib last some decades, it is one of the most hostile and driest regions of the world. The most beautiful place to marvel at the encounter of dunes and sea in the Namib desert is Sandwich Harbour. As part of a guided tour from Walvis Bay, you can take the off-road vehicle to the lagoon. The former port is subject to the forces of nature and gradually ships. Only the roof of the small house of the nature conservation authority can be seen under the sand. The tedious climbing on the dunes is worth it, because up there is an impressive view of the rough Atlantic Ocean, the blue sky and the golden-yellow dunes of the Namib.

The Legendary Skeleton Coast

Another peculiarity of the Atlantic is the skeleton coast of Namibia. The approximately 200 km long coastal section of Skeleton Coast National Park begins north of Swakopmund. Since dense fog often arises in this coastal strip, there are numerous shipwrecks here. Some of these wrecks are well visible from the beach and popular visitor magnets. Animal encounters await you at Cape Cross. The seal colony living here includes up to 250,000 dwarf pelt seals.
Our tip: Spend a few days at the Hoanib Sceton Coast Camp, located in the middle of a dry riverbed. On numerous activities you can explore the Skeleton Coast and enjoy the beauty of nature in the evening from the luxurious tent chalets.
